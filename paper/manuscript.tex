\begin{abstract}
This paper develops a measured-input estimator and a predictive controller
for lane keeping with vehicle collision avoidance. The estimator retains
the ego-motion-aware nonlinear relative motion model of the prior
high-gain design but not its differentiated coordinates: the measured
acceleration and yaw rate remain exogenous inputs, so the ego observer
stays at the order of its own kinematics and the target relative position,
velocity and acceleration form a covariant third-order chain, and neither
zero ego jerk nor zero ego yaw acceleration is assumed. Inverting the
kinematic single-track relation under a declared mismatch bound estimates
the ego sideslip pointwise and turns the GNSS course into a yaw
pseudo-measurement with an exactly computed circular radius, which
replaces the course-as-yaw identification without adding a sideslip state.
The target injection solves a normalized observer LMI whose metric a
unique Lyapunov identity fixes, and a two-multiplier dissipation
inequality certifies its decay against analytic global Lipschitz constants.
All stage couplings being feedforward, a lower-triangular Metzler
comparison system yields a linear copositive ISS certificate, closed-form
ultimate radii without a small-gain condition, and the online error
enclosure the controller consumes. The controller solves one quadratic
program per sample on a scheduled Frenet bicycle integrated exactly under
held inputs: rectangle separation, quadratic road boundaries, actuator,
slip-domain and state-domain rows are hard at every node of a horizon
terminating in a certified rest set. The objective combines input effort
with squared relaxation of a continuous-time control Lyapunov condition
obtained from the cruise Riccati equation. A shifted candidate gives
recursive feasibility without a second optimization. The estimation
theorem is continuous-time; the control certificate covers prediction
nodes of the declared model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{L}{ane}-keeping systems (LKSs) have been extensively studied
and widely deployed in production vehicles \cite{WeiPfefferEdelmann2024}.
Conventional LKSs, however, primarily regulate the ego vehicle's lateral
displacement and heading relative to a lane reference. When an obstacle vehicle
poses a collision risk, avoidance is often left to the driver. This limitation
has motivated considerable research on integrated lane-keeping and vehicle
collision-avoidance systems
\cite{TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013,FahmyGhanyBaumann2018,
ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021,BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022}. These systems retain lane
keeping as the nominal behavior and initiate an automated evasive response when
a collision risk arises.

Collision avoidance has also been studied extensively, but most existing
studies assume that the motion states of the ego and obstacle vehicles are
accurately available. This assumption ignores estimation uncertainty, which can
invalidate controller safety guarantees and increase collision risk. A
safety-critical controller must therefore account for errors in the estimated
states of both vehicles, especially in collision-avoidance scenarios, in which
the ego vehicle often performs complex maneuvers that make the estimation of
surrounding-vehicle states nontrivial. Alai \emph{et al.} developed high-gain nonlinear observers
for obstacle-vehicle trajectory estimation and demonstrated the importance of an
accurate relative motion model for such tracking
\cite{AlaiZemoucheRajamani2024,AlaiSharmaAlexanderRajamani2024}. Sharma
\emph{et al.} subsequently showed that reliable obstacle tracking under complex
ego maneuvers requires a nonlinear relative motion model (NRMM) that explicitly
accounts for ego motion \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}. Their observer
differentiates that model into third-order companion coordinates, which reduces
the design to a single high-gain theorem but introduces derivatives of the ego
acceleration and yaw rate and therefore assumes that the ego vehicle has zero
body-frame jerk and zero yaw acceleration. These assumptions do not generally
hold in the collision-avoidance scenarios
considered here. This paper therefore develops an estimator that retains their
relative motion model but not its differentiated ego coordinates, so that
neither zero ego jerk nor zero ego yaw acceleration is assumed.

Beyond estimation uncertainty, collision-avoidance performance also depends
on the fidelity of the vehicle model and the generality of the maneuver
formulation. To retain online tractability, many existing controllers rely on
reduced-order models and restricted operating assumptions. Examples include
kinematic bicycle or locally linear lateral models whose validity is tied to
small slip, small lateral acceleration, or low-curvature highway operation
\cite{HeZengZhangSreenath2021,TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013,
BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022}. Other formulations use simplified acceleration
models or two-degree-of-freedom vehicle dynamics under small-angle and linear
tire-force approximations
\cite{ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021,AmmourOrjuelaBasset2022,
DingZhuangHuEtAl2024}. These abstractions are appropriate within their intended
operating envelopes, but evasive steering and braking couple the longitudinal
and lateral vehicle dynamics through tire forces and load transfer
\cite{GuoHuWang2016,FahmyGhanyBaumann2018,HeLiuLvJiLiu2019,
BealBoyd2019}. Moreover, the available tire force becomes strongly nonlinear
as the tires approach saturation
\cite{ParkGerdes2017,ForsAnistratovOlofssonNielsen2021,
ThompsonDallasGohBalachandran2024}.
A further limitation is that collision avoidance is often organized around a
prescribed set of maneuvers. Finite-state or discrete high-level controllers
may select among lane keeping, braking, overtaking, left or right lane changes,
and returning to the original lane
\cite{HeZengZhangSreenath2021,AmmourOrjuelaBasset2022,
ChenAksunGuvenc2025}. Other methods evaluate a predetermined single-lane-change
trajectory or a small number of straight- or low-curvature-road obstacle
configurations
\cite{HeLiuLvJiLiu2019,TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013,
BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022,DingZhuangHuEtAl2024}. Such formulations can be
effective in the scenarios for which they are designed, and dedicated methods
have also been developed for emergency avoidance on curved roads
\cite{LaiYang2023}. However, accommodating oblique cut-ins or interactions that
do not conform to predefined lane and vehicle roles may require additional
maneuver modes, transition logic, or scenario-specific redesign. These
limitations motivate a unified collision-avoidance controller based on a
dynamic vehicle model that continuously coordinates steering and
braking without prescribing the avoidance maneuver in advance.

Model fidelity and maneuver flexibility alone do not establish closed-loop
safety. Several MPC-based collision-avoidance controllers impose geometric or
dynamic constraints over a finite prediction horizon and demonstrate effective
evasive maneuvers, but do not prove that collision-free feasibility is
preserved after each receding-horizon update
\cite{TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013,
ForsAnistratovOlofssonNielsen2021,AmmourOrjuelaBasset2022,
ThompsonDallasGohBalachandran2024}. Control barrier functions (CBFs) provide
sufficient conditions for rendering a prescribed safe set forward invariant
\cite{AmesXuGrizzleTabuada2017}, but this guarantee remains conditional on the
existence of an admissible input satisfying the barrier condition. Conventional
CBF--QP safety filters enforce this condition pointwise and typically minimize
the instantaneous deviation from a nominal input. Consequently, feasibility at
the current state does not ensure that safe inputs remain available at
subsequent states \cite{ZengZhangLiSreenath2021}, and a pointwise filter may
fail to recognize an evasive trajectory that temporarily reduces longitudinal
separation while building sufficient lateral clearance, resulting in myopic or
deadlocked behavior \cite{BlackJankovicSharmaPanagou2023}. Predictive CBF and
MPC--CBF formulations mitigate this limitation by incorporating future system
evolution. Zeng \emph{et al.} imposed discrete-time CBF constraints over an MPC
horizon, but identified persistent feasibility as an open problem for a fixed
CBF decay rate and assumed perfect vehicle-position estimates in their racing
example \cite{ZengZhangSreenath2021}. Breeden and Panagou constructed a
path-based predictive CBF, whereas Wabersich and Zeilinger and Huang
\emph{et al.} derived predictive CBFs from finite-horizon optimal-control value
functions associated with terminal safe sets
\cite{BreedenPanagou2022,WabersichZeilinger2023,
HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025}. The presence of a prediction horizon alone,
however, does not guarantee that a collision-free optimization remains feasible
under repeated receding-horizon updates, particularly when state-estimation
uncertainty is considered.

Rather than construct another predictive CBF, we carry the safe
continuation itself as controller state. Lane keeping enters only as a
relaxed continuous-time CLF condition whose squared relaxation is penalized
alongside input effort, every geometric and physical requirement is hard at each
prediction node, and the horizon terminates in a rest set separated from
the obstacle's complete future occupancy. The shifted stored plan is then
a feasible witness for the next sample, which gives recursive feasibility
without a second optimization or a degraded fallback. Section~\ref{sec:estimator}
develops the estimator and its error enclosure,
Section~\ref{sec:control} the controller that consumes it, and
Section~\ref{sec:results} the synthesized design and closed-loop trials.
The estimation theorem is continuous-time; the control certificate covers
prediction nodes of the declared model.

\section{System Overview}

\begin{figure}[!t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.94\columnwidth]
        {driving_environment_curved_v08.pdf}
    \caption{Representative two-lane driving environment. The road-edge
    lines separate the travel lanes from curb-side emergency lanes, each wide
    enough to accommodate a vehicle, while the road centerline lies midway
    between the curb-defined boundaries. The ego vehicle follows the
    nominal path, with an obstacle vehicle cutting into the ego
    vehicle's lane from the adjacent lane.}
    \label{fig:driving_environment}
\end{figure}

We consider an ego vehicle traveling on a road whose drivable surface is
bounded by road curbs. A nominal cruise path is supplied as known route
geometry, while other vehicles travel within
or enter the sensed road environment, as illustrated in
Fig.~\ref{fig:driving_environment}. No particular road shape is assumed. The ego vehicle is equipped with a global
navigation satellite system (GNSS) receiver for global position and velocity
measurements and with an inertial measurement unit (IMU) and a gyroscope
for body-frame
acceleration and yaw-rate measurements, respectively. LiDAR provides
geometric measurements of road curbs together with
relative-position measurements of obstacle vehicles, while camera-based
perception detects obstacle vehicles. The problem addressed
in this paper is to design an integrated perception--estimation--control
system such that, in the absence of a predicted collision risk, the ego
vehicle follows the nominal path at a prescribed constant speed.
When continuing this nominal behavior is predicted to create a collision
risk, the system coordinates steering and longitudinal actuation to maintain
collision separation while keeping the ego-vehicle footprint within the
curb-defined drivable surface, and subsequently returns the vehicle to the
nominal path and prescribed speed once the risk has been resolved. Thus, the
curb-defined road boundary is a hard safety constraint, whereas the
nominal path and reference speed are recoverable nominal objectives.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]
        {ego_obstacle_dynamics_v02.pdf}
    \caption{Ego--obstacle dynamics. The ego vehicle (blue) and an obstacle
    vehicle (orange) travel on courses that may lead to a collision. The
    vehicle frames are centered at the vehicle centers of mass, the yaw
    angles \(\psi_E\) and \(\psi_C\) orient them relative to the inertial
    \(X_I\) axis, the side slip angles \(\beta_E\) and \(\beta_C\)
    separate the velocities \(V_E\) and \(V_C\) from the vehicle
    longitudinal axes, and the course angles \(\gamma_E\) and
    \(\gamma_C\) orient the velocities relative to the inertial \(X_I\)
    axis.}
    \label{fig:ego_obstacle_frames}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:ego_obstacle_frames} details the vehicle dynamics behind
the scenario of Fig.~\ref{fig:driving_environment}: an ego vehicle
with speed \(V_E\) and yaw angle \(\psi_E\) and an obstacle vehicle
with speed \(V_C\) and yaw angle \(\psi_C\) travel on courses that may
lead to a collision. The controller declares one ego model, the dynamic
bicycle with locally linearized modified Fiala tires; the estimator uses only its kinematics and
the kinematic single-track relation between yaw rate, speed and sideslip,
so no cornering stiffness enters any estimation certificate. With
\(\bm p_E\in\R^2\) and \(\bm v_E\in\R^2\) the inertial position and
velocity,
\(\bm v^E=[v_{x,E}\;\;v_{y,E}]^{\trans}=R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_E\)
the body velocity, and
\begin{equation}
    R(\psi_E)=\begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\psi_E&-\sin\psi_E\\
        \sin\psi_E&\cos\psi_E
    \end{bmatrix}
    \label{eq:planar_rotation}
\end{equation}
the body-to-inertial rotation, the position kinematics are
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\bm p}_E=\bm v_E=R(\psi_E)\bm v^E .
    \label{eq:position_kinematics}
\end{equation}
The body-frame force and moment balances are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    m_E\dot v_{x,E}&=F_{x,E}+m_Ev_{y,E}\dot\psi_E,\\
    m_E\dot v_{y,E}&=F_{yf}+F_{yr}-m_Ev_{x,E}\dot\psi_E,\\
    I_{z,E}\ddot\psi_E&=l_{f,E}F_{yf}-l_{r,E}F_{yr},
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:bicycle_model}
\end{equation}
with the slip convention and modified Fiala forces
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \alpha_f&=\frac{v_{y,E}+l_{f,E}\dot\psi_E}{v_{x,E}}-\delta_F,
    & F_{yf}&=\Phi_f(\alpha_f;Q_f),\\
    \alpha_r&=\frac{v_{y,E}-l_{r,E}\dot\psi_E}{v_{x,E}},
    & F_{yr}&=\Phi_r(\alpha_r;Q_r).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:tire_forces}
\end{equation}
For axle \(i\in\{f,r\}\), let \(t=\tan\alpha\), \(C_i>0\) be its
nominal cornering stiffness, and \(Q_i=\eta_i\mu_iF_{zi}\). Following
\cite{FahmyGhanyBaumann2018}, Section II-A, equations (7), (11) and (13),
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_i(\alpha;Q_i)=
    \begin{cases}
    -C_it+\dfrac{C_i^2}{3Q_i}|t|t-\dfrac{C_i^3}{27Q_i^2}t^3,
       & |\alpha|<\alpha_{{\rm sl},i},\\[3pt]
    -Q_i\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha), & |\alpha|\geq\alpha_{{\rm sl},i},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:fiala_force}
\end{equation}
where \(\alpha_{{\rm sl},i}=\arctan(3Q_i/C_i)\),
\(\eta_i=\sqrt{1-\beta^2}\), and \(F_{xi}=\beta\mu_iF_{zi}\)
for \(|\beta|<1\). The same signed input \(\beta\) applies to both axles: negative values brake and positive values request throttle. Static loads are
\(F_{zf}=m_Egl_{r,E}/L\) and \(F_{zr}=m_Egl_{f,E}/L\), with
\(L=l_{f,E}+l_{r,E}\). Each axle represents an equivalent wheel pair.
Here \(m_E\) and \(I_{z,E}\) denote mass and yaw inertia; the lever arms
are \(l_{f,E},l_{r,E}\), and \(\delta_F\) is front steering.
We use the symmetric branch condition and the cited paper's equation (13);
the additional arctangent printed in its equation (12) is inconsistent
with (13). At \(|\beta|=1\), the force law is defined by
\(Q_i=0\), \(\Phi_i=0\), without evaluating its singular polynomial.
Joint derivatives are singular at these endpoints; linearization anchors
are kept strictly inside the input interval.
The controller linearizes this force locally as specified in
Section~\ref{subsec:frenetModel}; separate axle friction-limit analysis
is outside the present controller formulation.
The speed, sideslip, and course of
Fig.~\ref{fig:ego_obstacle_frames} are
\begin{equation}
    V_E=\normtwo{\bm v_E},
    \qquad
    \beta_E=\arctan\frac{v_{y,E}}{v_{x,E}},
    \qquad
    \gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E .
    \label{eq:yaw_from_course}
\end{equation}
The obstacle vehicle obeys the same model with subscript \(C\) and its
own inputs, which are not available to the ego vehicle.

The architecture that realizes this behavior comprises perception,
estimation, and control modules, as summarized in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}.
The perception module processes synchronized camera and LiDAR data jointly
for obstacle-vehicle detection and relative localization. LiDAR data are also
passed to a dedicated road-curb extraction component, whose output specifies
the curb-defined road boundary; the nominal path is known route geometry and
requires no extraction. The estimator combines the obstacle-relative
positions with the GNSS, IMU, and gyroscope measurements to estimate the motion
states of the ego vehicle and each tracked obstacle vehicle. Each
estimate is accompanied by a componentwise error enclosure, valid under
the declared sensor, model and operating-domain premises, with which the
controller tightens its own constraints. The enclosure bounds the current
estimate; propagating it over the prediction horizon is the controller's
own obligation. Because the road boundary describes the environment
rather than vehicle motion, it is supplied directly by the perception module
to the controller, and the known nominal path enters the same way. At each
sampling instant, the
controller constructs the nominal objective and safety constraints from these
geometric quantities, the estimated motion states, their enclosures, and the
prescribed reference speed, solves one convex program, checks its result
independently, and issues steering and longitudinal actuation commands to
the ego vehicle.

\subsection{Obstacle-Vehicle Perception}
Obstacle vehicles are detected in the camera images by a YOLO
detector \cite{RedmonDivvalaGirshickFarhadi2016}, which returns a
two-dimensional bounding box for each of them. The LiDAR point cloud
is then projected onto the image, as shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:obstacle_perception}(a). Each LiDAR point \(\bm q\) is
transformed into the camera frame and from there into pixel
coordinates,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \begin{bmatrix}X&Y&Z\end{bmatrix}^{\trans}
    =\bm R_{CL}\,\bm q+\bm t_{CL},\\
    \begin{bmatrix}u\\v\end{bmatrix}
    =\frac{f}{Z}
    \begin{bmatrix}X\\Y\end{bmatrix}
    +\begin{bmatrix}W/2\\H/2\end{bmatrix},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:lidar_camera_projection}
\end{equation}
where \((\bm R_{CL},\bm t_{CL})\) are the LiDAR-to-camera extrinsics,
\((X,Y,Z)\) are the point coordinates in the camera frame with \(Z>0\)
the optical depth, \(f\) is the focal length, and \(W\) and
\(H\) are the image width and height. The LiDAR points whose pixel coordinates \((u,v)\) fall
inside a detected bounding box are associated with that vehicle, and
the ground and other extraneous points among them are removed,
leaving the points reflected from the obstacle vehicle.

In this paper, the physical outline of a vehicle is regarded as a
rectangle in the top view. A rectangle is therefore fitted to the outermost
of the retained points, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:obstacle_perception}(b), and
the center of the fitted rectangle yields the measured relative
position of the obstacle vehicle,
\begin{equation}
    \bm\rho=\begin{bmatrix}\rho_x&\rho_y\end{bmatrix}^{\trans}\in\R^2 ,
    \label{eq:perception_relative_position}
\end{equation}
expressed in the ego-vehicle frame.

\subsection{Road-Curb Extraction and Tracking}

\subsubsection{Local Geometric Feature Analysis}
A curb is a step of a few centimetres in an otherwise smooth road
surface, so the local geometry of the LiDAR returns changes abruptly
where the beams cross it. Four local geometric features are considered
to capture this discontinuity---adjacent-ring compression, vertical
step, radial jump, and local shape. In the following, \(\bm p_{i,j}\)
and \(z_{i,j}\) denote the horizontal position and height of the
return at elevation ring \(i\) and azimuth \(j\), and
\(d_{i,j}=\|\bm p_{i,j}\|\) its horizontal range.

\paragraph{Adjacent-ring compression}
Flat ground predicts the horizontal spacing between consecutive rings
from the sensor height and beam angles alone, and a raised curb face
compresses the spacing \(\Delta_{i,j}\) observed between ring \(i\)
and its upper neighbor below that prediction \(\hat\Delta_{i}\),
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_{i,j}
    =\frac{\hat\Delta_{i}-\Delta_{i,j}}{\hat\Delta_{i}} .
    \label{eq:curb_ring_compression}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Vertical step}
The height of the curb face appears directly as a step between
same-ring neighbors,
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{i,j}
    =\max\bigl(|z_{i,j}-z_{i,j-1}|,\,|z_{i,j+1}-z_{i,j}|\bigr).
    \label{eq:curb_vertical_step}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Radial jump}
On flat ground the horizontal range varies smoothly with azimuth,
whereas a beam climbing the curb face is pulled inward, which breaks
the smoothness captured by the second difference
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_{i,j}
    =|d_{i,j-1}-2d_{i,j}+d_{i,j+1}| .
    \label{eq:curb_radial_jump}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Local shape}
On the road surface a ring sweeps an arc whose chord is perpendicular
to the line of sight, whereas along a curb the returns align with the
road edge, so the normalized projection of the chord of the same-ring
neighbors onto the line of sight
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{i,j}
    =\frac{\bigl|\bm p_{i,j}^{\trans}
        (\bm p_{i,j+1}-\bm p_{i,j-1})\bigr|}
      {\|\bm p_{i,j}\|\,\|\bm p_{i,j+1}-\bm p_{i,j-1}\|}
    \label{eq:curb_local_shape}
\end{equation}
rises where the ring turns along the curb.

\subsubsection{Curb Fitting}
The road heading \(\varphi\) is estimated from the single longest
near-linear structure among the candidates and is held near a prior
propagated with the ego motion. It defines a road-aligned frame in
which each curb is the quadratic
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{b}}(x)=c_{2}x^{2}+c_{1}x+c_{0},
    \qquad x\in[x_{\min},x_{\max}],
    \label{eq:curb_quadratic}
\end{equation}
one curve per side over a finite forward domain. The coefficients are
fitted to the side's candidate points by least squares.

\subsubsection{Curb Tracking}
A single-frame fit can latch onto a structure that merely resembles a curb and jump far from the tracked
geometry, so the fit of frame \(k\), with coefficients \(c_{i,k}\), is
admitted only through an amplitude-limiting filter on the coefficient
increments,
\begin{equation}
    |c_{i,k}-c_{i,k-1}|\le T_i,
    \qquad i\in\{0,1,2\},
    \label{eq:curb_amplitude_limit}
\end{equation}
and a fit violating any bound leaves the frame to the prediction. The
curve itself is tracked through three points \(\bm p_s\) placed on it
at constant longitudinal stations \(x_s\). Between frames each point
is propagated by the measured ego motion,
\begin{equation}
    \bm p_s^-=A\,\bm p_s+\bm b,
    \qquad
    P_s^-=A P_s A^{\trans}+Q,
    \label{eq:curb_point_prediction}
\end{equation}
where \((A,\bm b)\) is the rigid transform from the preceding to the
current road frame, \(P_s\) is the point covariance, and \(Q\) is the
process noise. The admitted fit, evaluated at the same stations,
yields the measured points \(\bm z_s\) with noise covariance \(R\),
and each tracked point follows the Kalman update
\begin{equation}
    K_s=P_s^-\,(P_s^-+R)^{-1},
    \qquad
    \bm p_s=\bm p_s^-+K_s(\bm z_s-\bm p_s^-).
    \label{eq:curb_point_update}
\end{equation}

\section{Estimator Design}
\label{sec:estimator}

The ego observer is designed first and the target tracker second, since the
relative-position measurement can be propagated only once the ego motion
entering its dynamics is estimated. The measurements are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm y_p&=\bm p_E+\bm n_p, &\quad
    \bm v_m&=\bm v_E+\bm n_v,\\
    \bm a_m&=\bm a_E+\bm n_a, &\quad
    u_3&=\dot\psi_E+d_\omega,\\
    \bm y_R&=\bm\rho+\bm n_R,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:ego_measurements}
\end{equation}
with \(\bm\rho\) the ego-frame relative position \eqref{eq:target_coordinates}
and uniform bounds \(\normtwo{\bm n_p}\leq\bar n_p\),
\(\normtwo{\bm n_v}\leq\bar n_v\), \(\normtwo{\bm n_a}\leq\bar n_a\),
\(|d_\omega|\leq\bar n_g\), \(\normtwo{\bm n_R}\leq\bar n_R\). Inertial
biases are compensated upstream, so no observer carries a bias state.

Two constructions of \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026} are replaced. Their
translational observer differentiates the ego kinematics into third-order
companion chains, which requires \(\dot{\bm a}_E\) and \(\ddot\psi_E\) and
therefore assumes both vanish; here \(\bm a_m\) and \(u_3\) remain
exogenous inputs of every stage and nothing is differentiated. Their yaw
observer reads the course as the yaw, which
\(\gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E\) makes exact only at \(\beta_E=0\); here the
same relation eliminates \(\beta_E\) through a certified inversion.

\begin{assumption}
\label{asm:ego}
\(0<V_E|_{\min}\leq V_E\leq V_E|_{\max}\) and
\(|\dot\psi_E|\leq\bar\omega_E\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}
\label{asm:singleTrack}
The ego kinematic single-track relation holds with bounded mismatch,
\begin{equation}
    \dot\psi_E=\frac{V_E\sin\beta_E}{l_{r,E}}+d_{\rm st},
    \qquad|d_{\rm st}|\leq\bar d_{\rm st},
    \label{eq:single_track_mismatch}
\end{equation}
and \(|\beta_E|\leq\beta_{\rm dom}<\pi/2\).
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{asm:singleTrack} bounds unmodeled rear-axle slip. The
identification it replaces is its special case \(\beta_E\equiv0\).

\subsection{Certified Course--Yaw Correspondence}
\label{subsec:courseCertificate}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:course}
Let \(V_m=\normtwo{\bm v_m}>\bar n_v\), put
\(\underline V=V_m-\bar n_v\), and suppose \(|l_{r,E}u_3|\leq V_m\).
Define
\begin{equation}
    \hat\beta=\arcsin\frac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m},
    \qquad
    y_F=\angle\bm v_m-\hat\beta .
    \label{eq:course_definition}
\end{equation}
Then, with
\begin{equation}
    \bar e_{\sin}
    =\frac{l_{r,E}}{\underline V}
    \Bigl(\bar n_g+\bar d_{\rm st}+\frac{|u_3|\bar n_v}{V_m}\Bigr),
    \label{eq:sine_error_bound}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \bar e_\beta=\max_{\pm}\Bigl|
    \arcsin\bigl(\Pi\bigl[\tfrac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m}\pm\bar e_{\sin}\bigr]\bigr)
    -\hat\beta\Bigr|,
    \label{eq:sideslip_error}
\end{equation}
where \(\Pi\) clips to \([-\sin\beta_{\rm dom},\sin\beta_{\rm dom}]\), one has
\(|\beta_E-\hat\beta|\leq\bar e_\beta\) and
\begin{equation}
    |y_F-\psi_E|\leq B_F:=\arcsin\frac{\bar n_v}{V_m}+\bar e_\beta .
    \label{eq:course_certificate}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
By \eqref{eq:single_track_mismatch},
\(\sin\beta_E=l_{r,E}(u_3-d_\omega-d_{\rm st})/V_E\), so
\begin{equation}
    \Bigl|\sin\beta_E-\frac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m}\Bigr|
    \leq l_{r,E}\Bigl[\frac{|d_\omega|+|d_{\rm st}|}{V_E}
    +|u_3|\frac{|V_m-V_E|}{V_EV_m}\Bigr],
    \label{eq:sine_derivation}
\end{equation}
and \(|V_m-V_E|\leq\bar n_v\), \(V_E\geq\underline V\) give
\eqref{eq:sine_error_bound}. Since \(|\sin\beta_E|\leq\sin\beta_{\rm dom}\),
the true sine lies in the clipped interval, and \(\arcsin\) is increasing
on it, which gives \eqref{eq:sideslip_error}. For the course, \(\bm v_E\)
lies in the disk of radius \(\bar n_v\) about \(\bm v_m\); the disk
excludes the origin, so \(\bm v_E\) lies in its tangent cone and
\(|\angle\bm v_m-\gamma_E|\leq\arcsin(\bar n_v/V_m)\). Subtracting
\(\gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E\) yields \eqref{eq:course_certificate}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The observer therefore carries no sideslip state; where an estimate of
\(\beta_E\) is needed downstream, \(\hat\beta\) serves.

\subsection{Ego Observers}
\label{subsec:egoObserver}

With \(J=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\),
\(\nabla_uz=\dot z+uJz\), and \(\bm v^E=R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_E\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \dot{\hat\psi}_E&=u_3+k_\psi\operatorname{wrap}(y_F-\hat\psi_E),\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm v}^E&=\bm a_m
        +k_v\bigl(R(\hat\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_m-\hat{\bm v}^E\bigr),\\
    \dot{\hat{\bm p}}_E&=R(\hat\psi_E)\hat{\bm v}^E
        +k_p(\bm y_p-\hat{\bm p}_E).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:ego_observer}
\end{equation}
Let \(e_\psi=\psi_E-\hat\psi_E\) on a compatible lift and
\(\bm e_v=\bm v^E-\hat{\bm v}^E\).

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:egoRows}
If
\begin{equation}
    B_\psi=\max\{|e_\psi(0)|,\;B_F+\bar n_g/k_\psi\},
    \qquad B_\psi+B_F<\pi,
    \label{eq:yaw_chart_condition}
\end{equation}
then \(|e_\psi(t)|\leq B_\psi\) for all \(t\geq0\) and
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    D^+|e_\psi|&\leq-k_\psi|e_\psi|+\bar n_g+k_\psi B_F,\\
    D^+\normtwo{\bm e_v}&\leq-k_v\normtwo{\bm e_v}
        +k_vV_E|_{\max}|e_\psi|+\bar\delta_v,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:ego_rows}
\end{equation}
with \(\bar\delta_v=\bar n_a+V_E|_{\max}\bar n_g+k_v\bar n_v\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Inside the chart \(\operatorname{wrap}(y_F-\hat\psi_E)=e_\psi+d_F\) with
\(|d_F|\leq B_F\), so \(\dot e_\psi=-k_\psi(e_\psi+d_F)+d_\omega\) and the
first row follows; at \(|e_\psi|=B_\psi\) its right-hand side is
nonpositive, so the chart is forward invariant and a first-exit argument
closes the induction. For the velocity, \(\nabla_{\dot\psi_E}\bm v^E=\bm a_E\)
and \eqref{eq:ego_observer} give
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\bm e}_v=-u_3J\bm e_v+d_\omega J\bm v^E+(\bm a_E-\bm a_m)
    -k_v\bigl(R(\hat\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_m-\hat{\bm v}^E\bigr).
    \label{eq:velocity_error_derivation}
\end{equation}
Writing \(R(\hat\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_m
=R(e_\psi)\bm v^E+R(\hat\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm n_v\) gives
\(R(\hat\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_m-\hat{\bm v}^E
=\bm e_v+(R(e_\psi)-I)\bm v^E+R(\hat\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm n_v\), and
\(\normtwo{(R(e_\psi)-I)\bm v^E}\leq V_E|_{\max}|e_\psi|\). Since \(J\) is
skew, \(\bm e_v^{\trans}J\bm e_v=0\) and the term \(-u_3J\bm e_v\) drops
from \(D^+\normtwo{\bm e_v}\).
\end{IEEEproof}

The position stage is downstream of \eqref{eq:ego_rows}:
\begin{equation}
    \norminf{\bm e_p}\leq\bar n_p
    +\bigl(\norminf{\bm e_v}+V_E|_{\max}\norminf{e_\psi}\bigr)/k_p .
    \label{eq:position_error_bound}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Covariant Target Tracker}
\label{subsec:targetTracker}

The target model of \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}---constant scalar
acceleration and constant sideslip, hence constant curvature
\(\kappa=\sin\beta_C/l_{r,C}\)---is retained in covariant companion
coordinates
\begin{equation}
    \bm\rho=R_E^{\trans}(\bm p_C-\bm p_E),\quad
    \bm q=R_E^{\trans}\bm v_C,\quad
    \bm s=R_E^{\trans}\bm a_C,
    \label{eq:target_coordinates}
\end{equation}
in which the exact dynamics are the third-order chain
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm\rho=\bm q-\bm v^E,\quad
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm q=\bm s,\quad
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm s=\Phi(\bm q,\bm s),\\
    \Phi=-\Omega^2\bm q+3A\Omega J\frac{\bm q}{V},\\
    V=\normtwo{\bm q},\quad
    A=\frac{\bm q^{\trans}\bm s}{V},\quad
    \Omega=\frac{(J\bm q)^{\trans}\bm s}{V^2}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_chain}
\end{equation}
Only \(\bm v^E\) and \(\omega_E\) enter, and the only extra terms are those
of the rotating frame; \(A\) and \(\Omega\) are reconstructions, not states.
The declared operating set is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    a\leq\normtwo{\bm q}\leq b,\quad
    |A|\leq A_{\max},\quad
    \normtwo{\bm\rho}\leq\rho_{\max},\\
    |\Omega|\leq\Omega_{\max}=\frac{b\sin\beta_{\max}}{l_{r,C}},\quad
    \normtwo{\bm s}\leq S=\sqrt{A_{\max}^2+b^2\Omega_{\max}^2}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_domain}
\end{equation}

Estimates may peak outside \eqref{eq:target_domain}, so the observer
evaluates the extension \(\Phi_e\) obtained by replacing \(\bm q,\bm s\) in
\(\Phi\) by their ball projections onto radii \(b,S\), clipping \(A\) and
\(\Omega\) to their bounds, and using \(\max(\normtwo{\bm q},a)\) in every
denominator. It agrees with \(\Phi\) on \eqref{eq:target_domain} and is
defined on \(\R^4\).

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:lipschitz}
With
\(c_A=S/a\), \(c_\Omega=S\max(a^{-2},2b^{-2})\),
\(c_{Q\Omega}=S\max(a^{-1},2b^{-1})\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    L_q=\Omega_{\max}^2+2\Omega_{\max}c_{Q\Omega}
        +3\Omega_{\max}\Bigl(\frac{A_{\max}}a+c_A\Bigr)\\
        +3A_{\max}c_\Omega,\\
    L_s=\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}
        0&2\Omega_{\max}\\3\Omega_{\max}&3A_{\max}/a
        \end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_2,
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:lipschitz_constants}
\end{equation}
\(\Phi_e\) satisfies, for all \((\bm q,\bm s),(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \normtwo{\Phi_e(\bm q,\bm s)-\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})}\\
    \leq L_q\normtwo{\bm q-\hat{\bm q}}+L_s\normtwo{\bm s-\hat{\bm s}} .
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:global_lipschitz}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Projections and clips are nonexpansive, so they only decrease derivative
bounds, and \(\Phi_e\) is continuous and piecewise differentiable; it
suffices to bound its almost-everywhere Jacobians and integrate along the
segment joining the two arguments. Differentiating in the radial and
tangential basis at fixed \(\bm s\), with \(r=\normtwo{\bm q}\), gives
\(|D_q\alpha|\leq S/\max(r,a)\) and \(|D_q\nu|\leq S/\max(r,a)^2\) for
\(r\leq b\) and \(|D_q\alpha|\leq bS/r^2\),
\(|D_q\nu|\leq2bS/r^3\) for \(r\geq b\), whose suprema over \(r>0\) are
\(c_A\) and \(c_\Omega\), while \(\normtwo{\bm Q}|D_q\nu|\leq c_{Q\Omega}\).
Collecting the four terms of \(\Phi_e\) yields \(L_q\). At fixed
\(\bm q\), the entrywise absolute values of the acceleration Jacobian in the
basis aligned with \(\bm q\) are dominated by the displayed matrix, and its
spectral norm dominates the induced norm; the case \(\bm q=0\) follows by
continuity.
\end{IEEEproof}

The tracker is the covariant high-gain chain
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm\rho}&=\hat{\bm q}-\hat{\bm v}^E
        +l_1\omega\,(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm\rho}),\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm q}&=\hat{\bm s}
        +l_2\omega^2(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm\rho}),\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm s}&=\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})
        +l_3\omega^3(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm\rho}).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_observer}
\end{equation}
Let \(A_c\) be the integrator-chain matrix, \(C=\bm e_1^{\trans}\), and
\(A_l=A_c-\bm lC\). The normalized direction \(\bm l\) solves the observer
LMI in \((\tilde P,\bm Y=\tilde P\bm l,\gamma)\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \min\gamma
    \quad\text{s.t.}\quad
    \tilde P\succeq I,\quad
    \Lambda+\Lambda^{\trans}+2\alpha\tilde P\prec0,\\
    \begin{bmatrix}
    \Lambda+\Lambda^{\trans}+I&-\bm Y\\-\bm Y^{\trans}&-\gamma
    \end{bmatrix}\prec0,
    \qquad \Lambda=\tilde PA_c-\bm YC,
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_lmi}
\end{equation}
an \(\alpha\)-stability region with a bounded-real radar-to-state gain, and
the unique solution of
\begin{equation}
    A_l^{\trans}P+PA_l=-I
    \label{eq:lyapunov_identity}
\end{equation}
fixes a dimensionless metric independent of the scaling of
\eqref{eq:target_lmi}. For the target error \(\bm e_T\) set
\begin{equation}
    \bm\epsilon=\diag(\omega^2I_2,\omega I_2,I_2)\bm e_T,
    \qquad
    W_T=\sqrt{\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(P\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon}.
    \label{eq:target_metric}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:dissipation}
Let \(\bm b_P=P\bm e_3\), let \(t_q,t_s>0\), and let
\begin{equation}
    M=\omega I-t_q\frac{L_q^2}{\omega^2}\bm e_2\bm e_2^{\trans}
    -t_sL_s^2\bm e_3\bm e_3^{\trans}
    -(t_q^{-1}+t_s^{-1})\bm b_P\bm b_P^{\trans}.
    \label{eq:dissipation_matrix}
\end{equation}
If \(M\succeq2\lambda_TP\) then \(\dot W_T\leq-\lambda_TW_T\) in the
absence of exogenous error inputs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
In the coordinate \(\bm\epsilon\) the injection block becomes
\(\omega A_l\otimes I_2\), and the common rotation \(-u_3(I_3\otimes J)\)
is skew and contributes zero to
\(\tfrac{d}{dt}\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(P\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon\). By
\eqref{eq:lyapunov_identity} the injection contributes
\(-\omega\normtwo{\bm\epsilon}^2\). Splitting
\(\Delta\Phi=\Delta\Phi_q+\Delta\Phi_s\) and applying
\eqref{eq:global_lipschitz} with the scaling \eqref{eq:target_metric} gives
\(\normtwo{\Delta\Phi_q}\leq(L_q/\omega)\normtwo{\bm\epsilon_2}\) and
\(\normtwo{\Delta\Phi_s}\leq L_s\normtwo{\bm\epsilon_3}\); each enters the
derivative through \(2\bm\epsilon^{\trans}\bm b_P\otimes I_2\). Applying
\(2\bm z^{\trans}\bm d\leq\normtwo{\bm z}^2/t+t\normtwo{\bm d}^2\) once per
channel gives
\(\tfrac{d}{dt}W_T^2\leq-\bm\epsilon^{\trans}M\bm\epsilon
\leq-2\lambda_TW_T^2\).
\end{IEEEproof}

\subsection{Cascade ISS}
\label{subsec:cascade}

All stage couplings are feedforward. With
\(\bm h=[\omega^2\rho_{\max},\;\omega b,\;S]^{\trans}\), Cauchy--Schwarz in
the metric \eqref{eq:target_metric} gives the directional coefficients
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    g_{Tv}=\omega^2\sqrt{P_{11}},\qquad
    g_R=\omega^3\sqrt{\bm l^{\trans}P\bm l},\\
    g_\omega=\sqrt{\bm h^{\trans}|P|\bm h},\qquad
    g_\Phi=\sqrt{P_{33}},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_disturbance_coefficients}
\end{equation}
\(|P|\) entrywise. Relaxing constant \(A\) and \(\kappa\) to declared rate
bounds adds the physical jerk
\(\dot A\bm e+\dot\kappa V^2J\bm e\), that is, a forcing of magnitude at
most \(\bar\nu_\Phi=(\bar{\dot A}^2+b^4\bar{\dot\kappa}^2)^{1/2}\) in the
last chain equation only. Stacking \eqref{eq:ego_rows}, Lemma~\ref{lem:dissipation}
and \eqref{eq:target_disturbance_coefficients},
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \bm\chi=\bigl[|e_\psi|\;\;\normtwo{\bm e_v}\;\;W_T\bigr]^{\trans},
    \qquad D^+\bm\chi\leq H\bm\chi+\bm d,\\
    H=\begin{bmatrix}
    -k_\psi&0&0\\
    k_vV_E|_{\max}&-k_v&0\\
    0&g_{Tv}&-\lambda_T
    \end{bmatrix},\\
    \bm d=\begin{bmatrix}
    \bar n_g+k_\psi B_F\\
    \bar\delta_v\\
    g_\omega\bar n_g+g_R\bar n_R+g_\Phi\bar\nu_\Phi
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:cascade_comparison}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Cascade ISS]
\label{thm:cascadeISS}
Let Assumptions~\ref{asm:ego}--\ref{asm:singleTrack}, the hypotheses of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:course}--\ref{lem:dissipation}, the operating set
\eqref{eq:target_domain} and the declared sensor and model-error bounds
hold, with \(k_\psi,k_v,k_p>0\). Then \(H\) is Metzler and Hurwitz,
\(\bm c=-H^{-\trans}\bm 1>0\) satisfies \(\bm c^{\trans}H=-\bm 1^{\trans}\)
so that \(V_c=\bm c^{\trans}\bm\chi\) is a linear copositive ISS Lyapunov
function, any \(\bm z_0\geq\bm\chi(0)\) gives the componentwise bound
\begin{equation}
    \bm\chi(t)\leq\bm z(t)
    =e^{Ht}\bm z_0+\int_0^te^{H(t-\tau)}\bm d\,d\tau,
    \label{eq:comparison_solution}
\end{equation}
and \(\limsup_{t\to\infty}\bm\chi(t)\leq-H^{-1}\bm d\). Componentwise,
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm e_{T,i}}\leq c_iW_T,
    \quad
    \bm c_T=\diag(\omega^{-2},\omega^{-1},1)\sqrt{\diag(P^{-1})} .
    \label{eq:component_extraction}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
\(H\) has nonnegative off-diagonal entries and is lower triangular with
negative diagonal, hence Metzler and Hurwitz, so \(e^{Ht}\geq0\) and the
differential comparison inequality gives \eqref{eq:comparison_solution};
letting \(t\to\infty\) and using \(-H^{-1}\geq0\) gives the limit and
\(\bm c>0\). Then
\(D^+V_c\leq\bm c^{\trans}H\bm\chi+\bm c^{\trans}\bm d
=-\bm 1^{\trans}\bm\chi+\bm c^{\trans}\bm d\), which is negative whenever
\(\bm 1^{\trans}\bm\chi>\bm c^{\trans}\bm d\). Inequality
\eqref{eq:component_extraction} is the ellipsoid support of
\(\bm e_i\) in the metric \(P\otimes I_2\) after undoing the scaling
\eqref{eq:target_metric}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The yaw row of \(\bm d\) carries only \(B_F\) from
Lemma~\ref{lem:course}, where reading the course as a yaw would carry
\(\beta_E|_{\max}\), and the velocity row carries \(\bar n_a\) rather than
a bound on \(\dot{\bm a}_E\). Triangularity of \(H\) removes both the
small-gain condition and an auxiliary comparison Lyapunov equation.

\subsection{Gain Selection}
\label{subsec:gainSelection}

Each radius in \(-H^{-1}\bm d\) has the form \(\beta+\alpha/k\) in its own
scalar gain and no interior minimum, so a settling preference replaces an
optimum. With the transit time
\(T_{\rm dom}=\rho_{\max}/(V_E|_{\max}+V_C|_{\max})\),
\begin{equation}
    \min_{k>0}k
    \ \ \text{s.t.}\ \ e^{-kT_{\rm dom}}\leq0.05
    \ \Longrightarrow\
    k_\psi=k_v=k_p=\frac{\log20}{T_{\rm dom}} .
    \label{eq:scalar_gain_rule}
\end{equation}
The bandwidth \(\omega\) minimizes the largest component of
\(-H^{-1}\bm d\) normalized by the declared physical scale of that
component, subject to \(\lambda_T\geq1/T_{\rm dom}\) and
\(\omega>1\,\mathrm s^{-1}\). No sample period enters
\eqref{eq:scalar_gain_rule} or the search.

\subsection{Enclosure Supplied to the Controller}
\label{subsec:enclosure}

The controller of Section~\ref{sec:control} requires a valid radius at
every instant, not an asymptotic one. Both are supplied by the same
comparison system: \eqref{eq:comparison_solution} holds for all \(t\), so
\eqref{eq:component_extraction} gives

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:enclosure}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:cascadeISS} and any
\(\bm z_0\geq\bm\chi(0)\),
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm\rho(t)-\hat{\bm\rho}(t)}\leq B_\rho(t):=c_1z_3(t),
    \quad c_1=\omega^{-2}\sqrt{(P^{-1})_{11}},
    \label{eq:relative_position_bound}
\end{equation}
and \(\limsup_{t\to\infty}B_\rho\leq c_1(-H^{-1}\bm d)_3\). An initial
metric bound follows from componentwise initial bounds \(\bm b_{T0}\) as
\(W_T(0)\leq\bigl((D\bm b_{T0})^{\trans}|P|(D\bm b_{T0})\bigr)^{1/2}\) with
\(D=\diag(\omega^2,\omega,1)\).
\end{corollary}

Inertial reconstruction of the relative vector costs one further yaw term,
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm p_C-\bm p_E-R(\hat\psi_E)\hat{\bm\rho}}
    \leq B_\rho+2\bar R\sin\frac{\min(z_1,\pi)}2 ,
    \label{eq:inertial_relative_bound}
\end{equation}
\(\bar R=\min(\rho_{\max},\normtwo{\hat{\bm\rho}})\), and the analogous
rotation bounds convert the remaining components of
\eqref{eq:component_extraction} into the componentwise ego and target
enclosures \(\bm b_E(t)\), \(\bm b_T(t)\) that the controller reads. These
certify the current estimate; they are not predictions of future observer
output, and their propagation over the control horizon is the controller's
own obligation, treated in Section~\ref{subsec:uncertainty}.

\section{Control Design}
\label{sec:control}

One convex program is solved per sample. Its decision is a complete input
sequence reaching a certified rest configuration, plus one nonnegative
scalar relaxing a continuous-time CLF condition. Collision, road,
actuator, tire and domain requirements are hard; the objective combines
input effort over the performance horizon with squared CLF relaxation.

\subsection{Prediction Model}
\label{subsec:frenetModel}

Let
\begin{equation}
    \bm x=[s,\,d,\,e_\psi,\,v_x,\,v_y,\,r]^{\trans},
    \qquad
    \bm u=[\delta_F,\,\beta]^{\trans},
    \label{eq:frenet_state}
\end{equation}
with \(s,d\) the station and signed lateral offset on the nominal path and
\(e_\psi\) the heading error relative to its tangent. For a scheduled speed
\(\bar v\geq0\) and curvature \(\kappa\), write \(\bar r=\kappa\bar v\) and
\(v_\tau=\max(\bar v,v_{\rm f})\) with \(v_{\rm f}>0\) a tire-denominator
floor. At \(\bar v_y=0\), use
\(\bar\delta_F=\arctan(L\kappa)\bar v/v_\tau\) and compute
\(\bar\alpha_i\) from \eqref{eq:tire_forces} with \(v_x=v_\tau\),
\(r=\bar r\). Define the constant acceleration scale
\(g_\beta=\sum_i\mu_iF_{zi}/m_E\), equal to \(\mu g\) when both axles
have the same coefficient. The nominal ratio is obtained from
\(\beta_j^{\rm ref}=((\bar v_{j+1}-\bar v_j)/T_s+F_R(\bar v_j)/m_E-b)/g_\beta\).
For derivative evaluation only, clip this anchor to
\([-1+\varepsilon_\beta,1-\varepsilon_\beta]\), where
\(\varepsilon_\beta=\sqrt{\epsilon_{\rm mach}}\); the optimization retains
its declared input endpoints. Each axle's nominal force is
\(\bar\beta_j\mu_iF_{zi}\), and road load is modeled separately.
Holding static loads and \(v_\tau\) fixed, define
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    k_i&=\left.\frac{\partial\Phi_i}{\partial\alpha}\right|_{\bar\alpha_i,\bar\beta},
    & h_i&=\left.\frac{\partial\Phi_i}{\partial\beta}\right|_{\bar\alpha_i,\bar\beta},\\
    d_i&=\Phi_i(\bar\alpha_i,\bar\beta)-k_i\bar\alpha_i-h_i\bar\beta,
    & K_i&=-k_i,\\
    F_{yi}^{\rm lin}&=k_i\alpha_i+h_i\beta+d_i .
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:fiala_tangent}
\end{equation}
In adhesion, with \(t_i=\tan\bar\alpha_i\),
\(\bar\eta=\sqrt{1-\bar\beta^2}\) and
\(q_i=C_i|t_i|/(3\mu_iF_{zi}\bar\eta)\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    k_i&=-C_i(1-q_i)^2\sec^2\bar\alpha_i,\\
    h_i&=C_it_iq_i(1-2q_i/3)\bar\beta/\bar\eta^2 .
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:fiala_derivatives}
\end{equation}
In saturation, \(k_i=0\) and
\(h_i=\mu_iF_{zi}\bar\beta\operatorname{sgn}(\bar\alpha_i)/\bar\eta\).
At zero slip, \(K_i=C_i\), \(h_i=d_i=0\). The beta derivative carries
braking--steering coupling directly into the QP. The approximation is local
in \((\alpha,\beta)\); the tire-speed denominator remains scheduled.

The scheduled affine dynamics
\(\dot{\bm x}=A^c\bm x+B^c\bm u+\bm c^c\) are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \dot s&=v_x+\kappa\bar vd, \qquad
    \dot d=\bar ve_\psi+v_y,\\
    \dot e_\psi&=r-\kappa v_x-\kappa^2\bar vd,\\
    \dot v_x&=g_\beta\beta-\widehat F_R(v_x;\bar v)/m_E+\bar rv_y+b,\\
    \dot v_y&=-\bar rv_x-\frac{c_0}{m_Ev_\tau}v_y
        -\Bigl(\frac{c_1}{m_Ev_\tau}+\bar v\Bigr)r\\
        &\qquad+\frac{K_f}{m_E}\delta_F+\frac{h_f+h_r}{m_E}\beta+\bar r\bar v+\frac{d_f+d_r}{m_E},\\
    \dot r&=-\frac{c_1}{I_{z,E}v_\tau}v_y
        -\frac{c_2}{I_{z,E}v_\tau}r\\
        &\qquad+\frac{l_{f,E}K_f}{I_{z,E}}\delta_F
        +\frac{l_{f,E}h_f-l_{r,E}h_r}{I_{z,E}}\beta\\
        &\qquad+\frac{l_{f,E}d_f-l_{r,E}d_r}{I_{z,E}},
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:scheduled_bicycle}
\end{equation}
with \(c_0=K_f+K_r\), \(c_1=l_{f,E}K_f-l_{r,E}K_r\),
\(c_2=l_{f,E}^2K_f+l_{r,E}^2K_r\), and \(b\) a declared constant
longitudinal acceleration bias. The signed ratio maps to total commanded force \(m_Eg_\beta\beta\). Passive flat-road load in
still air is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    F_R(v)&=\tfrac12\rho_{\rm air}C_dA_fv|v|\\
        &\quad+m_Eg(q_0+q_1|v|+q_4|v|^4)\tanh(v/v_{\rm tr}),\\
    \widehat F_R(v;\bar v)&=F_R(\bar v)+F_R'(\bar v)(v-\bar v),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:road_load}
\end{equation}
where the nonnegative rolling coefficients and positive transition speed
are declared model parameters. This reduced model excludes dynamic wheel
inertia, slip-dependent rolling moments, aerodynamic pitch, wind and grade.
The CLF and held-flow predictor use the same affine expansion.
Confining \(v_{\rm f}\) to the tire
denominators keeps the kinematic rows exact at \(\bar v=0\), which
Lemma~\ref{lem:rest} requires.

Each stage is integrated exactly under its held input,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{bmatrix}A_j&B_j&\bm c_j\\0&I_2&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
    =\exp\!\left(T_s\begin{bmatrix}A^c_j&B^c_j&\bm c^c_j\\0&0&0\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix}
    \right),
    \label{eq:held_flow}
\end{equation}
so \(\bm x_{j+1}=A_j\bm x_j+B_j\bm u_j+\bm c_j\), \(j=0,\dots,M-1\), and the
condensed map is \(\bm x_j=F_j\bm U+\bm f_j\) with
\(\bm U=[\bm u_0^{\trans},\dots,\bm u_{M-1}^{\trans}]^{\trans}\).

\subsection{Stage Constraints}
\label{subsec:hardRows}

Every stage carries the actuator box \(|\delta_F|\leq\delta_{\max}\),
\(-1\leq\beta_{\min}\leq\beta\leq\beta_{\max}\leq1\), the selected slip-angle model-domain rows
\(|\alpha_f|\leq\bar\alpha_f\), \(|\alpha_r|\leq\bar\alpha_r\) on the slip
angles of \eqref{eq:tire_forces} evaluated at \(v_\tau\), and the domain
rows \(0\leq v_x\leq v_{\max}\), \(|d|\leq d_{\max}\),
\(|e_\psi|\leq e_{\max}\), \(s\in[\underline s_j,\bar s_j]\).

The slip limits are below \(\pi/2\) and define the chosen model domain;
they do not certify friction feasibility or a bound on the Fiala tangent
error. The ratio input influences both longitudinal force and the local lateral force
through \eqref{eq:fiala_tangent}. No separate front/rear friction
circles, polygon facets, utilization checks or load-transfer limits are
imposed. The affine tangent can depart from the nonlinear force envelope
away from its operating point, so the certificate remains conditional on
the declared scheduled model and any justified residual bounds.

\subsection{Terminal Rest}
\label{subsec:terminal}

The horizon \(M=N+N_b\) extends the performance length \(N\) by a
continuation long enough to reach zero scheduled speed. Every one of the
\(M\) stages carries both inputs and the constraints of
Section~\ref{subsec:hardRows}; no reduced backup model is used.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:rest}
At \(\bar v=0\), every
\(\bm x_R=[s_R,\,d_R,\,e_{\psi,R},\,0,\,0,\,0]^{\trans}\) is a fixed point
of \eqref{eq:scheduled_bicycle} under \(\bm\pi_R=[0,\,-b/g_\beta]^{\trans}\), for
any curvature \(\kappa\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
At \(\bar v=0\) and \(v_x=v_y=r=0\) the first three rows of
\eqref{eq:scheduled_bicycle} vanish identically, \(\bar r=0\) removes the
remaining velocity couplings, \(F_R(0)=0\) gives \(\dot v_x=g_\beta\beta+b=0\) at \(\beta=-b/g_\beta\), and
\(\dot v_y=\dot r=0\) at \(\delta_F=0\), since every retained term is
proportional to \(v_y\) or \(r\).
\end{IEEEproof}

Consequently the rest step may be appended indefinitely, and the finite
program carries an infinite-horizon meaning. Because the resting vehicle
does not move, the terminal separation row must hold against the target's
\emph{complete} future occupancy \(\mathcal O_\infty\), not against its
position at one instant: with \(\sigma_{\mathcal O}(\bm n)\) the support of
the nominal complete-future center trajectory and \(R_T\) the target
circumradius,
\begin{equation}
    \bm n^{\trans}\bm p_{E,M}
    \geq\sigma_{\mathcal O}(\bm n)+R_T
    +\sigma_E(\bm n)+\epsilon,
    \label{eq:terminal_separation}
\end{equation}
for the selected \(\bm n\), where \(\sigma_E\) is the resting ego rectangle
support. Directions along which the target retains persistent velocity or
acceleration uncertainty have \(\sigma_{\mathcal O}=+\infty\) and cannot
certify rest; a finite error radius at the rest time is never substituted
for \(\sigma_{\mathcal O}\).

\subsection{Node Separation Rows}
\label{subsec:geometry}

Separation is imposed in Cartesian space. Over the station interval of node
\(j\) the affine chart of the path polyline satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \bm p=\bm o_j+\bm t_js+\bm m_jd+\bm r_p,
    \qquad \psi=\theta_j+e_\psi+r_\psi,\\
    |\bm r_p|\leq\bm b_{p,j},\qquad |r_\psi|\leq b_{\psi,j},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:affine_chart}
\end{equation}
where \(\bm b_{p,j}\) and \(b_{\psi,j}\) are computed rather than assumed:
the chart error is affine on each rectangle formed by an intersecting
polyline segment and the admitted lateral range, so its extrema occur at
those rectangles' corners, and \(b_{\psi,j}\) is the largest wrapped
segment-heading difference over the interval.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:separationRow}
Let \(\bm n\) be a fixed unit inertial normal, \(\sigma_E(\psi)\) the ego
rectangle support in direction \(-\bm n\), \(L\) an interval Lipschitz
constant of \(\sigma_E\) over the admitted heading range,
\(\bar\sigma_{E,j}=\sigma_E(\theta_j+\bar e_{\psi,j})\), and
\(\sigma_{T,j}\) the target support in direction \(\bm n\) inflated by its
predicted position and orientation uncertainty. If
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \underline g_j=\bm n^{\trans}(\bm p_{E,j}-\bm p_{T,j})
    -\sigma_{T,j}-\bar\sigma_{E,j}\\
    -L\bigl|e_{\psi,j}-\bar e_{\psi,j}\bigr|-\epsilon_j\geq0,
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:collision_row}
\end{equation}
with \(\epsilon_j\) the clearance margin plus
\(|\bm n|^{\trans}\bm b_{p,j}\) plus the directional support of the state
enclosure, then the two oriented rectangles are separated at node \(j\).
Both signs of the absolute value are affine rows in \(\bm U\).
\end{lemma}

Road boundaries are quadratic graphs \(y_{\rm b}(x)\) on finite parameter
ranges. Over the admitted parameter interval \([\underline x_j,\bar x_j]\)
of the ego footprint, \(\max y_{\rm b}\) is attained at an endpoint or at
the single stationary point \(-c_1/(2c_2)\), so one supporting halfspace of
the form \eqref{eq:collision_row} covers the whole footprint exactly; no
sampling of the boundary is involved. A boundary contributes a row only at
nodes whose entire admitted range lies inside its parameter range.

\subsection{Relaxed Continuous-Time CLF}
\label{subsec:clf}

Let \(S\bm x=[d,\,e_\psi,\,v_x,\,v_y,\,r]^{\trans}\) and let \(\bm r_0\) be
the steady cruise of \eqref{eq:scheduled_bicycle} at the local curvature,
obtained from the moment balance \(F_{yf}=l_{r,E}m_E\bar vr_0/L\),
\(F_{yr}=l_{f,E}m_E\bar vr_0/L\) with \(r_0=\kappa v_{\rm ref}\); the rear
slip fixes \(v_{y,0}\), the front slip fixes \(\delta_{F,0}\), and
\(a_0=-b-v_{y,0}r_0\). Put
\begin{equation}
    \bm e=S\bm x-\bm r_0,\qquad V(\bm x)=\bm e^{\trans}P\bm e .
    \label{eq:clf_definition}
\end{equation}
With \(A_e,B_e\) the \(S\)-block of \(A^c,B^c\) at \(\kappa=0\),
\(\bar v=v_{\rm ref}\), and weights \(Q\succ0\), \(R\succ0\), let
\((P,K)\) solve the Riccati equation, so that
\begin{equation}
    (A_e-B_eK)^{\trans}P+P(A_e-B_eK)=-(Q+K^{\trans}RK)=:-W,
    \label{eq:riccati}
\end{equation}
and set \(\alpha=\varsigma\lambda_{\min}(W,P)>0\),
\(\varsigma\in(0,1]\). Both \(P\) and \(\alpha\) are independent of \(T_s\).
Freezing \(\bm r_0\) and \(P\), the Lie derivatives at the current
scheduled point are
\begin{equation}
    L_fV=2\bm e_0^{\trans}PS(A^c\bm x_0+\bm c^c),
    \qquad
    L_gV=2\bm e_0^{\trans}PSB^c,
    \label{eq:lie_derivatives}
\end{equation}
and the performance row, in the form of \cite{AmesXuGrizzleTabuada2017}, is
\begin{equation}
    L_fV+L_gV\bm u_0\leq-\alpha V(\bm x_0)+\delta,\qquad\delta\geq0 .
    \label{eq:clf_row}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The Program}
\label{subsec:program}

Collecting the stage constraints, terminal conditions, separation rows and \eqref{eq:clf_row},
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \min_{\bm U,\;\delta\geq0}\quad&T_s\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\bm u_j^{\trans}R_u\bm u_j+\rho\delta^2\\
    \text{s.t.}\quad
    &\bm x_{j+1}=A_j\bm x_j+B_j\bm u_j+\bm c_j,\\
    &G_j\bm x_j+H_j\bm u_j\leq h_j, && j<M,\\
    &\underline g_{\ell j}(\bm U)\geq0, && \forall\,\ell,j,\\
    &(v_x,v_y,r)_M=0,\qquad \bm u_{M-1}=\bm\pi_R,\\
    &L_fV+L_gV\bm u_0\leq-\alpha V+\delta .
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:controller_program}
\end{equation}
Here \(N\) is the performance horizon, \(R_u\succ0\) is a diagonal
input-effort weight and \(\rho>0\). No input reference, continuation
regularization or safety slack is introduced. The stages \(N,\dots,M-1\)
provide the safe continuation without a performance penalty. The resulting
program is a convex QP with positive quadratic weights on the head inputs
and relaxation. A finite \(\rho\) trades input effort against CLF violation;
it need not select zero relaxation whenever zero relaxation is feasible.
The applied head inputs and relaxation are unique on a fixed convex feasible
domain, although the unpenalized continuation can remain nonunique. Thus
positive definiteness of the complete Hessian, assumed by the regularity
argument in \cite{AmesXuGrizzleTabuada2017}, is not asserted. All geometric
and physical rows remain hard independently of these performance weights.

\subsection{Recursive Feasibility}
\label{subsec:feasibility}

Controller state is the accepted pair \((\bm U^\star,\text{schedule})\).
At the next sample the shifted candidate
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\bm U}=[\bm u_1^\star,\dots,\bm u_{M-1}^\star,\bm\pi_R],
    \label{eq:shifted_candidate}
\end{equation}
with the schedule shifted verbatim and extended by \(\bar v=0\), is checked
against the new program before it is solved.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:recursive}
Assume feasible initial admission; exact execution of
\eqref{eq:held_flow}; a shifted schedule; unchanged applicable road
coverage; and target prediction consistent under the shift with
\(\mathcal O_\infty\) contained. Then \(\tilde{\bm U}\) is feasible for the
next instance of \eqref{eq:controller_program}, and feasibility is
preserved for all subsequent samples.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Shifting the schedule verbatim makes the stage data of overlapping stages
identical, so stages \(1,\dots,M-1\) of the previous solution satisfy the
same \((G_j,H_j,h_j)\) and the same \(\underline g_{\ell j}\) as stages
\(0,\dots,M-2\) of the new instance. The appended stage is the rest step:
by Lemma~\ref{lem:rest} it reproduces \(\bm x_R\) exactly, so its domain,
actuator and slip-domain rows are those already verified at stage \(M-1\), and
\eqref{eq:terminal_separation} holds at the same point because
\(\sigma_{\mathcal O}\) bounds the complete future occupancy and is
therefore shift-invariant. Finally \(\delta\) is unbounded above and only
lower bounded by \(0\), so \eqref{eq:clf_row} admits \(\tilde{\bm u}_0\)
with \(\delta=\max(0,L_fV+L_gV\tilde{\bm u}_0+\alpha V)\). Hence
\((\tilde{\bm U},\delta)\) is feasible, and induction gives the claim.
\end{IEEEproof}

Because \(\tilde{\bm U}\) is feasible and verified independently, a solver
failure requires no second optimization and no relaxation of a safety row:
the command is either an accepted optimizer or \(\tilde{\bm u}_1\). The
guarantee is confined to the prediction nodes of \eqref{eq:held_flow}: it
says nothing about the intervals between them, for which a fixed-separator
margin \(T_s(\bar V_E+\bar V_T+R_E\bar\omega_E+R_T\bar\omega_T)\) would be
sufficient but conservative.

\subsection{State Uncertainty}
\label{subsec:uncertainty}

The enclosure of Corollary~\ref{cor:enclosure} enters as an initial box
\(\bm x_0\in\bm z_0+[-\bm r_0,\bm r_0]\). Under the declared continuous
derivative-error bound \(\bar{\bm w}\) and the stored open-loop input,
\begin{equation}
    \bm r_{j+1}=|A_j|\bm r_j+\bm d_j,
    \quad
    \bm d_j=\left[\exp\!\left(T_s
    \begin{bmatrix}M_j&\bar{\bm w}\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\right)
    \right]_{1:6,\,7},
    \label{eq:box_propagation}
\end{equation}
where \(M_j\) is the Metzler comparison matrix of \(A^c_j\)
(\(M_{ii}=A^c_{ii}\), \(M_{ik}=|A^c_{ik}|\), \(i\neq k\)), so that
\(|e^{A^c_j\tau}|\leq e^{M_j\tau}\) entrywise and \(\bm d_j\) dominates the
integrated forcing, including its transport into other channels. Each
affine row is tightened by its own support,
\begin{equation}
    G_j\bm z_j+H_j\bm u_j+|G_j|\bm r_j\leq h_j ,
    \label{eq:robust_row}
\end{equation}
which is sufficient since \(G_j(\bm x_j-\bm z_j)\leq|G_j|\bm r_j\) and
assumes no independence between components. Between samples containment is
maintained by intersection rather than by an assumed observer contraction:
with \(\bm\Delta=\hat{\bm x}-\bm z^-\) and the new enclosure
\(\hat{\bm x}+[-\bm b,\bm b]\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \underline{\bm\eta}=\max(-\bm r^-,\bm\Delta-\bm b),\quad
    \overline{\bm\eta}=\min(\bm r^-,\bm\Delta+\bm b),\\
    \bm r^{+}=\min\bigl(\bm r^-,
        \max(|\underline{\bm\eta}|,|\overline{\bm\eta}|)\bigr),
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:box_intersection}
\end{equation}
which satisfies
\(\mathcal P\cap\mathcal E\subseteq\bm z^-+[-\bm r^+,\bm r^+]\subseteq\mathcal P\)
about the retained nominal center.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:terminalBox}
Let \(\bar{\bm w}=0\) and let the propagated terminal radius satisfy
\((\bm r_M)_{4:6}=0\). Then \(\mathcal X_R=\bm x_R+[-\bm r_M,\bm r_M]\) is
invariant under \(\bm\pi_R\), and Proposition~\ref{prop:recursive} holds
with \eqref{eq:robust_row} in place of the nominal rows. If instead
\((\bm r_M)_4=\varepsilon>0\), no input sequence renders
\((v_x)_M=0\) for the whole set.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
At \(\bar v=0\) and zero velocity radii, \(A_R\) restricted to pose
perturbations is the identity and \(\bm d_R=0\), so
\(|A_R|\bm r_M+\bm d_R=\bm r_M\) and every point of \(\mathcal X_R\) is
fixed by Lemma~\ref{lem:rest}; the terminal rows of
Sections~\ref{subsec:terminal}--\ref{subsec:geometry} certify the set
because they are already tightened by \(|G|\bm r_M\). For the converse, on
a straight schedule \(v_{x,k+1}=v_{x,k}+T_sa_k+bT_s\), so two admissible
initial speeds differing by \(2\varepsilon\) differ by \(2\varepsilon\)
under every common input sequence and cannot both vanish; moreover the
symmetric interval about zero violates \(v_x\geq0\).
\end{IEEEproof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:terminalBox} delimits the supported class exactly:
pose uncertainty is admitted, velocity uncertainty at the terminal stage is
not. Removing that restriction requires an ancillary feedback tube, in
which the applied input \(\bm u=\bm v+K(\hat{\bm x}-\bm z)\) makes the
error recursion \(\bm d^+=(A+BK)\bm d-BK\bm e+\bm w\) and the
measurement term \(-BK\bm e\) must be enclosed
\cite{KohlerMullerAllgower2021}, together with a physically specified
brake-and-hold mode and a target-motion contract that keeps
\(\sigma_{\mathcal O}\) finite.

the speed and yaw-rate domains alone; a one-second radar dropout raises the
mean to \(2.53\)\,m and the trial maximum to \(34.2\)\,m.

Against the differentiated-companion baseline of
\cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026} on identical truth, noise draws and timing,
the five-seed mean errors at 50\,Hz are
\(0.0469\)\,m, \(0.480\)\,m/s and \(1.82\)\,m/s\(^2\) in relative position,
velocity and acceleration, against \(0.0935\)\,m, \(2.72\)\,m/s and
\(29.6\)\,m/s\(^2\); under a one-second dropout the position errors are
\(0.240\)\,m and \(2.85\)\,m. The mean acceleration peak over the first two
seconds falls from \(346\) to \(34.4\)\,m/s\(^2\).

\subsection{Closed Loop}

The physical experiments in this subsection predate the modified Fiala and signed-ratio input
revision. They used the earlier linear-cornering controller and its former
constraint set. These retained results diagnose that earlier implementation;
they do not validate the revised tire model or establish its physical
performance. New plant experiments are required for that purpose.

\label{subsec:controlResults}

The recorded trials and fixed-state diagnostics preceding the quadratic
comparison below use the preceding
relaxation-only objective, without input cost. They motivate the quadratic
objective in \eqref{eq:controller_program}; they are not measurements of that
updated objective.

Four deterministic 10\,s trials were run against a 14-degree-of-freedom
passenger-vehicle plant: a crossing-target avoidance and its target-free
counterfactual, on a straight road and a 400\,m arc. Parameters were
\(T_s=0.05\)\,s, \(N=24\), \(N_b=74\), \(v_{\rm ref}=15\)\,m/s, target
speed 8\,m/s acquired at 50\,m, \(4.8\times1.9\)\,m rectangles, boundaries
at \(\pm6\)\,m, \(\epsilon=0.25\)\,m. The estimator was bypassed, isolating
the controller.

Both counterfactuals reached contact and both avoidance runs stayed
separated. The minimum sampled separating-axis clearance was \(1.462\)\,m
(straight) and \(1.837\)\,m (arc), the minimum road margin \(7.022\)\,m and
\(6.915\)\,m, and the largest violation of any row of
\eqref{eq:controller_program} was \(1.02\times10^{-6}\). All 800 calls
returned an accepted plan; none used \(\tilde{\bm U}\), and none exceeded
the 50\,ms period, with medians of \(23\)--\(32\)\,ms.

Recovery inside \([9,10]\)\,s was not achieved
(Table~\ref{tab:recovery}): the speed criterion passes and the two pose
criteria fail, although both final poses lie inside the tolerances.
Extending the same two trials to 30\,s on an unchanged controller, the
window \([25,30]\)\,s does meet all three tolerances, with maxima
\(0.2599\)\,m/s, \(0.0300\)\,m, \(0.00768\)\,rad on the straight road and
\(0.2630\)\,m/s, \(0.1639\)\,m, \(0.01453\)\,rad on the arc. Passing these finite windows does not establish nominal invariance or
asymptotic convergence. Replaying the trials
and probing alternative plans at fixed measured states isolates three
mechanisms, all in the performance layer.

At the fixed reference \(P\) splits as
\(V=P_{vv}(v_x-v_{\rm ref})^2+V_{\rm lat}\), and \eqref{eq:clf_row}
constrains only the sum. At straight-road \(t=7.2\)\,s,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \dot V_{\rm speed}=-8.4837,\qquad
    \dot V_{\rm lat}=+1.1918,\\
    \dot V=-7.2918\leq-6.0489,
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:clf_split}
\end{equation}
so the lateral energy grows at zero relaxation. This is not a solver
artifact: linear programs minimizing \(\dot V_{\rm lat}\) at twelve probed
states in the earlier controller return a positive minimum at each, and adding a hard
\(\dot V_{\rm lat}\leq-\alpha V_{\rm lat}\) at \(\delta=0\) is infeasible
at five of the twelve. Next, with \(\bm r_0\) and \(P\) frozen at both
nodes,
\begin{equation}
    V(\bm e+\Delta\bm e)-V(\bm e)
    =2\bm e^{\trans}P\Delta\bm e+\Delta\bm e^{\trans}P\Delta\bm e ,
    \label{eq:quadratic_increment}
\end{equation}
and \eqref{eq:clf_row} bounds neither the quadratic term nor the
flow--tangent gap; over twenty recovery calls the predicted \(V\) increases
in six straight and nine arc calls despite \(\delta=0\) throughout. Last,
all twenty inputs attain the optimum \(\delta=0\) while the optimal set
stays wide: at arc \(t=9.7\)\,s the feasible first steering angle spans
\([-0.0673,+0.0518]\)\,rad, and a certified zero-slack alternative predicts
\(V_{\rm lat}=0.0276\) where the applied plan predicts \(0.0351\).

Proposition~\ref{prop:recursive} was exercised by denying every solver
result after the first sample. In an oncoming-target scenario at 15\,m/s
with \(N=12\), the controller runs on \(\tilde{\bm U}\) through rest and
three samples beyond: over 90 states the minimum rectangle clearance is
\(0.3176\)\,m for \(\epsilon=0.25\)\,m, the maximum deviation between the
shifted stored state and the recomputed one is \(9.95\times10^{-14}\), and
the terminal velocity residual is \(9.40\times10^{-13}\). With the eight
vertices of a \([0.1\,\mathrm m,0.1\,\mathrm m,0.001\,\mathrm{rad}]\) pose
box and exact velocity channels, the case admitted by
Proposition~\ref{prop:terminalBox}, the maximum excess of a true state
beyond \eqref{eq:box_propagation} over 81 samples is
\(6.94\times10^{-17}\).


\subsubsection{Joint quadratic objective}
The objective in \eqref{eq:controller_program} was then evaluated in two
30\,s avoidance trials with the same physical configurations and an
extended road. All 1200 commands completed without fallback or sampled
contact. Minimum sampled SAT margins were 1.0883\,m and 1.5109\,m.
In the complete 9--10\,s window, maximum speed errors were 0.65785 and
0.72103\,m/s, exceeding 0.5\,m/s; lateral/heading errors were
0.000705\,m/0.000194\,rad and 0.11377\,m/0.006580\,rad, satisfying
both pose limits. All three limits hold from 9.40\,s through 30\,s.

In 25--30\,s, acceleration-step RMS decreases from 8.4179 to
0.000199\,m/s$^2$ (straight) and from 8.7075 to 0.30146\,m/s$^2$ (arc).
Straight lateral/heading errors approach numerical zero, but the speed
retains a mean error of $-0.1774$\,m/s. Arc lateral/heading RMS falls
from 0.089892\,m/0.006616\,rad to 0.042872\,m/0.001600\,rad, while
steering-step RMS increases from 0.012272 to 0.015457\,rad. The objective
therefore reduces large acceleration swings without eliminating all
oscillation or proving exact nominal recovery.

Each trial has one call above the 50\,ms period: 109.440 and 73.950\,ms,
at the first target-visible sample. Formulation/witness construction takes
67.684 and 47.461\,ms; QP solution takes 11.655 and 12.058\,ms.
The all-call deadline criterion fails. These trials bypass the estimator,
reuse earlier nominal trials only as historical scenario counterfactuals,
and establish neither intersample safety nor asymptotic convergence.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The two designs are certified at different levels. Theorem~\ref{thm:cascadeISS}
is a continuous-time statement with explicit premises, and its cost is
visible in Table~\ref{tab:ultimate}: worst-case radii and typical accuracy
differ by two orders of magnitude, and for \(\bm e_q\), \(\bm e_s\) the
radius exceeds the domain it bounds. Sampling, held measurements and
integration defects are outside it and are handled by the separate
finite-time argument that produces
\eqref{eq:relative_position_bound}; uniform exponential stability of the
hybrid observer is not claimed.

Proposition~\ref{prop:recursive} is conditional on exact arithmetic and
exact execution of \eqref{eq:held_flow}, and its scope is the prediction
nodes. Proposition~\ref{prop:terminalBox} shows the uncertainty class is
not merely narrow by implementation but bounded by the terminal condition
itself, so a feedback tube and a hold mode, not a larger margin, are what
would extend it.

The historical performance diagnosis in
\eqref{eq:clf_split}--\eqref{eq:quadratic_increment} identifies a
longitudinal--lateral trade and the gap between instantaneous and sampled
decrease. The joint quadratic objective removes the flat head-input cost
and improves measured pose recovery, but speed bias, arc input switching
and first-detection runtime peaks remain. Separate relaxed sampled
conditions are a possible further design: prior feasibility probes rule
out an unrelaxed split, and exact sampled conditions are quadratically
constrained \cite{TaylorDorobantuYueTabuadaAmes2021}. Their feasibility
and closed-loop benefit require separate validation.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Retaining the measured acceleration and yaw rate as exogenous inputs keeps
the ego observer at the order of its own kinematics and the target states
in the covariant chain \eqref{eq:target_chain}, which removes the zero-jerk
and zero-yaw-acceleration assumptions of the differentiated design.
Lemma~\ref{lem:course} replaces the course-as-yaw identification by an
inversion of the kinematic single-track relation with a computed circular
radius, so no sideslip state is carried; on the declared domain it reduces
the yaw measurement radius from \(0.054\) to \(0.0108\)\,rad. The target
injection follows from the LMI \eqref{eq:target_lmi} and the identity
\eqref{eq:lyapunov_identity}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:dissipation} certifies its
decay against the analytic constants of Lemma~\ref{lem:lipschitz}. All
couplings being feedforward, Theorem~\ref{thm:cascadeISS} gives closed-form
radii from a Metzler comparison system without a small-gain condition, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:enclosure} supplies the enclosure the controller reads.

The controller solves \eqref{eq:controller_program} once per sample:
oriented-rectangle separation, quadratic road boundaries, actuator, tire
and domain rows are hard at every node of a horizon terminating in the rest
set of Lemma~\ref{lem:rest}, separated from the target's complete future
occupancy. Input effort and squared CLF relaxation form the objective. The shifted
candidate \eqref{eq:shifted_candidate} makes Proposition~\ref{prop:recursive}
hold without a second optimization or a fallback command, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:terminalBox} delimits the admissible state
uncertainty exactly.

Open questions are an intersample extension of the node-wise certificate,
a terminal policy admitting velocity uncertainty, and a sampled Lyapunov
condition that shortens the settling time measured in
Section~\ref{subsec:controlResults}.
