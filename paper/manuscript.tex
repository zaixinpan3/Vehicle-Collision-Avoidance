\begin{abstract}
Lane keeping, cruise control, and vehicle collision avoidance are usually
handled by separate controllers.
We treat them as one: the vehicle holds its lane at a set speed, and
when another vehicle threatens a collision it steers and brakes around
it, stays on the road, and returns to its lane. The evasive maneuver is
the moment at which the other vehicle is hardest to track from the
moving car, and a safe action that exists now may not exist a moment
later. We design an estimator and a predictive controller for each
other. The estimator keeps the nonlinear relative motion model of
Sharma et al., which accounts for the ego vehicle's own motion, without
the differentiated coordinates that forced that observer to assume
constant ego acceleration and yaw rate; it carries an input-to-state
stability certificate and publishes an error enclosure with every
estimate. The controller is a predictive control barrier function in
the sense of Huang et al., with a terminal set built from the obstacle's
predicted motion and one braking maneuver of the ego, and a control
Lyapunov function for lane keeping, cruise control, and recovery. For the
uncertainty-aware mathematical design, we propagate the nonlinear
plant, future observer updates and causal feedback jointly, preserving the
same model-mismatch sources. Posterior intersections, retained prediction
certificates and a common zero-slack terminal continuation prove recursive
feasibility from an initially feasible problem without fixed tube width.
This robust derivation omits road boundaries. Applying a primary minimizer
makes relaxation nonincreasing and its zero set invariant. In the October 11
baseline, all fourteen
exact-state collision-threat encounters recover cruise without collision;
with the estimator and bounded noise, 74 of 84 recover, and none collides
or leaves the road. The direct-execution revision is not that campaign.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{L}{ane} keeping and cruise control jointly enable a vehicle
to travel within a designated lane at a prescribed speed without
continuous steering or speed corrections by the driver
\cite{WeiPfefferEdelmann2024,XuGrizzleTabuadaAmes2018}.
These functions alone, however, do not
provide the evasive response needed when a nearby vehicle poses a
collision threat, and timely driver intervention may still be required
\cite{ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021}. Integrating lane keeping and cruise control
with vehicle collision avoidance has therefore attracted sustained research interest
\cite{TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013,FahmyGhanyBaumann2018,
ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021,BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022}.

Artificial-potential-field and virtual-flow designs demonstrate evasive
path generation and lane-keeping integration, but their reported
avoidance results do not establish forward invariance of a
collision-free set \cite{WangGaoLiSunCheng2019,ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021}.
Control barrier functions (CBFs) provide an invariance condition, but
that condition must admit a control from the actual input set
throughout the claimed safe domain \cite{AmesXuGrizzleTabuada2017}.
For a vehicle with finite braking and turning authority, instantaneous
geometric clearance does not imply that collision remains avoidable.
A position-only distance function is therefore insufficient as a safety
certificate unless its derivative conditions and admissible state
domain are verified for the constrained dynamics; higher-order
constructions likewise require input compatibility
\cite{XiaoBelta2022}. This distinction has practical consequences:
Tayal \emph{et al.}'s collision-cone CBF analysis assumes unlimited
control authority, and the paper reports hardware failures caused by
saturation in high-speed cases \cite{TayalEtAl2026}.
Adding prediction does not by itself close the feasibility gap:
Zeng \emph{et al.}'s CBF-constrained model predictive control (MPC)
leaves persistent feasibility open \cite{ZengZhangSreenath2021}, while
Black \emph{et al.}'s future-focused CBFs demonstrate improved
feasibility empirically but leave its theoretical guarantee to future
work \cite{BlackJankovicSharmaPanagou2023}. The vehicle model also
restricts which avoidance maneuvers these guarantees can cover. The
steering-only formulation of Br\"uggemann \emph{et al.} fixes
longitudinal speed, excluding combined steering--braking maneuvers
\cite{BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022}. During such maneuvers, longitudinal
and lateral motion are dynamically coupled and share tire-force
capacity \cite{GuoHuWang2016,BealBoyd2019}; neglecting these
interactions can admit predicted force combinations that are jointly
unattainable. Coordinated steering and braking address the coupling,
but the tracking analysis in \cite{GuoHuWang2016} does not establish
invariance of a collision-free set. A safety guarantee for exact target
states does not extend to estimated states without an error analysis.
For example, the lane-change controller in
\cite{HeZengZhangSreenath2021} assumes accurate, delay-free perception
and leaves estimation and noisy measurements to future work. Evasive
ego motion further couples relative target dynamics to the ego
vehicle's translation and rotation, making an ego-motion-consistent
estimation model necessary
\cite{AlaiSharmaAlexanderRajamani2024,SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}.
These limitations motivate a safety and recursive-feasibility analysis
that combines coupled steering--braking dynamics, jointly realizable
control actions, and explicit bounds on target-state estimation error.

Two lines of prior work supply what such a system needs, and each stops
short. On the estimation side, Rajamani and co-workers showed that
tracking another vehicle from a maneuvering car requires a relative
motion model that accounts for the ego vehicle's own motion
\cite{JeonZemoucheRajamani2019,AlaiZemoucheRajamani2024,
AlaiSharmaAlexanderRajamani2024}, and Sharma \emph{et al.} built a
multistage high-gain observer on such a nonlinear relative motion model
(NRMM) and validated it on a full-scale vehicle
\cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}. Their observer, however, differentiates
the ego kinematics into companion coordinates and assumes constant
measured body acceleration and yaw rate, which an evasive maneuver can
violate. It also does not supply the online error enclosure required by
the safety constraints here. On the control side, the predictive
control barrier function (PCBF) of Huang \emph{et al.}
\cite{HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025} turns a finite-horizon problem into
a barrier function: the minimal relaxation of the state constraints over
the horizon is nonincreasing along the closed loop, so safety is
recovered whenever recovery is possible and, once recovered, is kept,
because the shifted plan remains available from one sample to the next.
Related constructions reach a barrier from a finite-horizon value
function or a backup policy
\cite{WabersichZeilinger2023,BreedenPanagou2022,
ChenJankovicSantilloAmes2021,ZengZhangSreenath2021}. Huang et al.'s
formulation uses an exactly known state and a time-invariant problem.
Its application to an estimated moving obstacle therefore requires
accounting for state-estimation error and for revisions of the forecast,
constraints and terminal set at successive samples.

This paper brings the two together. The estimator keeps Sharma's NRMM
but not its differentiated ego coordinates, so that measured
acceleration and yaw rate stay exogenous inputs and nothing is
differentiated; it remains valid during the ego's own maneuvers, carries
a cascade input-to-state stability (ISS) certificate, and publishes with
every estimate a bound on its error. The controller is a PCBF applied to
vehicle collision avoidance on a dynamic bicycle with modified Fiala
tires. Safety comes first, through a terminal set built from the
obstacle's own predicted motion and one braking maneuver of the ego: past
the horizon the obstacle keeps its forecast and the ego brakes to rest
with its steering held, and an endpoint is terminal when that joint
continuation stays on the road and clears the obstacle until it has left
the sensing range. Lane keeping, cruise control, and post-avoidance
recovery come second, through a quadratic control Lyapunov function (CLF) certified for a
cruise law on the sampled nonlinear model. The two parts are designed for
each other. The estimator measures the lateral velocity through the
lateral force balance of the controller's own tire model under the input
the controller holds; the controller keeps the car inside the sideslip
cone and below the rear-tire saturation at which that measurement would
stop being informative; and the estimator's enclosure tightens the
controller's constraints.

The mathematical link between the certificates is developed in
Section~\ref{subsec:robust}. One joint nonlinear prediction includes the
physical states, future measurements, observer updates and causal control
laws, with the same model-mismatch realization entering all affected
equations. Intersecting the next enclosure with the old reachable set
preserves the information needed to shift the policy and retain its tube
certificates. A common admissible zero-slack terminal continuation closes
the proof. Under the declared uncertainty contracts and initial feasibility,
the resulting mathematical problem is recursively feasible; executing a
primary minimizer also makes the optimal relaxation nonincreasing and its
zero set invariant. Tube widths may vary. This derivation temporarily
omits road boundaries, while the numerical controller and experiments
retain them. Its existence proof is separate from numerical solver return.

The contributions are the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item An estimator that retains the ego-motion-aware NRMM of
\cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026} without its zero-jerk and zero-yaw-%
acceleration assumptions, with a continuous-time cascade ISS certificate,
target gains chosen inside that certificate by a semidefinite program,
a model-aided lateral-velocity measurement shared with the controller,
and a published error enclosure (Section~\ref{sec:estimator}).
\item A PCBF controller for collision avoidance without maneuver
templates: one nonlinear rollout per sample on the Fiala bicycle, two
second-order cone programs in strict priority order, a terminal set
built from the obstacle's motion model and one braking law that is
forward invariant and makes the problem recursively feasible on the
declared model, and one CLF for lane keeping, cruise control, and recovery
(Section~\ref{sec:control}).
\item A joint nonlinear observer--controller formulation that propagates
future measurement feedback and shared model mismatch, with sound finite
image enclosures, retained tube certificates and a common terminal braking
rule. Posterior intersections and policy shifting prove recursive
feasibility of the road-free mathematical problem and optimal-relaxation
descent under the stated contracts (Section~\ref{subsec:robust}).
\item A closed-loop evaluation in fourteen collision-threat encounters
with exact states and with the estimator and bounded sensor noise for
six seeds, with an audit of the published enclosures against the truth
(Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}
Section~\ref{sec:overview} states the problem, the models and the
architecture, and lays out the safety argument as a chain of statements
whose status the rest of the paper reports. Throughout we separate what
is proved, under which premises, from what is measured.

\subsection{The Task}

An ego vehicle drives on a road bounded by curbs, along a nominal path
that is given as route geometry, straight or of constant curvature, at
a prescribed speed (Fig.~\ref{fig:driving_environment}). Other vehicles
travel within or enter the sensed environment. The vehicle carries a
global navigation satellite system (GNSS) receiver that measures its
position and velocity, an inertial unit that measures body-frame
acceleration and yaw rate, and a LiDAR and a camera that together
measure the relative position of one obstacle vehicle. We want the
following. While no collision is predicted, the ego follows the path at
the prescribed speed. When continuing to do so would lead to a
collision, it coordinates steering and longitudinal actuation so that
the two bodies stay apart and its own body stays on the paved road; it
may leave its lane for that. Once the risk is resolved it returns to the
path and the speed. The road edge is a physical constraint; the path and
the speed are objectives to be recovered. In the experiments of
Section~\ref{sec:results} the road edges enter as declared lateral
clearances on either side of the path, and the maneuvers leave the lane
but stay on the road.

\subsection{Vehicle Models}
\label{subsec:models}

Fig.~\ref{fig:ego_obstacle_frames} fixes the notation. The ego vehicle
has speed \(V_E\) and yaw \(\psi_E\), the obstacle speed \(V_C\) and yaw
\(\psi_C\). One ego model serves the whole system, a dynamic bicycle
with modified Fiala tires: the controller predicts with it, and the
estimator inverts its lateral force balance to measure the lateral
velocity (Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided}). With \(\bm p_E\in\R^2\) and
\(\bm v_E\in\R^2\) the inertial position and velocity,
\(\bm v^E=[v_{x,E}\;\;v_{y,E}]^{\trans}=R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_E\)
the body velocity, and
\begin{equation}
    R(\psi_E)=\begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\psi_E&-\sin\psi_E\\
        \sin\psi_E&\cos\psi_E
    \end{bmatrix}
    \label{eq:planar_rotation}
\end{equation}
the body-to-inertial rotation, the position kinematics are
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\bm p}_E=\bm v_E=R(\psi_E)\bm v^E .
    \label{eq:position_kinematics}
\end{equation}
The force and moment balances in the body frame read
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    m_E\dot v_{x,E}&=F_{xf}^{b}+F_{xr}-F_R(v_{x,E})
        +m_Ev_{y,E}\dot\psi_E,\\
    m_E\dot v_{y,E}&=F_{yf}^{b}+F_{yr}-m_Ev_{x,E}\dot\psi_E,\\
    I_{z,E}\ddot\psi_E&=l_{f,E}F_{yf}^{b}-l_{r,E}F_{yr},
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:bicycle_model}
\end{equation}
where $[F_{xf}^{b},F_{yf}^{b}]^{\trans}
=R(\delta_F)[F_{xf},F_{yf}]^{\trans}$ rotates the front wheel forces into
the body frame and $F_R$ is the passive road load
\eqref{eq:road_load}. The slip angles and lateral forces are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \alpha_f=\operatorname{atan2}(v_{y,E}+l_{f,E}\dot\psi_E,\,v_{x,E})
        -\delta_F,\\
    \alpha_r=\operatorname{atan2}(v_{y,E}-l_{r,E}\dot\psi_E,\,v_{x,E}),\\
    F_{yf}=\Phi_f(\alpha_f;Q_f),\qquad F_{yr}=\Phi_r(\alpha_r;Q_r).
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:tire_forces}
\end{equation}
For axle \(i\in\{f,r\}\), with \(t=\tan\alpha\), \(C_i>0\) the
nominal cornering stiffness and \(Q_i=\eta_i\mu_iF_{zi}\), the modified
Fiala law of \cite[Sec.~II-A, eqs.~(7), (11) and (13)]{FahmyGhanyBaumann2018}
is
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_i(\alpha;Q_i)=
    \begin{cases}
    -C_it+\dfrac{C_i^2}{3Q_i}|t|t-\dfrac{C_i^3}{27Q_i^2}t^3,
       & |\alpha|<\alpha_{{\rm sl},i},\\[3pt]
    -Q_i\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha), & |\alpha|\geq\alpha_{{\rm sl},i},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:fiala_force}
\end{equation}
with \(\alpha_{{\rm sl},i}=\arctan(3Q_i/C_i)\),
\(\eta_i=\sqrt{1-\beta^2}\) and \(F_{xi}=\beta\mu_iF_{zi}\) for
\(|\beta|<1\). One signed input \(\beta\) acts on both axles: negative
values brake, positive values drive. The static loads are
\(F_{zf}=m_Egl_{r,E}/L\) and \(F_{zr}=m_Egl_{f,E}/L\) with
\(L=l_{f,E}+l_{r,E}\), each axle stands for a wheel pair, \(m_E\) and
\(I_{z,E}\) are the mass and yaw inertia, \(l_{f,E}\) and \(l_{r,E}\) the
lever arms, and \(\delta_F\) the front steering angle. We use the
symmetric branch condition and \cite[eq.~(13)]{FahmyGhanyBaumann2018};
the additional arctangent printed in \cite[eq.~(12)]{FahmyGhanyBaumann2018}
is inconsistent with it. At \(|\beta|=1\) the force is defined by
\(Q_i=0\), \(\Phi_i=0\), without evaluating the singular polynomial;
the derivatives of \(\Phi_i\) are unbounded there, and every
linearization point is kept strictly inside the input interval. The tire
model is used above 4\,m/s. Below that speed the prediction model blends
into a kinematic bicycle so that the vehicle can come to rest
(Section~\ref{subsec:predictionModel}). The saturation branch bounds the
lateral force; the controller keeps the rear axle below it by a cone
constraint on the prediction (Section~\ref{subsec:envelope}) and leaves
the front axle free. Speed, sideslip and course are
\begin{equation}
    V_E=\normtwo{\bm v_E},
    \qquad
    \beta_E=\arctan\frac{v_{y,E}}{v_{x,E}},
    \qquad
    \gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E .
    \label{eq:yaw_from_course}
\end{equation}

The obstacle vehicle has inputs we do not observe. Following
\cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}, it is modeled with constant scalar
acceleration and constant sideslip, hence constant course curvature.
The tracker of Section~\ref{subsec:targetTracker} estimates its state in
that model, and the controller forecasts it in the same model with one
addition: braking ends at rest and never reverses the vehicle
(Section~\ref{subsec:predictionModel}).

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{subsec:architecture}

Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} shows how the parts are connected. A
perception stage turns camera and LiDAR data into the relative position
of the obstacle vehicle. The estimator fuses that measurement with the
GNSS, accelerometer and gyroscope signals into the motion states of the
ego and of one obstacle, and attaches to each estimate an error
enclosure that is valid for the continuous-time observer under declared
sensor, model and operating-domain bounds. The controller runs at 20\,Hz
on these estimates. At every sample it linearizes the vehicle model
along one nonlinear rollout, builds the collision constraints from the
obstacle estimate and the CLF from the path and the prescribed speed,
solves two convex programs in strict priority order, rolls their
solution out through the nonlinear model, and issues the first steering
and force-ratio command of that rollout. Three signals close the design
between the two parts. The controller passes the input it holds back to
the estimator, which inverts the tire model under it. The estimator's
operating domain is derived from the controller's state limits, and the
controller enforces the estimator's sideslip cone and a rear-adhesion
envelope so that the motion stays where the measurement is informative.
The estimator's enclosure tightens the controller's constraints.

The relative-position measurement is produced as follows. A YOLO
detector \cite{RedmonDivvalaGirshickFarhadi2016} returns a bounding box
for each vehicle in the camera image; the LiDAR points are projected
into the image through the extrinsic calibration $(\bm R_{CL},\bm t_{CL})$
and the pinhole model,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \begin{bmatrix}X&Y&Z\end{bmatrix}^{\trans}
    =\bm R_{CL}\,\bm q+\bm t_{CL},\\
    \begin{bmatrix}u\\v\end{bmatrix}
    =\frac{f}{Z}
    \begin{bmatrix}X\\Y\end{bmatrix}
    +\begin{bmatrix}W/2\\H/2\end{bmatrix},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:lidar_camera_projection}
\end{equation}
with $f$ the focal length and $W\times H$ the image size; the points
whose pixel coordinates fall inside a box, less ground and stray
returns, are the points reflected from that vehicle
(Fig.~\ref{fig:obstacle_perception}(a)). The vehicle's outline is a
rectangle of known length and width in the top view, and its planar pose
is fitted to the retained points by a damped Gauss--Newton iteration
whose objective penalizes the distance of lower-body points to the
rectangle edges and the distance of every point that falls outside the
rectangle; roof and trunk returns, which lie inside the footprint, would
otherwise be pulled onto the edges and rotate the fit
(Fig.~\ref{fig:obstacle_perception}(b)). The iteration starts from the
relative pose that the estimator predicts for the same instant. The
prediction is only a starting point: it does not enter the objective,
and a frame without a fit is passed on as a missing measurement, never
as the prediction itself. The center of the fitted rectangle is the
measured relative position
\begin{equation}
    \bm\rho=\begin{bmatrix}\rho_x&\rho_y\end{bmatrix}^{\trans}\in\R^2
    \label{eq:perception_relative_position}
\end{equation}
in the ego frame. This stage has been developed and tested on simulated
camera and LiDAR captures. It is not in the closed loop of
Section~\ref{sec:results}, where the relative position is synthetic with
bounded noise, and its measured errors on those captures exceed the
bound that the estimator trials assume; we return to this in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{The Safety Argument}
\label{subsec:argument}

The safety argument connects the interfaces of
Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} through the following statements.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The perception output lies within a declared bound of the
true relative position.
\item[(ii)] Under the same sensor and model-uncertainty contract as the
plant, the estimation error lies in the published enclosure
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cascadeISS}, Corollary~\ref{cor:enclosure}, and the
force-balance interval of Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided}).
\item[(iii)] Whenever a finite observer certificate is used, its
true-motion premises hold throughout the hold. The joint mathematical
constraints impose these premises on every admitted branch; the numerical
prediction and audits check the sideslip and rear-adhesion limits
(Section~\ref{subsec:envelope}).
\item[(iv)] Zero-deficit universal joint collision rows separate the true
bodies at the specified constraint instants
(Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}, Theorem~\ref{thm:robust}).
\item[(v)] A feasible mathematical policy exists at the next sample.
Proposition~\ref{prop:recursive} addresses exact information;
Theorem~\ref{thm:robust} includes estimation error, shared mismatch,
future observer feedback and revised information under its stated premises.
\item[(vi)] The vehicle returns to cruise after the encounter
(the cruise-law certificate and measured recovery of
Section~\ref{subsec:clf}).
\end{enumerate}
The joint-set construction closes the conditional mathematical argument
for (ii)--(v), with road boundaries omitted from the robust derivation.
The continuous-time observer proof and the sampled nonlinear cruise-law
certificate have their own stated domains. Numerical trials audit the
implemented approximations and measure recovery; they do not replace
universal joint-set constraints. Separation between the constrained
instants is not proved, and the perception bound in (i) remains declared
rather than established by the perception stage.

\section{Estimator Design}
\label{sec:estimator}

The estimator has to do two things that the observer of
\cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026} does not: stay valid while the ego vehicle
swerves and brakes, and tell the controller how wrong it may be. The
first is a matter of coordinates. Their translational observer
differentiates the ego kinematics into third-order companion chains,
which calls for \(\dot{\bm a}_E\) and \(\ddot\psi_E\) and therefore
assumes both are zero. Here the measured acceleration \(\bm a_m\) and
yaw rate \(u_3\) remain exogenous inputs of every stage and nothing is
differentiated. Their yaw stage switches between a heading inferred from
the acceleration direction and the course angle; reading the course as
the yaw is exact only at \(\beta_E=0\), since
\(\gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E\). Here the same relation supplies a direct
body-velocity measurement and, when it is informative, a certified
heading correspondence for a parallel yaw observer. The second is a
matter of certificates. The ego body-velocity observer and the target
tracker form a two-stage cascade whose error obeys a positive comparison
system; the comparison solution is the enclosure the controller
receives.

The measurements are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm y_p&=\bm p_E+\bm n_p, &\quad
    \bm v_m&=\bm v_E+\bm n_v,\\
    \bm a_m&=\bm a_E+\bm n_a, &\quad
    u_3&=\dot\psi_E+d_\omega,\\
    \bm y_R&=\bm\rho+\bm n_R,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:ego_measurements}
\end{equation}
with \(\bm a_E=R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\ddot{\bm p}_E\) the acceleration in
the ego frame, \(\bm\rho\) the relative position
\eqref{eq:target_coordinates}, whose measurement \(\bm y_R\) is the
perception output \eqref{eq:perception_relative_position}, and uniform
bounds \(\normtwo{\bm n_p}\leq\bar n_p\),
\(\normtwo{\bm n_v}\leq\bar n_v\), \(\normtwo{\bm n_a}\leq\bar n_a\),
\(|d_\omega|\leq\bar n_g\), \(\normtwo{\bm n_R}\leq\bar n_R\). Inertial
biases are compensated upstream; no observer carries a bias state.

\begin{assumption}
\label{asm:ego}
\(0\leq V_E|_{\min}\leq V_E\leq V_E|_{\max}\),
$v_{x,E}\geq V_E\cos\beta_{\rm dom}$, and
\(|\dot\psi_E|\leq\bar\omega_E\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}
\label{asm:singleTrack}
The kinematic single-track relation holds up to a bounded mismatch,
\begin{equation}
    \dot\psi_E=\frac{V_E\sin\beta_E}{l_{r,E}}+d_{\rm st},
    \qquad|d_{\rm st}|\leq\bar d_{\rm st},
    \label{eq:single_track_mismatch}
\end{equation}
and \(|\beta_E|\leq\beta_{\rm dom}<\pi/2\).
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{asm:singleTrack} bounds the unmodeled slip of the rear
axle. The identification of course and yaw that it replaces is the
case \(\beta_E\equiv0\). As Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided} explains, the
avoidance maneuvers violate a small \(\bar d_{\rm st}\), and in the
closed loop the relation is replaced by the tire model; the assumption
is kept for the kinematic branch, which is what the open-loop trials of
Section~\ref{subsec:estimatorResults} use.

\subsection{Certified Course--Yaw Correspondence}
\label{subsec:courseCertificate}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:course}
Let Assumption~\ref{asm:singleTrack} hold and
\(V_m=\normtwo{\bm v_m}>\bar n_v\), put
\(\underline V=V_m-\bar n_v\), and suppose \(|l_{r,E}u_3|\leq V_m\).
Define
\begin{equation}
    \hat\beta=\arcsin\frac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m},
    \qquad
    y_F=\angle\bm v_m-\hat\beta .
    \label{eq:course_definition}
\end{equation}
Then, with
\begin{equation}
    \bar e_{\sin}
    =\frac{l_{r,E}}{\underline V}
    \Bigl(\bar n_g+\bar d_{\rm st}+\frac{|u_3|\bar n_v}{V_m}\Bigr),
    \label{eq:sine_error_bound}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \bar e_\beta=\max_{\pm}\Bigl|
    \arcsin\bigl(\Pi\bigl[\tfrac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m}\pm\bar e_{\sin}\bigr]\bigr)
    -\hat\beta\Bigr|,
    \label{eq:sideslip_error}
\end{equation}
where \(\Pi\) clips to \([-\sin\beta_{\rm dom},\sin\beta_{\rm dom}]\), one has
\(|\beta_E-\hat\beta|\leq\bar e_\beta\) and
\begin{equation}
    |y_F-\psi_E|\leq B_F:=\arcsin\frac{\bar n_v}{V_m}+\bar e_\beta .
    \label{eq:course_certificate}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
By \eqref{eq:single_track_mismatch},
\(\sin\beta_E=l_{r,E}(u_3-d_\omega-d_{\rm st})/V_E\), so
\begin{equation}
    \Bigl|\sin\beta_E-\frac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m}\Bigr|
    \leq l_{r,E}\Bigl[\frac{|d_\omega|+|d_{\rm st}|}{V_E}
    +|u_3|\frac{|V_m-V_E|}{V_EV_m}\Bigr],
    \label{eq:sine_derivation}
\end{equation}
and \(|V_m-V_E|\leq\bar n_v\), \(V_E\geq\underline V\) give
\eqref{eq:sine_error_bound}. Since \(|\sin\beta_E|\leq\sin\beta_{\rm dom}\),
the true sine lies in the clipped interval, on which \(\arcsin\) is
increasing, which gives \eqref{eq:sideslip_error}. For the course,
\(\bm v_E\) lies in the disk of radius \(\bar n_v\) about \(\bm v_m\);
the disk excludes the origin, so \(\bm v_E\) lies in its tangent cone and
\(|\angle\bm v_m-\gamma_E|\leq\arcsin(\bar n_v/V_m)\). Subtracting
\(\gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E\) yields \eqref{eq:course_certificate}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The correspondence is used only where these conditions hold; otherwise
it contributes the whole circle to the yaw enclosure. Neither case
touches the body-velocity innovation, which is defined at standstill as
well.

\subsection{Ego Observers}
\label{subsec:egoObserver}

With $J=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$,
$\nabla_u\bm z=\dot{\bm z}+uJ\bm z$ and
$\bm v^E=R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_E$, define
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \phi(V,w)=\sqrt{\max\{V^2-w^2,V^2\cos^2\beta_{\rm dom}\}},\\
    \bm y_v=[\phi(V_m,l_{r,E}u_3),\ l_{r,E}u_3]^{\trans},\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm v}^E=\bm a_m+k_v(\bm y_v-\hat{\bm v}^E).
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:ego_observer}
\end{equation}
On the true forward cone $\phi(V_E,v_{y,E})=v_{x,E}$. The extension is
defined for every measured speed, including zero, and its lateral
component is never clipped, so that
$y_{v,2}-v_{y,E}=l_{r,E}(d_\omega+d_{\rm st})$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:egoRows}
Let $\bm e_v=\hat{\bm v}^E-\bm v^E$, $k_v>0$, and
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    C_\omega(k)=\max_{V\in\{V_E|_{\min},V_E|_{\max}\}}
    \Bigl[(V\sin\beta_{\rm dom}+kl_{r,E}\tan\beta_{\rm dom})^2\\
        +(kl_{r,E}-V\cos\beta_{\rm dom})^2\Bigr]^{1/2}.
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:common_gyro_bound}
\end{equation}
Under Assumptions~\ref{asm:ego}--\ref{asm:singleTrack},
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    D^+\normtwo{\bm e_v}\leq-k_v\normtwo{\bm e_v}+\bar\delta_v,\\
    \bar\delta_v=\bar n_a+k_v\sec\beta_{\rm dom}\,\bar n_v
      +C_\omega(k_v)\bar n_g
      +k_vl_{r,E}\sec\beta_{\rm dom}\,\bar d_{\rm st}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:ego_rows}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Put $\nu=V_m-V_E$, so $|\nu|\leq\bar n_v$. Integrating the piecewise
derivatives of $\phi$ along the segment between $(V_E,v_{y,E})$ and
$(V_m,l_{r,E}u_3)$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \bm y_v-\bm v^E=\alpha\bm e_1\nu
        +l_{r,E}\bm g(\zeta)(d_\omega+d_{\rm st}),\\
    \bm g(\zeta)=[-\zeta,1]^{\trans},\\
    \cos\beta_{\rm dom}\leq\alpha\leq\sec\beta_{\rm dom},\qquad
    |\zeta|\leq\tan\beta_{\rm dom}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:velocity_measurement_error}
\end{equation}
Continuity covers branch crossings and the origin. Substituting before
taking norms,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \dot{\bm e}_v={}&(-k_vI-u_3J)\bm e_v+\bm n_a+k_v\alpha\bm e_1\nu\\
        &+(k_vl_{r,E}\bm g(\zeta)-J\bm v^E)d_\omega
          +k_vl_{r,E}\bm g(\zeta)d_{\rm st}.
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:velocity_error_derivation}
\end{equation}
The common gyro column has components
$[v_{y,E}-k_vl_{r,E}\zeta,\ k_vl_{r,E}-v_{x,E}]^{\trans}$. Maximizing
its norm over $\zeta$ and the forward cone, then using the convexity of
its squared bound in the speed, gives \eqref{eq:common_gyro_bound}. The
rotation is skew and contributes nothing to the norm derivative. The
remaining terms give \eqref{eq:ego_rows}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The same gyro error enters the measurement and the propagation, and
bounding the two separately would throw away their cancellation; no
statistical independence is used anywhere in the lemma, and the yaw
error does not enter the velocity comparison at all.

Yaw and inertial position are estimated in parallel,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \dot{\hat\psi}_E&=u_3+\chi_m k_\psi
        \operatorname{wrap}(y_F-\hat\psi_E),\\
    \dot{\hat{\bm p}}_E&=\bm v_m+k_p(\bm y_p-\hat{\bm p}_E),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:parallel_outputs}
\end{equation}
where $\chi_m=1$ only when the course correspondence is informative.
While the innovation stays on one branch of the wrap the yaw error
\(e_\psi=\hat\psi_E-\psi_E\) obeys
\(\dot e_\psi=-k_\psi e_\psi+d_\omega+k_\psi(y_F-\psi_E)\) with
\(|y_F-\psi_E|\leq B_F\); during uninformative intervals only the gyro
propagates it. An orientation set $\Psi$ is propagated independently
with the integral of the gyro error bound and intersected with each
informative arc of Lemma~\ref{lem:course}. Disconnected components are
kept, and the published bound is
$B_\psi=\sup_{\theta\in\Psi}d_{S^1}(\hat\psi_E,\theta)$ about the actual
estimate. An empty intersection invalidates the orientation outputs; it
does not reset the observer. The position error
\(\bm e_p=\hat{\bm p}_E-\bm p_E\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    D^+\normtwo{\bm e_p}\leq-k_p\normtwo{\bm e_p}+\bar n_v+k_p\bar n_p,\\
    \limsup_{t\to\infty}\normtwo{\bm e_p}\leq\bar n_p+\bar n_v/k_p.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:position_error_bound}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Model-Aided Lateral Velocity}
\label{subsec:modelAided}

Assumption~\ref{asm:singleTrack} is a statement about the true motion.
With $l_{r,E}$ the rear-axle distance, $l_{r,E}d_{\rm st}$ is, up to
sign, the lateral slip speed of the rear tire, so a small
$\bar d_{\rm st}$ says that the rear tire barely slips. The avoidance
maneuvers of Section~\ref{sec:results} do not respect that: at 15\,m/s
the rear slip speed reached 0.9 to 4.3\,m/s, and bounds published under
$\bar d_{\rm st}$ failed to contain the truth. Enlarging
$\bar d_{\rm st}$ to the saturation slip of the tire makes every bound
five to seven times larger. The remedy is to use the model the
controller already has. When the estimator knows the ego model
\eqref{eq:bicycle_model}--\eqref{eq:fiala_force} and the input
$\bm u=[\delta_F,\beta]^{\trans}$ that the controller holds, the lateral
component of \eqref{eq:ego_observer} is taken from the lateral force
balance at the center of mass,
\begin{equation}
    m_Ea_{y}=G(v_{y,E}):=F_{xf}\sin\delta_F
        +F_{yf}(\alpha_f)\cos\delta_F+F_{yr}(\alpha_r),
    \label{eq:force_balance}
\end{equation}
where $a_y$ is the lateral component of $\bm a_E$, which the
accelerometer measures, and the slip angles \eqref{eq:tire_forces}
depend on $v_{y,E}$, on the yaw rate and, through $v_{x,E}$, on the
speed. For $v_{x,E}>0$ and $|\delta_F|<\pi/2$ both slip angles increase
with $v_{y,E}$ and both lateral forces are non-increasing in their slip
angle, so $G$ is non-increasing, and the measured $a_y$ determines
$v_{y,E}$ wherever $G$ is strictly decreasing.

The measurement $c$ solves \eqref{eq:force_balance} with the nominal
parameters and the measured $a_{m,y}$, $u_3$ and $V_m$, by bisection on
the cone $|v_{y,E}|\leq V\sin\beta_{\rm dom}$, and its radius is
computed alongside. Let the cornering stiffnesses and friction
coefficients lie within a relative uncertainty $\epsilon_{\rm par}$ of
their model values, and let $G_{\min}$ and $G_{\max}$ be the sums of the
per-axle extremes of the force over the sensor bounds on $u_3$ and $V_m$
and over this parameter box. Each axle force is monotone in each of
these quantities, so the extremes sit at the corners of the box, and
$G_{\min}$ and $G_{\max}$ are again non-increasing in $v_{y,E}$. Every
admissible lateral velocity then satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    G_{\min}(v_{y,E})\leq m_E(a_{m,y}+\bar n_a),\\
    G_{\max}(v_{y,E})\geq m_E(a_{m,y}-\bar n_a),
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:force_interval}
\end{equation}
which is an interval $[\underline c,\overline c]$, and
$\rho_c=\max(c-\underline c,\overline c-c)$ bounds $|c-v_{y,E}|$. An
empty interval means that the measurements contradict the model or the
cone, and the ego enclosure is declared invalid. With
$\bm y_v=[\phi(V_m,c),\,c]^{\trans}$ in \eqref{eq:ego_observer}, the
mean-value argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:egoRows} gives
$\normtwo{\bm y_v-\bm v^E}\leq\sec\beta_{\rm dom}(\bar n_v+\rho_c)$ on
the cone, and the velocity row of the comparison becomes
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    D^+\normtwo{\bm e_v}\leq{}&-k_v\normtwo{\bm e_v}+\bar n_a
        +\bar n_gV_E|_{\max}\\
        &+k_v\sec\beta_{\rm dom}\,(\bar n_v+\rho_c(t)).
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:model_aided_row}
\end{equation}
The kinematic mismatch $\bar d_{\rm st}$ is gone; the premises are now
the model structure, the parameter box and the cone. The gyro
cancellation of Lemma~\ref{lem:egoRows} is not retained in
\eqref{eq:model_aided_row}. The radius $\rho_c$ is computed online and is
not uniform: near saturation the force no longer determines the slip and
the interval widens, to about 3\,m/s for $\epsilon_{\rm par}=0.05$, and
becomes asymmetric, which is why its midpoint is not used as the
measurement. The controller keeps the rear tire below saturation and
the motion inside the cone (Section~\ref{subsec:envelope}), which keeps
the inversion informative. The same interval, divided by the speed
interval, replaces $\hat\beta\pm\bar e_\beta$ in the course
correspondence of Lemma~\ref{lem:course}. Without the model or a held
input, the kinematic measurement of Section~\ref{subsec:egoObserver} is
used unchanged. The premise is the Fiala model itself: the plant of
Section~\ref{sec:results} follows it exactly above 4\,m/s and blends
toward a kinematic bicycle below, where the inversion is only an
approximate model of the vehicle; ten of the 84 estimated-state runs
slow into that range, to 3.2\,m/s at the least.

\subsection{Covariant Target Tracker}
\label{subsec:targetTracker}

The target model of \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}, constant scalar
acceleration and constant sideslip, hence constant curvature
\(\kappa=\sin\beta_C/l_{r,C}\), is kept, but written in covariant
companion coordinates
\begin{equation}
    \bm\rho=R_E^{\trans}(\bm p_C-\bm p_E),\quad
    \bm q=R_E^{\trans}\bm v_C,\quad
    \bm s=R_E^{\trans}\bm a_C,
    \label{eq:target_coordinates}
\end{equation}
with \(R_E=R(\psi_E)\). With \(\omega_E=\dot\psi_E\), the exact
dynamics in these coordinates form the third-order chain
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm\rho=\bm q-\bm v^E,\quad
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm q=\bm s,\quad
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm s=\Phi(\bm q,\bm s),\\
    \Phi=-\Omega^2\bm q+3A\Omega J\frac{\bm q}{V},\\
    V=\normtwo{\bm q},\quad
    A=\frac{\bm q^{\trans}\bm s}{V},\quad
    \Omega=\frac{(J\bm q)^{\trans}\bm s}{V^2}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_chain}
\end{equation}
Only \(\bm v^E\) and \(\omega_E\) enter, and the only extra terms are
those of the rotating frame; \(A\) and \(\Omega\) are reconstructions,
not states. This is where the ego's own maneuver enters the tracker, as
a measured input rather than through differentiated coordinates. The
declared operating set is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    a\leq\normtwo{\bm q}\leq b,\quad
    |A|\leq A_{\max},\quad
    \normtwo{\bm\rho}\leq\rho_{\max},\\
    |\Omega|\leq\Omega_{\max}=\frac{b\sin\beta_{\max}}{l_{r,C}},\quad
    \normtwo{\bm s}\leq S=\sqrt{A_{\max}^2+b^2\Omega_{\max}^2},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_domain}
\end{equation}
with \(a\) and \(b\) the target speed bounds \(V_C|_{\min}\) and
\(V_C|_{\max}\), $0<a<b$. Estimates may leave
\eqref{eq:target_domain} during a transient, so the observer evaluates
an extension. With $\Pi_r$ the Euclidean projection onto the ball of
radius $r$ and $\operatorname{clip}_r$ the clip to $[-r,r]$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    d=\max(\normtwo{\bm q},a),\quad
    \bm Q=\Pi_b(\bm q),\quad\tilde{\bm s}=\Pi_S(\bm s),\\
    A_e=\operatorname{clip}_{A_{\max}}
        (\bm Q^{\trans}\tilde{\bm s}/d),\\
    \Omega_e=\operatorname{clip}_{\Omega_{\max}}
        ((J\bm Q)^{\trans}\tilde{\bm s}/d^2),\\
    \Phi_e(\bm q,\bm s)=-\Omega_e^2\bm Q
        +3A_e\Omega_e J\bm q/d,
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_extension}
\end{equation}
which agrees with $\Phi$ on \eqref{eq:target_domain} and is defined on
$\R^4$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:lipschitz}
With
\(c_A=S/a\), \(c_\Omega=S\max(a^{-2},2b^{-2})\),
\(c_{Q\Omega}=S\max(a^{-1},2b^{-1})\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    L_q=\Omega_{\max}^2+2\Omega_{\max}c_{Q\Omega}
        +3\Omega_{\max}\Bigl(\frac{A_{\max}}a+c_A\Bigr)\\
        +3A_{\max}c_\Omega,\\
    L_s=\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}
        0&2\Omega_{\max}\\3\Omega_{\max}&3A_{\max}/a
        \end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_2,
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:lipschitz_constants}
\end{equation}
\(\Phi_e\) satisfies, for all \((\bm q,\bm s),(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \normtwo{\Phi_e(\bm q,\bm s)-\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})}\\
    \leq L_q\normtwo{\bm q-\hat{\bm q}}+L_s\normtwo{\bm s-\hat{\bm s}} .
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:global_lipschitz}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Projections and clips are nonexpansive, and $a>0$ makes $\Phi_e$ locally
Lipschitz and piecewise differentiable, so it suffices to bound its
almost-everywhere Jacobians and integrate along the segment joining the
two arguments. Differentiating in the radial and tangential basis at
fixed \(\bm s\), with \(r=\normtwo{\bm q}\), \(\bm Q\) the projected
velocity and \(A_e\), \(\Omega_e\) the clipped reconstructions, gives
\(|D_qA_e|\leq S/\max(r,a)\) and \(|D_q\Omega_e|\leq S/\max(r,a)^2\) for
\(r\leq b\), and \(|D_qA_e|\leq bS/r^2\), \(|D_q\Omega_e|\leq2bS/r^3\)
for \(r\geq b\), whose suprema over \(r>0\) are \(c_A\) and
\(c_\Omega\), while \(\normtwo{\bm Q}|D_q\Omega_e|\leq c_{Q\Omega}\).
Using $\|D_q\bm Q\|_2\leq1$, $\normtwo{\bm q/d}\leq1$ and
$\|D_q(\bm q/d)\|_2\leq1/a$ in \eqref{eq:target_extension} gives $L_q$.
At fixed $\bm q\ne0$ the Jacobian with respect to $\tilde{\bm s}$, in
the basis aligned with $\bm q$, is bounded entrywise in absolute value
by the matrix in \eqref{eq:lipschitz_constants}, so its induced norm is
at most $L_s$; composing with $\tilde{\bm s}=\Pi_S(\bm s)$ preserves the
bound because $\|D_s\Pi_S\|_2\leq1$ almost everywhere, and continuity
covers $\bm q=0$.
\end{IEEEproof}

The tracker is the covariant high-gain chain
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm\rho}&=\hat{\bm q}-\hat{\bm v}^E
        +l_1\omega\,(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm\rho}),\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm q}&=\hat{\bm s}
        +l_2\omega^2(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm\rho}),\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm s}&=\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})
        +l_3\omega^3(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm\rho}).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_observer}
\end{equation}
Let \(A_c\) be the integrator-chain matrix, \(C=\bm e_1^{\trans}\) and
\(A_l=A_c-\bm lC\). The shape $\bm l$ and the bandwidth $\omega>0$ are
selected in Section~\ref{subsec:gainSelection}; the certificate below
holds for every pair that satisfies its matrix inequality. For
$\bm x_T=[\bm\rho^{\trans},\bm q^{\trans},\bm s^{\trans}]^{\trans}$ and
$\bm e_T=\hat{\bm x}_T-\bm x_T$, put $D=\diag(1,\omega^{-1},\omega^{-2})$
and
\begin{equation}
    \bm\epsilon=(D\otimes I_2)\bm e_T,
    \qquad
    W_T=\sqrt{\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(P\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon}.
    \label{eq:target_metric}
\end{equation}
Write $\Delta\Phi_e=\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})-\Phi_e(\bm q,\bm s)$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:dissipation}
Let $P\succ0$, \(\bm b_P=P\bm e_3\), \(t_q,t_s>0\), and
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    M={}&-\omega(A_l^{\trans}P+PA_l)
       -t_q\frac{L_q^2}{\omega^2}\bm e_2\bm e_2^{\trans}\\
       &-t_sL_s^2\bm e_3\bm e_3^{\trans}
       -(t_q^{-1}+t_s^{-1})\bm b_P\bm b_P^{\trans}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:dissipation_matrix}
\end{equation}
If \(M\succeq2\lambda_TP\) then \(D^+W_T\leq-\lambda_TW_T\) in the
absence of exogenous error inputs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
In the coordinate \(\bm\epsilon\) the injection block becomes
\(\omega A_l\otimes I_2\), and the common rotation \(-u_3(I_3\otimes J)\)
is skew and contributes nothing to
\(\tfrac{d}{dt}\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(P\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon\). The
injection contributes
$\omega\bm\epsilon^{\trans}[(A_l^{\trans}P+PA_l)\otimes I_2]\bm\epsilon$.
Splitting $\Delta\Phi_e/\omega^2$ into its velocity and acceleration
increments, \eqref{eq:global_lipschitz} bounds them by
$(L_q/\omega)\normtwo{\bm\epsilon_2}$ and $L_s\normtwo{\bm\epsilon_3}$,
and each enters the derivative through
$2\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(\bm b_P\otimes I_2)$. Applying
\(2\bm z^{\trans}\bm d\leq\normtwo{\bm z}^2/t+t\normtwo{\bm d}^2\) once
per channel gives
\(\tfrac{d}{dt}W_T^2\leq-\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(M\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon
\leq-2\lambda_TW_T^2\), and the square root gives the comparison,
including $W_T=0$ by continuity of the homogeneous error dynamics.
\end{IEEEproof}

\subsection{Cascade ISS}
\label{subsec:cascade}

Let $\bm\nu_\Phi$ be the model residual in the true last row,
$\nabla_{\omega_E}\bm s=\Phi(\bm q,\bm s)+\bm\nu_\Phi$, with
$\normtwo{\bm\nu_\Phi}\leq\bar\nu_\Phi$. For a target whose motion
varies, write the course curvature as $\kappa=\Omega/V$, which equals
$\sin\beta_C/l_{r,C}$ in the constant-sideslip model; differentiating
the target velocity gives
$\bm\nu_\Phi=\dot A\bm q/V+V^2\dot\kappa J\bm q/V$, so declared rate
bounds imply $\bar\nu_\Phi=(\bar{\dot A}^2+b^4\bar{\dot\kappa}^2)^{1/2}$.
Subtracting the true chain from \eqref{eq:target_observer},
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \dot{\bm\epsilon}={}&
        [\omega A_l\otimes I_2-u_3(I_3\otimes J)]\bm\epsilon\\
        &+\omega^{-2}(\bm e_3\otimes I_2)
            (\Delta\Phi_e-\bm\nu_\Phi)\\
        &-(\bm e_1\otimes I_2)\bm e_v
            -d_\omega(D\otimes J)\bm x_T\\
        &+\omega(\bm l\otimes I_2)\bm n_R.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_error_dynamics}
\end{equation}
All core couplings run forward, from the ego stage into the tracker.
Cauchy--Schwarz in the metric \eqref{eq:target_metric} bounds the four
input channels by
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    g_{Tv}=\sqrt{P_{11}},\qquad
    g_R=\omega\sqrt{\bm l^{\trans}P\bm l},\\
    g_\omega=\sqrt{\bm h^{\trans}|P|\bm h},\qquad
    g_\Phi=\sqrt{P_{33}}/\omega^2,\\
    \bm h=[\rho_{\max},\ b/\omega,\ S/\omega^2]^{\trans},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_disturbance_coefficients}
\end{equation}
with $|P|$ entrywise, so that
$D^+W_T\leq-\lambda_TW_T+g_{Tv}\normtwo{\bm e_v}
+g_\omega\bar n_g+g_R\bar n_R+g_\Phi\bar\nu_\Phi$. Together with
Lemma~\ref{lem:egoRows} this is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \bm\chi=[\normtwo{\bm e_v},\ W_T]^{\trans},\qquad
    D^+\bm\chi\leq H\bm\chi+\bm d,\\
    H=\begin{bmatrix}-k_v&0\\g_{Tv}&-\lambda_T\end{bmatrix},\qquad
    \bm d=\begin{bmatrix}\bar\delta_v\\
    g_\omega\bar n_g+g_R\bar n_R+g_\Phi\bar\nu_\Phi\end{bmatrix}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:cascade_comparison}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Cascade ISS]
\label{thm:cascadeISS}
Let Assumptions~\ref{asm:ego}--\ref{asm:singleTrack}, the hypotheses of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:egoRows}, \ref{lem:lipschitz} and \ref{lem:dissipation},
the true target domain \eqref{eq:target_domain} and the declared
continuous sensor and model-error bounds hold, with $k_v,\lambda_T>0$.
Then $H$ is Metzler and Hurwitz, and $\bm c=-H^{-\trans}\bm 1>0$ gives
the linear copositive ISS function $V_c=\bm c^{\trans}\bm\chi$. For any
$\bm z_0\geq\bm\chi(0)$,
\begin{equation}
    \bm\chi(t)\leq\bm z(t)
    =e^{Ht}\bm z_0+\int_0^t e^{H(t-\tau)}\bm d\,d\tau,
    \label{eq:comparison_solution}
\end{equation}
and $\limsup_{t\to\infty}\bm\chi(t)\leq-H^{-1}\bm d$. The physical
component bounds are
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm e_{T,i}}\leq c_{T,i}W_T,\qquad
    \bm c_T=\diag(1,\omega,\omega^2)\sqrt{\diag(P^{-1})}.
    \label{eq:component_extraction}
\end{equation}
For the fixed design, the core error
$\bm e=[\bm e_v^{\trans},\bm e_T^{\trans}]^{\trans}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm e(t)}\leq C_e e^{-\alpha_e t}\normtwo{\bm e(0)}
        +\Gamma_e\|\bm\eta\|_{\infty,[0,t]},
    \label{eq:core_exponential_iss}
\end{equation}
with constants $C_e,\alpha_e,\Gamma_e>0$ and the disturbance vector
$\bm\eta=[\bm n_a^{\trans},\bm n_v^{\trans},d_\omega,d_{\rm st},
\bm n_R^{\trans},\bm\nu_\Phi^{\trans}]^{\trans}$, where
$\|\bm\eta\|_{\infty,[0,t]}$ is the essential supremum of
$\normtwo{\bm\eta(\tau)}$ over $0\leq\tau\leq t$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
$H$ is lower triangular with a negative diagonal and nonnegative
off-diagonal entries, so $e^{Ht}\geq0$ and $-H^{-1}\geq0$, which give
\eqref{eq:comparison_solution}, the ultimate bound and $\bm c>0$. Since
$\bm c^{\trans}H=-\bm1^{\trans}$,
$D^+V_c\leq-V_c/c_{\max}+\bm c^{\trans}\bm d$ with
$c_{\max}=\max_i c_i$. The same comparisons hold with instantaneous
disturbance magnitudes in place of their uniform bounds; for fixed
gains their weighted forcing is at most $K\normtwo{\bm\eta(t)}$ for some
$K>0$. Since $P\succ0$ and $\omega>0$ is fixed, there are
$m_c,M_c>0$ with $m_c\normtwo{\bm e}\leq V_c\leq M_c\normtwo{\bm e}$,
and integrating the scalar comparison proves
\eqref{eq:core_exponential_iss} with $C_e=M_c/m_c$,
$\alpha_e=1/c_{\max}$ and $\Gamma_e=Kc_{\max}/m_c$. The bound also rules
out finite escape under the declared premises, and with all
disturbances zero the core error converges exponentially.
Equation~\eqref{eq:component_extraction} is the ellipsoid support in
$P\otimes I_2$ after undoing the position normalization.
\end{IEEEproof}

The core has two ego-velocity states and six states for the target. Its
certificate needs neither informative yaw measurements nor a yaw
innovation chart. The shared gyro couples the two core stages as well;
\eqref{eq:cascade_comparison} uses a triangular norm bound and does not
claim every cross-stage cancellation. Orientation and position remain
separately certified outputs.

\subsection{Gain Selection}
\label{subsec:gainSelection}

With $T_{\rm dom}=\rho_{\max}/(V_E|_{\max}+V_C|_{\max})$, the scalar
gains follow a transit-time preference,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \min_{k>0}k\quad\text{s.t.}\quad e^{-kT_{\rm dom}}\leq0.05,\\
    k_\psi=k_v=k_p=\frac{\ln20}{T_{\rm dom}},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:scalar_gain_rule}
\end{equation}
which is not an optimum of the common-error bound in
\eqref{eq:ego_rows}: increasing $k_v$ changes the combined gyro
coefficient and need not improve that bound.

The target gains are decision variables of a semidefinite program that
contains the certificate. In physical coordinates ($\omega=1$) let
$\bm L\in\R^3$ be the gain vector, $A_L=A_c-\bm LC$ and $\bm Y=P\bm L$,
and put $\Xi=A_c^{\trans}P+PA_c-\bm YC-C^{\trans}\bm Y^{\trans}$, which
equals $A_L^{\trans}P+PA_L$. The required decay rate of the target
metric is the floor $\lambda_T=1/T_{\rm dom}$, one $e$-folding per
transit time. With $\Delta_\Phi=\diag(0,t_qL_q^2,t_sL_s^2)$, solve
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \min_{P\succ0,\,\bm Y,\,t_q,t_s>0,\,\gamma}\ \gamma
    \quad\text{s.t.}\\
    \begin{bmatrix}
    \Xi+2\lambda_TP+\Delta_\Phi&P\bm e_3&P\bm e_3\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&-t_q&0\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&0&-t_s
    \end{bmatrix}\prec0,\\
    \Xi+\bm e_2\bm e_2^{\trans}\prec0,\qquad
    \begin{bmatrix}\gamma&\bm Y^{\trans}\\\bm Y&P\end{bmatrix}\succeq0 .
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:noise_shape_lmi}
\end{equation}
The first inequality is the Schur form of Lemma~\ref{lem:dissipation}
at $\omega=1$, so $\bm L=P^{-1}\bm Y$ satisfies the dissipation
inequality at the decay floor. The second makes $P$ an upper bound of
the observability Gramian of $(A_L,\bm e_2^{\trans})$
\cite{BoydElGhaouiFeronBalakrishnan1994}: for the linear error equation
$\dot{\bm e}=A_L\bm e+\bm Ln$ driven by white output noise $n$ of unit
intensity, the stationary variance of the velocity error is at most
$\bm L^{\trans}P\bm L\leq\gamma$. All conditions are linear in
$(P,\bm Y,t_q,t_s,\gamma)$. One metric serves both the Gramian and the
dissipation inequality, so $\gamma$ is an upper bound on the smallest
variance attainable among certified gains, not that minimum; the
variance is a performance preference that ignores the nonlinear term
and the sampled realization, and it is not an error bound.

The returned gains are written as
$\bm L=[l_1\omega_0,\,l_2\omega_0^2,\,l_3\omega_0^3]^{\trans}$ with
$\omega_0=\min_i|\operatorname{Re}\lambda_i(A_L)|$ the slowest
closed-loop rate, which fixes a shape $\bm l$ of unit slowest rate. For
each candidate bandwidth $\omega>1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ the certificate
metric is solved anew: minimize $\operatorname{tr}P$ over $P\succeq I$
and $t_q,t_s>0$ subject to
\begin{equation}
    \begin{bmatrix}
    Z&P\bm e_3&P\bm e_3\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&-t_q&0\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&0&-t_s
    \end{bmatrix}\preceq0,
    \label{eq:free_metric_lmi}
\end{equation}
where $Z=\omega(A_l^{\trans}P+PA_l)+2\lambda_TP
+\diag(0,t_qL_q^2/\omega^2,t_sL_s^2)$, whose Schur complement is
Lemma~\ref{lem:dissipation}. The normalization fixes the homogeneous
scale and leaves the metric free; the recovered matrix is checked in
the original inequality with numerical reserves. A bracketed scalar
search then minimizes the largest of the three ultimate bounds
$c_{T,i}(-H^{-1}\bm d)_2$ of \eqref{eq:component_extraction}, each
divided by its domain scale $\rho_{\max}$, $b$ or $S$. This is a
feasible local selection of the bandwidth, not a joint optimum, and
because the search may move the bandwidth away from $\omega_0$, the
variance bound $\gamma$ applies to the gains of
\eqref{eq:noise_shape_lmi}, not to the rescaled ones. The programs are
solved with YALMIP \cite{Lofberg2004} and SeDuMi \cite{Sturm1999}.
Neither simulated errors nor the sensor sample period enter the design.

\subsection{Error Enclosure and the Published Sets}
\label{subsec:enclosure}

A controller that tightens its constraints needs an error radius at
every sample, during the transient as well as at steady state. The
comparison system supplies one, because \eqref{eq:comparison_solution}
holds for all \(t\).

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:enclosure}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:cascadeISS} and any
\(\bm z_0\geq\bm\chi(0)\),
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm\rho(t)-\hat{\bm\rho}(t)}\leq B_\rho(t):=c_{T,1}z_2(t),
    \quad c_{T,1}=\sqrt{(P^{-1})_{11}},
    \label{eq:relative_position_bound}
\end{equation}
and \(\limsup_{t\to\infty}B_\rho\leq c_{T,1}(-H^{-1}\bm d)_2\). An
initial metric bound follows from componentwise initial bounds
\(\bm b_{T0}\) as
\(W_T(0)\leq\bigl((D\bm b_{T0})^{\trans}|P|(D\bm b_{T0})\bigr)^{1/2}\).
Moreover the comparison solution is bounded uniformly in time,
\begin{equation}
    \bm z(t)\leq\bar{\bm z}:=\sup_{\tau\geq0}\bm z(\tau)<\infty ,
    \label{eq:uniform_bound}
\end{equation}
so every radius derived from it is bounded by a constant that depends
only on the design, the declared bounds and \(\bm z_0\).
\end{corollary}

The uniform bound \eqref{eq:uniform_bound} is elementary, since
$e^{Ht}\to0$ and the integral in \eqref{eq:comparison_solution}
converges to $-H^{-1}\bm d$, but it is the quantity that the robust
argument of Section~\ref{subsec:robust} needs: the estimation error
that the controller has to absorb from one sample to the next is bounded
by a constant it can plan with.

Inertial reconstruction of the relative vector costs one further yaw
term,
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm p_C-\bm p_E-R(\hat\psi_E)\hat{\bm\rho}}
    \leq B_\rho+2\bar R\sin\frac{\min(B_\psi,\pi)}2 ,
    \label{eq:inertial_relative_bound}
\end{equation}
\(\bar R=\min(\rho_{\max},\normtwo{\hat{\bm\rho}})\), and the analogous
rotation bounds convert the remaining components of
\eqref{eq:component_extraction} into the componentwise ego and target
enclosures \(\bm b_E(t)\), \(\bm b_T(t)\). These certify the current
estimate; they are not predictions of future observer output. Two sets
are published to the controller at every sample $k$. The ego set is a
zonotope
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal E_k=\{G_0\bm\zeta:\norminf{\bm\zeta}\leq1\}
    \label{eq:ego_set}
\end{equation}
about the estimated controller state, whose generator matrix $G_0$
keeps the correlation between the yaw error and the body velocity
inferred from the GNSS velocity. The target set $\mathcal Q_k$ consists
of a ball of radius $B_\rho$ for the relative position (an inertial
ball once \eqref{eq:inertial_relative_bound} and the ego position radius
are added), an interval for the course, and intervals for the speed, the
scalar acceleration $A_C$ and the curvature $\kappa_C$, each intersected
with the declared domain \eqref{eq:target_domain}. The last two follow
from the reconstructions $A$ and $\Omega/V$ of \eqref{eq:target_chain}
and the component bounds \eqref{eq:component_extraction}. The digital
runtime accounts for held measurements, relative-position prediction and
outages in a separate finite-time enclosure. The continuous theorem does
not establish exponential stability of the sampled fourth-order
Runge--Kutta (RK4) realization, nor a directed-rounding certificate for
the computed bounds.

\section{Control Design}
\label{sec:control}

The controller solves, at every sample, the problem of
\cite{HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025} for the vehicle: minimize the
relaxation of the collision constraints over a horizon that ends in a
terminal set, then, with that relaxation held, minimize the violation of
a decrease condition on one CLF. Every sample builds one nonlinear
rollout, linearizes the vehicle model along it, and solves the two
programs on that affine model. The completed secondary input sequence is
issued directly. A nonlinear rollout supplies prediction and trust data
only, with no post-solve scaling or feasibility veto.
The horizon ends in a terminal set that is forward invariant under one
braking law, so that on the declared model with exact information the
plan of one sample, shifted by one hold, is an acceptable plan at the
next. No maneuver template is prescribed and no backup command exists;
the same programs run during avoidance and during the return to cruise.
The controller acts on the estimates of Section~\ref{sec:estimator} and
tightens its constraints by their enclosures. Section~\ref{subsec:robust}
defines the uncertainty-aware mathematical design with future observer
updates and causal feedback, and proves its recursive feasibility. The
obstacle vehicle of this section is the target of the
previous one.

\subsection{Prediction Model}
\label{subsec:predictionModel}

The subscript $E$ is dropped from the ego variables in this section.
The controller state and input are
\begin{equation}
    \bm x=[p_x,\,p_y,\,\psi,\,v_x,\,v_y,\,r]^{\trans},\qquad
    \bm u=[\delta_F,\,\beta]^{\trans},
    \label{eq:controller_state}
\end{equation}
the inertial pose, the body velocities and the yaw rate $r=\dot\psi$,
and the front steering angle and the signed longitudinal force ratio.
Equations \eqref{eq:position_kinematics}--\eqref{eq:fiala_force} define
$\dot{\bm x}=f(\bm x,\bm u)$ above the speed $v_2=4$\,m/s. Below
$v_1=2.5$\,m/s the body accelerations are those of a kinematic bicycle,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    m_E\dot v_x=\bigl[\beta_++\beta_-\tanh(v_x/v_{\rm h})\bigr]
        \bigl(\mu_fF_{zf}\cos\delta_F+\mu_rF_{zr}\bigr)\\
        \qquad{}-F_R(v_x)+m_Ev_yr,\\
    \dot v_y=\frac{l_{r,E}r_{\rm k}-v_y}{T_{\rm k}},\qquad
    \dot r=\frac{r_{\rm k}-r}{T_{\rm k}},\qquad
    r_{\rm k}=\frac{v_x\tan\delta_F}{L},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:kinematic_model}
\end{equation}
with $\beta_+=\max(\beta,0)$, $\beta_-=\min(\beta,0)$, a brake-hold
speed $v_{\rm h}$ and a lag $T_{\rm k}$. The yaw rate and the lateral
velocity relax to their kinematic values, a rear axle without slip; the
longitudinal force is the force ratio times the axle capacities; and
braking fades below $v_{\rm h}$, so that it ends at rest and never
reverses the vehicle, while driving moves it off from rest. Between
$v_1$ and $v_2$ the body accelerations of the two models are blended
with the weight $\lambda=s^2(3-2s)$, $s=(v_x-v_1)/(v_2-v_1)$; position
and heading follow the body velocities in all three regimes. A braked
vehicle slower than $v_{\rm h}/5$ at the end of a hold is taken to be at
rest exactly, with zero body velocities and zero sensitivities, because
the vanishing tail of the fading brake, with coefficients of $10^{-4}$
to $10^{-7}$, had left the second program without a numerical result
while the vehicle crept to a stop. Above $v_2$ the model is the Fiala
bicycle exactly, so the certificates of Section~\ref{subsec:clf},
computed at 8 and 15\,m/s, do not see the low-speed regime. That regime
exists so that the vehicle can stop: the terminal set of
Section~\ref{subsec:terminal} contains stops, and a model that ended at
the tire's 1\,m/s floor could not reach them. The passive flat-road
load in still air is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    F_R(v)={}&\tfrac12\rho_{\rm air}C_dA_fv|v|\\
       &+m_Eg(q_0+q_1|v|+q_4|v|^4)\tanh(v/v_{\rm tr}),
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:road_load}
\end{equation}
with air density $\rho_{\rm air}$, drag coefficient $C_d$, frontal area
$A_f$, nonnegative rolling coefficients $q_0,q_1,q_4$ and
$v_{\rm tr}>0$. The model uses static axle loads and leaves out wheel
inertia, wind, grade and dynamic load transfer.

An input is held for the sample period $T_s$. The prediction map
$F(\bm x,\bm u)$ is one classical RK4 step of $f$ over $T_s$, and its
Jacobians $D_xF$ and $D_uF$ come from the variational equations
propagated through the same step, with analytic tire-force derivatives
in both slip angle and force ratio. Linearization points stay strictly
inside $|\beta|<1$, where those derivatives exist. Constraints are also
imposed halfway through each hold; with one integration step per hold,
the mid-hold state is the endpoint interpolant
$\tfrac12(\bm x+F(\bm x,\bm u))$, not an independently integrated half
step.

The reference path is straight or of constant curvature
$\kappa_{\rm r}$ and is traversed at the prescribed speed $v_*$. Steady
motion along it has yaw rate $r_*=\kappa_{\rm r}v_*$ and a constant
sideslip; the three balances of \eqref{eq:bicycle_model} determine that
sideslip and the trim input $\bm u_*=[\delta_{F,*},\beta_*]^{\trans}$.
Deviation from cruise is measured by the transverse error
\begin{equation}
    \bm e(\bm x)=[e_y,\ e_\psi,\ v_x-v_{x,*},\ v_y-v_{y,*},\
        r-r_*]^{\trans},
    \label{eq:transverse_error}
\end{equation}
where $e_y$ is the signed lateral offset of the center of mass from the
path and $e_\psi$ the heading relative to the path tangent minus its
trim value. Position along the path is not an error: the nominal
behavior is the set of all phases of cruise on the path. The road is the
strip $-c_{\rm R}\leq e_y\leq c_{\rm L}$ with declared clearances
$c_{\rm R},c_{\rm L}>0$ to the right and left of the path.

The obstacle keeps the constant scalar acceleration $A_C$ and constant
sideslip $\beta_C$ of the tracker model, written in inertial
coordinates, with braking that ends at rest. With the curvature
$\kappa_C=\sin\beta_C/l_{r,C}$, the speed $V_C(t)=\max(0,V_{C,0}+A_Ct)$,
the arc length $s(t)$ its integral and $\vartheta(t)=\tfrac12\kappa_Cs(t)$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm p_C(t)&=\bm p_{C,0}+s\,\frac{\sin\vartheta}{\vartheta}
        \begin{bmatrix}\cos(\psi_{C,0}+\beta_C+\vartheta)\\
        \sin(\psi_{C,0}+\beta_C+\vartheta)\end{bmatrix},\\
    \psi_C(t)&=\psi_{C,0}+\kappa_Cs,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_flow}
\end{equation}
with $\sin\vartheta/\vartheta=1$ at $\vartheta=0$. No quadrature is
needed, and the yaw rate $\kappa_CV_C(t)$ varies with the speed. A
braking obstacle stops at the arc length $-V_{C,0}^2/(2A_C)$ and stays
there, with $A_C$ then zero; an obstacle at rest moves off only forward.
Every new estimate replaces the obstacle state and the parameters $A_C$
and $\beta_C$; the forecast runs forward from the latest estimate and
the sample at which it was taken, because a stop cannot be run
backward, and the parameters are constant within one prediction and may
change at the next sample. When no estimate arrives, the last forecast
continues. The obstacle is perceived only within the range $R_{\rm enc}$,
and collision constraints are imposed at the prediction nodes at which
the two reference points are closer than $R_{\rm enc}$, including after
a later re-entry.

An anchor trajectory $(\bar{\bm x}_i,\bar{\bm u}_i)$, $i=0,\ldots,M$,
with $\bar{\bm x}_0$ the supplied estimate and
$\bar{\bm x}_{i+1}=F(\bar{\bm x}_i,\bar{\bm u}_i)$, supplies every
linearization of the sample. With
$\Delta\bm x_i=\bm x_i-\bar{\bm x}_i$,
$\Delta\bm u_i=\bm u_i-\bar{\bm u}_i$,
$A_i=D_xF(\bar{\bm x}_i,\bar{\bm u}_i)$ and
$B_i=D_uF(\bar{\bm x}_i,\bar{\bm u}_i)$, the shared model is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \Delta\bm x_{i+1}=A_i\Delta\bm x_i+B_i\Delta\bm u_i,\qquad
    \Delta\bm x_0=0,\\
    |\Delta\bm x_i|\leq\bm\tau_x,\qquad
    |\Delta\bm u_i|\leq\sigma\bm\tau_u .
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:affine_model}
\end{equation}
The trust boxes limit the correction of one solve; they are not
certified bounds on the linearization remainder. The scale
$\sigma\in[\sigma_{\min},1]$ of the input box is estimated online
(Section~\ref{subsec:trust}) and starts at $\sigma_0$. The force ratio
obeys its actuator interval, and the body velocities $v_x$, $v_y$ and $r$
obey box bounds at the nodes and mid-hold points, with $v_x\geq0$. The
steering angle has neither a magnitude nor a rate bound.

The horizon length $M$ is not fixed. It has $N$ primary holds, on which
the collision constraints may be relaxed, and it ends at the first node
$M\geq N$ of the anchor that lies in the terminal set $\mathcal X_f$ of
Section~\ref{subsec:terminal}, or at a largest length $M_{\max}$ when
the anchor never reaches it.

\subsection{The Anchor}
\label{subsec:initialization}

At the first sample, and at a sample at which an obstacle appears that
the previous plan did not know of, the anchor is a rollout under a
guidance feedback: the path-guidance law $\bm\pi$ below without an
obstacle, and a potential-field guidance with one. It stops at its
first node $M\geq N$ in $\mathcal X_f$, or at $M_{\max}$. Every later
sample starts from the previous issued plan, which is itself a
nonlinear rollout (Section~\ref{subsec:program}), shifted by one hold
and rolled out again through $F$ from the new estimate; its endpoint
keeps its absolute time, so the plan is one hold shorter. While the
shifted plan is shorter than $N$, or its endpoint is no longer in
$\mathcal X_f$ because the forecast or the state has changed, holds of
the declared model are appended, each kept only when the hard rows of
Sections~\ref{subsec:geometry}--\ref{subsec:envelope} hold on it and its
endpoint lies in $\mathcal X_f$: first the hold of the cruise law
$\bm u=\bm u_*+K\bm e$ of Section~\ref{subsec:clf}; if that hold takes an
endpoint out of $\mathcal X_f$, holds of the guidance feedback through
states outside $\mathcal X_f$ until a node in $\mathcal X_f$ is reached
again, which lengthens the plan to complete a maneuver instead of
braking to keep its endpoint mild; and otherwise the hold nearest to
the cruise hold on a segment toward holding the previous steering angle,
at the cruise force ratio first, then at the ratio whose deceleration is
the terminal law's $a_{\rm b}$, then braking as hard as the rear
adhesion admits, each found by bisection. When no hold keeps the
endpoint in $\mathcal X_f$, the plan stays one hold shorter. Predicted
states of the previous affine solution are never reused as the anchor,
and a fresh rollout is used once more in a sample whose shifted problem
is infeasible (Section~\ref{subsec:program}).

The path-guidance law steers the course toward the path through a
lookahead and regulates the speed through the force ratio. With $\chi$
the course relative to the path tangent, $V$ the speed, $d_{\rm la}$ a
lookahead distance, $\chi_d$ the commanded course and $\dot\chi_d$ its
rate along the motion,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \chi_d=-\arctan\frac{e_y}{d_{\rm la}},\qquad
    \dot\chi_d=-\frac{d_{\rm la}V\sin\chi}{d_{\rm la}^2+e_y^2},\\
    r_d=\operatorname{sat}_{\bar r}\Bigl[
        \frac{\kappa_{\rm r}V\cos\chi}{1-\kappa_{\rm r}e_y}
        +\dot\chi_d-k_\chi(\chi-\chi_d)\Bigr],\\
    F_d=\frac{I_{z,E}k_r(r_d-r)+l_{r,E}F_{yr}}{l_{f,E}},\qquad
    \phi_d=\min\Bigl(\frac{|F_d|}{Q_f},c_\phi\Bigr),\\
    \delta_F=\operatorname{atan2}(v_y+l_{f,E}r,v_x)+\delta_0\\
        \qquad{}+\operatorname{sgn}(F_d)\arctan
        \frac{3Q_f[1-(1-\phi_d)^{1/3}]}{C_f},\\
    \beta=\operatorname{sat}_{\bar\beta}\bigl[
        \beta_*+k_\beta(v_{x,*}-v_x)\bigr].
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:guidance_law}
\end{equation}
The yaw-rate demand $r_d$ is limited to $\bar r=c_a\mu g/V$, a fraction
of the available lateral acceleration, with $\mu$ the smaller axle
friction coefficient. The front force demand $F_d$ follows from a
yaw-rate loop and the current rear force $F_{yr}$, the steering angle
inverts the front Fiala curve \eqref{eq:fiala_force} below the fraction
$c_\phi$ of its capacity $Q_f$, and $\delta_0$ is the constant that
makes the trim an equilibrium. The law is never issued as a command.

With an obstacle, the course demand follows a potential field adapted
from \cite{ZhaiEtAl2024}: obstacle influence that depends on direction,
repulsion that depends on the relative motion, and joint course and
speed guidance. At each rollout node the ego is previewed along the path
for a time $T_{\rm p}$, at its current speed along the path and with its
lateral offset decaying as $e^{-\tau/T_{\rm la}}$, and compared with the
obstacle prediction \eqref{eq:target_flow} at the same absolute times
$\tau_j$. With the longitudinal and lateral separations
$\Delta s_j,\Delta d_j$ in path coordinates and the summed half extents
$a_j,b_j$ of the two rectangles along and across the path, including a
padding,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    w=\max_jw_j,\qquad
    w_j=\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\Delta s_j^2}{2a_j^2}
        -\frac{\Delta d_j^2}{2b_j^2}-\frac{\tau_j}{2T_{\rm la}}\Bigr),\\
    v_d=-\frac{e_y}{T_{\rm la}}+0.7\,v_*w\Bigl(
        \frac{\Delta d_\star}{b_\star}+2c\Bigr),\\
    v_s=\max\bigl(1.5\,\mathrm{m/s},\ 0.5v_*,\
        v_*[1-0.35\,w\min(2,v_{\rm cl}/v_*)]\bigr),
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:potential_field}
\end{equation}
where the star marks the preview instant of largest influence,
$v_{\rm cl}\geq0$ is the closing speed along the path at that instant,
and $c=-\nu_{\rm p}\,\bm t_{\rm r}^{\trans}(\bm p-\bm p_C)/
\max(\normtwo{\bm p-\bm p_C},1\,\mathrm m)$ is a circulation term with
path tangent $\bm t_{\rm r}$ and passing side $\nu_{\rm p}\in\{-1,1\}$.
The commanded course is the path heading plus
$\operatorname{atan2}(v_d,v_s)$, with $v_d$ limited so that the body
stays on the road, and the speed demand is $v_s$. The side $\nu_{\rm p}$
is selected once per rollout from the lateral separation at the closest
predicted approach, the obstacle's transverse velocity and the road room
that would remain on either side after clearing the obstacle. The last
term matters with estimated states: without it a head-on encounter in
the lane was decided by the sign of the estimated obstacle sideslip,
which is noise. The field combines attraction, a normalized repulsion
and circulation and is not the gradient of one potential. A yaw-rate
loop, the inverse Fiala curve and a speed loop as in
\eqref{eq:guidance_law}, with limits that apply only to this rollout,
drive the map $F$. The rollout fixes the side on which the obstacle is
passed; the programs of Section~\ref{subsec:program} refine the
trajectory locally and do not revisit that choice. A fresh anchor is a
search initialization and nothing more: it can itself overlap the
obstacle or leave the road.

\subsection{Collision Constraints}
\label{subsec:geometry}

Both vehicles are rectangles. Let
$\mathcal C(t)=\{\bm m:H_C(\bm m-\bm p_C(t))\leq\bm h_C\}$ be the
obstacle rectangle, with $H_C=[I;\,-I]R(\psi_C(t))^{\trans}$ and
$\bm h_C$ its extents about $\bm p_C$, and let
$\bm p_j(\bm x)=\bm p+R(\psi)\bm o_j$, $j=1,\ldots,4$, be the ego
vertices with body-frame offsets $\bm o_j$. The Euclidean distance
between the two sets has the dual representation
\cite{ZhangLinigerBorrelli2021}
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    d(\bm x,t)=\max_{\bm\lambda\in\Lambda}\ \min_j\
        g_j(\bm x;\bm\lambda),\\
    g_j=\bm\lambda^{\trans}\bigl[H_C(\bm p_j(\bm x)-\bm p_C)
        -\bm h_C\bigr],\\
    \Lambda=\{\bm\lambda\geq0:\normtwo{H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda}\leq1\}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:distance_dual}
\end{equation}
Following \cite{LiZhangGuoLenzoGuo2023}, the multipliers are chosen at
the anchor and then held fixed while the trajectory is optimized.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:dualRows}
(i) For every $\bm\lambda\in\Lambda$ and every $\bm x$,
$d(\bm x,t)\geq\min_jg_j(\bm x;\bm\lambda)$.
(ii) If the rectangles are disjoint at $\bar{\bm x}$ and $\bm n$ is the
unit vector from a closest point of $\mathcal C$ to a closest point of
the ego rectangle, then
$\bm\lambda^\star=[\max(\bm w,0);\,\max(-\bm w,0)]$ with
$\bm w=R(\psi_C)^{\trans}\bm n$ satisfies
$H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda^\star=\bm n$ and
$\min_jg_j(\bar{\bm x};\bm\lambda^\star)=d(\bar{\bm x},t)$.
(iii) If a point of the ego rectangle lies in the interior of
$\mathcal C$, the maximum in \eqref{eq:distance_dual} is zero and
$\bm\lambda=0$ is its only maximizer.
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
(i) For $\bm m$ in the ego rectangle and $\bm m'\in\mathcal C$,
$\normtwo{\bm m-\bm m'}
\geq(H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda)^{\trans}(\bm m-\bm m')
\geq\bm\lambda^{\trans}[H_C(\bm m-\bm p_C)-\bm h_C]$, because
$\normtwo{H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda}\leq1$, $\bm\lambda\geq0$ and
$H_C(\bm m'-\bm p_C)\leq\bm h_C$. The right side is affine in $\bm m$,
so its minimum over the ego rectangle is attained at a vertex.
(ii) The direction $\bm n$ is the same for every closest pair.
$H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda^\star
=R(\psi_C)[\max(\bm w,0)-\max(-\bm w,0)]=\bm n$, and
$\bm h_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda^\star$ is the support of
$\mathcal C-\bm p_C$ in direction $\bm n$, attained at the closest point
$\bm m_C$. The line through the closest ego point with normal $\bm n$
supports the ego rectangle, hence
$\min_j\bm n^{\trans}(\bm p_j-\bm m_C)=d$. (iii) At such a point
$\bm m_0$ every component of $H_C(\bm m_0-\bm p_C)-\bm h_C$ is negative,
so $\min_jg_j\leq\bm\lambda^{\trans}[H_C(\bm m_0-\bm p_C)-\bm h_C]<0$
unless $\bm\lambda=0$.
\end{IEEEproof}

When the anchor is disjoint from the obstacle at a constrained instant,
the multipliers are those of Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(ii), computed in
closed form from the closest features of the two rectangles. When the
anchor overlaps, Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(iii) leaves only the zero
multiplier, whose row has no gradient in the ego pose, and the row is
built from another element of $\Lambda$: the unit normal $\bm n$ of the
rectangle axis, among the four axes of the two bodies, along which the
penetration is smallest. By Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(i) its value is
still a lower bound of the distance; it is negative at the anchor and
increases as the bodies separate along that axis, so the program keeps
a direction of escape. With $\bm\lambda$ so chosen at the anchor pose
and the obstacle pose taken from \eqref{eq:target_flow} at the same
absolute time, each constrained instant contributes the four rows
\begin{equation}
    g_j(\bar{\bm x};\bm\lambda)+\bm n^{\trans}\Delta\bm p
    +\bm n^{\trans}R(\bar\psi)J\bm o_j\,\Delta\psi-\theta_i+\xi_i
    \geq d_{\rm m},
    \label{eq:collision_rows}
\end{equation}
affine in the decision variables, with $\bm n=H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda$, a
node margin $d_{\rm m}$, a slack $\xi_i\geq0$ and a tightening
$\theta_i\geq0$ that accounts for the estimation error
(Section~\ref{subsec:uncertainty}; $\theta_i=0$ without an enclosure).
The constrained instants are the hold starts and mid-hold points within
$R_{\rm enc}$ and the endpoint of the horizon. Without an enclosure, one
slack per primary hold relaxes its start and mid-hold rows, and the rows
of later holds have none. With an enclosure, every hold has a slack on
its tightened rows, and beyond the primary holds the untightened rows
($\theta_i=0$) are imposed in addition without slack, so that the
formulation reduces to the former as the enclosure vanishes. Rows that
no correction inside the trust boxes can violate are not formed, which
leaves the feasible set unchanged (Section~\ref{subsec:computation}).
The vertex rotation is kept to first order, with remainder at most
$\tfrac12\normtwo{\bm o_j}\Delta\psi^2$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(i)
the rows \eqref{eq:collision_rows} with $\xi_i=\theta_i=0$ imply
$d\geq d_{\rm m}-\tfrac12r_E\Delta\psi_i^2$ for the affine-predicted
pose, where $r_E=\max_j\normtwo{\bm o_j}$; with the values of
Section~\ref{sec:results} ($r_E=2.58$\,m, $|\Delta\psi|\leq0.25$\,rad,
$d_{\rm m}=0.20$\,m) the right side is at least $0.119$\,m. The issued
plan is checked against the exact rectangle distance at the same
instants (Section~\ref{subsec:program}).

\subsection{Road, Handling Envelope and Estimator Domain}
\label{subsec:envelope}

The ego drives on the road: at every predicted node after the current
one and at every mid-hold point, each rectangle vertex satisfies
\begin{equation}
    -c_{\rm R}\leq e_y\bigl(\bm p_j(\bm x)\bigr)\leq c_{\rm L},
    \qquad j=1,\ldots,4,
    \label{eq:road_rows}
\end{equation}
linearized in position and heading on the path normal at the anchor
vertex. These rows carry no slack in either program.

Two more conditions tie the plan to the estimator of
Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided}. At both ends of every hold, under that
hold's force ratio $\beta_i$,
\begin{equation}
    \left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}(v_y-l_{r,E}r)/k_r^{\rm sl}\\
        \bar v_x\beta_i\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_2\leq v_x,
    \qquad k_r^{\rm sl}=\frac{3\mu_rF_{zr}}{C_r},
    \label{eq:adhesion_cone}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    |v_y|\leq v_x\tan\beta_{\rm dom}.
    \label{eq:sideslip_cone}
\end{equation}
With $\bar v_x$ replaced by $v_x$, \eqref{eq:adhesion_cone} reads
$|\tan\alpha_r|\leq k_r^{\rm sl}\sqrt{1-\beta_i^2}
=\tan\alpha_{{\rm sl},r}$: the rear tire stays below the saturation
branch of \eqref{eq:fiala_force} at the capacity left by the
longitudinal force. The implementation freezes $v_x$ at its anchor
value $\bar v_x$ in the bilinear product, which makes the condition one
second-order cone, and divides the cone by $\bar v_x$ so that its
entries are of order one. This is a stable-handling envelope in the
sense of \cite{BealGerdes2013}, since a saturated rear axle supplies no
restoring yaw moment, and it is at the same time an observability
condition, because a saturated force no longer determines the slip and
the inversion of \eqref{eq:force_balance} loses its information.
Condition \eqref{eq:sideslip_cone} is the cone premise of that
inversion, with the same $\beta_{\rm dom}$. The adhesion cone applies at
nodes whose anchor speed is above $v_1$, where the rear tire has a
lateral force to saturate, and the sideslip cone above $v_{\rm h}$,
where the sideslip is defined. Before these rows existed, a run with
estimated states at 15\,m/s combined a steering angle of $-0.40$\,rad
with a force ratio of $0.95$ and spun to a sideslip of 0.68\,rad.

\subsection{Terminal Set}
\label{subsec:terminal}

The horizon ends where a simple continuation of both vehicles is known
to be safe for the rest of the encounter. Past the endpoint
$\bm z=\bm x_M$ at time $t_M$ the obstacle keeps its forecast
\eqref{eq:target_flow}, and the ego follows one terminal law
$\kappa_{\rm f}$: a kinematic vehicle with its steering held, braking
at the constant rate $a_{\rm b}$ until it stops. With the steering held
a kinematic bicycle keeps its sideslip and its course curvature, so the
law keeps the ego in the same constant-acceleration, constant-curvature
family as the obstacle. With $V=\normtwo{\bm v^E}$,
$\beta_E=\operatorname{atan2}(v_y,v_x)$ and $\kappa_E=r/V$ at $\bm z$,
the speed is $V_j=\max(V-ja_{\rm b}T_s,0)$ at the $j$th node of the
continuation and linear in between, so the ego stops exactly at the end
of hold $J=\lceil V/(a_{\rm b}T_s)\rceil$; with $s(\tau)$ the arc length
of that schedule and $\vartheta=\tfrac12\kappa_Es$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm p(\tau)&=\bm p+s\,\frac{\sin\vartheta}{\vartheta}
        \begin{bmatrix}\cos(\psi+\beta_E+\vartheta)\\
        \sin(\psi+\beta_E+\vartheta)\end{bmatrix},\\
    \psi(\tau)&=\psi+\kappa_Es,\qquad \tau\in[0,JT_s].
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:continuation}
\end{equation}
The joint continuation is exact and in closed form; it agrees with an
RK4 integration of the same kinematics to $10^{-9}$\,m. It is sampled at
its nodes and mid-hold points, the instants at which the plan's own
collision rows are sampled. The measured course curvature $r/V$ is held
rather than the kinematic $\sin\beta_E/l_{r,E}$ of the obstacle model,
which presumes a rear axle without slip: for the Fiala state in the trim
of a 4\,m/s$^2$ left turn at 15\,m/s, whose sideslip at the center of
mass is $-0.0093$\,rad, that relation would continue the turn as a
right turn.

A state is \emph{mild} when its sideslip lies in the separate terminal
cone ($|\beta_E|\le0.05$~rad in the current configuration), inside
\eqref{eq:sideslip_cone} and the lateral acceleration of the held arc is
small, $|rV|\leq a_{\rm m}$. With $a_{\rm b}=4$\,m/s$^2$ and
$a_{\rm m}=2$\,m/s$^2$ the continuation uses 54\% of the friction
budget $\mu g=8.3$\,m/s$^2$, and the configuration requires
$\sqrt{a_{\rm b}^2+a_{\rm m}^2}<\mu g$. The terminal braking ratio is also checked against the actuator bounds and
rear adhesion. Along the continuation
$\beta_E$ is constant and $|rV|=|\kappa_E|V^2$ only falls, so a mild
state stays mild. An endpoint in the middle of a swerve is not mild: the
plan has to settle before it is terminal.

\begin{assumption}
\label{asm:mild}
In a mild state the prediction model of
Section~\ref{subsec:predictionModel} follows the kinematic vehicle under
the terminal law. No bound on the mismatch is computed.
\end{assumption}

This is a modeling assumption of the design, not a derived bound. It
enters only the recursive-feasibility statements of
Sections~\ref{subsec:recursive} and \ref{subsec:robust}: every hold the
controller appends to a plan is a hold of the prediction model whose
endpoint is checked in the terminal set, so the implementation does not
rely on it.

Let $\phi^j(\bm z)$, $j\geq0$, be the joint continuation at the nodes
and mid-hold points after $t_M$, $t_e(\bm z)$ the first of these
instants at which the obstacle's reference point is farther than
$R_{\rm enc}$ from the ego's ($+\infty$ if none), and $t_s(\bm z)=JT_s$
the stop. The terminal set is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal X_f=\bigl\{\bm z\ \text{mild}:\ \text{(a), (b) and (c) hold}\bigr\},
    \label{eq:terminal_set}
\end{equation}
where (a) every node, mid-hold pose and the parked pose of the
continuation keeps the ego rectangle on the road; (b) every node and
mid-hold pose before $t_e(\bm z)$ clears the forecast obstacle rectangle
by $d_{\rm m}$; and (c) if $t_e(\bm z)>t_s(\bm z)$, the obstacle's
remaining track until it leaves the range about the parked reference
point, or comes to rest, clears the parked rectangle by $d_{\rm m}$. The
first exit ends the encounter, and no property of the obstacle after it
is used. Condition (b) at $j=0$ includes the endpoint itself, so
$\mathcal X_f$ lies in the safe set. An enclosure, when published,
widens $d_{\rm m}$ in (b) and (c) and the road margin in (a) by the ego
position radius and the chord of its heading radius
(Section~\ref{subsec:uncertainty}).

Condition (c) is decided in closed form. After the stop the ego is a
fixed rectangle and the obstacle moves along its line or circle. The arc
lengths at which its reference point stays within $R_{\rm enc}$ of the
parked point form one interval (a quadratic inequality on a line, an arc
of angles on a circle, the whole circle when it lies inside the range),
and from its position the obstacle sweeps that stretch until it leaves,
or until it comes to rest and stays. The distance between two
rectangles is continuous along the track and, while they are apart, is
attained between a vertex of one and an edge of the other; since the two
are apart at the stop, a node of (b), the least distance over the
stretch is the least distance between a vertex path, a segment on a line
or an arc on a circle, and an edge, over the 32 vertex--edge pairs. The
arc-to-segment distance has three kinds of candidates: the ends of the
arc against the segment, the ends of the segment against the arc, and
the point of the circle nearest to the segment's line when it lies on
both. The check also returns the obstacle pose attaining the least
distance, the witness, which the convex program linearizes. In 2895
random cases it was never above the least distance of a dense sampling
of the forecast in time (spacing at most 2.4\,mm; above by at most
$5\cdot10^{-13}$\,m from rounding) and within $3\cdot10^{-7}$\,m of it.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:invariance}
Let $\bm z\in\mathcal X_f$ and let $\bm z'$ be the ego state one hold
later under $\kappa_{\rm f}$, with the obstacle on its forecast. Then
$\bm z'\in\mathcal X_f$, or the obstacle has left the range. No sampled
pose of the continuation from $\bm z$ meets the obstacle before the
exit, nor does the parked ego at any time after the stop while the
obstacle is in range. $\mathcal X_f$ is the largest such set within the settled admissible
domain for the law $\kappa_{\rm f}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
The continuation of $\bm z'$ is the continuation of $\bm z$ shifted by
one hold: the speed schedule from $V-a_{\rm b}T_s$ is the remainder of
the schedule from $V$, the arc continues on the same circle with the
same sideslip and curvature, and the forecast is evaluated at the same
absolute times. $\bm z'$ is mild because $\beta_E$ is held and $|rV|$
falls. Every sampled pose of the new continuation is a pose of the old
one, so (a) holds and (b) holds up to the same exit instant; the parked
pose and the obstacle's remaining track are the same, so (c) holds. The
safety statements are (b) and (c) themselves. A state in the settled admissible domain but outside
$\mathcal X_f$ has a continuation under $\kappa_{\rm f}$ that leaves the
road or meets the obstacle at a sampled instant before the exit or after
the stop, so no set that the law keeps safe contains it.
\end{IEEEproof}

The construction is the backup-set principle of
\cite{ChenJankovicSantilloAmes2021}, used only to define the terminal
set. The free holds of the plan enlarge the set of states from which the
problem is feasible, and the ego never has to brake: the law only
certifies that a safe continuation exists.

In the convex program, membership of the endpoint is imposed through
the conditions of \eqref{eq:terminal_set} linearized at the anchor
endpoint $\bar{\bm x}_M$ on the correction $\Delta\bm x_M$: the mildness
bound $|rV|\leq a_{\rm m}$, linearized in speed and yaw rate where the
anchor is within 1\,m/s$^2$ of it and keeping up to $0.1$\,m/s$^2$ of
the margin the anchor has; fixed-multiplier rows
\eqref{eq:collision_rows} at the nodes and mid-hold poses of the
continuation before the exit and at the parked pose against the
witness; and road rows \eqref{eq:road_rows} at every pose of the
continuation; each composed with the derivative of the closed-form pose
\eqref{eq:continuation} with respect to the endpoint state, with the
braking schedule held. Each row keeps up to 5\,cm of the margin the
anchor has, so that an endpoint inside $\mathcal X_f$ stays inside by
that much and the next sample's shift survives a small departure of the
plant or of an appended hold from the law; a row is never tighter than
the anchor itself, so when $\bar{\bm x}_M\in\mathcal X_f$ the zero
correction satisfies every row. Conditions with more than 5\,m to
spare, or whose margin exceeds the most their pose can move over the
trust boxes, are left out. Exact membership is used when building the
anchor. The current direct-execution policy does not apply a post-solve
terminal membership test to the issued inputs.

\subsection{Quadratic Control-Lyapunov Function and Cruise Law}
\label{subsec:clf}

One obstacle-independent function measures the distance from cruise at
every sample, during avoidance and after it:
\begin{equation}
    V(\bm x)=\bm e(\bm x)^{\trans}P\,\bm e(\bm x),\qquad P\succ0 .
    \label{eq:clf}
\end{equation}
The matrix $P$ and a gain $K$ are synthesized offline for each operating
point (vehicle, speed, path curvature, sample period) and only read at
run time. They certify the cruise law $\bm u=\bm u_*+K\bm e$ on the
sampled nonlinear model: on the sublevel set $\Omega=\{V\leq1\}$, one
hold under that law gives
$V(F(\bm x,\bm u_*+K\bm e))\leq\rho^\star V(\bm x)$ with
$\rho^\star<\rho_V=e^{-2T_s/T_V}$ for a convergence time constant
$T_V$, the inputs satisfy $|K_j\bm e|\leq\bar u_j$, and $\Omega$ lies
inside a certification box $|e_i|\leq b_i$.

Let $g(\bm e,\Delta\bm u)$ be the transverse error after one hold from
the state with error $\bm e$ under the input $\bm u_*+\Delta\bm u$, and
$G=[\partial g/\partial\bm e,\ \partial g/\partial\Delta\bm u]$ its
Jacobian from the RK4 variational equations and the path chart. The trim
is a fixed point, $g(0,0)=0$, so along the ray from the origin
\begin{equation}
    g(\bm e,\Delta\bm u)=\bar G(\bm e,\Delta\bm u)
        \begin{bmatrix}\bm e\\\Delta\bm u\end{bmatrix},\qquad
    \bar G=\int_0^1G(t\bm e,t\Delta\bm u)\,dt,
    \label{eq:ray_average}
\end{equation}
exactly. A discrete Lyapunov inequality that holds for every matrix of
an enclosure of the ray averages $\bar G$ over $\Omega$ along the cruise
law therefore holds for the nonlinear map on $\Omega$. The enclosure is
a zonotope in box-scaled coordinates, $G_0+\sum_k\delta_kD_k+R$ with
$|\delta_k|\leq1$ and $\normtwo{R}\leq\epsilon_G$: the Jacobian $G_0$ at
the trim, the six leading principal directions $D_k$ of the sampled
deviations with their coefficient bounds, and a residual, all widened by
a factor of 1.25. The ray averages are computed by eight-node
Gauss--Legendre quadrature, whose residual in \eqref{eq:ray_average} is
added to $\epsilon_G$. Enclosing the ray averages rather than the
pointwise Jacobians matters for a saturating tire: the averages spread
like the secants of the force curve and the pointwise Jacobians like its
tangents, and at the slips that $\Omega$ reaches the tangent spread is
two to three times the secant spread. With $S=P^{-1}$, $Y=KS$ and
$[A_v\ B_v]$ the vertices of the zonotope, the synthesis maximizes the
fill $t$ subject to
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \begin{bmatrix}\rho_VS&N_v^{\trans}&Z^{\trans}\\
        N_v&S-\lambda_vI&0\\Z&0&\lambda_vI\end{bmatrix}\succeq0,\\
    N_v=A_vS+B_vY,\qquad Z=\epsilon_G\begin{bmatrix}S\\Y\end{bmatrix},\\
    \begin{bmatrix}\bar u_j^2&Y_j\\Y_j^{\trans}&S\end{bmatrix}\succeq0,\qquad
    S_{ii}\leq b_i^2,\qquad S\succeq t\,\diag(\bm b)^2,
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:clf_lmi}
\end{equation}
for every vertex $v$ and $j=1,2$, where $Y_j$ is the $j$th row of $Y$.
The first family is the vertex inequalities
$(A_v+B_vK)^{\trans}P(A_v+B_vK)\preceq\rho_VP$ with the residual
absorbed by Petersen's lemma \cite{Petersen1987}, the second bounds the
inputs on $\Omega$, and the last two place $\Omega$ in the box and
maximize its fill. Because the enclosure depends on $\Omega$ and $K$,
the design iterates to self-consistency: each round encloses the ray
averages over an inflation of the current set and over inputs within a
band of the current gain, and solves \eqref{eq:clf_lmi} with the new set
inside that inflated set and the new gain within the band. Every
round's pair is therefore certified by the enclosure it was designed
with; the pair with the largest fill is kept, re-verified by
eigenvalues, and the smallest contraction it certifies is recorded as
$\rho^\star$. The one non-algebraic step is the enclosure hypothesis,
that the ray averages over $\Omega$ along the law lie in the recorded
zonotope. It is built from 1200 boundary and 600 interior samples with a
recorded seed and checked again on fresh samples with another seed. The
certificate is conditional on that hypothesis; it is not a proof for the
nonlinear map. A Lipschitz bound on the remainder of the trim's
linearization would make it one but fails by two orders of magnitude on
this tire, which saturates at slips of a few hundredths of a radian.

Every sample requests
\begin{equation}
    V\bigl(F(\bm x,\bm u_0)\bigr)\leq\rho_VV(\bm x)+\delta,
    \quad\rho_V=e^{-2T_s/T_V},\quad\delta\geq0,
    \label{eq:clf_row}
\end{equation}
with $\bm x$ the supplied estimate: without slack the error measure
$\sqrt V$ shrinks by a factor $1/e$ every $T_V$. Only the first input
enters. In the program the successor is the affine first node, and the
error is linearized there with the analytic Jacobian $D\bm e$ of the
path chart,
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{P^{1/2}\bigl[\bm e(\bar{\bm x}_1)
        +D\bm e(\bar{\bm x}_1)\Delta\bm x_1\bigr]}^2
    \leq\rho_VV(\bm x)+\delta,
    \label{eq:clf_model}
\end{equation}
one second-order cone. The left side is a local model, so $\delta=0$ in
the program does not prove \eqref{eq:clf_row} for the nonlinear map.
The row acts on the estimate and carries no enclosure
(Section~\ref{subsec:uncertainty}). The gain $K$ is never used by the
programs: it defines the cruise hold that extends the anchor
(Section~\ref{subsec:initialization}). On $\Omega$ that hold decreases
$V$ by at least the requested factor within the input bound, and the
extension checks the decrease and the hard rows on every hold it
appends rather than relying on the certificate.

\subsection{Use of the Estimation Enclosure}
\label{subsec:uncertainty}

This subsection describes the numerical approximation used in the reported
trials. The joint prediction and proof for the uncertainty-aware
mathematical design are given in Section~\ref{subsec:robust}.

The estimator publishes at every sample the ego generator matrix $G_0$
and the target set of Section~\ref{subsec:enclosure}. Every hold of the
plan will be issued from such an estimate, and hold $i$ is tightened by
the present enclosure propagated through that hold only,
\begin{equation}
    G_i(\tau)=\Phi_i(\tau)G_0,\qquad\tau\in\{T_s/2,T_s\},
    \label{eq:hold_generator}
\end{equation}
with $\Phi_i$ the affine map of hold $i$ to its mid-hold point and its
end, $\tfrac12(I+A_i)$ and $A_i$; the node that closes hold $i$ is
constrained with $A_iG_0$ and the present node with $G_0$. A row
$\bm a^{\trans}\Delta\bm x\leq b$ on the ego state is tightened by the
support $\normone{\bm a^{\trans}G_i}$ of the zonotope; this applies to
the road rows \eqref{eq:road_rows}, the body-velocity bounds and the
sideslip cone \eqref{eq:sideslip_cone}. The terminal rows widen the
clearances and road margins of \eqref{eq:terminal_set} by the position
radius of $G_0$ and the chord of its heading radius, as a static offset;
the continuation is not propagated from the enclosure. The adhesion
cone \eqref{eq:adhesion_cone} acts on the estimate, because the
certified lateral-velocity radius widens exactly as the rear slip nears
saturation, and its reserve excluded states well inside the envelope.

The target is measured relative to the ego, so an error of the ego pose
moves both bodies rigidly and cancels from their distance. For the
collision rows, the rigid motion generated by the pose columns of $G_0$
about the ego position at the start of the hold is therefore subtracted
from $G_i$, leaving a relative generator $G_i^{\rm rel}$. The target set
restarts at each hold from the predicted target state at the hold
start. With radii $b_p,b_\gamma,b_V,b_A,b_\kappa$ of the published
position, course, speed, acceleration and curvature sets, nominal arc
length $s$ over the elapsed time $\tau$ and
$b_s=b_V\tau+\tfrac12b_A\tau^2$, the constant-parameter family
\eqref{eq:target_flow} is enclosed by
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    b_p(\tau)&=b_p+b_s+|s|b_\gamma+\tfrac12s^2b_\kappa,\\
    b_\gamma(\tau)&=\min\bigl(\pi,\ b_\gamma+|s|b_\kappa
        +(|\kappa_C|+b_\kappa)b_s\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_tube}
\end{equation}
and the heading radius $b_{\psi_C}$ adds the sideslip radius to
$b_\gamma(\tau)$; braking that ends at rest keeps $b_s$ valid, because
the speed $\max(0,V_{C,0}+A_C\tau)$ is 1-Lipschitz in $(V_{C,0},A_C\tau)$
for forward obstacles. A directional version of the position bound,
which charges a speed error along the path of the target only, is used
when it is smaller. The tightening of \eqref{eq:collision_rows} is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \theta_i={}&\normone{\bm n^{\trans}G_{i,p}^{\rm rel}}
        +\sigma_C(\bm n)\\
      &+2r_E\sin\frac{\min(\pi,b_{\psi})}{2}
        +2r_C\sin\frac{b_{\psi_C}}{2},
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:collision_tightening}
\end{equation}
with $G_{i,p}^{\rm rel}$ the position rows of the relative generator,
$\sigma_C(\bm n)\leq b_p(\tau)$ the target position support, $b_\psi$
the ego heading radius and $r_C$ the circumradius of the obstacle
rectangle; the last two terms bound the displacement of any body point
under a heading error by its chord. The tightening is sound for every
pose in the sets: for a target rectangle translated by $\Delta\bm p_C$
and rotated by $\Delta\psi_C$ from the forecast pose, the row value of
Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(i) falls by at most
$\bm n^{\trans}\Delta\bm p_C+2r_C\sin(|\Delta\psi_C|/2)$, and the ego
terms are the supports of its own pose set.

For the first hold, \eqref{eq:hold_generator} and
\eqref{eq:target_tube} propagate the current enclosures through the affine
ego model and the declared target family. Nonlinear ego remainders are
additional and have not been certified by this affine propagation. For later holds they are an approximation: they assume that
the enclosure at the start of hold $i$ will equal the present one and
that the estimate will then lie on the plan. Neither the family of
possible future estimates nor the forecast error of the target beyond
one hold is covered. We tried the alternatives. Propagating the ego
generator over the whole horizon, $G_{i+1}=A_iG_i$ from $G_0$, which is
the corresponding affine open-loop propagation, grew with the variable
horizon
until the road and state rows were infeasible. Propagating the target
set from the present sample over the whole horizon recovered 31 of 84
noisy encounters against 55 for the per-hold form and produced one
collision, because the tightened and the nominal row of a node share
one slack within the primary holds. Section~\ref{subsec:robust} explains
what these measurements mean for the guarantee. The row
\eqref{eq:clf_model} uses the estimate for a similar reason: a worst
case over the enclosure asks that the largest successor value over the
successor's enclosure stay below $\rho_V$ times the smallest current
value, and near the path that budget is zero while the successor's
enclosure is not, so no input meets it; the second program then
minimized the next value greedily, the force ratio chattered, and a
15\,m/s head-on run did not recover in 100\,s. Estimation error enters
the decrease as a disturbance instead, and recovery of the true state
can at best reach a neighborhood of the path set by that error.

\subsection{The Two Programs and the Issued Plan}
\label{subsec:program}

Let $\mathcal A$ denote the constraints shared by both programs on
$(\Delta\bm x_{0:M},\Delta\bm u_{0:M-1},\bm\xi)$: the model
\eqref{eq:affine_model}, the force-ratio and body-velocity bounds, the
collision rows \eqref{eq:collision_rows} with $\bm\xi\geq0$, the road
rows \eqref{eq:road_rows}, the cones \eqref{eq:adhesion_cone} and rows
\eqref{eq:sideslip_cone}, and the terminal rows of
Section~\ref{subsec:terminal} on $\Delta\bm x_M$, each with its
tightening. The primary program is
\begin{equation}
    J_1^\star=\min\ \sum_i\xi_i
    \quad\text{subject to }\mathcal A,
    \label{eq:primary_program}
\end{equation}
and the secondary program is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \min_{\delta\geq0}\quad&
        \frac{\delta+\epsilon\,\ell(\bm x)\,\mathcal R(\Delta\bm u)}
            {\max(V(\bm x),1)}\\
    \text{s.t.}\quad&\mathcal A,\qquad\sum_i\xi_i
        \leq J_1^\star+\epsilon_{\rm lex}\max(1,J_1^\star),\\
    &\text{\eqref{eq:clf_model}},
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:secondary_program}
\end{equation}
with $\ell(\bm x)=\bm e(\bm x)^{\trans}Q_e\bm e(\bm x)$,
$Q_e=\diag(\varsigma_1^{-2},\ldots,\varsigma_5^{-2})$ for error scales
$\varsigma_i>0$, and
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal R(\Delta\bm u)=\frac1{2M}\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}
        \normtwo{(\sigma W_u)^{-1}\Delta\bm u_i}^2,\qquad
    W_u=\diag(\bm\tau_u).
    \label{eq:tie_term}
\end{equation}
Both are second-order cone programs. If the zero correction with zero
slacks satisfies $\mathcal A$, it attains $J_1^\star=0$ and the primary
program is not passed to the solver; the secondary program is always
solved. The ordering is that of the predictive barrier of
\cite{HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025}: the minimal relaxation of the
safety problem is computed first and then imposed as a budget on the
performance problem, as in \cite{WabersichZeilinger2023}.

Minimizing a first-step slack alone leaves the later inputs
undetermined, and the next anchor inherits them. The term
$\epsilon\ell\mathcal R$ makes the plan unique at a bounded cost. Since
$0\leq\mathcal R\leq1$ on the trust box, the slack of an exact optimum
exceeds the smallest attainable one by at most $\epsilon\,\ell(\bm x)$,
which vanishes at cruise. The penalty acts on increments from the
anchor and pulls the inputs neither toward zero nor toward $\bm\pi$. The
tolerance $\epsilon_{\rm lex}$ is $10^{-4}$\,m; with a cap of
$10^{-5}$\,m the zero-slack set is a slab a few micrometers thick, and
the cone solver repeatedly stalled on the secondary program. Both
programs are solved by an interior-point cone solver to feasibility and
gap tolerances of $10^{-6}$, and a solve that stalls is accepted at
$10^{-4}$. The current policy issues the completed input sequence directly;
positive numerical termination is not a nonlinear safety certificate.

When the solver certifies the primary program primal infeasible, the
input trust scale $\sigma$ is doubled on the same linearization, up to
$\sigma=1$, until the program is feasible; the formulated problem is
reused and only the input bounds change. If the program around a shifted
anchor is still infeasible, one fresh anchor is rolled out under the
guidance feedback and treated the same way. The secondary program is
then solved on the selected model.

The completed secondary program supplies its input sequence directly. Its
first input is applied without clipping, post-solve scaling, or a nonlinear
feasibility veto. A nonlinear rollout of that same sequence supplies prediction
and the trust remainder only; it does not replace the optimized slacks or
objective. A rollout outside the declared model domain is reported as
unavailable and the affine prediction is retained. A frame without a usable
primary and secondary result reports no optimization solution. Numerical
solver return is not part of the mathematical recursive-feasibility claim.
A positive primary value remains a relaxation of modeled margins, not a
statement of physical safety.

\subsection{Recursive Feasibility With Exact Information}
\label{subsec:recursive}

Call a plan \emph{accepted} when it is the rollout of its inputs through
$F$ from the current state, meets the road, state and handling rows at
every node and mid-hold point, clears the obstacle by $d_{\rm m}$ at
every node and mid-hold point after the $N$ primary holds, and ends in
$\mathcal X_f$. This defines feasibility of the ideal nonlinear problem; a
local convex output is not assumed to meet these exact rows. Consider the hypotheses: (H1) the plant is $F$
and the state is known exactly; (H2) the obstacle follows the forecast
used at sample $k$, with the same $A_C$ and $\beta_C$; (H3) from the
endpoint of the plan, some hold of $F$ keeps the endpoint in
$\mathcal X_f$ with the hard rows met and its mid-hold point clear.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:recursive}
Under (H1) and (H2), if the plan of sample $k$ is accepted and has at
least two holds, its inputs shifted by one hold form an accepted plan at
sample $k+1$, one hold shorter, whose summed clearance deficit over the
primary holds does not exceed that of sample $k$. Under (H3) as well,
the shifted plan extended by that hold is accepted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
By (H1) the new state is the plan's second node, so the shifted inputs
reproduce the old nodes $2,\ldots,M+1$ exactly, and every hard row of
the old plan holds at them. The old node $N+1$ was hard, with zero
deficit, and is now the last primary node, so the deficit sum loses the
old first node's deficit and gains zero. The endpoint is the old
endpoint at the same absolute time, and by (H2) the forecast is
unchanged, so its membership \eqref{eq:terminal_set} is the same
evaluation as before. An appended hold under (H3) meets the hard rows,
its mid-hold point clears the obstacle, and its endpoint lies in
$\mathcal X_f$, whose condition (b) at $j=0$ includes the node's own
clearance.
\end{IEEEproof}

This is an existence proof for the ideal problem, following
\cite[eq.~(10)]{HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025}. It does not assert that a
solver returns a command. Assumption~\ref{asm:mild} identifies the mild
model with the terminal kinematics; to infer (H3), the terminal input must
also meet actuator, rear-adhesion and any slew bounds. The runtime checks
these on appended holds of the declared model. The start of an encounter
and re-acquisition after its absorbing exit remain initial-feasibility
questions outside the proposition.

\subsection{Joint Observer--Controller Recursive Feasibility Under
Model and Estimation Uncertainty}
\label{subsec:robust}

This subsection defines the uncertainty-aware mathematical controller and
proves its recursive feasibility. Future observations and feedback are
part of prediction. The result concerns this joint nonlinear design;
numerical evaluation of its sets and solver return are separate questions.
We omit road-boundary constraints in this theorem. State, handling,
actuator, slew and collision conditions remain, and lane recovery remains
a performance objective. The road constraints and trials described
elsewhere in the paper retain their stated scope.

\subsubsection{One joint model and one source of each uncertainty}
Let $\bm\vartheta\in\Theta$ denote unknown true vehicle parameters.
The declared box in Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided} varies the front and
rear cornering stiffness and friction coefficients; it does not also
certify uncertain mass, inertia or geometry. Let $\bm w_i$ denote any
separately admitted residual plant disturbance and $\bm\nu_i$ the
primitive sensor-noise history during hold $i$. A value box does not
establish temporal constancy. Fixed parameters obey
$\bm\vartheta_{i+1}=\bm\vartheta_i$; varying parameters require a declared
transition $\bm\vartheta_{i+1}\in\Gamma(\bm\vartheta_i)$, including their
intra-hold history. A range-only contract may use $\Gamma(\bm\vartheta)=
\Theta$. The same parameter history is used throughout each branch.

Let $\bm q$ contain the true ego and obstacle states and parameters, the
observer's point, bound and history state, the measurements available for
control, previous input, and the required mode, encounter and policy
memory. Take decision instants after the measurements used by that input
are available. A causal rule $\pi_i$ uses the observer outputs and known
memory, never the unknown true state or parameter. The joint map is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm u_i&=\pi_i(\bm s_i,\bm y_i,\bm m_i),\\
    \bm q_{i+1}&=\mathcal G_{\pi_i}
        (\bm q_i,\bm w_i,\bm\nu_i).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:joint_sampling}
\end{equation}
Here $\bm s_i$ includes observer information and $\bm m_i$ includes the
required input and reference memory. The map executes the declared plant,
measurement generation, observer evolution, reconstruction and resets in
their prescribed sample/hold order. Future measurements are generated
from the same physical branch that the future feedback controls. A
published raw gyro is a noisy measurement, not true yaw rate.
At the root, the observer state and current measurements are known, so
all possible physical branches receive the same definite current input.
Future observer states and inputs can differ across measurement branches.

The published physical enclosure includes effects of past admitted
mismatch and noise. It initializes the state set; it is not another
independent noise to inject after \eqref{eq:joint_sampling} propagates
those same sources. Future mismatch still acts through the true plant.
For a predictor-timed linear illustration, take
\[
    \bm x^+=A\bm x+B\bm u+P\widetilde{\bm\vartheta}+\bm w,
    \qquad \bm y=C\bm x+\bm n,
\]
$\hat{\bm x}^+=A\hat{\bm x}+B\bm u+L(\bm y-C\hat{\bm x})$,
$\bm z^+=A\bm z+B\bm v$, and
$\bm u=\bm v+K(\hat{\bm x}-\bm z)$. With
$\bm\eta=\bm x-\bm z$, $\bm\epsilon=\bm x-\hat{\bm x}$ and
$\bm r=[\bm\eta^\trans,\bm\epsilon^\trans]^\trans$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    J&=\begin{bmatrix}A+BK&-BK\\0&A-LC\end{bmatrix},\\
    \bm r^+&=J\bm r+
        \begin{bmatrix}P\\P\end{bmatrix}\widetilde{\bm\vartheta}
        +\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\I&-L\end{bmatrix}
          \begin{bmatrix}\bm w\\\bm n\end{bmatrix}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:shared_model_error}
\end{equation}
The same parameter and plant-disturbance columns enter both errors.
Their direct contributions cancel in $\bm\eta^+-\bm\epsilon^+$, as
the nominal observer-point update requires. Independent copies would
lose that identity. Measurement correction before control or direct
measurement feedthrough has its corresponding immediate control path;
\eqref{eq:joint_sampling} retains its actual timing. This illustration
does not replace the NRMM equations.

Every enclosure used below must be valid for the same parameter,
disturbance and sensor contract as $\mathcal G_\pi$. An extra physical
lateral force, for example, changes the force-balance measurement as well
as the plant and must enter its interval bound. The tire-parameter box
does not cover arbitrary additional forces. Whenever a finite observer
certificate is used, its continuous-motion premises must hold over the
whole hold, not just at nodes and midpoints.

\subsubsection{Information sets and future feedback}
For a fixed causal rule define the exact joint image
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal T_\pi(\mathcal J)
    =\{\mathcal G_\pi(\bm q,\bm w,\bm\nu):
       \bm q\in\mathcal J,\ (\bm w,\bm\nu)\text{ admitted}\}.
    \label{eq:joint_image}
\end{equation}
The source histories and their shared parameter identities are retained.
The image is monotone under set inclusion; $\mathcal T_{\pi,\sigma}$
denotes its held-state image at constrained fraction $\sigma$.

Initialize $\mathcal I_0$ from the published physical enclosure, the
parameter set and the known observer, measurement and input memory.
A product initialization is sound if only marginal information is
available, but may lose parameter/state correlation. Let
$\mathcal R_{1|k}$ be the old first successor, exact or a proved outer
set. With a valid new joint enclosure $\mathcal Q_{k+1}^J$ and the set
$\mathcal A_{k+1}$ fixing the newly known memory, update
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal I_{k+1}
      =\mathcal R_{1|k}\cap\mathcal Q_{k+1}^J
         \cap\mathcal A_{k+1}.
    \label{eq:information_set}
\end{equation}
An unavailable or invalid finite enclosure contributes an unrestrictive
set; it cannot exclude possible truth. The absorbing encounter-ended
flag and the obstacle's stop reset use the same convention at both
samples. A re-acquisition is a new initial-feasibility problem.
Reference memory of the shifted policy remains an allowed choice.

For exact images, let $\mathcal S_k$ be the obstacle projection of
$\mathcal I_k$ and
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal F_k(t)=\varphi_{t-t_k}(\mathcal S_k),\qquad t\ge t_k.
    \label{eq:forecast_family}
\end{equation}
The autonomous obstacle flow includes its rest rule. Its acceleration
is reset at rest; intersection uses the propagated post-reset state.

\begin{lemma}[Truth containment and conditioning]
\label{lem:nested}
Under sound initial and new enclosures and the unchanged joint
uncertainty contract, the truth belongs to every $\mathcal I_k$, and
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal I_{k+1}\subseteq\mathcal R_{1|k}.
    \label{eq:nesting}
\end{equation}
For exact images under the autonomous obstacle/rest model,
$\mathcal F_{k+1}(t)\subseteq\mathcal F_k(t)$ for $t\ge t_{k+1}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{IEEEproof}
The next true state belongs to the sound first successor, the valid
new enclosure and its known-memory slice. Their intersection is
nonempty and contained in the successor. Induction gives truth
containment. For exact images,
$\mathcal S_{k+1}\subseteq\varphi_{T_s}(\mathcal S_k)$; the semigroup
property gives forecast nesting at the same absolute time.
\end{IEEEproof}
Consecutive raw observer balls need not be nested. For conservative
outer sets the theorem below uses retained joint successor certificates;
it does not require a separately recomputed marginal forecast to be
nested. Relative rectangle geometry is evaluated on coupled ego and
obstacle branches, rather than combining unrelated marginal extremes.

The innovation of the new center remains. Writing the sampled true
plant as $F_{\bm\vartheta}$ and its nominal counterpart as
$F_{\bm\vartheta_0}$ gives the identity
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \bm\delta_k={}&-\bm\epsilon_{k+1}
       +F_{\bm\vartheta}(\hat{\bm x}_k+\bm\epsilon_k,\bm u_k)\\
       &-F_{\bm\vartheta_0}(\hat{\bm x}_k,\bm u_k)+\bm w_k .
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:innovation}
\end{equation}
Its terms come from one joint branch, not independently chosen
estimation and mismatch errors.

\subsubsection{Sound nonlinear prediction and varying tube widths}
A prediction tube consists of sets satisfying
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal R_{0|k}=\mathcal I_k,\qquad
    \mathcal T_{\pi_{i|k}}(\mathcal R_{i|k})
        \subseteq\mathcal R_{i+1|k}.
    \label{eq:robust_image_certificate}
\end{equation}
Exact images satisfy this by definition. For outer sets, the following
construction supplies a nonlinear reserve.

\begin{lemma}[Joint nonlinear image enclosure]
\label{lem:jointImage}
Fix a rule $\pi$. On a convex cell $\mathcal D$ of the combined joint
state and primitive disturbance arguments $\bm a$, choose
$\bm a_c\in\mathcal D$ and a matrix $L_c$. Suppose $\mathcal G_\pi$
is continuously differentiable there and a convex matrix box
$\mathbf M_c$ encloses $D\mathcal G_\pi(\bm a)-L_c$ throughout
$\mathcal D$. Then
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \mathcal G_\pi(\mathcal D)\subseteq{}&
       \mathcal G_\pi(\bm a_c)\oplus L_c(\mathcal D-\bm a_c)\\
       &\oplus\mathbf M_c(\mathcal D-\bm a_c).
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:joint_nonlinear_enclosure}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{IEEEproof}
The mean-value integral along the segment from $\bm a_c$ to $\bm a$
equals the average Jacobian times $\bm a-\bm a_c$.
After subtracting $L_c(\bm a-\bm a_c)$, the average residual
Jacobian belongs to $\mathbf M_c$ by convexity.
\end{IEEEproof}
If $\bm\rho$ bounds coordinate deviations and $\Delta_c$ bounds
the absolute residual Jacobian entries, $\Delta_c\bm\rho$ is a
coordinate reserve. A componentwise Hessian bound $H_j$ gives the
smooth-cell reserve $\tfrac12\bm\rho^\trans H_j\bm\rho$.
The cell includes parameter/state and policy/input interactions.
Keep shared affine columns before adding the approximation reserve:
model mismatch belongs inside $\mathcal G_\pi$, not in a second
independent disturbance appended afterward.

Switching guards are split and all possible branch images are retained.
This covers tire saturation, low-speed modes, stop resets, angle
conventions and observer inversion/fallbacks. An undefined branch must
be excluded by a valid domain condition or handled by a declared mode.
If an exact inverse replaces the implemented bisection for analysis,
its termination error must also be bounded. Numerical plant integration
is either the declared sampled model or its defect from a continuous
model enters the disturbance contract once. Input and geometry rows
require their corresponding joint image bounds; a state enclosure alone
does not prove actuator or rectangle constraints.

Induction on \eqref{eq:robust_image_certificate} now contains every
admitted nonlinear trajectory over the finite horizon. It neither
restarts the current error at each hold nor requires equal tube widths.
For fixed parameters, a local representation with proved residuals is
\[
    \bm r_{i+1}=J_i\bm r_i+P_i\widetilde{\bm\vartheta}
        +D_i\bm\nu_i+\bm c_i+\bm e_i,\qquad \bm e_i\in U_i .
\]
Let $\Phi(i,j)=J_{i-1}\cdots J_j$, $\Phi(j,j)=I$, and
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    H_i&=\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\Phi(i,j+1)P_j,\\
    \bm m_i&=\Phi(i,0)\bm m_0+
       \sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\Phi(i,j+1)\bm c_j .
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:parameter_response}
\end{equation}
For independent root generators $G_0,G_\vartheta$ and normalized
unit-box sensor variables, the absolute support in direction $\bm\ell$
is bounded by
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    &|\bm\ell^\trans\bm m_i|
       +\normone{\bm\ell^\trans\Phi(i,0)G_0}
       +\normone{\bm\ell^\trans H_iG_\vartheta}\\
    &\quad+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}
        \normone{\bm\ell^\trans\Phi(i,j+1)D_j}\\
    &\quad+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}
        h_{\rm abs}\bigl(U_j,\Phi(i,j+1)^\trans\bm\ell\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:feedback_finite_support}
\end{equation}
where $h_{\rm abs}(U,\bm a)=\sup_{\bm e\in U}|\bm a^\trans\bm e|$.
This follows by unrolling the recursion and collecting the same
$\widetilde{\bm\vartheta}$ before taking supports. Shared root or
temporal noise columns are likewise combined before absolute values.
The $U_i$ must bound the residuals on the sets where they are used.
For varying parameters use the declared augmented transition instead
of the constant-parameter term $H_i$.

The products are those of observer and feedback dynamics, not a
frozen-input plant. Uniformly exponentially decaying error-block
products with bounded forcing
provide a sufficient bounded-tail mechanism, while individual steps
may expand. Persistent parameters have identity dynamics, so a Schur
test on the entire parameter-augmented state is not required.
The uncontrolled obstacle's absolute spread need not be bounded.
Recursive feasibility requires the sound images and constraints,
not a constant-radius or uniformly contracting nonlinear tube.

\subsubsection{A common robust terminal continuation}
Let $\mathcal B$ contain nonempty joint sets whose members are mild
and admit one common causal braking continuation. Along that rule
every branch satisfies the state, handling, actuator and slew rows,
and every active collision row has zero deficit, including the
parked phase. The family of terminal rules is closed under deleting
its first hold. This is a mathematical continuation witness.

Under the accepted mild kinematic contract of
Assumption~\ref{asm:mild}, a common held steering/braking rule keeps
each branch's sideslip and curvature fixed and uses a forward-only
absorbing stop. It does not select a last command using each unknown
true speed. If the realized braking satisfies $a(t)\ge a_{\min}>0$
while $V>0$, for every branch with $V(0)\le\overline V$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    V(t)\le\max(0,\overline V-a_{\min}t),\\
    t_{\rm stop}\le\frac{\overline V}{a_{\min}},\qquad
    s_{\rm stop}\le\frac{\overline V^2}{2a_{\min}} .
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:terminal_stop_bound}
\end{equation}
These inequalities follow by integrating $\dot V\le-a_{\min}$ to
the absorbing stop and integrating the resulting speed envelope.
The rate bound belongs to the declared terminal uncertainty contract.
A sampled delayed stop reset requires its delay allowance.
The nominal known-state last-hold schedule of
Section~\ref{subsec:terminal} is not independently applied to different
unknown speeds.

Along each such branch $|\kappa|V^2$ decreases and
\[
    \tan\alpha_r=\frac{\sin\beta-l_{r,E}\kappa}{\cos\beta}
\]
is constant until rest. For fixed geometry, a sufficient robust
rear-adhesion entry condition under held ratio $b_f$ is
\begin{equation}
    \sup_{\mathcal J}|\tan\alpha_r|
      \le\frac{3\mu_{r,\min}F_{z,r}\sqrt{1-b_f^2}}{C_{r,\max}} .
    \label{eq:robust_rear_entry}
\end{equation}
It therefore persists throughout the continuation for parameters
inside the box. Actuator admissibility and the first slew from the
stored prior input are checked explicitly; later held-command slew
differences are zero.

A finite membership construction checks all joint continuation sets
through $\lceil\overline V/(a_{\min}T_s)\rceil$ holds under the
continuous absorbing-stop contract, then checks the entire parked
ego rectangle family against the remaining obstacle family.
Pose uncertainty remains at rest. Measurement noise does not move
that physical set under the held policy; a residual physical
disturbance capable of doing so requires its own parked invariant set.
An invalid finite low-speed observer enclosure cannot replace the
propagated physical set, and no yaw-estimate contraction at rest is
required.

\begin{proposition}[Terminal closure on joint sets]
\label{prop:jointTerminal}
The exact terminal family is downward closed. For every
$\mathcal J\in\mathcal B$ its common first rule $\pi_f$ satisfies
all hard rows, has zero collision deficit, and
$\mathcal T_{\pi_f}(\mathcal J)\in\mathcal B$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
The same continuation controls any nonempty subset.
Every successor branch is already covered by the old continuation,
and its tail is an allowed rule. It certifies the successor.
Input and terminal memory are propagated, so the first slew and
latched steering conditions are retained.
\end{IEEEproof}
With outer sets, require a proved successor
$\mathcal T_{\pi_f}(\mathcal J)\subseteq\mathcal R_f(\mathcal J)$
with $\mathcal R_f(\mathcal J)\in\mathcal B$.
The nominal center-based set of Section~\ref{subsec:terminal} is
not substituted for this joint-set definition.

\subsubsection{The theoretical PCBF/CLF problem}
For $1\le N\le M\le M_{\max}$, choose causal rules
$\pi_{0|k},\ldots,\pi_{M-1|k}$, sets satisfying
\eqref{eq:robust_image_certificate}, and nonnegative prefix slacks.
Impose all hard rows universally on the appropriate hold images and
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    &\dist\bigl(\mathcal V_E(\bm q),\mathcal V_C(\bm q)\bigr)
        \ge d_{\rm m}-\xi_{i|k},\\
    &\bm q\in\mathcal T_{\pi_{i|k},\sigma}(\mathcal R_{i|k}),
      \qquad \sigma\in\{0,\tfrac12,1\}.
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:robust_row}
\end{equation}
Fractions are of $T_s$ and the same slack covers a whole hold.
Set $\xi_i=0$ for $i\ge N$ and require
$\mathcal R_{M|k}\in\mathcal B$. The primary value is
\begin{equation}
    V^\star(\mathcal I_k)=
       \min\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\xi_{i|k}.
    \label{eq:joint_primary}
\end{equation}
The policy and certificate class is closed under shifting and terminal
appending. For example it can contain scheduled affine output laws
$\pi_i=\bm v_i+K_i(\hat{\bm x}^{\rm pub}_i-\bm z_i)$ and the held
terminal rule. Nominal centers, stored gains and reference memory
can inherit the old tail; $\bm z_0$ is not forced to equal a new
estimate. The root physical set still uses the current information.

The current input $\pi_{0|k}$ evaluated on the known data is applied
directly. Future rules are conditional reactions to observations,
not commands chosen using unknown true states.
The exact-set formulation requires no numerical enclosure constants.
Proved outer sets give a sufficient alternative; if an approximate
tube is prescribed, its image and row bounds must be established.

\begin{theorem}[Joint nonlinear recursive feasibility]
\label{thm:robust}
Suppose the initial mathematical problem is feasible, the joint model
and uncertainty contracts are unchanged, all used enclosures contain
the truth, update \eqref{eq:information_set} is used, the rules and
certificates admit shifting and terminal appending, and the terminal
family has the closure properties of Proposition~\ref{prop:jointTerminal}.
Applying the definite first input of any feasible plan preserves
mathematical feasibility for every admitted model and sensor history.
If the executed plan attains the primary minimum, then
\[
    V^\star(\mathcal I_{k+1})
       \le V^\star(\mathcal I_k)-\xi_{0|k}^\star .
\]
The true applied hold satisfies the hard rows and has clearance at
least $d_{\rm m}-\xi_{0|k}$ at the specified constraint instants.
For safety-optimal plans the first-hold deficits are summable and
the zero-slack information states are invariant until the encounter ends.
\end{theorem}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:nested} gives $\mathcal I_{k+1}\subseteq\mathcal R_{1|k}$.
Shift the old rules $\pi_{1|k},\ldots,\pi_{M-1|k}$ and append a
terminal rule for $\mathcal R_{M|k}$. Retain
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathcal R}_0=\mathcal I_{k+1},\qquad
    \widehat{\mathcal R}_i=\mathcal R_{i+1|k},\quad 1\le i<M .
    \label{eq:tube_growth}
\end{equation}
For $M\ge2$, monotonicity of the exact image gives
$\mathcal T_{\pi_{1|k}}(\mathcal I_{k+1})
\subseteq\mathcal T_{\pi_{1|k}}(\mathcal R_{1|k})
\subseteq\mathcal R_{2|k}$.
All later old image and row certificates are unchanged.
The terminal rule supplies the last rows and a successor in $\mathcal B$.
For $M=1$ the new root is a subset of the old terminal set and the
same witness applies. Input memory preserves the slew rows.
The candidate has the same $M$, so both horizon limits hold.

Set $\widehat\xi_i=\xi_{i+1|k}$ for $i<N-1$ and
$\widehat\xi_{N-1}=0$. The last prefix stage was an old hard-tail
stage when $M>N$, or the appended terminal stage when $M=N$.
Universal rows hold on subsets, so the candidate cost is the old
cost minus $\xi_{0|k}$. Comparison with the next minimum proves
the value inequality for a primary minimizer.
Truth containment gives the applied-hold statement.
Nonnegativity and telescoping give summability and zero-set invariance.
\end{IEEEproof}

This extends the shifted-candidate argument of
\cite{HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025} to the joint information state.
A valid old tube can be retained even if a fresh adaptive outer
approximation is wider. Refinements may intersect sound images of
the same shifted rule with the retained tube.
No global monotonicity of a freshly computed numerical enclosure,
constant tube width, exact state information or successful solver
return is assumed. Positive collision slacks remain relaxation;
they are not a zero-deficit safety claim.
For an executed feasible cost $J_k$ the comparison is only
$V^\star(\mathcal I_{k+1})\le J_k-\xi_{0|k}$;
optimal-value descent additionally needs a primary minimizer.

\begin{corollary}[Feasibility of the soft CLF stage]
\label{cor:softClf}
After an attained primary minimum, retain its hard rows and slack
budget and add a CLF residual $g\le\rho_{\rm CLF}$ with
$\rho_{\rm CLF}\ge0$ and no finite upper bound.
If the residual is finite on a primary candidate's certified set,
the secondary mathematical problem is feasible.
\end{corollary}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Choose $\rho_{\rm CLF}=\max(0,\sup g)$ for that candidate.
It satisfies the added row and leaves every primary constraint unchanged.
\end{IEEEproof}
A hard CLF row or bounded slack needs an additional feasibility
argument. An exact primary budget preserves the optimal-value
statement; a numerical budget tolerance does not establish that
exact descent.

\subsubsection{Scope and numerical realization}
Theorem~\ref{thm:robust} is a complete conditional mathematical
statement for the defined joint-set design: its premises include
initial feasibility and the declared model/observer contracts.
It does not assert feasibility for every initial encounter.
Exact images establish containment by definition; terminal closure
and the policy shift are proved above.
Evaluating set radii or implementing a membership test is not a
missing step of that existence proof.

The executable two-program controller and the historical trials use
the approximations stated in Section~\ref{subsec:uncertainty}.
Their numerical outcomes do not establish or refute the theorem
for a different problem. A convex restriction claiming this same
guarantee must preserve the carried policy, tube and row certificates;
arbitrary new tangents or trust regions cannot be presumed to do so.
The existing theory documents
\path{controller/CLOSED_LOOP_PREDICTION_THEORY.md} and
\path{controller/ROBUST_RECURSIVE_FEASIBILITY.md} give the full
construction. Independent exact-arithmetic specializations check
the shared-source algebra, nonlinear reserve, tube reuse and
uncertain-rate braking, without replacing the proofs above.

\subsection{Trust Scale from the Model Remainder}
\label{subsec:trust}

The scale $\sigma$ of the input trust box is estimated from the
remainder of the linearization, measured in the same sample. For a
correction of size $s$, the largest input change from the anchor in
units of $\bm\tau_u$, the affine prediction differs from the nonlinear
rollout of the same inputs by a second-order remainder of the form
$Ls^2$, with a coefficient $L$ that depends on the operating point. The
prediction-only nonlinear rollout evaluates the full step. With
$\varpi(\Delta\bm x)=\normtwo{\Delta\bm p}+r_E|\Delta\psi|$, which bounds
the displacement of any body point, $\tilde{\bm x}_j$ that rollout,
$\hat{\bm x}_j$ the affine prediction of the same inputs and
$e_k=\max_j\varpi(\tilde{\bm x}_j-\hat{\bm x}_j)$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \hat L_k=\max\Bigl(
        \frac{e_k}{\max(s_k,\sigma_k/2)^2},\
        \gamma_L\hat L_{k-1}\Bigr),\\
    \sigma_{k+1}=\min\Bigl(1,\ \max\Bigl(\sigma_{\min},\
        \sqrt{\tau_e/\hat L_k}\Bigr)\Bigr).
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:trust_update}
\end{equation}
The scale is the step whose predicted remainder equals a target
remainder $\tau_e$. It shrinks at once and grows by at most
$\gamma_L^{-1/2}$ per sample, and a step far inside the box is not taken
as evidence about its boundary. The departure of the next estimate from
the plan's second node, the innovation of \eqref{eq:innovation}, is
recorded separately; it belongs to the estimator and the plant and does
not set the scale. An earlier rule that subtracted the estimator's
worst-case radius from the total innovation explained every innovation
by that radius, so the scale rose to its maximum and the encounters
became infeasible. The update is not a check of the issued solution,
and $\tau_e$ is an empirical consistency target, not a bound on the
remainder or on the separation.

\subsection{Scope of the Guarantees}
\label{subsec:scope}

The implemented optimizer enforces affine sampled constraints and its
linearized terminal rows. The completed inputs are applied directly; a
nonlinear prediction is not a certificate that those exact constraints
hold. Positive primary slack is relaxation. Zero CLF slack refers to the
local value model, not to a nonlinear decrease of the plant.

Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} concerns the declared terminal law.
Proposition~\ref{prop:recursive} proves feasibility of an ideal
exact-information problem under a common forecast and an admissible
appended hold. Theorem~\ref{thm:robust} proves it for the stated
road-free joint nonlinear problem, including shared model mismatch and
future observer feedback, under its information-update and terminal
contracts. Neither promises numerical solver return. The cruise-law
certificate is conditional on its sampled enclosure hypothesis and is
not a certificate for the input selected by the local convex programs.

Collision constraints are sampled at nodes and midpoints; separation
between them is not proved. Plant integration mismatch is measured,
not bounded. The executable per-hold affine tightening omits nonlinear
remainder bounds and does not implement the joint-set recursion or
robust terminal certificate. These distinctions remain necessary when
solver failures in an experiment are accepted. A forecast re-acquired
after the absorbing encounter exit requires a new initial-feasibility
problem.

\subsection{Computation}
\label{subsec:computation}

Every inequality row is a small dense block on the few variables it
involves, and the sparse matrices are assembled in one call; building
them row by row had cost time quadratic in the plan length. A row whose
largest value over the variables' boxes, the state box of
\eqref{eq:affine_model} and the input box at $\sigma=1$, is below its
bound cannot bind and is left out, which leaves the feasible set and the
optimum unchanged; most road rows, state-limit rows and collision rows
far from the obstacle go this way, and a collision multiplier is not
computed where the reference distance less both reaches, the margin and
the largest tightening exceeds the most the pose can move over the
boxes. A larger trust scale reuses the formulated problem. The nonlinear rollout now supplies prediction data only. Exact rectangle
checks remain in anchor construction and independent experimental replay. The cruise trim is solved by
Newton's method with the analytic tangent. On 120 captured problems the
block assembly reproduced the previous matrices to $4.4\cdot10^{-16}$,
and on 2880 candidate plans the batched rule gave the same decision as a
hold-by-hold scan. A warm-up action runs one synthetic encounter at
start-up, so that every code path is compiled before the first sample.

\subsection{Estimator Accuracy Against a Tuned Reference}
\label{subsec:estimatorResults}

Six open-loop scenarios of 6 to 12\,s are used. In the straight
oncoming scenario the ego drives straight at 15\,m/s and the target
approaches from 95\,m at 15\,m/s. In the arc crossing scenario the ego
follows an arc of radius 100\,m and the target moves on a straight line
that meets the arc. In the arc following scenario the target is 30\,m
ahead on the same arc at 15.5\,m/s. The swerve-and-braking scenario
adds a 3.5\,m double lane change with braking to the straight oncoming
geometry, with the target at 12\,m/s. In the low-relative-speed
scenario a lead target is 30\,m ahead at 15.3\,m/s. In the weaving
scenario a weaving, accelerating ego meets a turning, accelerating
target. The target obeys the constant-acceleration, constant-sideslip
model in all of them, and the ego truth obeys the kinematic
single-track relation without mismatch. The swerve and weaving
scenarios violate the zero-jerk premise of the reference observer.
Synchronized measurements arrive at 50\,Hz with bounded uniform noise at
the declared maxima, and the maximum RK4 step is 0.005\,s.

The reference is a reproduction of the multistage observer of
\cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}, with companion-form ego and target chains
and a switched course--acceleration yaw stage. As in that paper, its ego
chain uses GNSS position and the gyroscope and does not use the GNSS
velocity that the estimator of Section~\ref{sec:estimator} measures. Its
gains are selected from 54 candidates (two gain shapes, three ego rates,
three target rates and three yaw gains) on training seeds 101--102, once
for every scenario, by minimizing the sum of the position, speed and
course errors scaled by 0.1\,m, 0.5\,m/s and $5^\circ$. In five of the
six scenarios the selected candidate lies on the boundary of this grid.

The estimator receives no error-based tuning; its gains are synthesized
anew from the operating domain declared for each scenario. Only the
low-relative-speed scenario uses the domain of
Section~\ref{subsec:designResults}. The arc following scenario declares
$\beta_{\max}=0.03$\,rad, which gives the target gains
$[10.21,51.59,116.4]$. The straight, arc crossing and swerve scenarios
declare $\rho_{\max}=100$\,m (the swerve scenario also
$\bar\omega_E=0.40$\,rad/s and $\beta_{\rm dom}=0.06$\,rad), and the
weaving scenario declares $\rho_{\max}=80$\,m. For $\rho_{\max}=100$\,m
and 80\,m, respectively, the decay floors are 0.4 and 0.5\,s$^{-1}$, the
scalar gains 1.198 and 1.498\,s$^{-1}$, and the target gains
$[6.24,19.31,27.20]$ and $[6.50,20.93,30.23]$.

Both observers run on identical truth, sensor draws and initial offsets.

2\,s.

is our reproduction with gains selected empirically on a bounded grid,
not the authors' implementation or sensor suite. No
enclosure-containment or outage statistics are reported for these
open-loop trials.

distance of Section~\ref{subsec:designResults}. With these values the
synthesis \eqref{eq:clf_lmi} certifies, for the cruise law, contraction
factors $\rho^\star$ of 0.9744 to 0.9748 per sample against the
requested $\rho_V=0.9753$, that is, time constants of $\sqrt V$ of 3.85
to 3.92\,s against $T_V=4$\,s, on sets $\Omega$ whose half-extents are
1.49\,m (8\,m/s, straight), 1.59\,m (8\,m/s, curved) and 2.0\,m
(15\,m/s) of lateral offset, 0.114 to 0.144\,rad of heading, 0.49 to
0.74\,m/s of speed, 0.19 to 0.27\,m/s of lateral velocity and
0.20\,rad/s of yaw rate; the samples that built the enclosures reach
front slip angles of 0.080\,rad. The independent check on fresh samples
found worst contractions of 0.941 to 0.963 and no violated input bound
or state row.

Seven encounter geometries are run at reference speeds of 8 and
15\,m/s. In the head-on case the obstacle starts 24\,m ahead on the path
and approaches at 8\,m/s; in the accelerating head-on case it also
accelerates at 1\,m/s$^2$. The braking lead starts 1.45\,m ahead of the
ego body at the ego speed and brakes at 0.5\,m/s$^2$ until it stops. The
crossing obstacle moves at 8\,m/s perpendicular to the path and reaches
the path when the cruising ego does. The turning crossing obstacle
reaches the same point at 10\,m/s and perpendicular to the path at that
instant, while accelerating at $A_C=1$\,m/s$^2$ and turning with
$\beta_C=0.05$\,rad, a turning radius of about 32\,m. The curved head-on
and curved crossing cases use a path of curvature $0.005$\,m$^{-1}$,
with an opposing obstacle on the same circle at 8\,m/s and an obstacle
that crosses at 10\,m/s with $A_C=0.5$\,m/s$^2$ and $\beta_C=0.04$\,rad.
In every case the bodies are initially separated, and the ego under
unmodified cruise would collide. The road has four lanes of 3.66\,m and
paved shoulders of 3.05\,m, and the path is the center of the second
lane from the right, which gives the clearances $c_{\rm R}$ and
$c_{\rm L}$ of Table~\ref{tab:controllerParameters}.

Each issued input is held for $T_s$ on the model $f$ itself, including
its low-speed regime, integrated by an adaptive Runge--Kutta method with
relative and absolute tolerances $10^{-11}$ and $10^{-12}$. The plant
therefore differs from the prediction only through the integration
scheme and the linearization, and it is not snapped to rest below
$v_{\rm h}/5$. There is no parameter mismatch or actuator dynamics, the
obstacle follows \eqref{eq:target_flow} exactly with fixed $A_C$ and
$\beta_C$, and the measured computation time is not applied as a delay.
The body distance is evaluated at 31 instants per hold.

Two observation modes are used. With \emph{exact states}, the
controller receives the true ego and obstacle states and no enclosure.
With \emph{estimated states}, it receives only the output of the
estimator of Section~\ref{sec:estimator}, which runs at 80\,Hz on
synthetic GNSS, accelerometer, gyroscope and relative-position samples
of the true motion with bounded uniform noise at the maxima of
Table~\ref{tab:controllerParameters}. The relative-position sensor has
a range of $R_{\rm enc}$. An obstacle is acquired after 20 consecutive
detections, whose least-squares line initializes its velocity, and its
estimate is passed to the controller only while it is detected. The
estimator starts from a 0.5\,s measurement history before the first
control sample. Its ego domain is derived from the controller's limits
(speed 1 to 18\,m/s, $\beta_{\rm dom}$, yaw rate and acceleration from
the state bounds and tire friction), and its target domain is the hull
of the scenario obstacles within sensor range, widened by 10\%; the
gains are synthesized from this domain by the programs of
Section~\ref{subsec:gainSelection} and differ from those of
Section~\ref{subsec:designResults}. The lateral-velocity measurement is
that of Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided}. The estimated-state trials are
repeated for six noise seeds.

A run fails if the bodies touch or if a sample returns no solved
command. It succeeds if, in addition, cruise is recovered: all five
transverse errors within (0.1\,m, $1^\circ$, 0.1\,m/s, 0.05\,m/s,
0.01\,rad/s) during a dwell of 1\,s that starts after the last instant
at which unmodified cruise would be in contact. Runs last at most 2000
holds (100\,s). On the circular path the opposing obstacle of the curved
head-on case meets the ego a second time within that period, so those
runs contain two encounters and confirm recovery after the second. The
campaign uses MATLAB R2026a on an AMD Ryzen 7 7800X3D and the
interior-point cone solver Clarabel through a compiled interface; before
each run the controller's warm-up action compiles every code path. Six
runs shared the machine. Below, a primary optimum is called zero when
it does not exceed $\epsilon_{\rm lex}$ plus the feasibility tolerance
$10^{-5}$, and a CLF slack when it does not exceed
$10^{-5}\max(V,1)$.

Table~\ref{tab:closedLoop} and Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop} summarize the
98 runs. With exact states all fourteen runs succeed: no contact, no
departure from the road, and cruise recovered between 8.45 and
14.70\,s in the single-encounter cases. With estimated states 74 of the
84 runs succeed. The other ten stop without a solved command, between
0.25 and 5.95\,s into the run (Section~\ref{subsec:failureResults}); no
run collides or leaves the road. Two seeds succeed in thirteen
encounters and four in twelve; seven of the ten failures are curved
head-ons.

With exact states the controller uses the node margin almost
completely. In six of the fourteen runs the smallest sampled distance
is below $d_{\rm m}=0.20$\,m, down to 0.136\,m
(Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop}(d)): the margin is consumed between the
constrained instants and by the plant's departure from the sampled
model. The primary optimum is positive at one sample of one run, by
$1.2\cdot10^{-4}$\,m. The steering angle reaches 0.52\,rad, the yaw
rate 0.99\,rad/s and the sideslip 0.19\,rad, inside the cone of
0.25\,rad; the force ratio ranges from $-1.00$ to $0.97$, and the speed
stays above 4.9\,m/s, so the low-speed regime of the model is not
entered. The largest lateral deviation is 3.8 to 8.4\,m
(Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop}(e)): the maneuvers leave the lane and use the
adjacent lanes, and the body stays at least 2.67\,m from a road edge.
The horizon is $N$ holds in the median, 40 holds at the 95th percentile
and at most 117 holds; the first sample of a run plans 24 to 25 holds.

With estimated states the passes are wider. The median over the 84 runs
of the smallest distance is 1.80\,m, against 0.28\,m with exact states,
and no run comes within $d_{\rm m}$ (least 0.21\,m), because the
tightened rows \eqref{eq:collision_tightening} reserve the current
enclosure and the forecast of the obstacle changes from sample to
sample. The wider passes use the road: the lateral deviation reaches
11.0\,m, and the smallest distance to a road edge falls to 0.18\,m in a
15\,m/s curved crossing. The steering angle reaches 1.04\,rad, at
4.1\,m/s in an 8\,m/s turning crossing, the yaw rate 1.19\,rad/s and
the sideslip 0.21\,rad; the force ratio ranges from $-0.85$ to $1.00$.
Ten runs slow into the blended regime between $v_1$ and $v_2$, to
3.2\,m/s at the least, and none into the kinematic regime. Recovery is
confirmed after a median of 10.3\,s and at most 22.8\,s in the
single-encounter cases. Over all estimated-state samples the horizon
has a median of 16 holds, a 95th percentile of 74 and a maximum of 184.

The primary optimum is positive at 1109 of the 23083 estimated-state
samples, in 75 runs, and exceeds 1\,m at 665 of them. With an
enclosure it is the summed relaxation of the tightened rows over the
whole horizon, and the target tube \eqref{eq:target_tube} over a long
horizon can exceed the available room; the untightened rows beyond the
primary holds are hard. The issued plans themselves clear the obstacle
by $d_{\rm m}$ at every constrained instant beyond the primary holds, by
construction, and within the primary holds they fall short of $d_{\rm m}$
at 13 samples of six runs, by at most 0.22\,m; with exact states at 16
samples of five runs, by at most 0.5\,mm.

The CLF condition is met in the model more often than on the plant. In
the exact-state runs, 3702 consecutive state pairs have $V>10^{-3}$,
and 3078 of them have zero slack in \eqref{eq:secondary_program} and
the full step accepted. In 217 of those the requested decrease
\eqref{eq:clf_row} is not achieved by the plant, and in 60 $V$
increases; all 60 are in the 15\,m/s curved head-on run at $V<0.04$,
where the ego cruises on the curve between its two encounters with a
speed error within $\pm0.15$\,m/s, a lateral offset within 0.11\,m, and
zero modeled slack unattained at 58 of 601 samples. The tie term of
\eqref{eq:secondary_program} fixes the later inputs of the plan with a
weight that vanishes on the path, and the next anchor inherits those
inputs; the run at 8\,m/s on the same curve cruises without a visible
error, and the four estimated-state runs on the curve that recover hold
their speed within $\pm0.05$\,m/s. In the estimated-state runs, 15955
pairs have zero slack and the full step; 1965 miss the requested
decrease and 792 increase. Recovery in Table~\ref{tab:closedLoop} is an
observed dwell, not a certified decrease.

\subsection{The Shifted Plan and the Terminal Set in the Loop}
\label{subsec:feasibilityResults}

Proposition~\ref{prop:recursive} says that the shifted plan is accepted
whenever the plant is the sampled model and the forecast does not
change. With exact states the plant is the same differential equation
under another integrator and the forecast is the truth: the shifted
plan was accepted at all 5522 samples that had one, the remaining 16
samples being the first sample of each run and the two second
encounters on the circle, at which the obstacle re-enters the range and
a fresh rollout is used. The full step was accepted at 5477 samples and
a shorter one at 61; the zero step was never needed, no sample was
linearized twice, and no fresh rollout was needed for an infeasible
shifted problem. With estimated states the shifted plan was accepted at
22919 of 22966 samples (99.8\%). At the other 47 it failed a collision
row (32), the handling envelope (7), or the terminal set (8, one
endpoint no longer mild and seven whose continuation met the moved
forecast); the zero step was taken at 66 samples, a second
linearization at 6, and a fresh rollout after an infeasible shifted
problem at 28, all in 15\,m/s braking leads; five further samples
started from a fresh rollout because the obstacle re-entered the range.
The 47 are the two mechanisms of Section~\ref{subsec:robust} at work:
rows that the innovation moved, which a tube would have absorbed, and
a forecast that moved the terminal set, which nesting would have
prevented.

The extension of the shifted plan appended guidance holds through
states outside $\mathcal X_f$ at 275 exact-state and 521
estimated-state samples, braking holds at 20 and 26, and found no hold
that keeps the endpoint in $\mathcal X_f$ at 18 and 14, after which the
plan stayed one hold shorter; no run ran out of plan. The continuation
of the issued plan's endpoint ended with the obstacle's exit at 678
exact-state and 4697 estimated-state samples and with the parked check
at 1517 and 6889; the rest had no obstacle in range. So in most samples
with an obstacle the certificate behind the horizon is a stop: the ego
could brake to rest at any time and the obstacle's forecast would pass
the parked car.

\subsection{Estimation in the Loop}
\label{subsec:loopEstimation}

Over the 84 estimated-state runs, the 95th percentile of the ego
estimation error within a run is 0.002 to 0.010\,rad in heading, 0.009
to 0.16\,m/s in lateral velocity and 0.007 to 0.023\,m in position.
With the kinematic measurement of Section~\ref{subsec:egoObserver} and
a fixed declared domain, the same encounters gave heading errors of
0.07 to 0.31\,rad and lateral-velocity errors of 1.0 to 3.9\,m/s at
15\,m/s (one seed, on the controller of October 5), the ego enclosure
was lost in nine of thirteen runs, and only five of fourteen runs
recovered. The force-balance measurement of
Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided} and the handling envelope of
Section~\ref{subsec:envelope} were introduced together for this reason.

The published enclosures were audited against the recorded truth in
every sample of the fourteen runs of one seed, by replaying the
estimator on the recorded plant trajectory; the replay reproduced the
recorded estimates to $5\cdot10^{-15}$. The ego enclosure was valid and
available in all 4001 samples and the target enclosure in 2018 of them,
and neither failed to contain the truth. The medians of the ego radii
within a run were 0.062 to 0.064\,m in position, 0.005 to 0.018\,rad in
heading and 0.08 to 0.15\,m/s in lateral velocity. The true motion
stayed inside the sideslip cone \eqref{eq:sideslip_cone} and below the
adhesion condition \eqref{eq:adhesion_cone} in every sample, including
the two runs of the seed that slow into the blended regime, where the
plant's lateral forces are no longer those of the Fiala model that the
force-balance measurement inverts. The audit covers the sampled
instants of one seed and is not a proof of the premises.

The target enclosure remains wide where the controller needs it most.
The relative-position radius has a median of about 0.064\,m, but the
set of motion parameters used for prediction has median radii of 0.20
to 0.59\,rad in course and 1.4 to 4.3\,m/s in speed, and the whole
declared range for $A_C$ and $\kappa_C$. The point estimates of the
parameters are also noisy: between 0.5 and 3\,s, the root-mean-square
error within a run has a median of 0.26\,m/s$^2$ for $A_C$ (largest
0.61\,m/s$^2$) and 0.008\,rad for $\beta_C$ (largest 0.042\,rad). The
gain program \eqref{eq:noise_shape_lmi} bounds the variance of the
velocity error; the noise of the acceleration state, from which $A_C$
and $\beta_C$ are reconstructed, is not part of its objective. These
widths are the ones that Section~\ref{subsec:robust} measures the
robust problem against.

\subsection{Computation}
\label{subsec:computationResults}

One controller call took a median of 5.5\,ms and a 95th percentile of
19\,ms with exact states, and 7.3 and 36\,ms with estimated states, in
the campaign itself, with six runs sharing the machine
(Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop}(f)); 1.1\% of the exact-state calls and 2.3\%
of the estimated-state calls exceeded the 50\,ms hold, and the largest
calls took 0.11 and 0.30\,s. The estimator took a median of 12\,ms per
hold, for four estimator samples. The time grows with the plan length:
the slow calls are those with long horizons, up to 184 holds, and the
28 samples that solved a fresh rollout after an infeasible shifted
problem. In a separate benchmark with exact states, two runs sharing the
machine and the obstacle appearing after 2\,s of target-free driving,
the sample at which the obstacle appears took 26\,ms in the median and
72\,ms at most, and all samples 6.5\,ms in the median and 23\,ms at the
95th percentile. The measures of Section~\ref{subsec:computation} halved
these times against the previous implementation of the same problem,
whose 95th percentile with estimated states was 136\,ms; the solver
tolerances account for about 6\% of the time at the median and 15\% at
the 95th percentile, and cost 0.1\,s of recovery time at the median.
Because the computation time is not applied as a delay, the outcomes
describe the algorithm with instantaneous computation.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The paper set out to make lane keeping, cruise control, and vehicle
collision avoidance one system and to say, link by link, what is proved about its safety. We
discuss the links in the order in which they turned out to matter.

\subsubsection*{The estimator during the maneuver}
The point of keeping Sharma's relative motion model while giving up its
differentiated coordinates was to stay valid during the ego's own
maneuver, and the open-loop comparison bears this out where it counts:
in the swerve-and-braking and weaving scenarios, which violate the
zero-jerk premise of the reference observer, the speed error is an
order of magnitude lower, and the reference fails to converge in a
third and in all of the trials. The gain selection takes place inside
the certificate rather than beside it. With a shape chosen outside the
certificate only the scale can be adjusted to it, and the gains come
out larger than the certificate requires; choosing the shape inside
lowered the predicted velocity noise and the certified radii together.
The remaining losses against the tuned reference are consistent with
constraints of the certificate: at low relative speed the decay floor
keeps the certified gains noisier than the reference's uncertified
choice, and in arc following the sideslip bound declared for that
scenario raises $L_q$ from 0.80 to 2.29\,s$^{-2}$.

\subsubsection*{Closing the loop changed both designs}
Most changes came in pairs, because the estimator's premises are
statements about the true motion and the controller decides that
motion. The kinematic premise of the velocity measurement failed in
exactly the maneuvers the controller exists for, and the bounds
published under it were then wrong, not merely loose. Sharing the tire
model and the held input replaced that premise by one the controller
can enforce, and the controller in turn accepted two constraints, the
sideslip cone and the rear-adhesion cone, whose purpose is partly to
keep the estimator informative; in the audited seed the true motion
never left either. In the other direction, the estimator's enclosure
could not be used as a worst case throughout the horizon or in the
decrease condition: both made the programs infeasible or the commands
erratic. The baseline compromise tightens one affine hold and replans; it does
not certify the nonlinear hold without remainder bounds. The theoretical
construction of Section~\ref{subsec:robust} instead propagates the actual
observer and future feedback jointly; it does not repeatedly propagate
an independent estimation-error box through an open-loop plant.

\subsubsection*{The terminal set and the ten failures}
The terminal set is built from the obstacle's own motion model and one
braking law, is invariant under that law, and makes the problem
recursively feasible on the declared model
(Proposition~\ref{prop:recursive}). In the loop the statement held
where its premises held: the shifted plan was accepted at every
exact-state sample and at 99.8\% of the estimated-state samples. The
cost is that the forecast is now trusted until the obstacle leaves the
range, which for a stop in the lane means several seconds beyond the
plan, and the published course and curvature of an obstacle acquired
half a second earlier are not good enough for that on the curved road:
eight of the ten failures are of this kind, and an earlier design with a
weaker, non-invariant terminal condition recovered 82 of 84. The
published parameter sets contained the truth in every audited sample;
the controller forecasts with their centers. These historical failures concern the baseline approximation. Propagating
the published target marginal through the full horizon recovered 31 of 84
encounters in an earlier ablation, with one collision; that experiment
does not test the joint future-feedback formulation.

Theorem~\ref{thm:robust} resolves the mathematical shift argument by
retaining joint source identities, conditioning the next information set
and carrying the old policy and tube certificates. The common terminal
rule covers every admitted branch, including a nonzero parked pose set.
Neither a fixed-width invariant tube nor a freshly recomputed monotone
outer approximation is required. Exact reachable images give a complete
conditional existence proof; numerical membership and image algorithms
are a separate realization problem. Narrower valid parameter enclosures
can enlarge the initially feasible domain, but are not an additional
logical step in that proof. Feedback need not suppress growth of an
uncontrolled target's absolute marginal: collision rows use the joint
relative geometry, with all remaining spread accounted for.

\subsubsection*{The CLF}
One obstacle-independent function governs avoidance and recovery, every
completed run returns to cruise with the same function and programs
throughout, and the certificate of the cruise law rests on the sampled
nonlinear model rather than on its linearization at the trim, at the
cost of an enclosure hypothesis that is sampled, not proved. The
decrease requested of the issued input is a request on a local model,
and the plant misses it in 7\% of the zero-slack samples with exact
states. The one run in which $V$ increases, at 15\,m/s on the curve,
points at the tie term: near the path its weight vanishes, the later
inputs of the plan are then fixed by nothing but the solver's choice,
and the next anchor inherits them. A floor of one unit of the error
scales on that weight, applied to the shifted plan, removed the
oscillation in the revision of the controller that followed the
campaign of this section (cruise exact at both speeds on both paths, 14
of 14 and 73 of 84 encounters recovered); the results reported here
predate it.

\subsubsection*{Premises of the trials}
Several premises are favorable. The plant is the prediction model with
nominal parameters, so the structural premise of the force-balance
measurement holds by construction above 4\,m/s, and the 5\% parameter
box is a design margin, not a measured mismatch. The obstacle follows
its declared model. Computation is treated as instantaneous although 1
to 2\% of the calls exceed the hold. The steering angle is unbounded in
magnitude and rate, and the estimated-state runs use up to 1.04\,rad.
The road edges are declared, not perceived, and the relative-position
measurement is synthetic with a noise bound of 0.04\,m; on simulated
camera and LiDAR captures the perception stage of
Section~\ref{subsec:architecture} has so far produced position errors
above the 0.12\,m bound assumed in Section~\ref{subsec:designResults} on
most frames, and it has not been run in the loop.
Assumption~\ref{asm:mild} carries no mismatch bound, and the braking
phase of the terminal continuation is sampled like the plan. The
sampled realization of the observer is not covered by the
continuous-time theorem. Each of these premises has to be removed
before physical collision avoidance can be claimed.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Lane keeping, cruise control, and vehicle collision avoidance were
treated as one control problem, and the estimator and the controller that solve it were
designed for each other. The estimator keeps the ego-motion-aware
relative motion model of Sharma \emph{et al.} but takes the measured
acceleration and yaw rate as inputs instead of differentiating them
away, so it needs neither zero jerk nor zero yaw acceleration; it
carries a cascade ISS certificate, chooses its gains inside that
certificate, measures the lateral velocity through the controller's
tire model and held input, and publishes an enclosure with every
estimate. The controller is a predictive control barrier function for
the vehicle: one nonlinear rollout per sample on a Fiala bicycle, two
cone programs in priority order, a terminal set built from the
obstacle's forecast and one braking law that is invariant and makes the
problem recursively feasible on the declared model, and one quadratic
CLF for lane keeping, cruise control, and post-avoidance recovery. The
controller keeps the vehicle where the estimator's measurement is informative, and the enclosure
tightens each hold.

Theorem~\ref{thm:robust} completes the conditional mathematical link
for the road-free uncertainty-aware design. One nonlinear joint model
propagates the physical vehicle, measurements, observer and causal feedback,
with one shared model-mismatch history. Exact images, or proved nonlinear
outer bounds, contain every admitted branch. The posterior is a subset of
the old first successor; shifting the policy and retaining its certificates
then gives a feasible candidate ending in the common terminal continuation.
Nonzero estimation error and varying tube widths are allowed. A soft CLF
stage preserves feasibility, and executing an attained primary minimum
also gives optimal-relaxation descent. Initial feasibility and the declared
uncertainty and terminal contracts remain explicit premises.

In the October 11 baseline, in fourteen collision-threat encounters with exact states every run
ends collision-free on the road with cruise recovered and the shifted
plan feasible at every sample. With the estimator and bounded sensor
noise, 74 of 84 runs do so, none collides or leaves the road, and ten
stop without a solved command, eight of them within two seconds of the
start, where the published course and curvature of the obstacle place
its forecast meters from its motion. The published enclosures contained
the truth in every audited sample. These are simulations on the
prediction model itself. The reported implementation uses per-hold affine
tightening and center-based terminal rows, so these results are evidence
about that approximation. Implementing the joint certificate construction
and evaluating physical model, perception and intersample separation
contracts are future engineering work. They do not leave a missing step
in the stated mathematical recursive-feasibility proof.
