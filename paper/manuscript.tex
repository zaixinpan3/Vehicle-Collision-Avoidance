\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} driving has become a major area of research,
and an active collision-avoidance system is an essential component of an
autonomous vehicle. By detecting potential hazards and initiating an
evasive response, a collision-avoidance system enables a vehicle to avoid
obstacles without driver intervention. In road traffic, other vehicles are among the most common sources of
collision risk. Consequently, the design of systems that
can actively avoid collisions with surrounding vehicles has attracted
considerable research interest \cite{LiEtAl2022Emergency,ChenChengLiZhaoWang2024}.

Collision avoidance often requires the ego vehicle to perform complex
maneuvers, such as decelerating while turning \cite{TanWuLiLiuZhang2025}.
Such maneuvers pose two challenges. First, the vehicle's longitudinal and
lateral dynamics become strongly coupled, as changes in speed alter lateral
tire forces and yaw response while yaw motion couples the longitudinal and
lateral velocities \cite{BealBoyd2019,ParkGerdes2017}. Neglecting this
coupling can lead to large prediction errors and control commands that demand
maneuvers beyond the vehicle's physical capabilities. Second, sensors
such as LiDAR can provide relative-position measurements of surrounding
vehicles, but a more comprehensive assessment of collision risk benefits
from additional state information, such as velocity, acceleration, and heading. When only relative positions are
available, estimating these states is complicated by the ego vehicle's
evasive maneuvers, which also affect the measurements
\cite{AlaiSharmaAlexanderRajamani2024,SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}.

Regarding the ego vehicle's coupled dynamics, some approaches simplify
lateral control by assuming a constant longitudinal speed, thereby
decoupling it from the longitudinal dynamics. This assumption is used in
steering control \cite{JiKhajepourMelekHuang2017}, integrated path tracking
\cite{XiongLiuYangLeng2022}, and hierarchical planning
\cite{ChenAksunGuvenc2025}, but it cannot capture braking-induced speed
changes. Other approaches use kinematic models for trajectory planning
\cite{LiZhangGuoLenzoGuo2023}, which allow speed to vary but omit tire-force
dynamics. To capture the effect of speed variations on lateral dynamics,
lateral models can be formulated using linear parameter-varying control
\cite{AtouiSenameMilanesMartinez2022} or gain-scheduled robust control
\cite{LiLamLuLi2022}, and steering decisions can be computed for prescribed
braking and throttle profiles \cite{TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013}.
Although these methods capture the effect of speed on lateral motion, speed
dependence alone does not account for the reduction in lateral tire capacity
under braking. Combined demands can be constrained through acceleration
limits \cite{ChenPengGrizzle2018}, stability envelopes
\cite{ChenChengLiZhaoWang2024}, or stability-based speed adjustment
\cite{AttiaOrjuelaBasset2012,AttiaOrjuelaBasset2014}, while
assume--guarantee contracts bound the interaction between separate controllers
\cite{XuGrizzleTabuadaAmes2018}; their guarantees depend on the prescribed
bounds holding during operation. Such constraints need not, however,
represent the full nonlinear tire response. To describe the coupled dynamics
more directly, nonlinear feedback methods coordinate longitudinal and
lateral actions using backstepping \cite{GuoHuWang2016}, Lyapunov design
\cite{CheblyTaljCharara2017}, or dynamic inversion
\cite{SeoLeeBaekHorowitzChoi2022}. Predictive controllers optimize motion
using wheel-slip inputs \cite{ZhouJiaJingLiuGuvenc2018}, combined-slip tire
models \cite{ZhangJiangLiuHuWangChen2023}, load-transfer dynamics
\cite{ThompsonDallasGohBalachandran2024}, or drift-aware planning
\cite{ZhaoChenGaoYaoGaoHua2024}. Their accuracy depends on the adopted model,
and nonlinear prediction also increases computational demand.

Regarding the estimation of surrounding-vehicle motion, one option is to
express the motion model in an inertial frame using estimated ego position and
orientation \cite{SharmaRajamani2024}, but ego localization and heading errors
then feed directly into the target track. Working in the ego sensor frame
avoids this global transformation, yet some relative-motion observers do not
account for ego rotation
\cite{JeonZemoucheRajamani2019,AlaiZemoucheRajamani2024}. To address the estimation errors that this omission can cause during
evasive maneuvers, Alai \emph{et al.}
\cite{AlaiSharmaAlexanderRajamani2024} formulated a nonlinear relative motion
model (NRMM) that incorporates ego velocity and yaw rate explicitly,
Kyong \emph{et al.} \cite{KyongAlaiTamehRajamani2026} further demonstrated
the importance of ego sideslip-angle estimation for relative-motion target
tracking during sharp turns and U-turns, and Sharma
\emph{et al.} \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026} designed a multistage high-gain
observer (HGO) around the NRMM for simultaneous ego-state estimation and target
trajectory tracking. While the NRMM captures how an evasive maneuver alters
relative motion in the sensor frame, that multistage HGO transforms both the
ego kinematics and the NRMM relative-position dynamics into third-order
companion chains by assuming zero body jerk and zero yaw acceleration, an
assumption that breaks down during rapid steering and braking. In addition, its
nominal convergence and scalar yaw $H_\infty$ analyses do not provide an
explicit, time-varying state-error bound under sensor noise, model mismatch,
and initial transients, which a safety controller needs to tighten its
collision constraints.

Together, these two challenges raise the central problem of how to
construct a collision-avoidance controller with formal safety guarantees
that respects coupled tire and actuator limits while remaining feasible
under online estimation errors. Field-based
planners such as artificial potential fields \cite{WangGaoLiSunCheng2019}
and virtual fluid-flow models \cite{ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021} blend lane keeping
with obstacle repulsion intuitively, yet a collision-free
reference path does not certify closed-loop safety once tire saturation,
input bounds, and tracking errors intervene. Control barrier functions
(CBFs) enforce set forward invariance directly through affine conditions
on the control input \cite{AmesXuGrizzleTabuada2017,XiaoBelta2022}. Under
bounded actuation, however, myopic one-step CBF quadratic programs can
lose feasibility \cite{ZengZhangLiSreenath2021}. For lane keeping and
obstacle avoidance, Bruggemann \emph{et al.}
\cite{BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022} investigate input-constrained barrier
functions using a constant-speed lateral vehicle model. Hardware actuator
limits can also lead to saturation in high-speed corner cases when they are
omitted from the controller formulation
\cite{TayalEtAl2026}. Road experiments have also recorded safety
violations under nominal CBFs during vehicle cut-ins and delays in live
traffic \cite{GunterNiceBuntingSprinkleWork2022} and under unmodeled
actuation disturbances on automated trucks
\cite{AlanTaylorHeAmesOrosz2023}. Approaches to anticipating future
hazards include enforcing discrete-time barrier constraints within MPC
\cite{ZengZhangSreenath2021} and constructing barriers from predicted
motion under a prescribed policy \cite{BlackJankovicSharmaPanagou2023}.
Predictive control barrier functions (PCBFs) use finite-horizon
constraint relaxations and terminal constructions to establish recursive
feasibility and safety recovery under their respective assumptions
\cite{WabersichZeilinger2023,HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025}. Still,
these PCBF guarantees assume exact state measurements or fixed offline
disturbance bounds. Conversely,
observer-based CBFs account for moving-obstacle estimation errors via
input-to-state safety margins \cite{TezukaNakamura2020} or bounded-error
observer sets \cite{QuanZhouFriskChung2025}, yet remain one-step filters
without multi-step prediction under coupled tire and actuator limits.
Safe MPC for uncertain road users \cite{BatkovicGuptaZanonFalcone2023}
establishes recursive feasibility through reachable-set tightening, a
safe terminal set, and step-to-step prediction consistency, but relies on
a kinematic ego model and assumes ego sensing and localization are
already solved. In coupled maneuvers with online ego and target
estimation, refreshing the estimate and re-tightening constraints at each
sample can invalidate the prior backup plan unless successive uncertainty
sets remain compatible.

For nonlinear observer design, Sharma, Zemouche and Rajamani
\cite{SharmaZemoucheRajamani2026} develop derivative-based LMI conditions
for systems with vector arguments and introduce state scaling to improve
numerical feasibility. Their analysis informs the nonlinear-increment
bounds and scaled quadratic certificate used in the estimator below.

To address these challenges, this paper develops a joint NRMM-based
state estimator and PCBF--CLF controller built around a shared dynamic
bicycle model with modified Fiala tires. The system also includes a
camera--LiDAR perception pipeline for target-position measurement. The
design and evaluation of perception are not the focus of this paper: the
pipeline has been tested offline, while the closed-loop experiments use
synthetic relative-position measurements. The system architecture is
described in Section~\ref{sec:systemDesign}. The main contributions of
this work are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
\item We reformulate the NRMM target observer of
\cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026} in covariant coordinates so that measured
ego acceleration and yaw rate enter as exogenous inputs without
differentiation, eliminating the zero-jerk and zero-yaw-acceleration
assumptions. Combined with semidefinite-programming (SDP) gain synthesis
and a tire force-balance lateral-velocity measurement, the observer
admits a continuous-time cascade input-to-state stability (ISS)
certificate and publishes explicit, time-varying error enclosures online
(Section~\ref{sec:estimator}).
\item We design a compact nonlinear PCBF controller on the coupled Fiala
bicycle model that pairs fixed measurement-consistency ensemble inference
with condensed nonlinear sequential quadratic programming over coarse
control knots and exact dual rectangle separation at control-boundary
nodes. A terminal continuation that keeps the vehicles separated until
the target leaves sensing range supports horizon-shift feasibility under an explicit
physical handoff assumption, while a secondary tire-loading and rear-slip
preference guides selection among validated zero-deficit driving plans
without altering the primary safety objective (Section~\ref{sec:control}).
\item We establish a joint nonlinear output-feedback safety formulation
that propagates both vehicles and observers under future
measurement-dependent policies. Conditioning on the entire received
sensor block permits restriction of the old policy tail. Under explicit
uncertainty, attainment and terminal-closure assumptions, this proves
recursive feasibility, nonincreasing optimal safety relaxation and
decision-instant safety of its zero set for the road-free problem.
The adopted compact Fiala controller's approximations are stated
separately (Section~\ref{subsec:robust}).
\item We benchmark the proposed estimator against the reproduced
multistage HGO of \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}, verify the
information-set safety mechanisms on a longitudinal reference problem,
and preserve the historical October 11 closed-loop baseline across 14
collision-threat encounters under both exact states and bounded sensor
noise over six random seeds, including an audit of truth containment for
the published enclosures; closed-loop re-evaluation of the revised
compact controller remains pending (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:systemDesign} introduces the task and system architecture.
Sections~\ref{sec:estimator} and~\ref{sec:control} develop the state
estimator and the predictive controller, respectively. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the
numerical evaluation, and Sections~\ref{sec:discussion}
and~\ref{sec:conclusion} discuss implementation limitations and conclude
the paper.

\section{Two Vehicles in Relative Motion}\label{sec:systemDesign}

Let the two vehicles occupy congruent rectangles in the plane. The ego
vehicle follows a prescribed path at speed $V_{\rm ref}>0$ and observes
the target's relative position while it is within sensing range. During
this encounter the target has constant tangential acceleration and
constant sideslip. The control is one steering and longitudinal input,
chosen from the observations received so far. We seek to preserve
separation and to follow the nominal cruise command whenever safety permits.

Write $e(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)^\top$, let $J$ rotate a vector
through $\pi/2$, and let $R(\theta)=[e(\theta),Je(\theta)]$.
The ego state comprises its inertial position $p$, body heading $\psi$,
body velocity $v=(v_x,v_y)^\top$, and yaw rate $r$. Write $R_E=R(\psi)$.
Under steering angle $\delta$ and normalized longitudinal demand $b$, the
single-track force balances are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ego}
\begin{aligned}
 \dot p &=R_Ev, & \dot\psi&=r,\\
 m(\dot v+rJv)&=R(\delta)F_f+F_r-F_d(v_x)e(0),\\
 I_z\dot r&=l_f[R(\delta)F_f]_2-l_r[F_r]_2 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $F_f,F_r$ are axle forces in wheel axes, $l_f,l_r$ are axle
distances, $m,I_z$ are mass and yaw inertia, and $F_d$ is longitudinal
road load. For axle $i$, put $K_i=\mu_iF_{zi}$, with friction coefficient
$\mu_i$ and static normal load $F_{zi}$. The longitudinal force $bK_i$
leaves lateral capacity $D_i=K_i\sqrt{1-b^2}$. For $D_i>0$, set
$z_i=C_i|\tan\alpha_i|/(3D_i)$, where $C_i$ is the cornering
stiffness. The modified Fiala law gives
\[
 [F_i]_2=-D_i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha_i)
       \bigl[1-(1-z_i)_+^3\bigr],
\]
with zero lateral force at $D_i=0$ and $x_+=\max(x,0)$. The front and
rear slip angles are $\operatorname{atan2}(v_y+l_fr,v_x)-\delta$ and
$\operatorname{atan2}(v_y-l_rr,v_x)$. We work on the forward rolling
domain with positive wheel-longitudinal velocities. Physical parameters
$\vartheta$ are fixed along each motion. Admissible inputs respect tire
and actuator limits and give unique flows. An actuator with memory is
included in the physical state.

\subsection{The target orbit and its clock}

Let $\gamma$ denote the target's course, $V$ its speed, $A$ its
tangential acceleration, and $\beta$ its sideslip. With known rear axle
distance $l_T$ and $|\beta|<\pi/2$, its curvature is
$\kappa=\sin(\beta)/l_T$ and its body heading is $\psi_T=\gamma-\beta$.
The moving-target equations are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 \dot p_T=Ve(\gamma),\quad \dot V=A,\quad
 \dot\gamma=\kappa V,\quad \dot A=\dot\kappa=0 .
\end{equation}
Identify the plane with $\mathbb C$. Choose an epoch $a$ on a moving
arc and put $b_T=p_T(a)$, $d=e^{i\gamma(a)}$. At elapsed time $\tau$
the traveled length is $s(\tau)=V(a)\tau+A\tau^2/2$. Since
$d\gamma=\kappa\,ds$, the position $T_q$ of any body point $q$ follows
by integrating the course direction and rotating its body offset. Thus
\begin{align}
 F_\kappa(s)&=\int_0^s e^{i\kappa l}\,dl,\qquad F_0(s)=s,\nonumber\\
 T_q(a+\tau)&=b_T+d\bigl[F_\kappa(s)+e^{i(\kappa s-\beta)}q\bigr].
 \label{eq:orbit}
\end{align}
Thus three scalar quantities, $V(a),A,\kappa$, fix the entire motion
up to a planar rigid displacement. When braking brings $V$ to zero, the
stipulated absorbing stop holds $s$ at $-V(a)^2/(2A)$ and preserves all
four corners.

Equation~\eqref{eq:orbit} is also a representation of the nonlinear
relative motion model (NRMM) \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}. To see the
equivalence, put $\rho=R_E^\top(p_T-p)$, $w=R_E^\top\dot p_T$,
$f=R_E^\top\ddot p_T$ and $\nabla_r=\frac{d}{dt}+rJ$ in ego axes.
With $d_B=w/V$ and $\Omega=\kappa V$, differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nrmm}
 \nabla_r\rho=w-v,\quad \nabla_r w=f,\quad
 \nabla_r f=-\Omega^2w+3A\Omega Jd_B .
\end{equation}
Indeed $f=A d_B+V\Omega Jd_B$ and $\nabla_r d_B=\Omega Jd_B$.
Conversely, on $V>0$, one recovers $V=\|w\|$, $A=d_B^\top f$ and
$\kappa=(Jd_B)^\top f/V^2$. Equation~\eqref{eq:nrmm} gives
$\dot A=0$ and $\frac{d}{dt}((Jd_B)^\top f)=2A\Omega$, hence
$\dot\kappa=0$. Both representations therefore give the same relative
position and body orientation. For each fixed physical hypothesis, the
inertial target orbit is independent of the future ego input. Its
relative position $\rho$ depends on that input through $p$ and $R_E$.

\section{Observation as a Family of Target Orbits}\label{sec:estimator}

Index the possible motions at epoch $a$ by $z$. A label specifies the
ego state and parameters, the target tuple $(b_T,d,V(a),A,\kappa)$,
and any persistent sensor bias or disturbance memory. Take a compact
prior containing the true label. For each member, propagate the ego
under the input actually applied. The measured relative position has
the form $y_\rho=\rho+\eta_\rho+n_\rho$, with bias $\eta_\rho$ and
bounded residual $n_\rho$. The ego measurements of position, yaw rate
and inertial acceleration in body axes have noise-free parts $p$, $r$
and $\dot v+rJv$, respectively. Impose their joint error bounds on the
same label. The resulting set-membership estimate
\cite{BertsekasRhodes1971} is the family $\Theta_t$ of surviving
labels. In fixed epoch coordinates, receiving further observations gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditioning}
 \Theta_{t+h}=\{z\in\Theta_t:\text{the new observations agree with }z\}.
\end{equation}
For errors with temporal memory the label includes the compatible
history, or its sufficient memory, when taking this restriction. The
current estimate is the image of $\Theta_t$ at time $t$. Its forecast at
$t+h$ is the image of the same set at $t+h$ under \eqref{eq:orbit}.
In particular, each forecast retains the ego history that made that
target motion compatible with the radar.

\subsection{What a position chord determines}

Condition on an ego history and the radar bias. Let
$Y=p+R_E(y_\rho-\eta_\rho)$ be the observed inertial target position.
For a position error disk of radius $\epsilon_j$ at $t_j$, put
$F_j=F_\kappa(s(t_j-a))$. The admissible translations satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disks}
 b_T\in\bigcap_j \mathbb B(Y_j-dF_j,\epsilon_j),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb B$ denotes a closed Euclidean disk. With two exact fixes
and $F_1\ne F_0$, subtraction gives $d=(Y_1-Y_0)/(F_1-F_0)$.
Substituting into \eqref{eq:orbit} eliminates both pose variables. At
future time $w$, write $s_w=s(w-a)$ and
$G_q(w)=F_\kappa(s_w)+e^{i(\kappa s_w-\beta)}q$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chord}
 T_q(w)=Y_0+(Y_1-Y_0)\frac{G_q(w)-F_0}{F_1-F_0}.
\end{equation}
The same measured chord locates every future corner. A candidate clock
and curvature must still give $|d|=1$ and agree with the other fixes.

Three distinct, exact fixes in forward order identify the curvature on an
arc of less than one revolution. With $D_{ij}=Y_j-Y_i$, the signed
triangle area and circumradius give
\[
 \kappa=\frac{2\det(D_{01},D_{02})}
              {\|D_{01}\|\,\|D_{02}\|\,\|D_{12}\|}.
\]
The straight limit gives zero. The locations determine curvature
independently of the times at which the target passed through them.
The observation times then constrain its speed. On a moving minor arc,
a chord of length $l$ subtends the arc length
$L_\kappa(l)=2\arcsin(|\kappa|l/2)/|\kappa|$, with $L_0(l)=l$.
This follows by taking the modulus of $F_\kappa(s)$. If $l^-,l^+$
are the ends of the feasible chord-length interval, put
$\Delta=t_j-t_i$ and $\bar t=(t_j+t_i)/2$. The quadratic clock gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clockstrip}
 L_\kappa(l^-)\le\Delta\bigl[V(a)+(\bar t-a)A\bigr]\le L_\kappa(l^+).
\end{equation}
The position disks give the chord bounds by the triangle inequality,
intersected with $[0,2/|\kappa|]$ when $\kappa\ne0$.
For fixed $\kappa$, each observation pair thus cuts a strip in the
$(V(a),A)$ plane. Distinct pairs constrain the same two unknowns. For
example, on a straight arc an exact displacement at elapsed time
$\tau>0$ fixes $V(a)+\tau A/2$. Even when the allowable ranges of
$V(a)$ and $A$ individually stay wide, their contribution to displacement
at time $\tau$ is known exactly.

\subsection{Retaining the measured correlations}

In a local chart let $\lambda$ denote displacement from a fixed
reference label. A scalar observation of the form
$y=\bar y+a^\top\lambda+e$, $|e|\le r$, contributes the two inequalities
$|y-\bar y-a^\top\lambda|\le r$. Here $r$ includes measurement error
and a bound on the nonlinear residual over the chart. Write the retained
joint enclosure as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polytope}
 P_t=\{\lambda\in B:H\lambda\le b\},
\end{equation}
with coordinate box $B$. The raw observations and the conditional clock
strips enter in the same coordinates. Each row must hold for every
compatible member in its chart. Appending such rows preserves the truth
and narrows the same family on which future clearance is evaluated.

\section{Clearance of Two Uncertain Rectangles}\label{sec:control}

Let $B_0=[-L,L]\times[-W,W]$ and let $B_i=o_i+B_0$, $i=E,T$,
where $o_i$ locates the geometric center in its body frame. In ego axes
put $Q=R_E^\top R(\psi_T)$ and $c=\rho+Qo_T-o_E$. The two rectangles
intersect exactly when $c\in Z(Q)=B_0\oplus QB_0$. This configuration
obstacle is the sum of four line segments. For a compact set $S$ write
$h_S(n)=\sup_{s\in S}n^\top s$. The signed clearance reduces to
\begin{align}
 g(c,Q)&=\max_{\|n\|=1}\{n^\top c-h_0(n)-h_0(Q^\top n)\},
 \label{eq:distance}\\
 h_0(n)&=L|n_1|+W|n_2|.\nonumber
\end{align}
Outside $Z(Q)$ the closest point supplies an attaining normal. Inside,
the least supporting-plane slack is the radius of the largest disk
centered at $c$ inside $Z(Q)$, giving negative signed clearance.
At contact $g=0$. In half-space coordinates $B_0=\{x:Dx\le k\}$,
each support has the equivalent dual
$h_0(n)=\min_{\ell\ge0,\ D^\top\ell=n}k^\top\ell$
\cite{ZhangLinigerBorrelli2021}.

For one common candidate input $u$, let $g_z(h;u)$ be
\eqref{eq:distance} at time $t+h$ along member $z$. The observable
robust gap is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
 G_t(h;u)=\inf_{z\in\Theta_t}g_z(h;u).
\end{equation}
The infimum follows the choice of input and precedes the choice of
normal for each member. Only the input must be realizable from the
observations. If all variables except target translation are fixed and
the possible center displacements form a compact convex set $C$
disjoint from $Z(Q)$, a closest pair supports both sets. In this case
one normal is exact for the whole family, and
$G=\operatorname{dist}(C,Z(Q))=\max_{\|n\|=1}
[-h_C(-n)-h_{Z(Q)}(n)]$.

\subsection{A joint bound in the separating direction}\label{subsec:robust}

Let $E_i,T_j$ denote the four corners of the respective bodies in
common inertial axes. For fixed $u,h$, enclose their differences on
$P_t$ by
\[
 T_j-E_i=\bar D_{ij}+M_{ij}\lambda+e_{ij},\qquad
 \|e_{ij}\|\le r_{ij}.
\]
The columns of $M_{ij}$ describe variation with respect to the same
physical label. A shared translation has a zero column in the
difference. For any unit $n$, the least projected affine difference is
$n^\top\bar D_{ij}-h_{P_t}(-M_{ij}^\top n)$. Taking all vertex pairs
and then the best common normal gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:certificate}
 \sup_{\|n\|=1}\min_{i,j}
 \{n^\top\bar D_{ij}-h_{P_t}(-M_{ij}^\top n)-r_{ij}\}
 \ \le\ G_t(h;u).
\end{equation}
Indeed every projected pair exceeds its stated lower bound for every
member. A common normal gives a sufficient certificate even when
members would select different maximizers in \eqref{eq:distance}.

For nonempty $P_t$ and any support direction $c'$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
 h_{P_t}(c')=\min_{\mu\ge0}\{b^\top\mu+h_B(c'-H^\top\mu)\}.
\end{equation}
To obtain this identity, dualize the observation rows and the upper
and lower box faces. For fixed observation multipliers $\mu$, the box
multipliers have difference $c'-H^\top\mu$ and their least contribution
is the box support at that residual. Linear programming duality gives
equality, including for a lower-dimensional nonempty polytope. Each
nonnegative $\mu$ already gives an upper support bound with the correct
sign for \eqref{eq:certificate}.

The orbit provides an explicit choice of multipliers. At the current
epoch let $Y$ be the measured position, $d_0$ a reference course vector,
and write $p'=p_T-Y$, $v'=d-d_0$ in complex axes. For a past fix $Y_j$,
let $H_j$ be its signed reference chord from the current epoch and
$\eta_j$ a bound on its chord error. With $z_j=Y_j-Y-H_jd_0$ and
$r_j=\epsilon_j+\eta_j$, the observation implies
$\|p'+H_jv'-z_j\|_\infty\le r_j$ in real coordinates.
For example, if the true arc parameters are $(s',\kappa')$, then
\[
 |F_{\kappa'}(s')-F_\kappa(s)|
 \le |s'-s|+\tfrac12s^2|\kappa'-\kappa|,
\]
by the integral defining $F_\kappa$. A future corner with reference
coefficient $G$ has affine error $p'+Gv'$. The change in $G$ under the
uncertain clock and curvature bounds the remaining corner error since
the true course vector has unit length.
Whenever $H_j\ne0$, set $\omega=G/H_j$. The identity
\[
 p'+Gv'=(1-\omega)p'+\omega(p'+H_jv')
\]
eliminates the course error from the residual. Let $C_\omega$ denote
the real matrix of complex multiplication by $\omega$. For position
box $B_p$ and any unit $n$, set $\nu=C_\omega^\top n$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chorddual}
 n^\top(p'+Gv')\le h_{B_p}(C_{1-\omega}^\top n)
                       +\nu^\top z_j+r_j\|\nu\|_1 .
\end{equation}
The positive and negative parts of $\nu$ are the multipliers for the two
sides of the radar strips. Thus a position chord that helped identify
the orbit also supplies a witness for its clearance.

\section{A Predictive Barrier on the Information Set}

\subsection{Terminal continuation in relative position}

Fix one physically realizable continuation $u_f=(0,b_f)$ with
$|b_f|<1$. At handoff, set $r_0=p_T-p$ and let $\zeta$ collect the
orientations, velocities, persistent parameters and actuator state.
By translation invariance, the reference-point displacements $w_E(h)$,
$w_T(h)$ and the future rotations depend only on $\zeta$ under $u_f$.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forbidden}
\begin{aligned}
 K_h(\zeta)&=w_E(h)-w_T(h)+R_E(h)B_E\oplus(-R_T(h)B_T),\\
 \mathcal F(\zeta)&=\bigcup_{h\ge0}\bigl(K_h(\zeta)\oplus
                                      \mathbb B_{d_*}^{\circ}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $d_*>0$ is the design clearance and $\mathbb B_{d_*}^{\circ}$ is
the open disk about zero. At time $h$, ego and target occupy points
closer than $d_*$ precisely when $r_0$ belongs to the corresponding
open offset in \eqref{eq:forbidden}. Both footprints in each summand are
evaluated at the same time. The terminal set $\mathcal K_f$ consists
of the states with an admissible handoff and forward flow under $u_f$
for which $r_0\notin\mathcal F(\zeta)$.

For example, consider straight motion along one line. Let the ego
travel ahead at trim speed $V_*$, and let the target approach from
behind at initial speed $V_0$ with $A<0$. The gap closes only while the
target is faster. Integrating its excess speed gives the minimum
bumper gap $g_0-(V_0-V_*)_+^2/(2|A|)$ for initial gap $g_0$.
If this quantity is at least $d_*$, the
terminal condition holds even though the two spatial paths overlap.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:terminal}
$\mathcal K_f$ is positively invariant under $u_f$. Every nonempty
family contained in $\mathcal K_f$ admits the same safe continuation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At elapsed time $s$ the remaining motion is the suffix of the same forward
flow. Its relative displacement is $r_s=r_0+w_T(s)-w_E(s)$, and
$K_{s+h}(\zeta)=w_E(s)-w_T(s)+K_h(\zeta_s)$. Hence exclusion from every
open offset at the initial time implies exclusion at time $s$ for every
$h\ge0$. Forward admissibility passes to the suffix. The command $u_f$
is common to all members.
\end{proof}

A turning target provides a second consequence of \eqref{eq:forbidden}.
For $\kappa\ne0$, \eqref{eq:orbit} places its reference point on a circle
with center $C=b_T+i d/\kappa$ and radius $1/|\kappa|$. In any unit
direction $n$ every future target corner has projection at most
$H=n^\top C+1/|\kappa|+\max_{q\in B_T}\|q\|$, also after absorption.
Suppose the terminal ego flow satisfies
$\min_i n^\top E_i(h)\ge m+\alpha h-B$ with $\alpha>0$ and $B\ge0$.
A straight forward trim along $n$ has this property with $B=0$.
For $h\ge(H+d_*-m+B)_+/\alpha$ the projected gap alone guarantees
$d_*$. Under these hypotheses, terminal membership reduces to forward
admissibility and the same-time clearance on the finite interval up to
that cutoff.

\subsection{Safety deficit and nominal cruise}

Fix a horizon $T>0$. Let $\mathcal U_t(T)$ consist of admissible common
inputs giving $G_t(h;u)>0$ for all $h\in[0,T]$ and placing every
terminal member in $\mathcal K_f$. In the predictive-barrier construction
\cite{HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025}, a nonnegative slack spends design
clearance. For a given input its least value at time $h$ is
$c_t(h;u)=(d_*-G_t(h;u))_+$. The barrier value is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barrier}
 \mathcal V_t(T)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal U_t(T)}D_t(u),\qquad
 D_t(u)=\int_0^T c_t(h;u)\,dh .
\end{equation}
The empty feasible family has value $+\infty$. For a certified lower
gap $\ell_t\le G_t$, imposing $\ell_t>0$ and using
$(d_*-\ell_t)_+$ gives the corresponding sufficient formulation.
An early arrival in $\mathcal K_f$ extends under $u_f$ at zero further
deficit, so the entire maneuver fits one prediction interval.

The prescribed path enters through the nominal command $\bar u_t$.
For a path of curvature $k_\Gamma$ and tangent angle $\theta$, write
$e_y$ for lateral error and $\chi=\psi+\operatorname{atan2}(v_y,v_x)$
for course. In a regular arc-length chart,
$\dot e_y=\|v\|\sin(\chi-\theta)$ and
$\dot s_\Gamma=\|v\|\cos(\chi-\theta)/(1-k_\Gamma e_y)$.
At a realizable constant-curvature trim of speed $V_{\rm ref}$ and yaw
rate $k_\Gamma V_{\rm ref}$, let $e$ collect the errors in lateral position,
relative body heading, body velocity and yaw rate. For a stabilizable
transverse linearization $(A_c,B_c)$ choose a feasible local gain $K$
and $P\succ0$ with
$(A_c+B_cK)^\top P+P(A_c+B_cK)\preceq-2\alpha P$, $\alpha>0$.
Then $V_c=e^\top P e$ satisfies
$\dot V_c\le-2\alpha V_c+o(\|e\|^2)$ locally. A varying-curvature path
requires the corresponding inequality along its realizable reference
motion. The nominal forecast must be causal in the available ego
information. Among inputs attaining the minimum in \eqref{eq:barrier},
minimize $\int_0^T (u-\bar u_t)^\top W(u-\bar u_t)\,dh$, $W\succ0$.
Whenever nominal cruise attains the minimum deficit, its deviation cost
is zero.

\subsection{Recursive feasibility}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:shift}
Suppose the initial information contains the truth and admits a
feasible plan. Assume the encounter model and measurement bounds hold,
the admissible input class is closed under restriction and terminal
concatenation, and each new posterior satisfies \eqref{eq:conditioning}.
Execute a prefix of length $0<\Delta\le T$ of a feasible plan $u$.
For every new horizon $T'\ge T-\Delta$ of positive length there exists
a feasible continuation $u'$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:descent}
 D_{t+\Delta}(u')\le D_t(u)-\int_0^\Delta c_t(h;u)\,dh .
\end{equation}
Executing feasible replacements that respect this remaining budget
preserves physical separation throughout a continuing encounter. A plan
with zero initial deficit preserves the design clearance $d_*$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On $[0,T-\Delta]$, take $u'(h)=u(h+\Delta)$. Each label in
$\Theta_{t+\Delta}$ belonged to $\Theta_t$ and experienced the executed
prefix. Uniqueness reproduces its former two footprints at the same
physical time. Taking the infimum over the surviving labels gives
\[
 G_{t+\Delta}(h;u')\ge G_t(h+\Delta;u).
\]
Thus the remaining deficit cannot increase. All surviving terminal
members inherit the former handoff. Append $u_f$ up to $T'$ and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:terminal}. The appended interval has zero deficit.
Integration gives \eqref{eq:descent}. For an attained old minimum the
same bound holds for the new optimal value. Induction preserves
feasibility and strict separation on successive prefixes whose decision
times cover the encounter. If the budget is zero, any strict shortfall
below $d_*$ along the true continuous footprint motion would persist
on a time interval of positive length. Its integral would contradict
the zero budget.
\end{proof}

The same proof applies to \eqref{eq:certificate} on a retained chart.
At a common physical time keep its former normal, corner differences
and residual bounds. A newly appended strip extends every old
multiplier by zero in \eqref{eq:dual}, while a nested box can only decrease
the remaining box support. The old lower gap and its remaining deficit
budget are therefore still certified. A change of chart requires
establishing this bound on the new enclosure. The theorem concerns
encounters admitting a common safe input at entry. Once every compatible
position has left sensing range, nominal driving resumes. A subsequent
encounter requires feasible entry for its own information set.
