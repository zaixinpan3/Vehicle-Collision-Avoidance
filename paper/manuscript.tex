\begin{abstract}
This paper develops a measured-input estimator and a predictive controller
for lane keeping with vehicle collision avoidance. The estimator retains
the
ego-motion-aware nonlinear relative motion model of the prior high-gain
design but not its differentiated ego coordinates: measured acceleration
and yaw rate remain exogenous inputs, so neither zero ego jerk nor zero
yaw acceleration is assumed. A body-velocity measurement built from GNSS
speed and yaw rate gives a cascade input-to-state stability certificate.
The shape
of the target gains is obtained from a semidefinite program that
minimizes a bound on the noise-induced velocity-error variance among
gains satisfying that certificate. In synthetic trials against a
reproduced reference observer tuned per scenario, the estimator has the
lower post-transient mean position, speed and course error in five of
six scenarios each; whole-run position error and most acceleration
errors favor the reference. The controller linearizes a dynamic bicycle
model with modified Fiala tires along a nonlinear rollout and normally
solves two second-order cone programs per sample. The first minimizes the
relaxation of sampled collision constraints written with fixed
distance-dual multipliers; the second keeps that relaxation and minimizes
the violation of one control-Lyapunov decrease condition on the
cost-to-go of a path-guidance feedback. In fourteen exact-state
collision-threat runs, twelve end collision-free with cruise recovered,
one collides, and one stops on a numerical check; every controller call
exceeds the 50\,ms hold. The estimation certificate is continuous-time.
The controller carries no collision-avoidance or recursive-feasibility
certificate for the nonlinear vehicle, and the two components are not
yet evaluated together.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{L}{ane}-keeping systems (LKSs) have been extensively studied
and widely deployed in production vehicles \cite{WeiPfefferEdelmann2024}.
Conventional LKSs, however, primarily regulate the ego vehicle's lateral
displacement and heading relative to a lane reference. When an obstacle vehicle
poses a collision risk, avoidance is often left to the driver. This limitation
has motivated considerable research on integrated lane-keeping and vehicle
collision-avoidance systems
\cite{TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013,FahmyGhanyBaumann2018,
ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021,BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022}. These systems retain lane
keeping as the nominal behavior and initiate an automated evasive response when
a collision risk arises.

Collision avoidance has also been studied extensively, but most existing
studies assume that the motion states of the ego and obstacle vehicles are
accurately available. This assumption ignores estimation uncertainty, which can
invalidate controller safety guarantees and increase collision risk. A
safety-critical controller must therefore account for errors in the estimated
states of both vehicles, especially in collision-avoidance scenarios, in which
the ego vehicle often performs complex maneuvers that make the estimation of
surrounding-vehicle states nontrivial. Alai \emph{et al.} developed high-gain nonlinear observers
for obstacle-vehicle trajectory estimation and demonstrated the importance of an
accurate relative motion model for such tracking
\cite{AlaiZemoucheRajamani2024,AlaiSharmaAlexanderRajamani2024}. Sharma
\emph{et al.} subsequently showed that reliable obstacle tracking under complex
ego maneuvers requires a nonlinear relative motion model (NRMM) that explicitly
accounts for ego motion \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}. Their observer
differentiates that model into third-order companion coordinates, which reduces
the design to a single high-gain theorem but introduces derivatives of the ego
acceleration and yaw rate and therefore assumes that the ego vehicle has zero
body-frame jerk and zero yaw acceleration. These assumptions do not generally
hold in the collision-avoidance scenarios
considered here. This paper therefore develops an estimator that retains their
relative motion model but not its differentiated ego coordinates, so that
neither zero ego jerk nor zero ego yaw acceleration is assumed.

Beyond estimation uncertainty, collision-avoidance performance also depends
on the fidelity of the vehicle model and the generality of the maneuver
formulation. To retain online tractability, many existing controllers rely on
reduced-order models and restricted operating assumptions. Examples include
kinematic bicycle or locally linear lateral models whose validity is tied to
small slip, small lateral acceleration, or low-curvature highway operation
\cite{HeZengZhangSreenath2021,TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013,
BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022}. Other formulations use simplified acceleration
models or two-degree-of-freedom vehicle dynamics under small-angle and linear
tire-force approximations
\cite{ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021,AmmourOrjuelaBasset2022,
DingZhuangHuEtAl2024}. These abstractions are appropriate within their intended
operating envelopes, but evasive steering and braking couple the longitudinal
and lateral vehicle dynamics through tire forces and load transfer
\cite{GuoHuWang2016,FahmyGhanyBaumann2018,HeLiuLvJiLiu2019,
BealBoyd2019}. Moreover, the available tire force becomes strongly nonlinear
as the tires approach saturation
\cite{ParkGerdes2017,ForsAnistratovOlofssonNielsen2021,
ThompsonDallasGohBalachandran2024}.
A further limitation is that collision avoidance is often organized around a
prescribed set of maneuvers. Finite-state or discrete high-level controllers
may select among lane keeping, braking, overtaking, left or right lane changes,
and returning to the original lane
\cite{HeZengZhangSreenath2021,AmmourOrjuelaBasset2022,
ChenAksunGuvenc2025}. Other methods evaluate a predetermined single-lane-change
trajectory or a small number of straight- or low-curvature-road obstacle
configurations
\cite{HeLiuLvJiLiu2019,TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013,
BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022,DingZhuangHuEtAl2024}. Such formulations can be
effective in the scenarios for which they are designed, and dedicated methods
have also been developed for emergency avoidance on curved roads
\cite{LaiYang2023}. However, accommodating oblique cut-ins or interactions that
do not conform to predefined lane and vehicle roles may require additional
maneuver modes, transition logic, or scenario-specific redesign. These
limitations motivate a unified collision-avoidance controller based on a
dynamic vehicle model that continuously coordinates steering and
braking without prescribing the avoidance maneuver in advance.

Model fidelity and maneuver flexibility alone do not establish closed-loop
safety. Several MPC-based collision-avoidance controllers impose geometric or
dynamic constraints over a finite prediction horizon and demonstrate effective
evasive maneuvers, but do not prove that collision-free feasibility is
preserved after each receding-horizon update
\cite{TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013,
ForsAnistratovOlofssonNielsen2021,AmmourOrjuelaBasset2022,
ThompsonDallasGohBalachandran2024}. Control barrier functions (CBFs) provide
sufficient conditions for rendering a prescribed safe set forward invariant
\cite{AmesXuGrizzleTabuada2017}, but this guarantee remains conditional on the
existence of an admissible input satisfying the barrier condition. Conventional
CBF--QP safety filters enforce this condition pointwise and typically minimize
the instantaneous deviation from a nominal input. Consequently, feasibility at
the current state does not ensure that safe inputs remain available at
subsequent states \cite{ZengZhangLiSreenath2021}, and a pointwise filter may
fail to recognize an evasive trajectory that temporarily reduces longitudinal
separation while building sufficient lateral clearance, resulting in myopic or
deadlocked behavior \cite{BlackJankovicSharmaPanagou2023}. Predictive CBF and
MPC--CBF formulations mitigate this limitation by incorporating future system
evolution. Zeng \emph{et al.} imposed discrete-time CBF constraints over an MPC
horizon, but identified persistent feasibility as an open problem for a fixed
CBF decay rate and assumed perfect vehicle-position estimates in their racing
example \cite{ZengZhangSreenath2021}. Breeden and Panagou constructed a
path-based predictive CBF, whereas Wabersich and Zeilinger and Huang
\emph{et al.} derived predictive CBFs from finite-horizon optimal-control value
functions associated with terminal safe sets
\cite{BreedenPanagou2022,WabersichZeilinger2023,
HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025}. The presence of a prediction horizon alone,
however, does not guarantee that a collision-free optimization remains feasible
under repeated receding-horizon updates, particularly when state-estimation
uncertainty is considered.

This paper does not close that gap. It reports the estimator and the
controller of an intended integrated design, evaluated separately, and
separates what is certified from what is only measured. The estimator of
Section~\ref{sec:estimator} carries a continuous-time input-to-state
stability (ISS) certificate under declared sensor, model and
operating-domain bounds, and the shape of its target gains is chosen
inside that certificate by a semidefinite program that minimizes a bound
on the velocity-error variance caused by relative-position measurement
noise. The controller of Section~\ref{sec:control} uses no maneuver
template. At each sample it linearizes the dynamic bicycle model with
modified Fiala tires along a nonlinear rollout and solves two
second-order cone programs in strict priority order. The first minimizes
the relaxation of sampled predictive collision constraints, written with
distance-dual multipliers that are held fixed during the solve
\cite{LiZhangGuoLenzoGuo2023}. The second, without increasing that
relaxation, minimizes the violation of a single control-Lyapunov
decrease condition whose value function is the cost-to-go of a
path-guidance feedback; the same function is used during avoidance and
during the return to cruise. A potential-flow rollout around the moving
obstacle, guided by a time-aligned Gaussian lateral profile in the
manner of \cite{ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021}, initializes the linearization and
thereby fixes the side on which the obstacle is passed, and a freely
placed invariant cruise core closes the horizon. The ordering of the two
programs resembles the predictive barrier construction of
\cite{WabersichZeilinger2023}, and the scheme follows the
real-time-iteration idea of \cite{DiehlBockSchloder2005}, but its
constraints are sampled and affine, and it carries no certificate of
collision avoidance or recursive feasibility for the nonlinear vehicle.
The controller reported here is itself certainty-equivalent, uses static
axle loads, imposes no friction limit on its affine prediction and no
road boundary, and takes the passing side from its initialization.
Section~\ref{sec:results} therefore reports failures alongside
successes. In fourteen exact-state collision-threat runs the controller
ends twelve collision-free with cruise recovered, collides in one and is
interrupted by a numerical check in one, and its computation time
exceeds the control period. The failure mechanisms are analyzed because
they state what a certificate must control. The estimation theorem is
continuous-time, and the estimator and controller are not yet evaluated
together in closed loop.

\section{System Overview}

\begin{figure}[!t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.94\columnwidth]
        {driving_environment_curved_v08.pdf}
    \caption{Representative two-lane driving environment. The road-edge
    lines separate the travel lanes from curb-side emergency lanes, each wide
    enough to accommodate a vehicle, while the road centerline lies midway
    between the curb-defined boundaries. The ego vehicle follows the
    nominal path, with an obstacle vehicle cutting into the ego
    vehicle's lane from the adjacent lane.}
    \label{fig:driving_environment}
\end{figure}

We consider an ego vehicle traveling on a road whose drivable surface is
bounded by road curbs. A nominal cruise path is supplied as known route
geometry, while other vehicles travel within
or enter the sensed road environment, as illustrated in
Fig.~\ref{fig:driving_environment}. The reference is straight or of
constant curvature. The ego vehicle is equipped with a global
navigation satellite system (GNSS) receiver for global position and velocity
measurements and with an inertial measurement unit (IMU) and a gyroscope
for body-frame
acceleration and yaw-rate measurements, respectively. LiDAR provides
geometric measurements of road curbs together with
relative-position measurements of obstacle vehicles, while camera-based
perception detects obstacle vehicles. The problem addressed
in this paper is to design an integrated perception--estimation--control
system such that, in the absence of a predicted collision risk, the ego
vehicle follows the nominal path at a prescribed constant speed.
When continuing this nominal behavior is predicted to create a collision
risk, the system coordinates steering and longitudinal actuation to maintain
collision separation while keeping the ego-vehicle footprint within the
curb-defined drivable surface, and subsequently returns the vehicle to the
nominal path and prescribed speed once the risk has been resolved. Thus, the
curb-defined road boundary is a physical constraint of the intended system,
whereas the nominal path and reference speed are recoverable nominal
objectives. The controller developed below addresses the reference-following
and vehicle-separation part of this problem. It imposes no road-boundary
constraint: the curb perception module does not yet enter the control
law, and the avoidance maneuvers recorded in Section~\ref{sec:results}
leave the lane.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]
        {ego_obstacle_dynamics_v02.pdf}
    \caption{Ego--obstacle dynamics. The ego vehicle (blue) and an obstacle
    vehicle (orange) travel on courses that may lead to a collision. The
    vehicle frames are centered at the vehicle centers of mass, the yaw
    angles \(\psi_E\) and \(\psi_C\) orient them relative to the inertial
    \(X_I\) axis, the sideslip angles \(\beta_E\) and \(\beta_C\)
    separate the velocities \(V_E\) and \(V_C\) from the vehicle
    longitudinal axes, and the course angles \(\gamma_E\) and
    \(\gamma_C\) orient the velocities relative to the inertial \(X_I\)
    axis.}
    \label{fig:ego_obstacle_frames}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:ego_obstacle_frames} details the vehicle dynamics behind
the scenario of Fig.~\ref{fig:driving_environment}: an ego vehicle
with speed \(V_E\) and yaw angle \(\psi_E\) and an obstacle vehicle
with speed \(V_C\) and yaw angle \(\psi_C\) travel on courses that may
lead to a collision. The controller predicts with one ego model, the dynamic
bicycle with modified Fiala tires; the estimator uses only its kinematics and
the kinematic single-track relation between yaw rate, speed and sideslip,
so no cornering stiffness enters any estimation certificate. With
\(\bm p_E\in\R^2\) and \(\bm v_E\in\R^2\) the inertial position and
velocity,
\(\bm v^E=[v_{x,E}\;\;v_{y,E}]^{\trans}=R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_E\)
the body velocity, and
\begin{equation}
    R(\psi_E)=\begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\psi_E&-\sin\psi_E\\
        \sin\psi_E&\cos\psi_E
    \end{bmatrix}
    \label{eq:planar_rotation}
\end{equation}
the body-to-inertial rotation, the position kinematics are
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\bm p}_E=\bm v_E=R(\psi_E)\bm v^E .
    \label{eq:position_kinematics}
\end{equation}
The body-frame force and moment balances are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    m_E\dot v_{x,E}&=F_{xf}^{b}+F_{xr}-F_R(v_{x,E})
        +m_Ev_{y,E}\dot\psi_E,\\
    m_E\dot v_{y,E}&=F_{yf}^{b}+F_{yr}-m_Ev_{x,E}\dot\psi_E,\\
    I_{z,E}\ddot\psi_E&=l_{f,E}F_{yf}^{b}-l_{r,E}F_{yr},
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:bicycle_model}
\end{equation}
where $[F_{xf}^{b},F_{yf}^{b}]^{\trans}
=R(\delta_F)[F_{xf},F_{yf}]^{\trans}$ rotates the front wheel forces into
the body frame, and $F_R$ is the passive road load
\eqref{eq:road_load}. The slip convention and modified Fiala
forces are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \alpha_f=\operatorname{atan2}(v_{y,E}+l_{f,E}\dot\psi_E,\,v_{x,E})
        -\delta_F,\\
    \alpha_r=\operatorname{atan2}(v_{y,E}-l_{r,E}\dot\psi_E,\,v_{x,E}),\\
    F_{yf}=\Phi_f(\alpha_f;Q_f),\qquad F_{yr}=\Phi_r(\alpha_r;Q_r).
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:tire_forces}
\end{equation}
The model is used on the forward-speed domain
$v_{x,E}>v_{\rm f}>0$. For axle \(i\in\{f,r\}\), let \(t=\tan\alpha\), \(C_i>0\) be its
nominal cornering stiffness, and \(Q_i=\eta_i\mu_iF_{zi}\). Following
\cite[Sec.~II-A, eqs.~(7), (11) and (13)]{FahmyGhanyBaumann2018},
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_i(\alpha;Q_i)=
    \begin{cases}
    -C_it+\dfrac{C_i^2}{3Q_i}|t|t-\dfrac{C_i^3}{27Q_i^2}t^3,
       & |\alpha|<\alpha_{{\rm sl},i},\\[3pt]
    -Q_i\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha), & |\alpha|\geq\alpha_{{\rm sl},i},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:fiala_force}
\end{equation}
where \(\alpha_{{\rm sl},i}=\arctan(3Q_i/C_i)\),
\(\eta_i=\sqrt{1-\beta^2}\), and \(F_{xi}=\beta\mu_iF_{zi}\)
for \(|\beta|<1\). The same signed input \(\beta\) applies to both axles: negative values brake and positive values request throttle. Static loads are
\(F_{zf}=m_Egl_{r,E}/L\) and \(F_{zr}=m_Egl_{f,E}/L\), with
\(L=l_{f,E}+l_{r,E}\). Each axle represents an equivalent wheel pair.
Here \(m_E\) and \(I_{z,E}\) denote mass and yaw inertia; the lever arms
are \(l_{f,E},l_{r,E}\), and \(\delta_F\) is front steering.
We use the symmetric branch condition and
\cite[eq.~(13)]{FahmyGhanyBaumann2018}; the additional arctangent printed
in \cite[eq.~(12)]{FahmyGhanyBaumann2018} is inconsistent with it. At
\(|\beta|=1\), the force law is defined by
\(Q_i=0\), \(\Phi_i=0\), without evaluating its singular polynomial.
The derivatives of \(\Phi_i\) with respect to \((\alpha,\beta)\) are
unbounded at these endpoints; linearization points are kept strictly
inside the input interval.
The controller differentiates this force along its linearization
trajectory (Section~\ref{subsec:predictionModel}). The saturation branch
bounds the lateral force of the nonlinear model; the affine prediction
built from it carries no friction-limit constraint.
The speed, sideslip, and course of
Fig.~\ref{fig:ego_obstacle_frames} are
\begin{equation}
    V_E=\normtwo{\bm v_E},
    \qquad
    \beta_E=\arctan\frac{v_{y,E}}{v_{x,E}},
    \qquad
    \gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E .
    \label{eq:yaw_from_course}
\end{equation}
The obstacle vehicle has its own unobserved inputs. The tracker of
Section~\ref{subsec:targetTracker} and the obstacle prediction of
Section~\ref{subsec:predictionModel} both use the constant-acceleration,
constant-sideslip motion model of \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}.

The architecture that realizes this behavior comprises perception,
estimation, and control modules, as summarized in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}.
The perception module processes synchronized camera and LiDAR data jointly
for obstacle-vehicle detection and relative localization. LiDAR data are also
passed to a dedicated road-curb extraction component, whose output specifies
the curb-defined road boundary; the nominal path is known route geometry and
requires no extraction. The estimator combines the obstacle-relative
positions with the GNSS, IMU, and gyroscope measurements to estimate the motion
states of the ego vehicle and the single tracked obstacle vehicle. Each
estimate is accompanied by a componentwise error enclosure, valid for
the continuous-time observer under the declared sensor, model and
operating-domain premises. The present controller does not yet use this
enclosure: it treats the supplied states as exact, and in the trials of
Section~\ref{sec:results} it receives exact states and one obstacle
state at the first sample. The road-boundary branch represents the
intended perception interface; the controller imposes no road-boundary
constraint. The known nominal path supplies the control reference. At
each sampling instant, the controller linearizes the vehicle model along
a nonlinear rollout. It builds the collision constraints from the
obstacle state and the control-Lyapunov function from the reference path
and the prescribed speed, solves two convex programs in strict priority
order, and issues the steering and longitudinal actuation commands of
the second.

\subsection{Obstacle-Vehicle Perception}
Obstacle vehicles are detected in the camera images by a YOLO
detector \cite{RedmonDivvalaGirshickFarhadi2016}, which returns a
two-dimensional bounding box for each of them. The LiDAR point cloud
is then projected onto the image, as shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:obstacle_perception}(a). Each LiDAR point \(\bm q\) is
transformed into the camera frame and from there into pixel
coordinates,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \begin{bmatrix}X&Y&Z\end{bmatrix}^{\trans}
    =\bm R_{CL}\,\bm q+\bm t_{CL},\\
    \begin{bmatrix}u\\v\end{bmatrix}
    =\frac{f}{Z}
    \begin{bmatrix}X\\Y\end{bmatrix}
    +\begin{bmatrix}W/2\\H/2\end{bmatrix},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:lidar_camera_projection}
\end{equation}
where \((\bm R_{CL},\bm t_{CL})\) are the LiDAR-to-camera extrinsics,
\((X,Y,Z)\) are the point coordinates in the camera frame with \(Z>0\)
the optical depth, \(f\) is the focal length, and \(W\) and
\(H\) are the image width and height. The LiDAR points whose pixel coordinates \((u,v)\) fall
inside a detected bounding box are associated with that vehicle, and
the ground and other extraneous points among them are removed,
leaving the points reflected from the obstacle vehicle.

In this paper, the physical outline of a vehicle is regarded as a
rectangle in the top view. A rectangle is therefore fitted to the outermost
of the retained points, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:obstacle_perception}(b), and
the center of the fitted rectangle yields the measured relative
position of the obstacle vehicle,
\begin{equation}
    \bm\rho=\begin{bmatrix}\rho_x&\rho_y\end{bmatrix}^{\trans}\in\R^2 ,
    \label{eq:perception_relative_position}
\end{equation}
expressed in the ego-vehicle frame.

\subsection{Road-Curb Extraction and Tracking}

\subsubsection{Local Geometric Feature Analysis}
A curb is a step of a few centimetres in an otherwise smooth road
surface, so the local geometry of the LiDAR returns changes abruptly
where the beams cross it. Four local geometric features are considered
to capture this discontinuity---adjacent-ring compression, vertical
step, radial jump, and local shape. In the following, \(\bm p_{i,j}\)
and \(z_{i,j}\) denote the horizontal position and height of the
return at elevation ring \(i\) and azimuth \(j\), and
\(d_{i,j}=\|\bm p_{i,j}\|\) its horizontal range.

\paragraph{Adjacent-ring compression}
Flat ground predicts the horizontal spacing between consecutive rings
from the sensor height and beam angles alone, and a raised curb face
compresses the spacing \(\Delta_{i,j}\) observed between ring \(i\)
and its upper neighbor below that prediction \(\hat\Delta_{i}\),
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_{i,j}
    =\frac{\hat\Delta_{i}-\Delta_{i,j}}{\hat\Delta_{i}} .
    \label{eq:curb_ring_compression}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Vertical step}
The height of the curb face appears directly as a step between
same-ring neighbors,
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{i,j}
    =\max\bigl(|z_{i,j}-z_{i,j-1}|,\,|z_{i,j+1}-z_{i,j}|\bigr).
    \label{eq:curb_vertical_step}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Radial jump}
On flat ground the horizontal range varies smoothly with azimuth,
whereas a beam climbing the curb face is pulled inward, which breaks
the smoothness captured by the second difference
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_{i,j}
    =|d_{i,j-1}-2d_{i,j}+d_{i,j+1}| .
    \label{eq:curb_radial_jump}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Local shape}
On the road surface a ring sweeps an arc whose chord is perpendicular
to the line of sight, whereas along a curb the returns align with the
road edge, so the normalized projection of the chord of the same-ring
neighbors onto the line of sight
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{i,j}
    =\frac{\bigl|\bm p_{i,j}^{\trans}
        (\bm p_{i,j+1}-\bm p_{i,j-1})\bigr|}
      {\|\bm p_{i,j}\|\,\|\bm p_{i,j+1}-\bm p_{i,j-1}\|}
    \label{eq:curb_local_shape}
\end{equation}
rises where the ring turns along the curb.

\subsubsection{Curb Fitting}
The road heading \(\varphi\) is estimated from the single longest
near-linear structure among the candidates and is held near a prior
propagated with the ego motion. It defines a road-aligned frame in
which each curb is the quadratic
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{b}}(x)=c_{2}x^{2}+c_{1}x+c_{0},
    \qquad x\in[x_{\min},x_{\max}],
    \label{eq:curb_quadratic}
\end{equation}
one curve per side over a finite forward domain. The coefficients are
fitted to the side's candidate points by least squares.

\subsubsection{Curb Tracking}
A single-frame fit can latch onto a structure that merely resembles a curb and jump far from the tracked
geometry, so the fit of frame \(k\), with coefficients \(c_{i,k}\), is
admitted only through an amplitude-limiting filter on the coefficient
increments,
\begin{equation}
    |c_{i,k}-c_{i,k-1}|\le T_i,
    \qquad i\in\{0,1,2\},
    \label{eq:curb_amplitude_limit}
\end{equation}
and a fit violating any bound leaves the frame to the prediction. The
curve itself is tracked through three points \(\bm p_s\) placed on it
at constant longitudinal stations \(x_s\). Between frames each point
is propagated by the measured ego motion,
\begin{equation}
    \bm p_s^-=A\,\bm p_s+\bm b,
    \qquad
    P_s^-=A P_s A^{\trans}+Q,
    \label{eq:curb_point_prediction}
\end{equation}
where \((A,\bm b)\) is the rigid transform from the preceding to the
current road frame, \(P_s\) is the point covariance, and \(Q\) is the
process noise. The admitted fit, evaluated at the same stations,
yields the measured points \(\bm z_s\) with noise covariance \(R\),
and each tracked point follows the Kalman update
\begin{equation}
    K_s=P_s^-\,(P_s^-+R)^{-1},
    \qquad
    \bm p_s=\bm p_s^-+K_s(\bm z_s-\bm p_s^-).
    \label{eq:curb_point_update}
\end{equation}

\section{Estimator Design}
\label{sec:estimator}

The ego body-velocity observer is designed first and the target tracker
second. Their dynamics form a two-stage cascade; the parallel yaw observer
and inertial position output are used for reconstruction and do not feed
this cascade. The measurements are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm y_p&=\bm p_E+\bm n_p, &\quad
    \bm v_m&=\bm v_E+\bm n_v,\\
    \bm a_m&=\bm a_E+\bm n_a, &\quad
    u_3&=\dot\psi_E+d_\omega,\\
    \bm y_R&=\bm\rho+\bm n_R,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:ego_measurements}
\end{equation}
with \(\bm a_E=R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\ddot{\bm p}_E\) the acceleration resolved
in the ego frame, \(\bm\rho\) the ego-frame relative position
\eqref{eq:target_coordinates}, whose measurement \(\bm y_R\) is the
perception output \eqref{eq:perception_relative_position}, and uniform
bounds \(\normtwo{\bm n_p}\leq\bar n_p\),
\(\normtwo{\bm n_v}\leq\bar n_v\), \(\normtwo{\bm n_a}\leq\bar n_a\),
\(|d_\omega|\leq\bar n_g\), \(\normtwo{\bm n_R}\leq\bar n_R\). Inertial
biases are compensated upstream, so no observer carries a bias state.

Two constructions of \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026} are replaced. Their
translational observer differentiates the ego kinematics into third-order
companion chains, which requires \(\dot{\bm a}_E\) and \(\ddot\psi_E\) and
therefore assumes both vanish; here \(\bm a_m\) and \(u_3\) remain
exogenous inputs of every stage and nothing is differentiated. Their yaw
stage switches between a heading inferred from the acceleration direction
and the course angle, and reading the course as the yaw is exact only at
\(\beta_E=0\) because \(\gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E\); here the
same relation supplies a direct body-velocity measurement and, when
informative, a certified heading correspondence for the parallel yaw observer.

\begin{assumption}
\label{asm:ego}
\(0\leq V_E|_{\min}\leq V_E\leq V_E|_{\max}\),
$v_{x,E}\geq V_E\cos\beta_{\rm dom}$, and
\(|\dot\psi_E|\leq\bar\omega_E\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}
\label{asm:singleTrack}
The ego kinematic single-track relation holds with bounded mismatch,
\begin{equation}
    \dot\psi_E=\frac{V_E\sin\beta_E}{l_{r,E}}+d_{\rm st},
    \qquad|d_{\rm st}|\leq\bar d_{\rm st},
    \label{eq:single_track_mismatch}
\end{equation}
and \(|\beta_E|\leq\beta_{\rm dom}<\pi/2\).
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{asm:singleTrack} bounds unmodeled rear-axle slip. The
identification of course and yaw that it replaces corresponds to
\(\beta_E\equiv0\).

\subsection{Certified Course--Yaw Correspondence}
\label{subsec:courseCertificate}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:course}
Let Assumption~\ref{asm:singleTrack} hold and
\(V_m=\normtwo{\bm v_m}>\bar n_v\), put
\(\underline V=V_m-\bar n_v\), and suppose \(|l_{r,E}u_3|\leq V_m\).
Define
\begin{equation}
    \hat\beta=\arcsin\frac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m},
    \qquad
    y_F=\angle\bm v_m-\hat\beta .
    \label{eq:course_definition}
\end{equation}
Then, with
\begin{equation}
    \bar e_{\sin}
    =\frac{l_{r,E}}{\underline V}
    \Bigl(\bar n_g+\bar d_{\rm st}+\frac{|u_3|\bar n_v}{V_m}\Bigr),
    \label{eq:sine_error_bound}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \bar e_\beta=\max_{\pm}\Bigl|
    \arcsin\bigl(\Pi\bigl[\tfrac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m}\pm\bar e_{\sin}\bigr]\bigr)
    -\hat\beta\Bigr|,
    \label{eq:sideslip_error}
\end{equation}
where \(\Pi\) clips to \([-\sin\beta_{\rm dom},\sin\beta_{\rm dom}]\), one has
\(|\beta_E-\hat\beta|\leq\bar e_\beta\) and
\begin{equation}
    |y_F-\psi_E|\leq B_F:=\arcsin\frac{\bar n_v}{V_m}+\bar e_\beta .
    \label{eq:course_certificate}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
By \eqref{eq:single_track_mismatch},
\(\sin\beta_E=l_{r,E}(u_3-d_\omega-d_{\rm st})/V_E\), so
\begin{equation}
    \Bigl|\sin\beta_E-\frac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m}\Bigr|
    \leq l_{r,E}\Bigl[\frac{|d_\omega|+|d_{\rm st}|}{V_E}
    +|u_3|\frac{|V_m-V_E|}{V_EV_m}\Bigr],
    \label{eq:sine_derivation}
\end{equation}
and \(|V_m-V_E|\leq\bar n_v\), \(V_E\geq\underline V\) give
\eqref{eq:sine_error_bound}. Since \(|\sin\beta_E|\leq\sin\beta_{\rm dom}\),
the true sine lies in the clipped interval, and \(\arcsin\) is increasing
on it, which gives \eqref{eq:sideslip_error}. For the course, \(\bm v_E\)
lies in the disk of radius \(\bar n_v\) about \(\bm v_m\); the disk
excludes the origin, so \(\bm v_E\) lies in its tangent cone and
\(|\angle\bm v_m-\gamma_E|\leq\arcsin(\bar n_v/V_m)\). Subtracting
\(\gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E\) yields \eqref{eq:course_certificate}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The correspondence is used only where these informativeness conditions
hold. Otherwise it supplies the whole circle as a yaw enclosure. Neither
case changes the body-velocity innovation developed next, which remains
defined at standstill.

\subsection{Ego Observers}
\label{subsec:egoObserver}

With $J=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$,
$\nabla_u\bm z=\dot{\bm z}+uJ\bm z$, and
$\bm v^E=R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_E$, define
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \phi(V,w)=\sqrt{\max\{V^2-w^2,V^2\cos^2\beta_{\rm dom}\}},\\
    \bm y_v=[\phi(V_m,l_{r,E}u_3),\ l_{r,E}u_3]^{\trans},\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm v}^E=\bm a_m+k_v(\bm y_v-\hat{\bm v}^E).
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:ego_observer}
\end{equation}
On the true forward cone, $\phi(V_E,v_{y,E})=v_{x,E}$. The extension
remains defined for every measured speed, including zero, and its lateral
component is not clipped. Thus
$y_{v,2}-v_{y,E}=l_{r,E}(d_\omega+d_{\rm st})$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:egoRows}
Let $\bm e_v=\hat{\bm v}^E-\bm v^E$, $k_v>0$, and
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    C_\omega(k)=\max_{V\in\{V_E|_{\min},V_E|_{\max}\}}
    \Bigl[(V\sin\beta_{\rm dom}+kl_{r,E}\tan\beta_{\rm dom})^2\\
        +(kl_{r,E}-V\cos\beta_{\rm dom})^2\Bigr]^{1/2}.
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:common_gyro_bound}
\end{equation}
Under Assumptions~\ref{asm:ego}--\ref{asm:singleTrack},
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    D^+\normtwo{\bm e_v}\leq-k_v\normtwo{\bm e_v}+\bar\delta_v,\\
    \bar\delta_v=\bar n_a+k_v\sec\beta_{\rm dom}\,\bar n_v
      +C_\omega(k_v)\bar n_g
      +k_vl_{r,E}\sec\beta_{\rm dom}\,\bar d_{\rm st}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:ego_rows}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Put $\nu=V_m-V_E$, so $|\nu|\leq\bar n_v$. Integrating the
piecewise derivatives of $\phi$ along the segment between
$(V_E,v_{y,E})$ and $(V_m,l_{r,E}u_3)$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \bm y_v-\bm v^E=\alpha\bm e_1\nu
        +l_{r,E}\bm g(\zeta)(d_\omega+d_{\rm st}),\\
    \bm g(\zeta)=[-\zeta,1]^{\trans},\\
    \cos\beta_{\rm dom}\leq\alpha\leq\sec\beta_{\rm dom},\qquad
    |\zeta|\leq\tan\beta_{\rm dom}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:velocity_measurement_error}
\end{equation}
Continuity covers branch crossings and the origin. Substituting before
taking norms yields
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \dot{\bm e}_v={}&(-k_vI-u_3J)\bm e_v+\bm n_a+k_v\alpha\bm e_1\nu\\
        &+(k_vl_{r,E}\bm g(\zeta)-J\bm v^E)d_\omega
          +k_vl_{r,E}\bm g(\zeta)d_{\rm st}.
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:velocity_error_derivation}
\end{equation}
The common gyro column has components
$[v_{y,E}-k_vl_{r,E}\zeta,\ k_vl_{r,E}-v_{x,E}]^{\trans}$.
Maximizing its norm over $\zeta$ and the forward cone, then using
convexity of its squared bound in speed, gives
\eqref{eq:common_gyro_bound}. The rotation is skew and contributes zero
to the norm derivative. The remaining terms give \eqref{eq:ego_rows}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The same gyro error enters measurement and propagation. Bounding those
terms separately would discard their cancellation; no statistical
independence is used in Lemma~\ref{lem:egoRows}. In particular, yaw error
does not enter the velocity comparison.

Yaw and inertial position are estimated in parallel:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \dot{\hat\psi}_E&=u_3+\chi_m k_\psi
        \operatorname{wrap}(y_F-\hat\psi_E),\\
    \dot{\hat{\bm p}}_E&=\bm v_m+k_p(\bm y_p-\hat{\bm p}_E),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:parallel_outputs}
\end{equation}
where $\chi_m=1$ only when the course correspondence is informative.
While the innovation stays on one branch of the wrap, the yaw error
\(e_\psi=\hat\psi_E-\psi_E\) obeys
\(\dot e_\psi=-k_\psi e_\psi+d_\omega+k_\psi(y_F-\psi_E)\) with
\(|y_F-\psi_E|\leq B_F\); during uninformative intervals only gyro
propagation is available. An independent orientation set $\Psi$ is
propagated with the gyro-error integral and intersected with each
informative circular arc from Lemma~\ref{lem:course}. Disconnected
components are retained, and the published bound is
$B_\psi=\sup_{\theta\in\Psi}d_{S^1}(\hat\psi_E,\theta)$ about the
actual observer estimate. An empty intersection invalidates orientation
outputs; it does not reset the observer. The position error
\(\bm e_p=\hat{\bm p}_E-\bm p_E\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    D^+\normtwo{\bm e_p}\leq-k_p\normtwo{\bm e_p}+\bar n_v+k_p\bar n_p,\\
    \limsup_{t\to\infty}\normtwo{\bm e_p}\leq\bar n_p+\bar n_v/k_p.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:position_error_bound}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Covariant Target Tracker}
\label{subsec:targetTracker}

The target model of \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}---constant scalar
acceleration and constant sideslip, hence constant curvature
\(\kappa=\sin\beta_C/l_{r,C}\)---is retained in covariant companion
coordinates
\begin{equation}
    \bm\rho=R_E^{\trans}(\bm p_C-\bm p_E),\quad
    \bm q=R_E^{\trans}\bm v_C,\quad
    \bm s=R_E^{\trans}\bm a_C,
    \label{eq:target_coordinates}
\end{equation}
with \(R_E=R(\psi_E)\). With \(\omega_E=\dot\psi_E\), the exact dynamics
in these coordinates are the third-order chain
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm\rho=\bm q-\bm v^E,\quad
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm q=\bm s,\quad
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm s=\Phi(\bm q,\bm s),\\
    \Phi=-\Omega^2\bm q+3A\Omega J\frac{\bm q}{V},\\
    V=\normtwo{\bm q},\quad
    A=\frac{\bm q^{\trans}\bm s}{V},\quad
    \Omega=\frac{(J\bm q)^{\trans}\bm s}{V^2}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_chain}
\end{equation}
Only \(\bm v^E\) and \(\omega_E\) enter, and the only extra terms are those
of the rotating frame; \(A\) and \(\Omega\) are reconstructions, not states.
The declared operating set is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    a\leq\normtwo{\bm q}\leq b,\quad
    |A|\leq A_{\max},\quad
    \normtwo{\bm\rho}\leq\rho_{\max},\\
    |\Omega|\leq\Omega_{\max}=\frac{b\sin\beta_{\max}}{l_{r,C}},\quad
    \normtwo{\bm s}\leq S=\sqrt{A_{\max}^2+b^2\Omega_{\max}^2}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_domain}
\end{equation}

Here \(a\) and \(b\) are the target speed bounds \(V_C|_{\min}\) and
\(V_C|_{\max}\). Estimates may peak outside \eqref{eq:target_domain}, so the observer
evaluates the extension \(\Phi_e\) obtained by replacing \(\bm q,\bm s\) in
\(\Phi\) by their ball projections onto radii \(b,S\), clipping \(A\) and
\(\Omega\) to their bounds, and using \(\max(\normtwo{\bm q},a)\) in every
denominator. It agrees with \(\Phi\) on \eqref{eq:target_domain} and is
defined on \(\R^4\).

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:lipschitz}
With
\(c_A=S/a\), \(c_\Omega=S\max(a^{-2},2b^{-2})\),
\(c_{Q\Omega}=S\max(a^{-1},2b^{-1})\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    L_q=\Omega_{\max}^2+2\Omega_{\max}c_{Q\Omega}
        +3\Omega_{\max}\Bigl(\frac{A_{\max}}a+c_A\Bigr)\\
        +3A_{\max}c_\Omega,\\
    L_s=\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}
        0&2\Omega_{\max}\\3\Omega_{\max}&3A_{\max}/a
        \end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_2,
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:lipschitz_constants}
\end{equation}
\(\Phi_e\) satisfies, for all \((\bm q,\bm s),(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \normtwo{\Phi_e(\bm q,\bm s)-\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})}\\
    \leq L_q\normtwo{\bm q-\hat{\bm q}}+L_s\normtwo{\bm s-\hat{\bm s}} .
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:global_lipschitz}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Projections and clips are nonexpansive, so they only decrease derivative
bounds, and \(\Phi_e\) is continuous and piecewise differentiable; it
suffices to bound its almost-everywhere Jacobians and integrate along the
segment joining the two arguments. Differentiating in the radial and
tangential basis at fixed \(\bm s\), with \(r=\normtwo{\bm q}\),
\(\bm Q\) the projected velocity and \(A_e\), \(\Omega_e\) the clipped
reconstructions that \(\Phi_e\) evaluates, gives
\(|D_qA_e|\leq S/\max(r,a)\) and \(|D_q\Omega_e|\leq S/\max(r,a)^2\) for
\(r\leq b\) and \(|D_qA_e|\leq bS/r^2\),
\(|D_q\Omega_e|\leq2bS/r^3\) for \(r\geq b\), whose suprema over \(r>0\) are
\(c_A\) and \(c_\Omega\), while
\(\normtwo{\bm Q}|D_q\Omega_e|\leq c_{Q\Omega}\).
Collecting the four terms of \(\Phi_e\) yields \(L_q\). At fixed
\(\bm q\), the entrywise absolute values of the acceleration Jacobian in the
basis aligned with \(\bm q\) are dominated by the displayed matrix, and its
spectral norm dominates the induced norm; the case \(\bm q=0\) follows by
continuity.
\end{IEEEproof}

The tracker is the covariant high-gain chain
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm\rho}&=\hat{\bm q}-\hat{\bm v}^E
        +l_1\omega\,(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm\rho}),\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm q}&=\hat{\bm s}
        +l_2\omega^2(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm\rho}),\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm s}&=\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})
        +l_3\omega^3(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm\rho}).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_observer}
\end{equation}
Let \(A_c\) be the integrator-chain matrix, \(C=\bm e_1^{\trans}\), and
\(A_l=A_c-\bm lC\). The shape \(\bm l\) and the bandwidth \(\omega\) are
selected in Section~\ref{subsec:gainSelection}; the certificate below
holds for every pair that satisfies its matrix inequality.
For the estimate-minus-truth target error $\bm e_T$, set
\begin{equation}
    \bm\epsilon=\diag(I_2,\omega^{-1}I_2,\omega^{-2}I_2)\bm e_T,
    \qquad
    W_T=\sqrt{\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(P\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon}.
    \label{eq:target_metric}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:dissipation}
Let $P\succ0$, \(\bm b_P=P\bm e_3\), \(t_q,t_s>0\), and
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    M={}&-\omega(A_l^{\trans}P+PA_l)
       -t_q\frac{L_q^2}{\omega^2}\bm e_2\bm e_2^{\trans}\\
       &-t_sL_s^2\bm e_3\bm e_3^{\trans}
       -(t_q^{-1}+t_s^{-1})\bm b_P\bm b_P^{\trans}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:dissipation_matrix}
\end{equation}
If \(M\succeq2\lambda_TP\) then \(\dot W_T\leq-\lambda_TW_T\) in the
absence of exogenous error inputs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
In the coordinate \(\bm\epsilon\) the injection block becomes
\(\omega A_l\otimes I_2\), and the common rotation \(-u_3(I_3\otimes J)\)
is skew and contributes zero to
\(\tfrac{d}{dt}\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(P\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon\). The injection contributes
$\omega\bm\epsilon^{\trans}[(A_l^{\trans}P+PA_l)\otimes I_2]\bm\epsilon$.
Splitting $\Delta\Phi/\omega^2$ into its velocity and acceleration
increments, \eqref{eq:global_lipschitz} gives respective bounds
$(L_q/\omega)\normtwo{\bm\epsilon_2}$ and
$L_s\normtwo{\bm\epsilon_3}$. Each enters the derivative through
$2\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(\bm b_P\otimes I_2)$. Applying
\(2\bm z^{\trans}\bm d\leq\normtwo{\bm z}^2/t+t\normtwo{\bm d}^2\) once per
channel gives
\(\tfrac{d}{dt}W_T^2\leq-\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(M\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon
\leq-2\lambda_TW_T^2\).
\end{IEEEproof}

\subsection{Cascade ISS}
\label{subsec:cascade}

All core couplings are feedforward. Cauchy--Schwarz in the metric
\eqref{eq:target_metric} gives
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    g_{Tv}=\sqrt{P_{11}},\qquad
    g_R=\omega\sqrt{\bm l^{\trans}P\bm l},\\
    g_\omega=\sqrt{\bm h^{\trans}|P|\bm h},\qquad
    g_\Phi=\sqrt{P_{33}}/\omega^2,\\
    \bm h=[\rho_{\max},\ b/\omega,\ S/\omega^2]^{\trans},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_disturbance_coefficients}
\end{equation}
where $|P|$ is entrywise. Declared scalar-acceleration and curvature rates
add a forcing bounded by
$\bar\nu_\Phi=(\bar{\dot A}^2+b^4\bar{\dot\kappa}^2)^{1/2}$ to the last
chain equation. Lemmas~\ref{lem:egoRows} and \ref{lem:dissipation} give
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \bm\chi=[\normtwo{\bm e_v},\ W_T]^{\trans},\qquad
    D^+\bm\chi\leq H\bm\chi+\bm d,\\
    H=\begin{bmatrix}-k_v&0\\g_{Tv}&-\lambda_T\end{bmatrix},\qquad
    \bm d=\begin{bmatrix}\bar\delta_v\\
    g_\omega\bar n_g+g_R\bar n_R+g_\Phi\bar\nu_\Phi\end{bmatrix}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:cascade_comparison}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Cascade ISS]
\label{thm:cascadeISS}
Let Assumptions~\ref{asm:ego}--\ref{asm:singleTrack}, the hypotheses of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:egoRows}, \ref{lem:lipschitz} and \ref{lem:dissipation},
the true target domain \eqref{eq:target_domain} and the declared continuous
sensor and model-error bounds hold, with $k_v,\lambda_T>0$. Then $H$ is
Metzler and Hurwitz, and $\bm c=-H^{-\trans}\bm 1>0$ gives the linear
copositive ISS function $V_c=\bm c^{\trans}\bm\chi$. For any
$\bm z_0\geq\bm\chi(0)$,
\begin{equation}
    \bm\chi(t)\leq\bm z(t)
    =e^{Ht}\bm z_0+\int_0^t e^{H(t-\tau)}\bm d\,d\tau,
    \label{eq:comparison_solution}
\end{equation}
and $\limsup_{t\to\infty}\bm\chi(t)\leq-H^{-1}\bm d$. The physical
component bounds are
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm e_{T,i}}\leq c_{T,i}W_T,\qquad
    \bm c_T=\diag(1,\omega,\omega^2)\sqrt{\diag(P^{-1})}.
    \label{eq:component_extraction}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
$H$ is lower triangular with negative diagonal and nonnegative
off-diagonal entries. Thus $e^{Ht}\geq0$ and $-H^{-1}\geq0$, which give
\eqref{eq:comparison_solution}, the ultimate bound and $\bm c>0$.
Since $\bm c^{\trans}H=-\bm1^{\trans}$,
$D^+V_c\leq-\bm1^{\trans}\bm\chi+\bm c^{\trans}\bm d$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:component_extraction} is the ellipsoid support in
$P\otimes I_2$ after undoing the position normalization.
\end{IEEEproof}

The core contains two ego-velocity states and six states for the single target. Its
certificate requires neither informative yaw measurements nor a yaw
innovation chart. The shared gyro also couples the two core stages;
\eqref{eq:cascade_comparison} uses a triangular norm bound and does not
claim to retain all cross-stage cancellations. Orientation and position
remain separately certified outputs.

\subsection{Gain Selection}
\label{subsec:gainSelection}

With $T_{\rm dom}=\rho_{\max}/(V_E|_{\max}+V_C|_{\max})$, the scalar
gain preference is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \min_{k>0}k\quad\text{s.t.}\quad e^{-kT_{\rm dom}}\leq0.05,\\
    k_\psi=k_v=k_p=\frac{\ln20}{T_{\rm dom}}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:scalar_gain_rule}
\end{equation}
This is a transit-time preference, not an optimum of the common-error
bound in \eqref{eq:ego_rows}. Increasing $k_v$ changes the combined gyro
coefficient and need not improve that bound.

The target gains are decision variables of a semidefinite program that
contains the certificate. In physical coordinates ($\omega=1$) let
$\bm L\in\R^3$ be the gain vector, $A_L=A_c-\bm LC$ and $\bm Y=P\bm L$,
and put
$\Xi=A_c^{\trans}P+PA_c-\bm YC-C^{\trans}\bm Y^{\trans}$, which equals
$A_L^{\trans}P+PA_L$. The required decay rate of the target metric is
the decay floor $\lambda_T=1/T_{\rm dom}$, one $e$-folding per transit
time, a weaker requirement than \eqref{eq:scalar_gain_rule}. With
$\Delta_\Phi=\diag(0,t_qL_q^2,t_sL_s^2)$, solve
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \min_{P\succ0,\,\bm Y,\,t_q,t_s>0,\,\gamma}\ \gamma
    \quad\text{s.t.}\\
    \begin{bmatrix}
    \Xi+2\lambda_TP+\Delta_\Phi&P\bm e_3&P\bm e_3\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&-t_q&0\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&0&-t_s
    \end{bmatrix}\prec0,\\
    \Xi+\bm e_2\bm e_2^{\trans}\prec0,\qquad
    \begin{bmatrix}\gamma&\bm Y^{\trans}\\\bm Y&P\end{bmatrix}\succeq0 .
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:noise_shape_lmi}
\end{equation}
The first inequality is the Schur form of
Lemma~\ref{lem:dissipation} at $\omega=1$, so $\bm L=P^{-1}\bm Y$
already satisfies the dissipation inequality at the decay floor. The
second makes $P$ an upper bound of the observability Gramian of
$(A_L,\bm e_2^{\trans})$ \cite{BoydElGhaouiFeronBalakrishnan1994}. For
the linear error equation $\dot{\bm e}=A_L\bm e+\bm Ln$ driven by white
output noise $n$ of unit intensity, the stationary variance of the
velocity error is therefore at most
$\bm L^{\trans}P\bm L\leq\gamma$. All conditions are linear in
$(P,\bm Y,t_q,t_s,\gamma)$. One metric serves the Gramian and the
dissipation inequality, so $\gamma$ is an upper bound on the smallest
variance attainable among certified gains, not that minimum. The
variance is a performance preference. It ignores the nonlinear term and
the sampled realization, and it is not a deterministic error bound.

The returned gains are written as
$\bm L=[l_1\omega_0,\,l_2\omega_0^2,\,l_3\omega_0^3]^{\trans}$ with
$\omega_0=\min_i|\operatorname{Re}\lambda_i(A_L)|$ the slowest
closed-loop rate, which fixes a shape $\bm l$ of unit slowest rate. For
each candidate $\omega>1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ in the search domain the
certificate metric is then solved anew:
minimize $\operatorname{tr}P$ over $P\succeq I$ and $t_q,t_s>0$ subject
to
\begin{equation}
    \begin{bmatrix}
    Z&P\bm e_3&P\bm e_3\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&-t_q&0\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&0&-t_s
    \end{bmatrix}\preceq0,
    \label{eq:free_metric_lmi}
\end{equation}
where
$Z=\omega(A_l^{\trans}P+PA_l)+2\lambda_TP
+\diag(0,t_qL_q^2/\omega^2,t_sL_s^2)$.
Its Schur complement is Lemma~\ref{lem:dissipation}. The normalization
fixes the homogeneous scale while leaving the metric free; the recovered
matrix is checked in the original inequality with numerical reserves.
A bracketed scalar search then minimizes the largest of the three
ultimate bounds $c_{T,i}(-H^{-1}\bm d)_2$ of
\eqref{eq:component_extraction}, each divided by its domain scale
$\rho_{\max}$, $b$ or $S$. This gives a feasible local bandwidth
selection, not a global joint optimum. Because the search may move the
bandwidth away from $\omega_0$, the variance bound $\gamma$ applies to
the gains returned by \eqref{eq:noise_shape_lmi}, not to the rescaled
gains. The programs are solved with
YALMIP \cite{Lofberg2004} and SeDuMi \cite{Sturm1999}. Neither
simulation errors nor the sensor sample period enter the design.

\subsection{Error Enclosure}
\label{subsec:enclosure}

A controller that tightens its constraints needs a transient error
radius at each observation. For the continuous observer, the same
comparison system supplies it: \eqref{eq:comparison_solution} holds for
all \(t\), so \eqref{eq:component_extraction} gives

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:enclosure}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:cascadeISS} and any
\(\bm z_0\geq\bm\chi(0)\),
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm\rho(t)-\hat{\bm\rho}(t)}\leq B_\rho(t):=c_{T,1}z_2(t),
    \quad c_{T,1}=\sqrt{(P^{-1})_{11}},
    \label{eq:relative_position_bound}
\end{equation}
and \(\limsup_{t\to\infty}B_\rho\leq c_{T,1}(-H^{-1}\bm d)_2\). An initial
metric bound follows from componentwise initial bounds \(\bm b_{T0}\) as
\(W_T(0)\leq\bigl((D\bm b_{T0})^{\trans}|P|(D\bm b_{T0})\bigr)^{1/2}\) with
\(D=\diag(1,\omega^{-1},\omega^{-2})\).
\end{corollary}

Inertial reconstruction of the relative vector costs one further yaw term,
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm p_C-\bm p_E-R(\hat\psi_E)\hat{\bm\rho}}
    \leq B_\rho+2\bar R\sin\frac{\min(B_\psi,\pi)}2 ,
    \label{eq:inertial_relative_bound}
\end{equation}
\(\bar R=\min(\rho_{\max},\normtwo{\hat{\bm\rho}})\), and the analogous
rotation bounds convert the remaining components of
\eqref{eq:component_extraction} into the componentwise ego and target
enclosures \(\bm b_E(t)\), \(\bm b_T(t)\). These certify the current
estimate; they are not predictions of future observer output. The
controller of Section~\ref{sec:control} is certainty-equivalent and does
not yet consume them. The digital
runtime additionally accounts for held measurements, relative-position
prediction and
outages in a separate finite-time enclosure. The continuous ISS theorem
does not establish exponential stability of the sampled fourth-order
Runge--Kutta (RK4) realization
or a directed-rounding certificate for its computed bounds.

\section{Control Design}
\label{sec:control}

In a normal sample two convex programs are solved on one affine model.
The first minimizes the relaxation of the sampled collision constraints.
The second keeps that relaxation and minimizes the violation of a
control-Lyapunov decrease condition, and its first input is applied. No
maneuver template is prescribed and no backup command exists; the same
programs are solved during avoidance and during the return to cruise.
The controller is certainty-equivalent: it treats the supplied ego and
obstacle states as exact and does not consume the enclosure of
Section~\ref{subsec:enclosure}. The obstacle vehicle of this section is
the target of Section~\ref{sec:estimator}.

\subsection{Prediction Model}
\label{subsec:predictionModel}

The subscript $E$ is dropped from the ego state variables in this
section. The controller state and input are
\begin{equation}
    \bm x=[p_x,\,p_y,\,\psi,\,v_x,\,v_y,\,r]^{\trans},\qquad
    \bm u=[\delta_F,\,\beta]^{\trans},
    \label{eq:controller_state}
\end{equation}
the inertial pose, the body velocities and the yaw rate $r=\dot\psi$,
and the front steering angle and signed longitudinal force ratio.
Equations \eqref{eq:position_kinematics}--\eqref{eq:fiala_force} define
$\dot{\bm x}=f(\bm x,\bm u)$ for $v_x>v_{\rm f}$ and $|\beta|\leq1$.
Passive flat-road load in still air is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    F_R(v)={}&\tfrac12\rho_{\rm air}C_dA_fv|v|\\
       &+m_Eg(q_0+q_1|v|+q_4|v|^4)\tanh(v/v_{\rm tr}),
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:road_load}
\end{equation}
with air density $\rho_{\rm air}$, drag coefficient $C_d$, frontal area
$A_f$, nonnegative rolling coefficients $q_0,q_1,q_4$ and
$v_{\rm tr}>0$. The model uses static axle loads and excludes wheel
inertia, wind, grade and dynamic load transfer.

An input is held for the sample period $T_s$. The prediction map
$F(\bm x,\bm u)$ is one classical RK4 step
of $f$ over $T_s$. Its Jacobians $D_xF$ and $D_uF$ are obtained by
propagating the variational equations through the same step, with
analytic tire-force derivatives in both slip angle and force ratio.
Linearization points are kept strictly inside $|\beta|<1$, where those
derivatives exist. Collision constraints are also imposed halfway
through each hold. With one integration step per hold, the mid-hold
state is the endpoint interpolant $\tfrac12(\bm x+F(\bm x,\bm u))$, not
an independently integrated half step.

The reference path is straight or of constant curvature
$\kappa_{\rm r}$ and is traversed at the prescribed speed $v_*$. Steady
motion along it has yaw rate $r_*=\kappa_{\rm r}v_*$ and a constant
sideslip. The three force and moment balances of
\eqref{eq:bicycle_model} determine that sideslip and the trim input
$\bm u_*=[\delta_{F,*},\beta_*]^{\trans}$; the front wheel forces are
rotated into the body frame before the balances are solved. Deviation
from this cruise is measured by the transverse error
\begin{equation}
    \bm e(\bm x)=[e_y,\ e_\psi,\ v_x-v_{x,*},\ v_y-v_{y,*},\
        r-r_*]^{\trans},
    \label{eq:transverse_error}
\end{equation}
where $e_y$ is the signed lateral offset of the center of mass from the
path and $e_\psi$ is the heading relative to the path tangent minus its
trim value. Position along the path is not an error: the nominal
behavior is the set of all phases of cruise on the path.

The obstacle keeps the constant scalar acceleration $A_C$ and constant
sideslip $\beta_C$ of the tracker model of
Section~\ref{subsec:targetTracker}, written in inertial coordinates.
With the curvature $\kappa_C=\sin\beta_C/l_{r,C}$ of that model, arc
length $s(t)=V_{C,0}t+\tfrac12A_Ct^2$ and
$\vartheta(t)=\tfrac12\kappa_Cs(t)$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm p_C(t)&=\bm p_{C,0}+s\,\frac{\sin\vartheta}{\vartheta}
        \begin{bmatrix}\cos(\psi_{C,0}+\beta_C+\vartheta)\\
        \sin(\psi_{C,0}+\beta_C+\vartheta)\end{bmatrix},\\
    \psi_C(t)&=\psi_{C,0}+\kappa_Cs,\qquad V_C(t)=V_{C,0}+A_Ct,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_flow}
\end{equation}
with $\sin\vartheta/\vartheta=1$ at $\vartheta=0$. No quadrature is
needed, and the yaw rate $\kappa_CV_C(t)$ varies with speed. The flow is
not clipped at zero speed: a braking obstacle would reverse after
$V_{C,0}/|A_C|$, which is later than any instant constrained in the
reported runs. The obstacle state is fixed at the first sample of a run.
Later observations must agree with \eqref{eq:target_flow}; the
implementation accepts no updated obstacle state within a run. The
motion model is assumed only while the two bodies are closer than an
encounter range $R_{\rm enc}$. The first prediction node at which the
body distance exceeds $R_{\rm enc}$ is the encounter exit, and later
nodes carry no obstacle constraint; an obstacle that is beyond
$R_{\rm enc}$ at the supplied state imposes no constraint in that
sample.

The horizon has $M=N+\lceil T_{\rm c}/T_s\rceil$ holds: $N$ primary
holds and a completion tail of duration $T_{\rm c}$. An anchor trajectory
$(\bar{\bm x}_i,\bar{\bm u}_i)$, with $\bar{\bm x}_0$ the supplied
(measured) state and
$\bar{\bm x}_{i+1}=F(\bar{\bm x}_i,\bar{\bm u}_i)$, supplies every
linearization of the sample. With
$\Delta\bm x_i=\bm x_i-\bar{\bm x}_i$,
$\Delta\bm u_i=\bm u_i-\bar{\bm u}_i$,
$A_i=D_xF(\bar{\bm x}_i,\bar{\bm u}_i)$ and
$B_i=D_uF(\bar{\bm x}_i,\bar{\bm u}_i)$, the shared model is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \Delta\bm x_{i+1}=A_i\Delta\bm x_i+B_i\Delta\bm u_i,\qquad
    \Delta\bm x_0=0,\\
    |\Delta\bm x_i|\leq\bm\tau_x,\qquad|\Delta\bm u_i|\leq\bm\tau_u .
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:affine_model}
\end{equation}
The trust boxes $\bm\tau_x$ and $\bm\tau_u$ limit the correction of one
iteration; they are not certified bounds on the linearization remainder.
The force ratio obeys its actuator interval, and the body velocities
$v_x$, $v_y$ and $r$ obey box bounds at the nodes and mid-hold points.
The steering angle has neither a magnitude nor a rate bound in the
present formulation, and no tire-slip or friction-circle constraint is
imposed on the affine prediction.

\subsection{Initialization}
\label{subsec:initialization}

When the previous sample returned a plan, its inputs are shifted by one
hold, completed with the straight-cruise trim input of
Section~\ref{subsec:terminal}, and rolled out through $F$ from the new
state. Predicted states of the previous affine solution are never reused
as the anchor. Otherwise, or when the restoration of
Section~\ref{subsec:program} requests it, a fresh anchor is rolled out
under a guidance feedback. Over the primary holds and without an
obstacle this is the path-guidance law $\bm\pi$ of
Section~\ref{subsec:clf}. With an obstacle, the guidance follows a
potential flow around a disk that moves with the obstacle, for as long
as the two interact. The remaining holds of a fresh anchor use the
terminal-core feedback of Section~\ref{subsec:terminal}.

Let $R_{\rm d}$ be the sum of the two body circumradii and the node
margin $d_{\rm m}$ introduced below. On the horizon, the nominal station
$s_0+v_*t$ is paired with the predicted obstacle center at the same
times, projected onto the path as $(s_C(t),d_C(t))$. With
$\Delta s_i=s_0+v_*t_i-s_C(t_i)$ at sample times $t_i$,
$\mathcal I=\{i:|\Delta s_i|<R_{\rm d}\}$ and
$h_i=(R_{\rm d}^2-\Delta s_i^2)^{1/2}$, a Gaussian lateral guide in the
manner of \cite{ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021},
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    d_G(t)=\sigma_Ga_G\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{(t-t_G)^2}{2T_G^2}\Bigr),\\
    a_G=\max\Bigl(0,\ \max_{i\in\mathcal I}\,
        \bigl[\sigma_Gd_C(t_i)+h_i\bigr]
        \exp\!\frac{(t_i-t_G)^2}{2T_G^2}\Bigr),
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:gaussian_guide}
\end{equation}
covers the sampled exclusion disk on the side
$\sigma_G\in\{-1,1\}$ that needs the smaller amplitude. Its center $t_G$
is the time of closest nominal approach, which lies in the interaction
interval spanned by $\{t_i:i\in\mathcal I\}$, and its width $T_G$ is at
least half the duration of that interval, so that the exponential factor
in $a_G$ is at most $e^2$. The amplitude is zero when the nominal
motion never enters the disk. This rule fixes the side on which the
obstacle is passed; the programs of Section~\ref{subsec:program} refine
the trajectory locally and do not revisit that choice. The guide defines
a stream
$\bm v_0=v_*\bm t_{\rm r}+[\dot d_G-k_G(e_y-d_G)]\bm n_{\rm r}$ with
path tangent $\bm t_{\rm r}$, path normal $\bm n_{\rm r}$ and a gain
$k_G$. The guidance velocity at the ego position $\bm p$ is this stream
deflected by a unit cylinder transported with the obstacle body center
$\bm p_C(t)$:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \tilde{\bm p}=\frac{\bm p-\bm p_C}{R_{\rm d}},\quad
    \tilde r^2=\max(1,\tilde{\bm p}^{\trans}\tilde{\bm p}),\quad
    \tilde{\bm v}=\frac{\bm v_0-\dot{\bm p}_C}{R_{\rm d}},\\
    \bm v_F=\dot{\bm p}_C+R_{\rm d}\Bigl[(1+\tilde r^{-2})\tilde{\bm v}
        -2\tilde r^{-4}\tilde{\bm p}\tilde{\bm p}^{\trans}\tilde{\bm v}
        +\Gamma\tilde r^{-2}J\tilde{\bm p}\Bigr].
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:moving_flow}
\end{equation}
The circulation $\Gamma$ carries the sign $-\sigma_G$ and sends the
stream around the selected side. On $\normtwo{\tilde{\bm p}}=1$ the
relative velocity $\bm v_F-\dot{\bm p}_C$ is tangent to the disk, so a
point transported by $\bm v_F$ does not enter it. The vehicle is not
such a point. A linear feedback on its heading and speed errors relative
to $\bm v_F$, with limits on the force ratio and the front slip angle
that apply only to this rollout, drives the nonlinear map $F$, and the
obstacle is evaluated at the same absolute times as the rollout. The
time-dependent envelope fit and its composition with the transported
flow extend the static-obstacle construction of
\cite{ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021}. A fresh anchor is a search initialization,
not a safety certificate: it can itself overlap the obstacle.

\subsection{Collision Constraints}
\label{subsec:geometry}

Both vehicles are rectangles. Let
$\mathcal C(t)=\{\bm m:H_C(\bm m-\bm p_C(t))\leq\bm h_C\}$ be the
obstacle rectangle, with
$H_C=[I;\,-I]R(\psi_C(t))^{\trans}$ and $\bm h_C$ its extents about
$\bm p_C$, and let $\bm p_j(\bm x)=\bm p+R(\psi)\bm o_j$,
$j=1,\ldots,4$, be the ego vertices with body-frame offsets $\bm o_j$.
The Euclidean distance between the two sets has the dual representation
\cite{ZhangLinigerBorrelli2021}
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    d(\bm x,t)=\max_{\bm\lambda\in\Lambda}\ \min_j\
        g_j(\bm x;\bm\lambda),\\
    g_j=\bm\lambda^{\trans}\bigl[H_C(\bm p_j(\bm x)-\bm p_C)
        -\bm h_C\bigr],\\
    \Lambda=\{\bm\lambda\geq0:\normtwo{H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda}\leq1\}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:distance_dual}
\end{equation}
Following \cite{LiZhangGuoLenzoGuo2023}, the multipliers are optimized
at the anchor and then held fixed while the trajectory is optimized.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:dualRows}
(i) For every $\bm\lambda\in\Lambda$ and every $\bm x$,
$d(\bm x,t)\geq\min_jg_j(\bm x;\bm\lambda)$.
(ii) If the rectangles are disjoint at $\bar{\bm x}$ and $\bm n$ is the
unit vector from a closest point of $\mathcal C$ to a closest point of
the ego rectangle, then
$\bm\lambda^\star=[\max(\bm w,0);\,\max(-\bm w,0)]$ with
$\bm w=R(\psi_C)^{\trans}\bm n$ satisfies
$H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda^\star=\bm n$ and
$\min_jg_j(\bar{\bm x};\bm\lambda^\star)=d(\bar{\bm x},t)$.
(iii) If a point of the ego rectangle lies in the interior of
$\mathcal C$, the maximum in \eqref{eq:distance_dual} is zero and
$\bm\lambda=0$ is its only maximizer.
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
(i) For $\bm m$ in the ego rectangle and $\bm m'\in\mathcal C$,
$\normtwo{\bm m-\bm m'}
\geq(H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda)^{\trans}(\bm m-\bm m')
\geq\bm\lambda^{\trans}[H_C(\bm m-\bm p_C)-\bm h_C]$, because
$\normtwo{H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda}\leq1$, $\bm\lambda\geq0$ and
$H_C(\bm m'-\bm p_C)\leq\bm h_C$. The right side is affine in $\bm m$,
so its minimum over the ego rectangle is attained at a vertex.
(ii) The direction $\bm n$ is the same for every closest pair.
$H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda^\star
=R(\psi_C)[\max(\bm w,0)-\max(-\bm w,0)]=\bm n$, and
$\bm h_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda^\star$ is the support of
$\mathcal C-\bm p_C$ in direction $\bm n$, attained at the closest point
$\bm m_C$. The line through the closest ego point with normal $\bm n$
supports the ego rectangle, hence
$\min_j\bm n^{\trans}(\bm p_j-\bm m_C)=d$. (iii) At such a point
$\bm m_0$ every component of $H_C(\bm m_0-\bm p_C)-\bm h_C$ is negative,
so $\min_jg_j\leq\bm\lambda^{\trans}[H_C(\bm m_0-\bm p_C)-\bm h_C]<0$
unless $\bm\lambda=0$.
\end{IEEEproof}

For hold $i$, with $\bm\lambda^\star$ computed at the anchor pose and
the obstacle pose taken from \eqref{eq:target_flow} at the same absolute
time, each constrained instant contributes the four rows
\begin{equation}
    g_j(\bar{\bm x};\bm\lambda^\star)+\bm n^{\trans}\Delta\bm p
    +\bm n^{\trans}R(\bar\psi)J\bm o_j\,\Delta\psi+\xi_i\geq d_{\rm m},
    \label{eq:collision_rows}
\end{equation}
which are affine in the decision variables. Here
$\bm n=H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda^\star$ is the unit separating normal when
the anchor is disjoint from the obstacle and vanishes when it overlaps;
at exact contact the maximizer is not unique. The constrained instants
are the hold starts and mid-hold points up to the encounter exit. One
slack $\xi_i\geq0$, $i=0,\ldots,N-1$, per primary hold relaxes its start
and mid-hold rows; tail rows have no slack. The vertex rotation is
retained to first order, with remainder at most
$\tfrac12\normtwo{\bm o_j}\Delta\psi^2$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(i), rows \eqref{eq:collision_rows} with
$\xi_i=0$ therefore imply
$d\geq d_{\rm m}-\tfrac12r_E\Delta\psi_i^2$ for the affine-predicted
pose, where $r_E=\max_j\normtwo{\bm o_j}$. With the values of
Section~\ref{sec:results} ($r_E=2.58$\,m, $|\Delta\psi|\leq0.25$\,rad,
$d_{\rm m}=0.10$\,m) the right side is at least $0.019$\,m.

Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(iii) leads to a degenerate case that matters
in Section~\ref{sec:results}. When the anchor overlaps the obstacle at a
constrained instant, the multipliers vanish, the row carries no gradient
in the ego pose, and it reduces to $\xi_i\geq d_{\rm m}$ on a primary
hold and to the infeasible $0\geq d_{\rm m}$ on a tail hold. A primary
optimum equal to $d_{\rm m}$ is the signature of this case. The ordinary
distance cannot rank a shallow overlap against a deep one, and no signed
penetration measure is substituted for it. In the implementation
evaluated below, the maximization \eqref{eq:distance_dual} is solved
numerically as a five-variable cone program at every constrained instant
and its value is compared with the exact rectangle distance; a
difference above $10^{-6}$\,m aborts the controller call.
Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(ii) gives the same multipliers in closed form.

\subsection{Terminal Core}
\label{subsec:terminal}

The horizon ends in a neighborhood of straight cruise that can be placed
anywhere in the plane. Let
$\bm y=(\bm p,\psi,v_x,v_y,r,\bm u^-)\in\R^8$ append the previous input
to the state, so that the final input is part of the terminal condition,
and let $\bm e_c=(\bm a,\bm z)$ be its deviation from a straight cruise
line of speed $v_*$, with the pose deviation $\bm a\in\R^3$ expressed in
the frame of that line and
$\bm z=(v_x-v_*,\,v_y,\,r,\,\bm u^--\bm u_{\rm c})\in\R^5$, where
$\bm u_{\rm c}$ is the trim input of straight cruise. A discrete Riccati
design for the linearized cruise, weighted by the error scales of
Section~\ref{subsec:clf}, gives a feedback and a metric
$P_c=R_c^{\trans}R_c\succ0$. Let
$\mathcal F_c$ be the closed-loop error map of $F$ under this feedback,
and $\mathcal B=\{\bm e_c:\normtwo{R_c\bm e_c}\leq r_B\}$. Once, before
the first sample, an outward-rounded interval enclosure of the Jacobian
of $\mathcal F_c$ over $\mathcal B$ establishes
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \sup_{\bm e_c\in\mathcal B}
        \normtwo{R_cD\mathcal F_c(\bm e_c)R_c^{-1}}\leq\gamma_c<1,\\
    \normtwo{R_c\mathcal F_c(0)}\leq d_c,\qquad
    \gamma_cr_B+d_c<r_B,
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:core_contraction}
\end{equation}
together with admissibility of the force ratio and of the model domain
on $\mathcal B$; the radius $r_B$ is halved until these conditions hold.
By the mean-value inequality on the convex set $\mathcal B$,
$\normtwo{R_c\mathcal F_c(\bm e_c)}\leq d_c+\gamma_cr_B<r_B$, so
$\mathcal F_c(\mathcal B)\subseteq\mathcal B$ for the numerical map $F$.
Because $f$ does not depend on absolute position or heading and no road
constraint is imposed, $F$ commutes with planar rigid motions, and the
same core is invariant about every placed cruise line.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:freePose}
Partition $P_c$ conformably with $(\bm a,\bm z)$, let
$S_c=P_{zz}-P_{za}P_{aa}^{-1}P_{az}$ and $G=-P_{aa}^{-1}P_{az}$, and
measure the heading deviation in $(-\pi,\pi]$. If the heading component
of $G\bm z$ lies in $(-\pi,\pi)$, the minimum of
$\bm e_c^{\trans}P_c\bm e_c$ over all placements of the cruise line is
$\bm z^{\trans}S_c\bm z$, attained at $\bm a=G\bm z$. Hence $\bm y$ lies
in the core of some placed cruise line if and only if
$\bm z^{\trans}S_c\bm z\leq r_B^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Completing the square gives
$\bm e_c^{\trans}P_c\bm e_c
=(\bm a-G\bm z)^{\trans}P_{aa}(\bm a-G\bm z)+\bm z^{\trans}S_c\bm z$.
Translating and rotating the cruise line realizes every pose deviation
$\bm a$ without changing $\bm z$.
\end{IEEEproof}

The endpoint constraint is therefore one second-order cone on the final
body velocities and the final input,
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{S_c^{1/2}(\bar{\bm z}_M+\Delta\bm z_M)}\leq r_B,
    \label{eq:terminal_cone}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\bm z}_M+\Delta\bm z_M$ is the $\bm z$ part of the predicted
endpoint, and the final pose is free. For the vehicle of
Section~\ref{sec:results} the construction returns the dimensionless
radii $r_B=2.4\times10^{-4}$ at 8\,m/s and $9.8\times10^{-4}$ at
15\,m/s, with $\gamma_c=0.9996$ and $0.9987$. The cone is thus close to
a terminal equality: every plan must end in straight cruise at $v_*$, at
a pose of its own choosing. The core is a straight cruise also when the
reference path is circular, so that on a curved path the end of every
plan differs from the path trim; whether this affected the curved runs
of Section~\ref{sec:results} was not isolated.

If the endpoint is still within $R_{\rm enc}$ of the obstacle, the
endpoint node carries four hard rows \eqref{eq:collision_rows}, and
further linearized rows separate the cruise line fitted to the anchor
endpoint by Lemma~\ref{lem:freePose} from the obstacle's later motion. A
sufficient separation between the ray of that line and the obstacle's
entire predicted path, which uses the motion model beyond
$R_{\rm enc}$, is imposed when it holds at the anchor. Otherwise the
line is followed at matching absolute times, in strides chosen from a
relative-speed bound, until the obstacle is beyond $R_{\rm enc}$, and
the closest visited configuration supplies four rows of the form
\eqref{eq:collision_rows}. These rows are evaluated on the anchor and
linearized in the fitted pose; their construction is not reproduced
here. Neither they nor the invariance of the core certify the realized
nonlinear motion.

\subsection{Nominal Cost-to-Go Lyapunov Function}
\label{subsec:clf}

One obstacle-independent function measures the distance from cruise at
every sample, during avoidance and after it. Let
$\ell(\bm x)=\bm e(\bm x)^{\trans}Q_e\bm e(\bm x)$ with
$Q_e=\diag(\varsigma_1^{-2},\ldots,\varsigma_5^{-2})$ and error scales
$\varsigma_i>0$. A path-guidance feedback $\bm\pi(\bm x)$ steers the
course toward the path through a lookahead law and regulates the speed
through the force ratio. With $\chi$ the course relative to the path
tangent, $V$ the speed, $d_{\rm la}$ a lookahead distance, $\chi_d$ the
commanded
course and $\dot\chi_d$ its rate along the motion,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \chi_d=-\arctan\frac{e_y}{d_{\rm la}},\qquad
    \dot\chi_d=-\frac{d_{\rm la}V\sin\chi}{d_{\rm la}^2+e_y^2},\\
    r_d=\operatorname{sat}_{\bar r}\Bigl[
        \frac{\kappa_{\rm r}V\cos\chi}{1-\kappa_{\rm r}e_y}
        +\dot\chi_d-k_\chi(\chi-\chi_d)\Bigr],\\
    F_d=\frac{I_{z,E}k_r(r_d-r)+l_{r,E}F_{yr}}{l_{f,E}},\qquad
    \phi_d=\min\Bigl(\frac{|F_d|}{Q_f},c_\phi\Bigr),\\
    \delta_F=\operatorname{atan2}(v_y+l_{f,E}r,v_x)+\delta_0\\
        \qquad{}+\operatorname{sgn}(F_d)\arctan
        \frac{3Q_f[1-(1-\phi_d)^{1/3}]}{C_f},\\
    \beta=\operatorname{sat}_{\bar\beta}\bigl[
        \beta_*+k_\beta(v_{x,*}-v_x)\bigr].
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:guidance_law}
\end{equation}
The yaw-rate demand $r_d$ is limited to $\bar r=c_a\mu g/V$, a fraction
of the available lateral acceleration, with $\mu$ the smaller axle
friction coefficient. The front force demand $F_d$
follows from a yaw-rate loop and the current rear force $F_{yr}$, and
the steering angle inverts the front Fiala curve \eqref{eq:fiala_force}
below the fraction $c_\phi$ of its capacity $Q_f$; $\delta_0$ is a constant
that makes the trim an equilibrium. The law does not depend on the
obstacle, and it is never issued as a command. Let
$F_\pi(\bm x)=F(\bm x,\bm\pi(\bm x))$ and let $A_\pi$ be the Jacobian of
its error dynamics at the trim. The spectral radius of $A_\pi$ is
checked to be less than one, and $P_f$ solves
\begin{equation}
    A_\pi^{\trans}P_fA_\pi-P_f=-2Q_e .
    \label{eq:tail_lyapunov}
\end{equation}
For a fixed number $K$ of policy-evaluation steps and
$V_f=\bm e^{\trans}P_f\bm e$, define
\begin{equation}
    V_K(\bm x)=\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\ell\bigl(F_\pi^{\,j}(\bm x)\bigr)
        +V_f\bigl(F_\pi^{\,K}(\bm x)\bigr).
    \label{eq:cost_to_go}
\end{equation}
$K$ is the same for all states and adds no decision variable. Because
the path is straight or circular, $V_K$ depends on $\bm x$ only through
$\bm e(\bm x)$. The reported trials use no rate limit on the force
ratio; with one, the previous input would have to be included in the
argument of $V_K$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:costToGo}
Let $\mathcal X_f$ be positively invariant under $F_\pi$ with
$V_f(F_\pi(\bm x))-V_f(\bm x)\leq-\ell(\bm x)$ on $\mathcal X_f$, and let
$\mathcal D$ be the set of states for which the iterates $F_\pi^{\,j}$,
$j\leq K$, are defined and $F_\pi^{\,K}(\bm x)\in\mathcal X_f$. Then
$\mathcal D$ is positively invariant under $F_\pi$, and for every
$\bm x\in\mathcal D$,
\begin{equation}
    V_K(F_\pi(\bm x))-V_K(\bm x)\leq-\ell(\bm x).
    \label{eq:cost_to_go_decrease}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
With $\bm x_j=F_\pi^{\,j}(\bm x)$ the two sums telescope:
$V_K(\bm x_1)-V_K(\bm x)=-\ell(\bm x)+\ell(\bm x_K)
+V_f(\bm x_{K+1})-V_f(\bm x_K)\leq-\ell(\bm x)$. Since
$\bm x_{K+1}\in\mathcal X_f$ by invariance, $\bm x_1\in\mathcal D$.
\end{IEEEproof}

The factor two in \eqref{eq:tail_lyapunov} leaves a reserve for the
nonlinear remainder. If the error dynamics are
$A_\pi\bm e+\bm\varphi(\bm e)$ with
$\normtwo{\bm\varphi(\bm e)}\leq c\normtwo{\bm e}^2$, then
$V_f(F_\pi)-V_f\leq-2\ell+2c\normtwo{P_fA_\pi}\normtwo{\bm e}^3
+c^2\normtwo{P_f}\normtwo{\bm e}^4$, so a sufficiently small sublevel
set of $V_f$ qualifies as $\mathcal X_f$. The construction follows the
terminal-decrease argument of receding-horizon control
\cite{JadbabaieYuHauser2001,RawlingsMayneDiehl2017}. The implementation
neither computes $c$ nor characterizes $\mathcal D$: the premises of
Proposition~\ref{prop:costToGo} are tested at sampled states, not proved
on a region. On $\mathcal D$ the proposition shows that a decrease input
exists, namely $\bm\pi(\bm x)$. It does not show that this input is
compatible with the trust boxes, the collision rows or the terminal
cone.

Every sample requests
\begin{equation}
    V_K\bigl(F(\bm x,\bm u_0)\bigr)-V_K(\bm x)
    \leq-\theta\,\ell(\bm x)+\delta,\qquad\delta\geq0,
    \label{eq:clf_row}
\end{equation}
with $0<\theta<1$ and $\bm x$ the supplied state. Only the first input
enters. Write $V_K(F(\bm x,\bm u_0))=\normtwo{\bm\varrho(\bm u_0)}^2$,
where $\bm\varrho$ stacks the weighted errors
$Q_e^{1/2}\bm e(F_\pi^{\,j}(\bm x^+))$, $j<K$, and
$P_f^{1/2}\bm e(F_\pi^{\,K}(\bm x^+))$ along the nominal rollout from
$\bm x^+=F(\bm x,\bm u_0)$. A centered difference stencil in the two
inputs gives the Jacobian $G_\varrho$ of $\bm\varrho$ and the Hessian
$H_V$ of the scalar function at $\bar{\bm u}_0$. The program uses
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    m(\Delta\bm u_0)={}&\normtwo{\bar{\bm\varrho}
        +G_\varrho\Delta\bm u_0}^2\\
      &+\tfrac12\Delta\bm u_0^{\trans}
        \bigl(H_V-2G_\varrho^{\trans}G_\varrho\bigr)_+\Delta\bm u_0,
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:clf_model}
\end{equation}
the Gauss--Newton model plus the positive semidefinite part of the
curvature it omits. The model is nonnegative and convex, and it agrees
with $V_K\circ F$ to first order at the anchor. It is a local model, not
an upper bound over the trust box, so $\delta=0$ in the program does not
prove \eqref{eq:clf_row} for the nonlinear map.

\subsection{The Two Programs}
\label{subsec:program}

Let $\mathcal A$ denote the constraints shared by both programs on
$(\Delta\bm x_{0:M},\Delta\bm u_{0:M-1},\bm\xi)$: the model
\eqref{eq:affine_model}, the force-ratio and body-velocity bounds, the
rows \eqref{eq:collision_rows} with $\bm\xi\geq0$, the cone
\eqref{eq:terminal_cone} and the terminal separation rows. The primary
program is
\begin{equation}
    J_1^\star=\min\ \sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\xi_i
    \quad\text{subject to }\mathcal A,
    \label{eq:primary_program}
\end{equation}
and the secondary program is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \min_{\delta\geq0,\ \sigma}\quad&
        \frac{\delta}{\max(V_K(\bm x),1)}+\epsilon\,\sigma\\
    \text{s.t.}\quad&\mathcal A,\qquad\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\xi_i
        \leq J_1^\star+\epsilon_{\rm lex},\\
    &m(\Delta\bm u_0)\leq V_K(\bm x)-\theta\,\ell(\bm x)+\delta,\\
    &\frac1{2M}\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}
        \normtwo{W_u^{-1}\Delta\bm u_i}^2\leq\sigma\leq1,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:secondary_program}
\end{equation}
with $W_u=\diag(\bm\tau_u)$. Both are second-order cone programs. The
ordering resembles the predictive barrier construction of
\cite{WabersichZeilinger2023}, in which the minimal relaxation of a
predictive safety problem is computed before the performance problem.
The conditions under which that construction yields a control barrier
function, in particular an exact prediction model and a terminal safe
set for the complete problem, are not established here.

Minimizing a first-step slack alone leaves the later inputs
undetermined, and the next anchor inherits them; in a diagnostic run
without the term $\epsilon\sigma$ they repeatedly placed the next trust
box where it capped the steering angle. The term makes the plan unique
at a bounded cost. Since $0\leq\sigma\leq1$ on the trust box, the scaled
slack of an exact optimum exceeds the smallest attainable one by at most
$\epsilon$. The penalty acts on increments from the anchor; it pulls the
inputs neither toward zero nor toward $\bm\pi$.

At the first node the state correction is zero, so its rows bound
$\xi_0$ from below and
$J_1^\star\geq J_{\rm low}
=\max\bigl(0,\,d_{\rm m}-\min_jg_j(\bm x;\bm\lambda^\star)\bigr)$,
evaluated at the supplied state. Restoration replaces the anchor: if a
shifted anchor yields no primary solution, or a value above
$J_{\rm low}$, one fresh anchor is formulated and the smaller primary
value is kept, with ties favoring the shift. A fresh problem that is
reported infeasible may double its input trust box once. The secondary
program is then solved on the selected model. A normal sample therefore
solves two programs. Restoration adds at most two primary solves, and a
secondary program that returns no point may use the fresh anchor if it
is still unused.

The applied command is the first input of the secondary solution,
unclipped. A finite solver result is executed even when a solver exit
flag reports non-convergence and even when $J_1^\star>0$; a positive
relaxation is recorded, not interpreted as safety. If a program returns
no finite point, or the multiplier check of
Section~\ref{subsec:geometry} fails, no command is issued and the run
stops. Neither the previous plan nor a primary-only point is
substituted.

\subsection{Scope of the Guarantees}
\label{subsec:scope}

Three statements hold in exact arithmetic. For an executed plan whose
programs converged with $J_1^\star=0$, the affine prediction satisfies
the rows \eqref{eq:collision_rows} with
$\sum_i\xi_i\leq\epsilon_{\rm lex}$, so the predicted rectangles are
separated by at least
$d_{\rm m}-\epsilon_{\rm lex}-\tfrac12r_E\Delta\psi_i^2$ at the
constrained instants up to the encounter exit. The predicted endpoint of
such a plan lies in a placed core by Lemma~\ref{lem:freePose}.
Independently of the plan, on $\mathcal D$ a decrease input for $V_K$
exists by Proposition~\ref{prop:costToGo}.

None of these is a statement about the vehicle. The constraints act on
the affine model \eqref{eq:affine_model} at nodes and mid-hold points.
The nonlinear rollout of the same inputs can violate them, and two
rectangles that are separated at consecutive constrained instants can
intersect in between. The trust boxes and the node margin $d_{\rm m}$
are not linked by a bound on the accumulated linearization remainder.
Feasibility is not recursive: the shifted plan is an initialization, the
next anchor is a new nonlinear rollout with new multipliers, and the
terminal rows are refitted to it, so the solution of one sample need not
be feasible at the next. A positive $J_1^\star$ is executed and may
describe an overlapping plan. Zero slack in \eqref{eq:secondary_program}
is a statement about the local model \eqref{eq:clf_model}. The scheme
follows the real-time-iteration idea of \cite{DiehlBockSchloder2005}: it
solves convex programs on at most two linearizations per sample instead
of iterating to convergence, and it inherits neither nominal stability
nor safety from the nonlinear problem that it approximates.

\subsection{Estimator Accuracy Against a Tuned Reference}
\label{subsec:estimatorResults}

Six open-loop scenarios of 6 to 12\,s are used. In the straight
oncoming scenario the ego drives straight at 15\,m/s and the target
approaches from 95\,m at 15\,m/s. In the arc crossing scenario the ego
follows an arc of radius 100\,m and the target moves on a straight line
that meets the arc. In the arc following scenario the target is 30\,m
ahead on the same arc at 15.5\,m/s. The swerve-and-braking scenario
adds a 3.5\,m double lane change with braking to the straight oncoming
geometry, with the target at 12\,m/s. In the low-relative-speed
scenario a lead target is 30\,m ahead at 15.3\,m/s. In the weaving
scenario a weaving, accelerating ego meets a turning, accelerating
target. The target obeys the constant-acceleration, constant-sideslip
model in all of them, and the ego truth obeys the kinematic
single-track relation without mismatch. The swerve and weaving
scenarios violate the zero-jerk premise of the reference observer.
Synchronized measurements arrive at 50\,Hz with bounded uniform noise at
the declared maxima, and the maximum RK4 step is 0.005\,s.

The reference is a reproduction of the multistage observer of
\cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}, with companion-form ego and target chains
and a switched course--acceleration yaw stage. As in that paper, its ego
chain uses GNSS position and the gyroscope and does not use the GNSS
velocity that the estimator of Section~\ref{sec:estimator} measures. Its
gains are selected from 54 candidates (two gain shapes, three ego rates,
three target rates and three yaw gains) on training seeds 101--102, once
for every scenario, by minimizing the sum of the position, speed and
course errors scaled by 0.1\,m, 0.5\,m/s and $5^\circ$. In five of the
six scenarios the selected candidate lies on the boundary of this grid.

The estimator receives no error-based tuning, but its gains are
synthesized anew from the operating domain declared for each scenario.
Only the low-relative-speed scenario uses the domain of
Section~\ref{subsec:designResults}. The arc following scenario declares
$\beta_{\max}=0.03$\,rad, which gives the target gains
$[10.21,51.59,116.4]$. The straight, arc crossing and swerve scenarios
declare $\rho_{\max}=100$\,m (the swerve scenario also
$\bar\omega_E=0.40$\,rad/s and $\beta_{\rm dom}=0.06$\,rad), and the
weaving scenario declares $\rho_{\max}=80$\,m. For
$\rho_{\max}=100$\,m and 80\,m, respectively, the decay floors are 0.4
and 0.5\,s$^{-1}$, the scalar gains 1.198 and 1.498\,s$^{-1}$, and the
target gains $[6.24,19.31,27.20]$ and $[6.50,20.93,30.23]$.

Both observers run on identical truth, sensor draws and initial offsets.

RMSE in all ten (0.0238 against 0.0283\,m and 0.083 against 0.117\,m/s

both, and the noise-free errors of the noise-variance shape are larger.

injection gains, the covariant chain and the companion chain agree
within 0.2\% in speed RMSE in three of the four scenarios with constant
ego motion (0.053365 and 0.053356\,m/s in the straight scenario) and
differ in the arc crossing scenario (0.049 against 0.057\,m/s). The
coordinate change is therefore not the main source of the measured
differences; the experiment does not apportion the remainder among the
ego stage, the gain selection and the validity of the models during ego
maneuvers. The noise-variance criterion was adopted after a comparison
of the bounded-real shape on the same test seeds was known, in which
the estimator led in two of six scenarios in position and three in
speed. The criterion is a predicted variance and uses no simulated
error, but the rerun is not a preregistered confirmation. The reference
is our reproduction with gains selected empirically on a bounded grid,
not the authors' implementation or sensor suite. No
enclosure-containment or outage statistics are reported for this design.

Table~\ref{tab:controllerParameters} lists the parameters. The vehicle
is a generic passenger car and is not the vehicle whose rear-axle
distance the estimator uses; the two parameter sets are not yet unified.
With these values the spectral radius of $A_\pi$ is 0.968 to 0.969 for
both speeds on the straight and the curved path.

Seven encounter geometries are run at reference speeds of 8 and
15\,m/s. In the head-on case the obstacle starts 24\,m ahead on the path
and approaches at 8\,m/s; in the accelerating head-on case it also
accelerates at 1\,m/s$^2$. The braking lead starts 1.45\,m ahead of the
ego body at the ego speed and brakes at 0.5\,m/s$^2$. The crossing
obstacle moves at 8\,m/s perpendicular to the path and reaches the path
when the cruising ego does. The turning crossing obstacle reaches the
same point at 10\,m/s and perpendicular to the path at that instant,
while accelerating at $A_C=1$\,m/s$^2$ and turning with
$\beta_C=0.05$\,rad, a turning radius of about 32\,m. The curved head-on
and curved crossing cases use a path of curvature $0.005$\,m$^{-1}$,
with an opposing obstacle on the same circle at 8\,m/s and an obstacle
that crosses at 10\,m/s with $A_C=0.5$\,m/s$^2$ and
$\beta_C=0.04$\,rad. In every case the bodies are initially separated,
and the ego under unmodified cruise would collide, with first contact
between 0.82 and 2.41\,s.

Each issued input is held for $T_s$ on the model $f$ itself, integrated
by an adaptive Runge--Kutta method with relative and absolute tolerances
$10^{-11}$ and $10^{-12}$, and the resulting state is supplied exactly to
the next call. The plant therefore differs from the prediction only
through the integration scheme and the linearization. There is no
parameter mismatch, sensing error, actuator dynamics or road constraint,
the obstacle follows \eqref{eq:target_flow} exactly, and the measured
computation time is not applied as a delay. The body distance is
evaluated at 31 instants per hold, and the minimum of every run is
reproduced within $10^{-9}$\,m by an independent implementation.

A run succeeds if the distance stays strictly positive and cruise is
recovered. Recovery requires all five transverse errors within
(0.1\,m, $1^\circ$, 0.1\,m/s, 0.05\,m/s, 0.01\,rad/s) during a dwell of
1\,s that starts no earlier than 8\,s, so that the earliest
confirmation is at 9.05\,s. Runs are observed for 8\,s and then
continued, for at most 60\,s, until the dwell is confirmed. The
campaign is deterministic. It uses MATLAB R2026a with one computational
thread on an AMD Ryzen 7 7800X3D and the interior-point cone solver of
the Optimization Toolbox, with an iteration limit of 400 per program and
a time limit of 5\,s per call. Below, a primary optimum or a slack is
called zero when it does not exceed the feasibility tolerance
$10^{-5}$, taken relative to $\max(V_K,1)$ for the control-Lyapunov
slack.

Table~\ref{tab:closedLoop} and Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop} summarize the
fourteen runs. Twelve end collision-free with cruise recovered, between
9.05 and 19.05\,s. The 15\,m/s turning crossing collides
(Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop}(c)) and recovers afterwards. The 8\,m/s
braking lead is interrupted at 10.35\,s with a positive distance and
without confirmed recovery. Neither failure is counted as a success.

The runs expose three limits of the formulation even where the outcome
is a success. In seven of the twelve successful runs the smallest
sampled distance is below the node margin $d_{\rm m}=0.10$\,m, down to
0.033\,m (Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop}(d)): the margin is consumed between
the constrained instants, by the linearization and, in four of these
runs, by executed relaxations. The maneuvers are not confined to the
road. The largest lateral deviation is 2.5 to 9.9\,m
(Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop}(e)), and in twelve of the fourteen runs the
body leaves a corridor of half-width 4\,m about the path; the steering
angle reaches 0.59\,rad, the yaw rate 0.91\,rad/s, and the force ratio
ranges from $-0.68$ to $0.56$. A positive primary optimum occurs at 22
samples of five runs, all with crossing obstacles and all between 0.80
and 1.50\,s, and is executed each time. Four of these runs nevertheless
keep a positive distance, including the 15\,m/s curved crossing, which
executes the degenerate optimum $J_1^\star=d_{\rm m}$ of
Section~\ref{subsec:geometry} at three samples and passes with
0.033\,m.

The control-Lyapunov condition is met in the model more often than on
the plant. Of 3694 consecutive state pairs, 2636 have zero slack in
\eqref{eq:secondary_program}. In 154 of those the requested decrease of
$V_K$ is not achieved, and in 36 the value increases, by at most 0.034.
Recovery in Table~\ref{tab:closedLoop} is therefore an observed dwell,
not a monotone decrease.

The controller is not real-time in this implementation
(Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop}(f)). All 3708 calls exceed the 50\,ms hold;
the median is 0.107\,s and the 95th percentile 0.360\,s. The maximum of
0.976\,s is the first call after a run was resumed in a new MATLAB
process and includes its start-up; the largest other call takes
0.902\,s. The braking-lead runs are the slowest, with medians of 0.30
and 0.35\,s; the cause was not profiled. The two programs account for
about two thirds of the time and the formulation for most of the rest.
At 113 samples the executed solution carries a non-convergence exit
flag. Because the computation time is not applied as a delay, the
outcomes describe the algorithm with instantaneous computation.

The initialization matters. Without the time-aligned guide
\eqref{eq:gaussian_guide} in the fresh anchor, the otherwise identical
controller completed the first 8\,s in ten of the fourteen scenarios;
this ablation was not continued to recovery. Two runs stopped without a
primary solution, two stopped on the multiplier check, and one of the
latter had already collided.

An exact replay of the 31 controller calls that precede the collision
reproduces every issued input, and
Table~\ref{tab:turningCrossing} compares the two predictions of each
plan at the constrained instants. The collision has three stages.

First, the set defined by the affine rows is optimistic. Plans with a
primary optimum of at most $2.1\times10^{-8}$\,m overlap the obstacle
when their inputs are propagated through $F$. For the plan issued at
1.05\,s and the instant 1.525\,s, the affine row value is 0.1000\,m,
whereas the same multipliers on the nonlinear pose give $-0.045$\,m
(the separating-axis gap of Table~\ref{tab:turningCrossing} is
$-0.040$\,m). The pose differs by 0.071\,m in position, which removes
0.070\,m of separation, and by 0.031\,rad in heading, which removes
0.075\,m. Integration error is not the cause: the one-step RK4 position
differs from the adaptive integration by $8\times10^{-5}$\,m at that
instant. The error is in the tangent model. The secondary program
repeatedly uses the full steering correction of $0.075$\,rad, over
which the Fiala curve is far from linear. At the initial sample the
front-force tangent predicts 3225\,N for the returned input where the
tire model gives 1365\,N. The secondary program also uses the
slack-free affine set completely. At 0.85\,s the primary solution has a
nonlinear clearance of 0.093\,m and the secondary solution
$-0.009$\,m; at 1.05\,s the values are 0.005 and $-0.040$\,m. Both
satisfy the same affine rows.

Second, the constrained instants are sparse. The affine prediction
issued at 1.05\,s has a distance of 0.100\,m at its nodes and mid-hold
points, but its interpolated motion falls to 0.007\,m between them.

Third, restoration fails once both anchors overlap. A fresh anchor
restores a zero primary optimum when it is itself separated: over the
campaign it is formulated at 27 samples and succeeds at 7, among them
the 0.90\,s sample of this run, whose shifted anchor overlapped
(Table~\ref{tab:turningCrossing} lists the fresh plan). The 22 samples
with a positive optimum are the 20 unsuccessful ones and two at which
the optimum equals the lower bound $J_{\rm low}$, so that no fresh
anchor is formulated. At 1.10\,s the shifted rollout again overlaps the
obstacle at a future instant. Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(iii) applies: the
computed multipliers have a normal of norm $10^{-9}$, the row reduces to
$\xi_i\geq d_{\rm m}$, and $J_1^\star=0.100$\,m is an exact optimum of
a problem that cannot distinguish shallow from deep overlap. This time,
and at every later sample before contact, the fresh anchor does not
lower the optimum. The anchor restarted at 1.10\,s overlaps at the same
instant, because the vehicle lags the guidance field: at 1.50\,s its
lateral position is 3.03\,m where the guide
\eqref{eq:gaussian_guide} requests 5.75\,m. The positive-slack plans
are executed and the bodies touch at 1.512\,s. This sequence does not
show that no avoiding trajectory existed from the state at 1.10\,s.

The interruption of the 8\,m/s braking lead is numerical. At 10.35\,s
the cone program for \eqref{eq:distance_dual} at an anchor instant 2.5\,s
ahead, where the bodies are 27.63\,m apart, stops with a primal--dual
difference of $1.68\times10^{-6}$\,m against a tolerance of
$10^{-6}$\,m, and the controller raises an error before either program
is solved. The value $V_K$ was decreasing over the preceding samples
(6.41, 5.90, 5.46, 5.06). In a counterfactual continuation from the
saved state and controller memory, with the multipliers computed in
closed form by Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(ii) and no other change to the
controller, the recovery dwell is confirmed at 12.65\,s with a smallest
distance of 0.138\,m. The counterfactual is not counted in
Table~\ref{tab:closedLoop}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The two components stand at different levels of assurance.
Theorem~\ref{thm:cascadeISS} is a continuous-time statement about the
velocity--target cascade, and the gain selection of
Section~\ref{subsec:gainSelection} takes place inside its certificate.
With a shape chosen outside the certificate, only the scale can be
adjusted to it, and the gains come out larger than the certificate
requires; choosing the shape inside the certificate lowered the
predicted velocity noise and the certified radii together. The remaining
losses against the tuned reference are consistent with constraints of
the certificate. At low relative speed the decay floor
$\lambda_T=0.8$\,s$^{-1}$ keeps the certified gains noisier than the
reference's uncertified choice, and in arc following the target sideslip
bound $\beta_{\max}=0.03$\,rad declared for that scenario raises $L_q$
from 0.80 to 2.29\,s$^{-2}$. Less conservative Lipschitz multipliers,
and separate metrics for the variance bound and the dissipation
inequality, are the natural refinements. The large difference between
observed errors and certified radii remains, and it will matter as soon
as a controller consumes the enclosure.

The controller has no certificate for the nonlinear vehicle, and the
results show why one cannot be inferred from solver success. The three
stages of the turning-crossing collision correspond to three missing
links. The trust box and the node margin are not related by a bound on
the linearization remainder, so a plan can satisfy every affine row and
overlap in the nonlinear rollout. A remedy must bound that remainder or
shorten the step until the two predictions agree; relinearization within
the hold, with an explicit agreement test before a plan is issued, is
the direct next step and is not evaluated here. Separation is imposed at
nodes and mid-hold points only, and whole-hold separation needs a swept
or interval condition. The ordinary distance is flat under overlap, so
restoration from an overlapping anchor needs a measure that ranks
penetration, or an anchor that is known to be separated; a guidance
field cannot supply the latter while the vehicle lags it. The
braking-lead interruption adds an avoidable dependency: the availability
of the control problem rested on micrometer accuracy of a numerical
subproblem whose solution Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(ii) gives in closed
form.

Several premises of the closed-loop trials are favorable. The plant is
the prediction model and its state is known exactly. The obstacle
follows its declared model, and its trajectory is fixed at the first
sample, so a noisy estimate cannot yet be supplied to the controller.
Computation is treated as instantaneous although every call exceeds the
hold. The steering angle is unbounded in magnitude and rate, road
boundaries are absent, and the maneuvers leave the lane. The estimator's
enclosure is not consumed, and the two components use different vehicle
parameter sets. The obstacle speeds and sideslip angles of the
closed-loop scenarios, and the ego yaw rates of the maneuvers, also lie
outside the operating domain for which the estimator of
Section~\ref{subsec:designResults} is certified. The terminal core is so
small that the endpoint cone acts as an equality on the final
velocities, which the completion tail must then make reachable. Each of
these premises has to be removed before physical collision avoidance can
be claimed.

The control-Lyapunov construction behaves as intended in one respect:
one obstacle-independent function governs avoidance and recovery, and
every completed run returns to cruise with the same function and
programs throughout. The measured increases of $V_K$ under zero modeled
slack show, however, that the local model \eqref{eq:clf_model} is not a
bound, and the premises of Proposition~\ref{prop:costToGo} are not
verified on a region. The recovery results are therefore empirical.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Retaining measured acceleration and yaw rate as exogenous inputs removes
the zero-jerk and zero-yaw-acceleration assumptions of the differentiated
ego design. The direct velocity measurement in \eqref{eq:ego_observer}
keeps the common gyro error in one column and yields the cascade
comparison of Theorem~\ref{thm:cascadeISS}, and a parallel yaw observer
with an orientation set supplies inertial reconstruction. Shaping the
target gains by a semidefinite program that contains the Lipschitz
certificate reduces the predicted noise and the certified radii
together. In synthetic trials with operating domains declared per
scenario, the estimator has a lower mean error than a per-scenario tuned
reproduction of the reference observer in five of six scenarios for each
of position, speed and course after the initial transient; the position
error over the complete run and most acceleration errors favor the
reference.

The controller linearizes a dynamic bicycle model with modified Fiala
tires along a nonlinear rollout and, in a normal sample, solves two cone
programs: the relaxation of distance-dual collision rows first, and the
violation of one cost-to-go Lyapunov decrease condition second, with a
flow-based initialization and a freely placed terminal core. In fourteen
exact-state collision-threat runs it ends twelve collision-free with
cruise recovered. It collides in one, where a plan satisfied the affine
rows while its nonlinear rollout overlapped and restoration then stalled
on the zero-gradient overlap of the ordinary distance. It stops in one
on a numerical check of a subproblem that has a closed-form solution.
Its call time exceeds the control period, its maneuvers leave the road,
and it carries no certificate for the nonlinear vehicle. A bound that
links the linearization step to the collision margin, whole-hold
separation, a restoration measure that is informative under overlap,
road and actuator constraints, real-time computation and an experiment
with the estimator in the loop remain necessary before physical
collision avoidance can be claimed.
