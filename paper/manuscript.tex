\begin{abstract}
This paper develops a measured-input estimator and a predictive controller
for lane keeping with vehicle collision avoidance, and evaluates them
together in simulation. The estimator retains the ego-motion-aware
nonlinear relative motion model of the prior high-gain design but not its
differentiated ego coordinates: measured acceleration and yaw rate remain
exogenous inputs, so neither zero ego jerk nor zero yaw acceleration is
assumed. A direct body-velocity measurement gives a cascade
input-to-state stability certificate, and the target gains are obtained
from a semidefinite program that minimizes a bound on the noise-induced
velocity-error variance among gains satisfying that certificate. In
closed loop the lateral velocity is measured through the lateral force
balance of the controller's own tire model, because the kinematic
relation fails while the rear tire slips. The controller linearizes a
dynamic bicycle model with modified Fiala tires along one nonlinear
rollout per sample and solves two second-order cone programs. The first
minimizes the relaxation of sampled collision constraints written with
fixed distance-dual multipliers; the second keeps that relaxation and
minimizes the violation of a decrease condition on one quadratic
control-Lyapunov function. The horizon extends until the plan separates
from the obstacle and can remain on the road, and the estimator's current
error enclosure tightens the constraints of each hold. In fourteen
collision-threat encounters with exact states, all runs end
collision-free on the road with cruise recovered. With the estimator and
bounded sensor noise, 82 of 84 runs do so and two stop without a solved
command; none collides or leaves the road. The plant of these trials is
the prediction model itself. The estimation certificate is
continuous-time, the constraints act on a sampled affine prediction, and
the controller carries no collision-avoidance or recursive-feasibility
certificate for the nonlinear vehicle.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{L}{ane}-keeping systems (LKSs) have been extensively studied
and widely deployed in production vehicles \cite{WeiPfefferEdelmann2024}.
Conventional LKSs, however, primarily regulate the ego vehicle's lateral
displacement and heading relative to a lane reference. When an obstacle vehicle
poses a collision risk, avoidance is often left to the driver. This limitation
has motivated considerable research on integrated lane-keeping and vehicle
collision-avoidance systems
\cite{TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013,FahmyGhanyBaumann2018,
ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021,BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022}. These systems retain lane
keeping as the nominal behavior and initiate an automated evasive response when
a collision risk arises.

Collision avoidance has also been studied extensively, but most existing
studies assume that the motion states of the ego and obstacle vehicles are
accurately available. This assumption ignores estimation uncertainty, which can
invalidate controller safety guarantees and increase collision risk. A
safety-critical controller must therefore account for errors in the estimated
states of both vehicles, especially in collision-avoidance scenarios, in which
the ego vehicle often performs complex maneuvers that make the estimation of
surrounding-vehicle states nontrivial. Alai \emph{et al.} developed high-gain nonlinear observers
for obstacle-vehicle trajectory estimation and demonstrated the importance of an
accurate relative motion model for such tracking
\cite{AlaiZemoucheRajamani2024,AlaiSharmaAlexanderRajamani2024}. Sharma
\emph{et al.} subsequently showed that reliable obstacle tracking under complex
ego maneuvers requires a nonlinear relative motion model (NRMM) that explicitly
accounts for ego motion \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}. Their observer
differentiates that model into third-order companion coordinates, which reduces
the design to a single high-gain theorem but introduces derivatives of the ego
acceleration and yaw rate and therefore assumes that the ego vehicle has zero
body-frame jerk and zero yaw acceleration. These assumptions do not generally
hold in the collision-avoidance scenarios
considered here. This paper therefore develops an estimator that retains their
relative motion model but not its differentiated ego coordinates, so that
neither zero ego jerk nor zero ego yaw acceleration is assumed.

Beyond estimation uncertainty, collision-avoidance performance also depends
on the fidelity of the vehicle model and the generality of the maneuver
formulation. To retain online tractability, many existing controllers rely on
reduced-order models and restricted operating assumptions. Examples include
kinematic bicycle or locally linear lateral models whose validity is tied to
small slip, small lateral acceleration, or low-curvature highway operation
\cite{HeZengZhangSreenath2021,TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013,
BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022}. Other formulations use simplified acceleration
models or two-degree-of-freedom vehicle dynamics under small-angle and linear
tire-force approximations
\cite{ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021,AmmourOrjuelaBasset2022,
DingZhuangHuEtAl2024}. These abstractions are appropriate within their intended
operating envelopes, but evasive steering and braking couple the longitudinal
and lateral vehicle dynamics through tire forces and load transfer
\cite{GuoHuWang2016,FahmyGhanyBaumann2018,HeLiuLvJiLiu2019,
BealBoyd2019}. Moreover, the available tire force becomes strongly nonlinear
as the tires approach saturation
\cite{ParkGerdes2017,ForsAnistratovOlofssonNielsen2021,
ThompsonDallasGohBalachandran2024}.
A further limitation is that collision avoidance is often organized around a
prescribed set of maneuvers. Finite-state or discrete high-level controllers
may select among lane keeping, braking, overtaking, left or right lane changes,
and returning to the original lane
\cite{HeZengZhangSreenath2021,AmmourOrjuelaBasset2022,
ChenAksunGuvenc2025}. Other methods evaluate a predetermined single-lane-change
trajectory or a small number of straight- or low-curvature-road obstacle
configurations
\cite{HeLiuLvJiLiu2019,TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013,
BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022,DingZhuangHuEtAl2024}. Such formulations can be
effective in the scenarios for which they are designed, and dedicated methods
have also been developed for emergency avoidance on curved roads
\cite{LaiYang2023}. However, accommodating oblique cut-ins or interactions that
do not conform to predefined lane and vehicle roles may require additional
maneuver modes, transition logic, or scenario-specific redesign. These
limitations motivate a unified collision-avoidance controller based on a
dynamic vehicle model that continuously coordinates steering and
braking without prescribing the avoidance maneuver in advance.

Model fidelity and maneuver flexibility alone do not establish closed-loop
safety. Several MPC-based collision-avoidance controllers impose geometric or
dynamic constraints over a finite prediction horizon and demonstrate effective
evasive maneuvers, but do not prove that collision-free feasibility is
preserved after each receding-horizon update
\cite{TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013,
ForsAnistratovOlofssonNielsen2021,AmmourOrjuelaBasset2022,
ThompsonDallasGohBalachandran2024}. Control barrier functions (CBFs) provide
sufficient conditions for rendering a prescribed safe set forward invariant
\cite{AmesXuGrizzleTabuada2017}, but this guarantee remains conditional on the
existence of an admissible input satisfying the barrier condition. Conventional
CBF--QP safety filters enforce this condition pointwise and typically minimize
the instantaneous deviation from a nominal input. Consequently, feasibility at
the current state does not ensure that safe inputs remain available at
subsequent states \cite{ZengZhangLiSreenath2021}, and a pointwise filter may
fail to recognize an evasive trajectory that temporarily reduces longitudinal
separation while building sufficient lateral clearance, resulting in myopic or
deadlocked behavior \cite{BlackJankovicSharmaPanagou2023}. Predictive CBF and
MPC--CBF formulations mitigate this limitation by incorporating future system
evolution. Zeng \emph{et al.} imposed discrete-time CBF constraints over an MPC
horizon, but identified persistent feasibility as an open problem for a fixed
CBF decay rate and assumed perfect vehicle-position estimates in their racing
example \cite{ZengZhangSreenath2021}. Breeden and Panagou constructed a
path-based predictive CBF, whereas Wabersich and Zeilinger and Huang
\emph{et al.} derived predictive CBFs from finite-horizon optimal-control value
functions associated with terminal safe sets
\cite{BreedenPanagou2022,WabersichZeilinger2023,
HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025}. The presence of a prediction horizon alone,
however, does not guarantee that a collision-free optimization remains feasible
under repeated receding-horizon updates, particularly when state-estimation
uncertainty is considered.

This paper does not close that gap. It reports an estimator and a
controller designed as one system, evaluated together in simulation, and
separates what is certified from what is only measured. The estimator of
Section~\ref{sec:estimator} carries a continuous-time input-to-state
stability (ISS) certificate under declared sensor, model and
operating-domain bounds, and the shape of its target gains is chosen
inside that certificate by a semidefinite program that minimizes a bound
on the velocity-error variance caused by relative-position measurement
noise. Its operating domain is derived from the controller's state
limits, and in closed loop its lateral-velocity measurement inverts the
lateral force balance of the controller's tire model under the input the
controller holds. The controller of Section~\ref{sec:control} uses no
maneuver template. At each sample it linearizes the dynamic bicycle model
with modified Fiala tires along one nonlinear rollout and solves two
second-order cone programs in strict priority order. The first minimizes
the relaxation of sampled predictive collision constraints, written with
distance-dual multipliers that are held fixed during the solve
\cite{LiZhangGuoLenzoGuo2023}. The second, without increasing that
relaxation, minimizes the violation of a decrease condition on one
quadratic control-Lyapunov function, synthesized offline by a linear
matrix inequality; the same function is used during avoidance and during
the return to cruise. Road edges, a rear-tire adhesion envelope and the
estimator's sideslip cone are constraints of both programs, and the
estimator's current error enclosure tightens the constraints of every
hold. The horizon is not fixed: it ends at the first node of the
linearization rollout at which the ego separates from the obstacle and
can stop its lateral motion before either road edge. A potential-field
rollout around the moving obstacle initializes the first sample and
thereby fixes the side on which the obstacle is passed. The ordering of
the two programs resembles the predictive barrier construction of
\cite{WabersichZeilinger2023}, and the scheme follows the
real-time-iteration idea of \cite{DiehlBockSchloder2005}, but its
constraints are sampled and affine, its terminal condition is not
invariant, and it carries no certificate of collision avoidance or
recursive feasibility for the nonlinear vehicle.
Section~\ref{sec:results} reports fourteen collision-threat encounters
with exact states and the same encounters with the estimator and bounded
sensor noise for six noise seeds. All exact-state runs and 82 of the 84
estimator-in-the-loop runs end collision-free on the road with cruise
recovered; the other two stop without a solved command, and their cause
is analyzed. The plant of these trials is the prediction model with
nominal parameters, and the perception pipeline of
Section~\ref{sec:perception} is not yet in that loop.

\section{System Overview}

\begin{figure}[!t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.94\columnwidth]
        {driving_environment_curved_v08.pdf}
    \caption{Representative two-lane driving environment. The road-edge
    lines separate the travel lanes from curb-side emergency lanes, each wide
    enough to accommodate a vehicle, while the road centerline lies midway
    between the curb-defined boundaries. The ego vehicle follows the
    nominal path, with an obstacle vehicle cutting into the ego
    vehicle's lane from the adjacent lane.}
    \label{fig:driving_environment}
\end{figure}

We consider an ego vehicle traveling on a road whose drivable surface is
bounded by road curbs. A nominal cruise path is supplied as known route
geometry, while other vehicles travel within
or enter the sensed road environment, as illustrated in
Fig.~\ref{fig:driving_environment}. The reference is straight or of
constant curvature. The ego vehicle is equipped with a global
navigation satellite system (GNSS) receiver for global position and velocity
measurements and with an inertial measurement unit (IMU) and a gyroscope
for body-frame
acceleration and yaw-rate measurements, respectively. LiDAR provides
geometric measurements of road curbs together with
relative-position measurements of obstacle vehicles, while camera-based
perception detects obstacle vehicles. The problem addressed
in this paper is to design an integrated perception--estimation--control
system such that, in the absence of a predicted collision risk, the ego
vehicle follows the nominal path at a prescribed constant speed.
When continuing this nominal behavior is predicted to create a collision
risk, the system coordinates steering and longitudinal actuation to maintain
collision separation while keeping the ego-vehicle footprint within the
curb-defined drivable surface, and subsequently returns the vehicle to the
nominal path and prescribed speed once the risk has been resolved. Thus, the
curb-defined road boundary is a physical constraint of the intended system,
whereas the nominal path and reference speed are recoverable nominal
objectives. The controller developed below imposes the road edges as
declared lateral clearances on either side of the nominal path. The curb
perception module does not yet supply them: in
Section~\ref{sec:results} the clearances are scenario data, and the
avoidance maneuvers leave the lane but stay on the paved road.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]
        {ego_obstacle_dynamics_v02.pdf}
    \caption{Ego--obstacle dynamics. The ego vehicle (blue) and an obstacle
    vehicle (orange) travel on courses that may lead to a collision. The
    vehicle frames are centered at the vehicle centers of mass, the yaw
    angles \(\psi_E\) and \(\psi_C\) orient them relative to the inertial
    \(X_I\) axis, the sideslip angles \(\beta_E\) and \(\beta_C\)
    separate the velocities \(V_E\) and \(V_C\) from the vehicle
    longitudinal axes, and the course angles \(\gamma_E\) and
    \(\gamma_C\) orient the velocities relative to the inertial \(X_I\)
    axis.}
    \label{fig:ego_obstacle_frames}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:ego_obstacle_frames} details the vehicle dynamics behind
the scenario of Fig.~\ref{fig:driving_environment}: an ego vehicle
with speed \(V_E\) and yaw angle \(\psi_E\) and an obstacle vehicle
with speed \(V_C\) and yaw angle \(\psi_C\) travel on courses that may
lead to a collision. The controller predicts with one ego model, the dynamic
bicycle with modified Fiala tires. The estimator core uses only its
kinematics. Its lateral-velocity measurement comes either from the
kinematic single-track relation between yaw rate, speed and sideslip, in
which no cornering stiffness appears, or, when the controller's held input
is available, from the lateral force balance of the same tire model
(Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided}). With
\(\bm p_E\in\R^2\) and \(\bm v_E\in\R^2\) the inertial position and
velocity,
\(\bm v^E=[v_{x,E}\;\;v_{y,E}]^{\trans}=R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_E\)
the body velocity, and
\begin{equation}
    R(\psi_E)=\begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\psi_E&-\sin\psi_E\\
        \sin\psi_E&\cos\psi_E
    \end{bmatrix}
    \label{eq:planar_rotation}
\end{equation}
the body-to-inertial rotation, the position kinematics are
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\bm p}_E=\bm v_E=R(\psi_E)\bm v^E .
    \label{eq:position_kinematics}
\end{equation}
The body-frame force and moment balances are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    m_E\dot v_{x,E}&=F_{xf}^{b}+F_{xr}-F_R(v_{x,E})
        +m_Ev_{y,E}\dot\psi_E,\\
    m_E\dot v_{y,E}&=F_{yf}^{b}+F_{yr}-m_Ev_{x,E}\dot\psi_E,\\
    I_{z,E}\ddot\psi_E&=l_{f,E}F_{yf}^{b}-l_{r,E}F_{yr},
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:bicycle_model}
\end{equation}
where $[F_{xf}^{b},F_{yf}^{b}]^{\trans}
=R(\delta_F)[F_{xf},F_{yf}]^{\trans}$ rotates the front wheel forces into
the body frame, and $F_R$ is the passive road load
\eqref{eq:road_load}. The slip convention and modified Fiala
forces are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \alpha_f=\operatorname{atan2}(v_{y,E}+l_{f,E}\dot\psi_E,\,v_{x,E})
        -\delta_F,\\
    \alpha_r=\operatorname{atan2}(v_{y,E}-l_{r,E}\dot\psi_E,\,v_{x,E}),\\
    F_{yf}=\Phi_f(\alpha_f;Q_f),\qquad F_{yr}=\Phi_r(\alpha_r;Q_r).
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:tire_forces}
\end{equation}
The model is used on the forward-speed domain
$v_{x,E}>v_{\rm f}>0$. For axle \(i\in\{f,r\}\), let \(t=\tan\alpha\), \(C_i>0\) be its
nominal cornering stiffness, and \(Q_i=\eta_i\mu_iF_{zi}\). Following
\cite[Sec.~II-A, eqs.~(7), (11) and (13)]{FahmyGhanyBaumann2018},
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_i(\alpha;Q_i)=
    \begin{cases}
    -C_it+\dfrac{C_i^2}{3Q_i}|t|t-\dfrac{C_i^3}{27Q_i^2}t^3,
       & |\alpha|<\alpha_{{\rm sl},i},\\[3pt]
    -Q_i\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha), & |\alpha|\geq\alpha_{{\rm sl},i},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:fiala_force}
\end{equation}
where \(\alpha_{{\rm sl},i}=\arctan(3Q_i/C_i)\),
\(\eta_i=\sqrt{1-\beta^2}\), and \(F_{xi}=\beta\mu_iF_{zi}\)
for \(|\beta|<1\). The same signed input \(\beta\) applies to both axles: negative values brake and positive values request throttle. Static loads are
\(F_{zf}=m_Egl_{r,E}/L\) and \(F_{zr}=m_Egl_{f,E}/L\), with
\(L=l_{f,E}+l_{r,E}\). Each axle represents an equivalent wheel pair.
Here \(m_E\) and \(I_{z,E}\) denote mass and yaw inertia; the lever arms
are \(l_{f,E},l_{r,E}\), and \(\delta_F\) is front steering.
We use the symmetric branch condition and
\cite[eq.~(13)]{FahmyGhanyBaumann2018}; the additional arctangent printed
in \cite[eq.~(12)]{FahmyGhanyBaumann2018} is inconsistent with it. At
\(|\beta|=1\), the force law is defined by
\(Q_i=0\), \(\Phi_i=0\), without evaluating its singular polynomial.
The derivatives of \(\Phi_i\) with respect to \((\alpha,\beta)\) are
unbounded at these endpoints; linearization points are kept strictly
inside the input interval.
The controller differentiates this force along its linearization
trajectory (Section~\ref{subsec:predictionModel}). The saturation branch
bounds the lateral force of the nonlinear model; on the affine prediction
the rear axle is kept below that branch by a cone constraint
(Section~\ref{subsec:envelope}), and the front axle is not constrained.
The speed, sideslip, and course of
Fig.~\ref{fig:ego_obstacle_frames} are
\begin{equation}
    V_E=\normtwo{\bm v_E},
    \qquad
    \beta_E=\arctan\frac{v_{y,E}}{v_{x,E}},
    \qquad
    \gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E .
    \label{eq:yaw_from_course}
\end{equation}
The obstacle vehicle has its own unobserved inputs. The tracker of
Section~\ref{subsec:targetTracker} and the obstacle prediction of
Section~\ref{subsec:predictionModel} both use the constant-acceleration,
constant-sideslip motion model of \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}.

The architecture that realizes this behavior comprises perception,
estimation, and control modules, as summarized in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}.
The perception module processes synchronized camera and LiDAR data jointly
for obstacle-vehicle detection and relative localization. LiDAR data are also
passed to a dedicated road-curb extraction component, whose output specifies
the curb-defined road boundary; the nominal path is known route geometry and
requires no extraction. The estimator combines the obstacle-relative
positions with the GNSS, IMU, and gyroscope measurements to estimate the motion
states of the ego vehicle and the single tracked obstacle vehicle. Each
estimate is accompanied by an error enclosure, valid for
the continuous-time observer under the declared sensor, model and
operating-domain premises. The controller consumes this enclosure: it
tightens the collision, road, state and terminal constraints of every
hold by the enclosure published at the current sample, and it refreshes
the obstacle state and its motion parameters from every new estimate.
The controller in turn passes the input it holds to the estimator, whose
operating domain is derived from the controller's state limits. The
known nominal path supplies the control reference and, with declared
lateral clearances, the road edges. At each sampling instant, the
controller linearizes the vehicle model along a nonlinear rollout. It
builds the collision constraints from the obstacle estimate and the
control-Lyapunov function from the reference path and the prescribed
speed, solves two convex programs in strict priority order, and issues
the steering and longitudinal actuation commands of the second. The
perception module is developed and tested separately on simulated camera
and LiDAR captures; in the closed-loop trials the relative-position
measurement is synthetic with bounded noise, and the perceived
road-boundary branch is not used.

\subsection{Obstacle-Vehicle Perception}
Obstacle vehicles are detected in the camera images by a YOLO
detector \cite{RedmonDivvalaGirshickFarhadi2016}, which returns a
two-dimensional bounding box for each of them. The LiDAR point cloud
is then projected onto the image, as shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:obstacle_perception}(a). Each LiDAR point \(\bm q\) is
transformed into the camera frame and from there into pixel
coordinates,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \begin{bmatrix}X&Y&Z\end{bmatrix}^{\trans}
    =\bm R_{CL}\,\bm q+\bm t_{CL},\\
    \begin{bmatrix}u\\v\end{bmatrix}
    =\frac{f}{Z}
    \begin{bmatrix}X\\Y\end{bmatrix}
    +\begin{bmatrix}W/2\\H/2\end{bmatrix},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:lidar_camera_projection}
\end{equation}
where \((\bm R_{CL},\bm t_{CL})\) are the LiDAR-to-camera extrinsics,
\((X,Y,Z)\) are the point coordinates in the camera frame with \(Z>0\)
the optical depth, \(f\) is the focal length, and \(W\) and
\(H\) are the image width and height. The LiDAR points whose pixel coordinates \((u,v)\) fall
inside a detected bounding box are associated with that vehicle, and
the ground and other extraneous points among them are removed,
leaving the points reflected from the obstacle vehicle.

In this paper, the physical outline of a vehicle is regarded as a
rectangle of known length and width in the top view. Its planar pose,
center and heading, is fitted to the retained points, as shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:obstacle_perception}(b), by a damped Gauss--Newton
iteration. The objective penalizes the distance of the lower-body points
to the rectangle edges and the distance of every point outside the
rectangle; roof and trunk returns, which lie inside the footprint, would
otherwise be pulled onto its edges and rotate the fit. The iteration
starts from the relative pose predicted by the estimator of
Section~\ref{sec:estimator} for the same instant. The prediction is only
the starting point: it does not enter the objective, and a frame without
a fit is passed to the estimator as a missing measurement, never as the
prediction itself. The center of the fitted rectangle yields the
measured relative position of the obstacle vehicle,
\begin{equation}
    \bm\rho=\begin{bmatrix}\rho_x&\rho_y\end{bmatrix}^{\trans}\in\R^2 ,
    \label{eq:perception_relative_position}
\end{equation}
expressed in the ego-vehicle frame.

\subsection{Road-Curb Extraction and Tracking}

\subsubsection{Local Geometric Feature Analysis}
A curb is a step of a few centimetres in an otherwise smooth road
surface, so the local geometry of the LiDAR returns changes abruptly
where the beams cross it. Four local geometric features are considered
to capture this discontinuity---adjacent-ring compression, vertical
step, radial jump, and local shape. In the following, \(\bm p_{i,j}\)
and \(z_{i,j}\) denote the horizontal position and height of the
return at elevation ring \(i\) and azimuth \(j\), and
\(d_{i,j}=\|\bm p_{i,j}\|\) its horizontal range.

\paragraph{Adjacent-ring compression}
Flat ground predicts the horizontal spacing between consecutive rings
from the sensor height and beam angles alone, and a raised curb face
compresses the spacing \(\Delta_{i,j}\) observed between ring \(i\)
and its upper neighbor below that prediction \(\hat\Delta_{i}\),
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_{i,j}
    =\frac{\hat\Delta_{i}-\Delta_{i,j}}{\hat\Delta_{i}} .
    \label{eq:curb_ring_compression}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Vertical step}
The height of the curb face appears directly as a step between
same-ring neighbors,
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{i,j}
    =\max\bigl(|z_{i,j}-z_{i,j-1}|,\,|z_{i,j+1}-z_{i,j}|\bigr).
    \label{eq:curb_vertical_step}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Radial jump}
On flat ground the horizontal range varies smoothly with azimuth,
whereas a beam climbing the curb face is pulled inward, which breaks
the smoothness captured by the second difference
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_{i,j}
    =|d_{i,j-1}-2d_{i,j}+d_{i,j+1}| .
    \label{eq:curb_radial_jump}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Local shape}
On the road surface a ring sweeps an arc whose chord is perpendicular
to the line of sight, whereas along a curb the returns align with the
road edge, so the normalized projection of the chord of the same-ring
neighbors onto the line of sight
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{i,j}
    =\frac{\bigl|\bm p_{i,j}^{\trans}
        (\bm p_{i,j+1}-\bm p_{i,j-1})\bigr|}
      {\|\bm p_{i,j}\|\,\|\bm p_{i,j+1}-\bm p_{i,j-1}\|}
    \label{eq:curb_local_shape}
\end{equation}
rises where the ring turns along the curb.

\subsubsection{Curb Fitting}
The road heading \(\varphi\) is estimated from the single longest
near-linear structure among the candidates and is held near a prior
propagated with the ego motion. It defines a road-aligned frame in
which each curb is the quadratic
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{b}}(x)=c_{2}x^{2}+c_{1}x+c_{0},
    \qquad x\in[x_{\min},x_{\max}],
    \label{eq:curb_quadratic}
\end{equation}
one curve per side over a finite forward domain. The coefficients are
fitted to the side's candidate points by least squares.

\subsubsection{Curb Tracking}
A single-frame fit can latch onto a structure that merely resembles a curb and jump far from the tracked
geometry, so the fit of frame \(k\), with coefficients \(c_{i,k}\), is
admitted only through an amplitude-limiting filter on the coefficient
increments,
\begin{equation}
    |c_{i,k}-c_{i,k-1}|\le T_i,
    \qquad i\in\{0,1,2\},
    \label{eq:curb_amplitude_limit}
\end{equation}
and a fit violating any bound leaves the frame to the prediction. The
curve itself is tracked through three points \(\bm p_s\) placed on it
at constant longitudinal stations \(x_s\). Between frames each point
is propagated by the measured ego motion,
\begin{equation}
    \bm p_s^-=A\,\bm p_s+\bm b,
    \qquad
    P_s^-=A P_s A^{\trans}+Q,
    \label{eq:curb_point_prediction}
\end{equation}
where \((A,\bm b)\) is the rigid transform from the preceding to the
current road frame, \(P_s\) is the point covariance, and \(Q\) is the
process noise. The admitted fit, evaluated at the same stations,
yields the measured points \(\bm z_s\) with noise covariance \(R\),
and each tracked point follows the Kalman update
\begin{equation}
    K_s=P_s^-\,(P_s^-+R)^{-1},
    \qquad
    \bm p_s=\bm p_s^-+K_s(\bm z_s-\bm p_s^-).
    \label{eq:curb_point_update}
\end{equation}

\section{Estimator Design}
\label{sec:estimator}

The ego body-velocity observer is designed first and the target tracker
second. Their dynamics form a two-stage cascade; the parallel yaw observer
and inertial position output are used for reconstruction and do not feed
this cascade. The measurements are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm y_p&=\bm p_E+\bm n_p, &\quad
    \bm v_m&=\bm v_E+\bm n_v,\\
    \bm a_m&=\bm a_E+\bm n_a, &\quad
    u_3&=\dot\psi_E+d_\omega,\\
    \bm y_R&=\bm\rho+\bm n_R,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:ego_measurements}
\end{equation}
with \(\bm a_E=R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\ddot{\bm p}_E\) the acceleration resolved
in the ego frame, \(\bm\rho\) the ego-frame relative position
\eqref{eq:target_coordinates}, whose measurement \(\bm y_R\) is the
perception output \eqref{eq:perception_relative_position}, and uniform
bounds \(\normtwo{\bm n_p}\leq\bar n_p\),
\(\normtwo{\bm n_v}\leq\bar n_v\), \(\normtwo{\bm n_a}\leq\bar n_a\),
\(|d_\omega|\leq\bar n_g\), \(\normtwo{\bm n_R}\leq\bar n_R\). Inertial
biases are compensated upstream, so no observer carries a bias state.

Two constructions of \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026} are replaced. Their
translational observer differentiates the ego kinematics into third-order
companion chains, which requires \(\dot{\bm a}_E\) and \(\ddot\psi_E\) and
therefore assumes both vanish; here \(\bm a_m\) and \(u_3\) remain
exogenous inputs of every stage and nothing is differentiated. Their yaw
stage switches between a heading inferred from the acceleration direction
and the course angle, and reading the course as the yaw is exact only at
\(\beta_E=0\) because \(\gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E\); here the
same relation supplies a direct body-velocity measurement and, when
informative, a certified heading correspondence for the parallel yaw observer.

\begin{assumption}
\label{asm:ego}
\(0\leq V_E|_{\min}\leq V_E\leq V_E|_{\max}\),
$v_{x,E}\geq V_E\cos\beta_{\rm dom}$, and
\(|\dot\psi_E|\leq\bar\omega_E\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}
\label{asm:singleTrack}
The ego kinematic single-track relation holds with bounded mismatch,
\begin{equation}
    \dot\psi_E=\frac{V_E\sin\beta_E}{l_{r,E}}+d_{\rm st},
    \qquad|d_{\rm st}|\leq\bar d_{\rm st},
    \label{eq:single_track_mismatch}
\end{equation}
and \(|\beta_E|\leq\beta_{\rm dom}<\pi/2\).
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{asm:singleTrack} bounds unmodeled rear-axle slip. The
identification of course and yaw that it replaces corresponds to
\(\beta_E\equiv0\).

\subsection{Certified Course--Yaw Correspondence}
\label{subsec:courseCertificate}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:course}
Let Assumption~\ref{asm:singleTrack} hold and
\(V_m=\normtwo{\bm v_m}>\bar n_v\), put
\(\underline V=V_m-\bar n_v\), and suppose \(|l_{r,E}u_3|\leq V_m\).
Define
\begin{equation}
    \hat\beta=\arcsin\frac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m},
    \qquad
    y_F=\angle\bm v_m-\hat\beta .
    \label{eq:course_definition}
\end{equation}
Then, with
\begin{equation}
    \bar e_{\sin}
    =\frac{l_{r,E}}{\underline V}
    \Bigl(\bar n_g+\bar d_{\rm st}+\frac{|u_3|\bar n_v}{V_m}\Bigr),
    \label{eq:sine_error_bound}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \bar e_\beta=\max_{\pm}\Bigl|
    \arcsin\bigl(\Pi\bigl[\tfrac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m}\pm\bar e_{\sin}\bigr]\bigr)
    -\hat\beta\Bigr|,
    \label{eq:sideslip_error}
\end{equation}
where \(\Pi\) clips to \([-\sin\beta_{\rm dom},\sin\beta_{\rm dom}]\), one has
\(|\beta_E-\hat\beta|\leq\bar e_\beta\) and
\begin{equation}
    |y_F-\psi_E|\leq B_F:=\arcsin\frac{\bar n_v}{V_m}+\bar e_\beta .
    \label{eq:course_certificate}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
By \eqref{eq:single_track_mismatch},
\(\sin\beta_E=l_{r,E}(u_3-d_\omega-d_{\rm st})/V_E\), so
\begin{equation}
    \Bigl|\sin\beta_E-\frac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m}\Bigr|
    \leq l_{r,E}\Bigl[\frac{|d_\omega|+|d_{\rm st}|}{V_E}
    +|u_3|\frac{|V_m-V_E|}{V_EV_m}\Bigr],
    \label{eq:sine_derivation}
\end{equation}
and \(|V_m-V_E|\leq\bar n_v\), \(V_E\geq\underline V\) give
\eqref{eq:sine_error_bound}. Since \(|\sin\beta_E|\leq\sin\beta_{\rm dom}\),
the true sine lies in the clipped interval, and \(\arcsin\) is increasing
on it, which gives \eqref{eq:sideslip_error}. For the course, \(\bm v_E\)
lies in the disk of radius \(\bar n_v\) about \(\bm v_m\); the disk
excludes the origin, so \(\bm v_E\) lies in its tangent cone and
\(|\angle\bm v_m-\gamma_E|\leq\arcsin(\bar n_v/V_m)\). Subtracting
\(\gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E\) yields \eqref{eq:course_certificate}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The correspondence is used only where these informativeness conditions
hold. Otherwise it supplies the whole circle as a yaw enclosure. Neither
case changes the body-velocity innovation developed next, which remains
defined at standstill.

\subsection{Ego Observers}
\label{subsec:egoObserver}

With $J=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$,
$\nabla_u\bm z=\dot{\bm z}+uJ\bm z$, and
$\bm v^E=R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_E$, define
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \phi(V,w)=\sqrt{\max\{V^2-w^2,V^2\cos^2\beta_{\rm dom}\}},\\
    \bm y_v=[\phi(V_m,l_{r,E}u_3),\ l_{r,E}u_3]^{\trans},\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm v}^E=\bm a_m+k_v(\bm y_v-\hat{\bm v}^E).
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:ego_observer}
\end{equation}
On the true forward cone, $\phi(V_E,v_{y,E})=v_{x,E}$. The extension
remains defined for every measured speed, including zero, and its lateral
component is not clipped. Thus
$y_{v,2}-v_{y,E}=l_{r,E}(d_\omega+d_{\rm st})$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:egoRows}
Let $\bm e_v=\hat{\bm v}^E-\bm v^E$, $k_v>0$, and
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    C_\omega(k)=\max_{V\in\{V_E|_{\min},V_E|_{\max}\}}
    \Bigl[(V\sin\beta_{\rm dom}+kl_{r,E}\tan\beta_{\rm dom})^2\\
        +(kl_{r,E}-V\cos\beta_{\rm dom})^2\Bigr]^{1/2}.
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:common_gyro_bound}
\end{equation}
Under Assumptions~\ref{asm:ego}--\ref{asm:singleTrack},
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    D^+\normtwo{\bm e_v}\leq-k_v\normtwo{\bm e_v}+\bar\delta_v,\\
    \bar\delta_v=\bar n_a+k_v\sec\beta_{\rm dom}\,\bar n_v
      +C_\omega(k_v)\bar n_g
      +k_vl_{r,E}\sec\beta_{\rm dom}\,\bar d_{\rm st}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:ego_rows}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Put $\nu=V_m-V_E$, so $|\nu|\leq\bar n_v$. Integrating the
piecewise derivatives of $\phi$ along the segment between
$(V_E,v_{y,E})$ and $(V_m,l_{r,E}u_3)$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \bm y_v-\bm v^E=\alpha\bm e_1\nu
        +l_{r,E}\bm g(\zeta)(d_\omega+d_{\rm st}),\\
    \bm g(\zeta)=[-\zeta,1]^{\trans},\\
    \cos\beta_{\rm dom}\leq\alpha\leq\sec\beta_{\rm dom},\qquad
    |\zeta|\leq\tan\beta_{\rm dom}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:velocity_measurement_error}
\end{equation}
Continuity covers branch crossings and the origin. Substituting before
taking norms yields
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \dot{\bm e}_v={}&(-k_vI-u_3J)\bm e_v+\bm n_a+k_v\alpha\bm e_1\nu\\
        &+(k_vl_{r,E}\bm g(\zeta)-J\bm v^E)d_\omega
          +k_vl_{r,E}\bm g(\zeta)d_{\rm st}.
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:velocity_error_derivation}
\end{equation}
The common gyro column has components
$[v_{y,E}-k_vl_{r,E}\zeta,\ k_vl_{r,E}-v_{x,E}]^{\trans}$.
Maximizing its norm over $\zeta$ and the forward cone, then using
convexity of its squared bound in speed, gives
\eqref{eq:common_gyro_bound}. The rotation is skew and contributes zero
to the norm derivative. The remaining terms give \eqref{eq:ego_rows}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The same gyro error enters measurement and propagation. Bounding those
terms separately would discard their cancellation; no statistical
independence is used in Lemma~\ref{lem:egoRows}. In particular, yaw error
does not enter the velocity comparison.

Yaw and inertial position are estimated in parallel:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \dot{\hat\psi}_E&=u_3+\chi_m k_\psi
        \operatorname{wrap}(y_F-\hat\psi_E),\\
    \dot{\hat{\bm p}}_E&=\bm v_m+k_p(\bm y_p-\hat{\bm p}_E),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:parallel_outputs}
\end{equation}
where $\chi_m=1$ only when the course correspondence is informative.
While the innovation stays on one branch of the wrap, the yaw error
\(e_\psi=\hat\psi_E-\psi_E\) obeys
\(\dot e_\psi=-k_\psi e_\psi+d_\omega+k_\psi(y_F-\psi_E)\) with
\(|y_F-\psi_E|\leq B_F\); during uninformative intervals only gyro
propagation is available. An independent orientation set $\Psi$ is
propagated with the gyro-error integral and intersected with each
informative circular arc from Lemma~\ref{lem:course}. Disconnected
components are retained, and the published bound is
$B_\psi=\sup_{\theta\in\Psi}d_{S^1}(\hat\psi_E,\theta)$ about the
actual observer estimate. An empty intersection invalidates orientation
outputs; it does not reset the observer. The position error
\(\bm e_p=\hat{\bm p}_E-\bm p_E\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    D^+\normtwo{\bm e_p}\leq-k_p\normtwo{\bm e_p}+\bar n_v+k_p\bar n_p,\\
    \limsup_{t\to\infty}\normtwo{\bm e_p}\leq\bar n_p+\bar n_v/k_p.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:position_error_bound}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Model-Aided Lateral Velocity}
\label{subsec:modelAided}

Assumption~\ref{asm:singleTrack} is a premise on the true motion. With
$l_{r,E}$ the rear-axle distance, $l_{r,E}d_{\rm st}$ is, up to sign,
the lateral slip speed of the rear tire, and a small $\bar d_{\rm st}$
therefore asserts that the rear tire barely slips. The avoidance
maneuvers of Section~\ref{sec:results} violate it: at 15\,m/s the rear
slip speed reached 0.9 to 4.3\,m/s, and bounds published under
$\bar d_{\rm st}$ failed to contain the truth. Enlarging
$\bar d_{\rm st}$ to the saturation slip of the tire makes every bound
five to seven times larger. When the estimator knows the ego model
\eqref{eq:bicycle_model}--\eqref{eq:fiala_force} and the input
$\bm u=[\delta_F,\beta]^{\trans}$ that the controller holds, the lateral
component of \eqref{eq:ego_observer} is instead taken from the
lateral force balance at the center of mass,
\begin{equation}
    m_Ea_{y}=G(v_{y,E}):=F_{xf}\sin\delta_F
        +F_{yf}(\alpha_f)\cos\delta_F+F_{yr}(\alpha_r),
    \label{eq:force_balance}
\end{equation}
where $a_y$ is the lateral component of $\bm a_E$, which the
accelerometer measures, and the slip angles \eqref{eq:tire_forces} depend
on $v_{y,E}$, on the yaw rate and, through $v_{x,E}$, on the speed. For
$v_{x,E}>0$ and $|\delta_F|<\pi/2$ both slip angles increase with
$v_{y,E}$ and both lateral forces are non-increasing in their slip
angle, so $G$ is non-increasing and the measured $a_y$ determines
$v_{y,E}$ wherever $G$ is strictly decreasing.

The measurement $c$ is the solution of \eqref{eq:force_balance} with the
nominal parameters and the measured $a_{m,y}$, $u_3$ and $V_m$, found by
bisection on the cone $|v_{y,E}|\leq V\sin\beta_{\rm dom}$. Its radius
is computed at the same time. Let the cornering stiffnesses and friction
coefficients lie within a relative uncertainty $\epsilon_{\rm par}$ of
their model values, and let $G_{\min}$ and $G_{\max}$ be the sums of the
per-axle extremes of the force over the sensor bounds on $u_3$ and
$V_m$ and over this parameter box. Each axle force is monotone in each of
these quantities, so the extremes are attained at the corners of the box,
and $G_{\min}$ and $G_{\max}$ are again non-increasing in $v_{y,E}$.
Every admissible lateral velocity then satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    G_{\min}(v_{y,E})\leq m_E(a_{m,y}+\bar n_a),\\
    G_{\max}(v_{y,E})\geq m_E(a_{m,y}-\bar n_a),
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:force_interval}
\end{equation}
which is an interval $[\underline c,\overline c]$, and
$\rho_c=\max(c-\underline c,\overline c-c)$ bounds $|c-v_{y,E}|$. An
empty interval means that the measurements contradict the model or the
cone; the ego enclosure is then declared invalid. With
$\bm y_v=[\phi(V_m,c),\,c]^{\trans}$ in \eqref{eq:ego_observer}, the
mean-value argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:egoRows} gives
$\normtwo{\bm y_v-\bm v^E}\leq\sec\beta_{\rm dom}(\bar n_v+\rho_c)$ on
the cone, and the velocity row of the comparison becomes
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    D^+\normtwo{\bm e_v}\leq{}&-k_v\normtwo{\bm e_v}+\bar n_a
        +\bar n_gV_E|_{\max}\\
        &+k_v\sec\beta_{\rm dom}\,(\bar n_v+\rho_c(t)).
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:model_aided_row}
\end{equation}
The kinematic mismatch $\bar d_{\rm st}$ no longer appears; the premises
are the model structure, the parameter box and the cone. The gyro
cancellation of Lemma~\ref{lem:egoRows} is not retained in
\eqref{eq:model_aided_row}. The radius $\rho_c$ is computed online and
is not uniform: near saturation the force no longer determines the slip,
and the interval widens, up to about 3\,m/s for
$\epsilon_{\rm par}=0.05$. The interval is then wide and asymmetric,
which is why its midpoint is not used as the measurement. The controller
keeps the rear tire below saturation and the motion inside the cone
(Section~\ref{subsec:envelope}), which keeps the inversion informative.
The same interval, divided by the speed interval, replaces
$\hat\beta\pm\bar e_\beta$ in the course correspondence of
Lemma~\ref{lem:course}. Without the model or a held input, the kinematic
measurement of Section~\ref{subsec:egoObserver} is used unchanged.

\subsection{Covariant Target Tracker}
\label{subsec:targetTracker}

The target model of \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}---constant scalar
acceleration and constant sideslip, hence constant curvature
\(\kappa=\sin\beta_C/l_{r,C}\)---is retained in covariant companion
coordinates
\begin{equation}
    \bm\rho=R_E^{\trans}(\bm p_C-\bm p_E),\quad
    \bm q=R_E^{\trans}\bm v_C,\quad
    \bm s=R_E^{\trans}\bm a_C,
    \label{eq:target_coordinates}
\end{equation}
with \(R_E=R(\psi_E)\). With \(\omega_E=\dot\psi_E\), the exact dynamics
in these coordinates are the third-order chain
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm\rho=\bm q-\bm v^E,\quad
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm q=\bm s,\quad
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm s=\Phi(\bm q,\bm s),\\
    \Phi=-\Omega^2\bm q+3A\Omega J\frac{\bm q}{V},\\
    V=\normtwo{\bm q},\quad
    A=\frac{\bm q^{\trans}\bm s}{V},\quad
    \Omega=\frac{(J\bm q)^{\trans}\bm s}{V^2}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_chain}
\end{equation}
Only \(\bm v^E\) and \(\omega_E\) enter, and the only extra terms are those
of the rotating frame; \(A\) and \(\Omega\) are reconstructions, not states.
The declared operating set is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    a\leq\normtwo{\bm q}\leq b,\quad
    |A|\leq A_{\max},\quad
    \normtwo{\bm\rho}\leq\rho_{\max},\\
    |\Omega|\leq\Omega_{\max}=\frac{b\sin\beta_{\max}}{l_{r,C}},\quad
    \normtwo{\bm s}\leq S=\sqrt{A_{\max}^2+b^2\Omega_{\max}^2}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_domain}
\end{equation}

Here \(a\) and \(b\) are the target speed bounds \(V_C|_{\min}\) and
\(V_C|_{\max}\), with $0<a<b$. Estimates may peak outside
\eqref{eq:target_domain}. Let $\Pi_r$ denote Euclidean projection onto
the ball of radius $r$ and $\operatorname{clip}_r$ scalar clipping to
$[-r,r]$. The observer evaluates
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    d=\max(\normtwo{\bm q},a),\quad
    \bm Q=\Pi_b(\bm q),\quad\tilde{\bm s}=\Pi_S(\bm s),\\
    A_e=\operatorname{clip}_{A_{\max}}
        (\bm Q^{\trans}\tilde{\bm s}/d),\\
    \Omega_e=\operatorname{clip}_{\Omega_{\max}}
        ((J\bm Q)^{\trans}\tilde{\bm s}/d^2),\\
    \Phi_e(\bm q,\bm s)=-\Omega_e^2\bm Q
        +3A_e\Omega_e J\bm q/d.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_extension}
\end{equation}
The direction factor uses $\bm q/d$. This extension agrees with $\Phi$
on \eqref{eq:target_domain} and is defined on $\R^4$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:lipschitz}
With
\(c_A=S/a\), \(c_\Omega=S\max(a^{-2},2b^{-2})\),
\(c_{Q\Omega}=S\max(a^{-1},2b^{-1})\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    L_q=\Omega_{\max}^2+2\Omega_{\max}c_{Q\Omega}
        +3\Omega_{\max}\Bigl(\frac{A_{\max}}a+c_A\Bigr)\\
        +3A_{\max}c_\Omega,\\
    L_s=\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}
        0&2\Omega_{\max}\\3\Omega_{\max}&3A_{\max}/a
        \end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_2,
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:lipschitz_constants}
\end{equation}
\(\Phi_e\) satisfies, for all \((\bm q,\bm s),(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \normtwo{\Phi_e(\bm q,\bm s)-\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})}\\
    \leq L_q\normtwo{\bm q-\hat{\bm q}}+L_s\normtwo{\bm s-\hat{\bm s}} .
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:global_lipschitz}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Projections and clips are nonexpansive, and $a>0$ makes $\Phi_e$ locally
Lipschitz and piecewise differentiable. It suffices to bound its
almost-everywhere Jacobians and integrate along the
segment joining the two arguments. Differentiating in the radial and
tangential basis at fixed \(\bm s\), with \(r=\normtwo{\bm q}\),
\(\bm Q\) the projected velocity and \(A_e\), \(\Omega_e\) the clipped
reconstructions that \(\Phi_e\) evaluates, gives
\(|D_qA_e|\leq S/\max(r,a)\) and \(|D_q\Omega_e|\leq S/\max(r,a)^2\) for
\(r\leq b\) and \(|D_qA_e|\leq bS/r^2\),
\(|D_q\Omega_e|\leq2bS/r^3\) for \(r\geq b\), whose suprema over \(r>0\) are
\(c_A\) and \(c_\Omega\), while
\(\normtwo{\bm Q}|D_q\Omega_e|\leq c_{Q\Omega}\).
Using $\|D_q\bm Q\|_2\leq1$, $\normtwo{\bm q/d}\leq1$ and
$\|D_q(\bm q/d)\|_2\leq1/a$ in \eqref{eq:target_extension} gives $L_q$.
At fixed $\bm q\ne0$, the Jacobian with respect to $\tilde{\bm s}$,
in the basis aligned with $\bm q$, is bounded entrywise in absolute value
by the matrix in \eqref{eq:lipschitz_constants}. Its induced norm is
therefore at most $L_s$. Composing with $\tilde{\bm s}=\Pi_S(\bm s)$
preserves this norm bound because $\|D_s\Pi_S\|_2\leq1$ almost
everywhere; continuity covers $\bm q=0$.
\end{IEEEproof}

The tracker is the covariant high-gain chain
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm\rho}&=\hat{\bm q}-\hat{\bm v}^E
        +l_1\omega\,(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm\rho}),\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm q}&=\hat{\bm s}
        +l_2\omega^2(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm\rho}),\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm s}&=\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})
        +l_3\omega^3(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm\rho}).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_observer}
\end{equation}
Let \(A_c\) be the integrator-chain matrix, \(C=\bm e_1^{\trans}\), and
\(A_l=A_c-\bm lC\). The constant shape $\bm l$ and bandwidth $\omega>0$ are
selected in Section~\ref{subsec:gainSelection}; the certificate below
holds for every pair that satisfies its matrix inequality.
For $\bm x_T=[\bm\rho^{\trans},\bm q^{\trans},\bm s^{\trans}]^{\trans}$
and $\bm e_T=\hat{\bm x}_T-\bm x_T$, put
$D=\diag(1,\omega^{-1},\omega^{-2})$ and set
\begin{equation}
    \bm\epsilon=(D\otimes I_2)\bm e_T,
    \qquad
    W_T=\sqrt{\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(P\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon}.
    \label{eq:target_metric}
\end{equation}
Write $\Delta\Phi_e=\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})-\Phi_e(\bm q,\bm s)$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:dissipation}
Let $P\succ0$, \(\bm b_P=P\bm e_3\), \(t_q,t_s>0\), and
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    M={}&-\omega(A_l^{\trans}P+PA_l)
       -t_q\frac{L_q^2}{\omega^2}\bm e_2\bm e_2^{\trans}\\
       &-t_sL_s^2\bm e_3\bm e_3^{\trans}
       -(t_q^{-1}+t_s^{-1})\bm b_P\bm b_P^{\trans}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:dissipation_matrix}
\end{equation}
If \(M\succeq2\lambda_TP\) then \(D^+W_T\leq-\lambda_TW_T\) in the
absence of exogenous error inputs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
In the coordinate \(\bm\epsilon\) the injection block becomes
\(\omega A_l\otimes I_2\), and the common rotation \(-u_3(I_3\otimes J)\)
is skew and contributes zero to
\(\tfrac{d}{dt}\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(P\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon\). The injection contributes
$\omega\bm\epsilon^{\trans}[(A_l^{\trans}P+PA_l)\otimes I_2]\bm\epsilon$.
Splitting $\Delta\Phi_e/\omega^2$ into its velocity and acceleration
increments, \eqref{eq:global_lipschitz} gives respective bounds
$(L_q/\omega)\normtwo{\bm\epsilon_2}$ and
$L_s\normtwo{\bm\epsilon_3}$. Each enters the derivative through
$2\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(\bm b_P\otimes I_2)$. Applying
\(2\bm z^{\trans}\bm d\leq\normtwo{\bm z}^2/t+t\normtwo{\bm d}^2\) once per
channel gives
\(\tfrac{d}{dt}W_T^2\leq-\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(M\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon
\leq-2\lambda_TW_T^2\).
Taking the square root gives the comparison, including $W_T=0$ by
continuity of the homogeneous error dynamics.
\end{IEEEproof}

\subsection{Cascade ISS}
\label{subsec:cascade}

Let $\bm\nu_\Phi$ be the model residual in the true last row,
$\nabla_{\omega_E}\bm s=\Phi(\bm q,\bm s)+\bm\nu_\Phi$, with
$\normtwo{\bm\nu_\Phi}\leq\bar\nu_\Phi$. For varying motion, write the
course curvature as $\kappa=\Omega/V$, which equals
$\sin\beta_C/l_{r,C}$ in the retained constant-sideslip model.
Differentiating the target velocity gives
$\bm\nu_\Phi=\dot A\bm q/V+V^2\dot\kappa J\bm q/V$, so declared
rate bounds imply
$\bar\nu_\Phi=(\bar{\dot A}^2+b^4\bar{\dot\kappa}^2)^{1/2}$.
Subtracting the true chain from \eqref{eq:target_observer} gives
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \dot{\bm\epsilon}={}&
        [\omega A_l\otimes I_2-u_3(I_3\otimes J)]\bm\epsilon\\
        &+\omega^{-2}(\bm e_3\otimes I_2)
            (\Delta\Phi_e-\bm\nu_\Phi)\\
        &-(\bm e_1\otimes I_2)\bm e_v
            -d_\omega(D\otimes J)\bm x_T\\
        &+\omega(\bm l\otimes I_2)\bm n_R.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_error_dynamics}
\end{equation}
All core couplings are feedforward. Cauchy--Schwarz in the metric
\eqref{eq:target_metric} bounds the four input channels by
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    g_{Tv}=\sqrt{P_{11}},\qquad
    g_R=\omega\sqrt{\bm l^{\trans}P\bm l},\\
    g_\omega=\sqrt{\bm h^{\trans}|P|\bm h},\qquad
    g_\Phi=\sqrt{P_{33}}/\omega^2,\\
    \bm h=[\rho_{\max},\ b/\omega,\ S/\omega^2]^{\trans},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_disturbance_coefficients}
\end{equation}
where $|P|$ is entrywise. Thus
$D^+W_T\leq-\lambda_TW_T+g_{Tv}\normtwo{\bm e_v}
+g_\omega\bar n_g+g_R\bar n_R+g_\Phi\bar\nu_\Phi$.
Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:egoRows}, this gives
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \bm\chi=[\normtwo{\bm e_v},\ W_T]^{\trans},\qquad
    D^+\bm\chi\leq H\bm\chi+\bm d,\\
    H=\begin{bmatrix}-k_v&0\\g_{Tv}&-\lambda_T\end{bmatrix},\qquad
    \bm d=\begin{bmatrix}\bar\delta_v\\
    g_\omega\bar n_g+g_R\bar n_R+g_\Phi\bar\nu_\Phi\end{bmatrix}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:cascade_comparison}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Cascade ISS]
\label{thm:cascadeISS}
Let Assumptions~\ref{asm:ego}--\ref{asm:singleTrack}, the hypotheses of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:egoRows}, \ref{lem:lipschitz} and \ref{lem:dissipation},
the true target domain \eqref{eq:target_domain} and the declared continuous
sensor and model-error bounds hold, with $k_v,\lambda_T>0$. Then $H$ is
Metzler and Hurwitz, and $\bm c=-H^{-\trans}\bm 1>0$ gives the linear
copositive ISS function $V_c=\bm c^{\trans}\bm\chi$. For any
$\bm z_0\geq\bm\chi(0)$,
\begin{equation}
    \bm\chi(t)\leq\bm z(t)
    =e^{Ht}\bm z_0+\int_0^t e^{H(t-\tau)}\bm d\,d\tau,
    \label{eq:comparison_solution}
\end{equation}
and $\limsup_{t\to\infty}\bm\chi(t)\leq-H^{-1}\bm d$. The physical
component bounds are
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm e_{T,i}}\leq c_{T,i}W_T,\qquad
    \bm c_T=\diag(1,\omega,\omega^2)\sqrt{\diag(P^{-1})}.
    \label{eq:component_extraction}
\end{equation}
For the fixed design, the core error
$\bm e=[\bm e_v^{\trans},\bm e_T^{\trans}]^{\trans}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm e(t)}\leq C_e e^{-\alpha_e t}\normtwo{\bm e(0)}
        +\Gamma_e\|\bm\eta\|_{\infty,[0,t]},
    \label{eq:core_exponential_iss}
\end{equation}
with constants $C_e,\alpha_e,\Gamma_e>0$ and disturbance vector
$\bm\eta=[\bm n_a^{\trans},\bm n_v^{\trans},d_\omega,d_{\rm st},
\bm n_R^{\trans},\bm\nu_\Phi^{\trans}]^{\trans}$.
Here $\|\bm\eta\|_{\infty,[0,t]}$
is the essential supremum of $\normtwo{\bm\eta(\tau)}$ over $0\leq\tau\leq t$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
$H$ is lower triangular with negative diagonal and nonnegative
off-diagonal entries. Thus $e^{Ht}\geq0$ and $-H^{-1}\geq0$, which give
\eqref{eq:comparison_solution}, the ultimate bound and $\bm c>0$.
Since $\bm c^{\trans}H=-\bm1^{\trans}$,
$D^+V_c\leq-V_c/c_{\max}+\bm c^{\trans}\bm d$, where
$c_{\max}=\max_i c_i$. The same comparisons hold with instantaneous
disturbance magnitudes in place of their uniform bounds. For fixed gains,
their weighted forcing is at most $K\normtwo{\bm\eta(t)}$ for some
$K>0$. Since $P\succ0$ and $\omega>0$ is fixed, there are constants
$m_c,M_c>0$ such that
$m_c\normtwo{\bm e}\leq V_c\leq M_c\normtwo{\bm e}$.
Integrating the scalar comparison proves
\eqref{eq:core_exponential_iss} with
$C_e=M_c/m_c$, $\alpha_e=1/c_{\max}$ and
$\Gamma_e=Kc_{\max}/m_c$. The bound also precludes finite-time escape
under the declared premises. With all disturbances zero, the core error
converges exponentially to zero.
Equation~\eqref{eq:component_extraction} is the ellipsoid support in
$P\otimes I_2$ after undoing the position normalization.
\end{IEEEproof}

The core contains two ego-velocity states and six states for the single target. Its
certificate requires neither informative yaw measurements nor a yaw
innovation chart. The shared gyro also couples the two core stages;
\eqref{eq:cascade_comparison} uses a triangular norm bound and does not
claim to retain all cross-stage cancellations. Orientation and position
remain separately certified outputs.

\subsection{Gain Selection}
\label{subsec:gainSelection}

With $T_{\rm dom}=\rho_{\max}/(V_E|_{\max}+V_C|_{\max})$, the scalar
gain preference is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \min_{k>0}k\quad\text{s.t.}\quad e^{-kT_{\rm dom}}\leq0.05,\\
    k_\psi=k_v=k_p=\frac{\ln20}{T_{\rm dom}}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:scalar_gain_rule}
\end{equation}
This is a transit-time preference, not an optimum of the common-error
bound in \eqref{eq:ego_rows}. Increasing $k_v$ changes the combined gyro
coefficient and need not improve that bound.

The target gains are decision variables of a semidefinite program that
contains the certificate. In physical coordinates ($\omega=1$) let
$\bm L\in\R^3$ be the gain vector, $A_L=A_c-\bm LC$ and $\bm Y=P\bm L$,
and put
$\Xi=A_c^{\trans}P+PA_c-\bm YC-C^{\trans}\bm Y^{\trans}$, which equals
$A_L^{\trans}P+PA_L$. The required decay rate of the target metric is
the decay floor $\lambda_T=1/T_{\rm dom}$, one $e$-folding per transit
time, a weaker requirement than \eqref{eq:scalar_gain_rule}. With
$\Delta_\Phi=\diag(0,t_qL_q^2,t_sL_s^2)$, solve
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \min_{P\succ0,\,\bm Y,\,t_q,t_s>0,\,\gamma}\ \gamma
    \quad\text{s.t.}\\
    \begin{bmatrix}
    \Xi+2\lambda_TP+\Delta_\Phi&P\bm e_3&P\bm e_3\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&-t_q&0\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&0&-t_s
    \end{bmatrix}\prec0,\\
    \Xi+\bm e_2\bm e_2^{\trans}\prec0,\qquad
    \begin{bmatrix}\gamma&\bm Y^{\trans}\\\bm Y&P\end{bmatrix}\succeq0 .
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:noise_shape_lmi}
\end{equation}
The first inequality is the Schur form of
Lemma~\ref{lem:dissipation} at $\omega=1$, so $\bm L=P^{-1}\bm Y$
already satisfies the dissipation inequality at the decay floor. The
second makes $P$ an upper bound of the observability Gramian of
$(A_L,\bm e_2^{\trans})$ \cite{BoydElGhaouiFeronBalakrishnan1994}. For
the linear error equation $\dot{\bm e}=A_L\bm e+\bm Ln$ driven by white
output noise $n$ of unit intensity, the stationary variance of the
velocity error is therefore at most
$\bm L^{\trans}P\bm L\leq\gamma$. All conditions are linear in
$(P,\bm Y,t_q,t_s,\gamma)$. One metric serves the Gramian and the
dissipation inequality, so $\gamma$ is an upper bound on the smallest
variance attainable among certified gains, not that minimum. The
variance is a performance preference. It ignores the nonlinear term and
the sampled realization, and it is not a deterministic error bound.

The returned gains are written as
$\bm L=[l_1\omega_0,\,l_2\omega_0^2,\,l_3\omega_0^3]^{\trans}$ with
$\omega_0=\min_i|\operatorname{Re}\lambda_i(A_L)|$ the slowest
closed-loop rate, which fixes a shape $\bm l$ of unit slowest rate. For
each candidate $\omega>1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ in the search domain the
certificate metric is then solved anew:
minimize $\operatorname{tr}P$ over $P\succeq I$ and $t_q,t_s>0$ subject
to
\begin{equation}
    \begin{bmatrix}
    Z&P\bm e_3&P\bm e_3\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&-t_q&0\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&0&-t_s
    \end{bmatrix}\preceq0,
    \label{eq:free_metric_lmi}
\end{equation}
where
$Z=\omega(A_l^{\trans}P+PA_l)+2\lambda_TP
+\diag(0,t_qL_q^2/\omega^2,t_sL_s^2)$.
Its Schur complement is Lemma~\ref{lem:dissipation}. The normalization
fixes the homogeneous scale while leaving the metric free; the recovered
matrix is checked in the original inequality with numerical reserves.
A bracketed scalar search then minimizes the largest of the three
ultimate bounds $c_{T,i}(-H^{-1}\bm d)_2$ of
\eqref{eq:component_extraction}, each divided by its domain scale
$\rho_{\max}$, $b$ or $S$. This gives a feasible local bandwidth
selection, not a global joint optimum. Because the search may move the
bandwidth away from $\omega_0$, the variance bound $\gamma$ applies to
the gains returned by \eqref{eq:noise_shape_lmi}, not to the rescaled
gains. The programs are solved with
YALMIP \cite{Lofberg2004} and SeDuMi \cite{Sturm1999}. Neither
simulation errors nor the sensor sample period enter the design.

\subsection{Error Enclosure}
\label{subsec:enclosure}

A controller that tightens its constraints needs a transient error
radius at each observation. For the continuous observer, the same
comparison system supplies it: \eqref{eq:comparison_solution} holds for
all \(t\), so \eqref{eq:component_extraction} gives

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:enclosure}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:cascadeISS} and any
\(\bm z_0\geq\bm\chi(0)\),
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm\rho(t)-\hat{\bm\rho}(t)}\leq B_\rho(t):=c_{T,1}z_2(t),
    \quad c_{T,1}=\sqrt{(P^{-1})_{11}},
    \label{eq:relative_position_bound}
\end{equation}
and \(\limsup_{t\to\infty}B_\rho\leq c_{T,1}(-H^{-1}\bm d)_2\). An initial
metric bound follows from componentwise initial bounds \(\bm b_{T0}\) as
\(W_T(0)\leq\bigl((D\bm b_{T0})^{\trans}|P|(D\bm b_{T0})\bigr)^{1/2}\).
\end{corollary}

Inertial reconstruction of the relative vector costs one further yaw term,
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm p_C-\bm p_E-R(\hat\psi_E)\hat{\bm\rho}}
    \leq B_\rho+2\bar R\sin\frac{\min(B_\psi,\pi)}2 ,
    \label{eq:inertial_relative_bound}
\end{equation}
\(\bar R=\min(\rho_{\max},\normtwo{\hat{\bm\rho}})\), and the analogous
rotation bounds convert the remaining components of
\eqref{eq:component_extraction} into the componentwise ego and target
enclosures \(\bm b_E(t)\), \(\bm b_T(t)\). These certify the current
estimate; they are not predictions of future observer output. Two sets
are published to the controller of Section~\ref{sec:control}. The ego set
is a zonotope $\{G_0\bm\zeta:\norminf{\bm\zeta}\leq1\}$ about the
estimated controller state, whose generator matrix $G_0$ keeps the
correlation between the yaw error and the body velocity inferred from
the GNSS velocity. The target set consists of a ball of radius $B_\rho$
for the relative position, an interval for the course and intervals for
the speed, the scalar acceleration $A_C$ and the curvature $\kappa_C$,
each intersected with the declared domain \eqref{eq:target_domain}. The
last two follow from the reconstructions $A$ and $\Omega/V$ of
\eqref{eq:target_chain} and the component bounds
\eqref{eq:component_extraction}. The digital
runtime additionally accounts for held measurements, relative-position
prediction and
outages in a separate finite-time enclosure. The continuous ISS theorem
does not establish exponential stability of the sampled fourth-order
Runge--Kutta (RK4) realization
or a directed-rounding certificate for its computed bounds.

\section{Control Design}
\label{sec:control}

Every sample builds one nonlinear rollout, linearizes the vehicle model
along it, and solves two convex programs on that affine model. The first
minimizes the relaxation of the sampled collision constraints. The
second keeps that relaxation and minimizes the violation of a
control-Lyapunov decrease condition, and its first input is applied. No
maneuver template is prescribed and no backup command exists; the same
programs are solved during avoidance and during the return to cruise.
The controller acts on the estimates of Section~\ref{sec:estimator} and
tightens its constraints by their current enclosures. The obstacle
vehicle of this section is the target of that section.

\subsection{Prediction Model}
\label{subsec:predictionModel}

The subscript $E$ is dropped from the ego state variables in this
section. The controller state and input are
\begin{equation}
    \bm x=[p_x,\,p_y,\,\psi,\,v_x,\,v_y,\,r]^{\trans},\qquad
    \bm u=[\delta_F,\,\beta]^{\trans},
    \label{eq:controller_state}
\end{equation}
the inertial pose, the body velocities and the yaw rate $r=\dot\psi$,
and the front steering angle and signed longitudinal force ratio.
Equations \eqref{eq:position_kinematics}--\eqref{eq:fiala_force} define
$\dot{\bm x}=f(\bm x,\bm u)$ for $v_x>v_{\rm f}$ and $|\beta|\leq1$.
Passive flat-road load in still air is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    F_R(v)={}&\tfrac12\rho_{\rm air}C_dA_fv|v|\\
       &+m_Eg(q_0+q_1|v|+q_4|v|^4)\tanh(v/v_{\rm tr}),
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:road_load}
\end{equation}
with air density $\rho_{\rm air}$, drag coefficient $C_d$, frontal area
$A_f$, nonnegative rolling coefficients $q_0,q_1,q_4$ and
$v_{\rm tr}>0$. The model uses static axle loads and excludes wheel
inertia, wind, grade and dynamic load transfer.

An input is held for the sample period $T_s$. The prediction map
$F(\bm x,\bm u)$ is one classical RK4 step
of $f$ over $T_s$. Its Jacobians $D_xF$ and $D_uF$ are obtained by
propagating the variational equations through the same step, with
analytic tire-force derivatives in both slip angle and force ratio.
Linearization points are kept strictly inside $|\beta|<1$, where those
derivatives exist. Constraints are also imposed halfway through each
hold. With one integration step per hold, the mid-hold state is the
endpoint interpolant $\tfrac12(\bm x+F(\bm x,\bm u))$, not an
independently integrated half step.

The reference path is straight or of constant curvature
$\kappa_{\rm r}$ and is traversed at the prescribed speed $v_*$. Steady
motion along it has yaw rate $r_*=\kappa_{\rm r}v_*$ and a constant
sideslip. The three force and moment balances of
\eqref{eq:bicycle_model} determine that sideslip and the trim input
$\bm u_*=[\delta_{F,*},\beta_*]^{\trans}$. Deviation from this cruise is
measured by the transverse error
\begin{equation}
    \bm e(\bm x)=[e_y,\ e_\psi,\ v_x-v_{x,*},\ v_y-v_{y,*},\
        r-r_*]^{\trans},
    \label{eq:transverse_error}
\end{equation}
where $e_y$ is the signed lateral offset of the center of mass from the
path and $e_\psi$ is the heading relative to the path tangent minus its
trim value. Position along the path is not an error: the nominal
behavior is the set of all phases of cruise on the path. The road is the
strip $-c_{\rm R}\leq e_y\leq c_{\rm L}$ with declared clearances
$c_{\rm R},c_{\rm L}>0$ to the right and left of the path.

The obstacle keeps the constant scalar acceleration $A_C$ and constant
sideslip $\beta_C$ of the tracker model of
Section~\ref{subsec:targetTracker}, written in inertial coordinates.
With the curvature $\kappa_C=\sin\beta_C/l_{r,C}$ of that model, arc
length $s(t)=V_{C,0}t+\tfrac12A_Ct^2$ and
$\vartheta(t)=\tfrac12\kappa_Cs(t)$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm p_C(t)&=\bm p_{C,0}+s\,\frac{\sin\vartheta}{\vartheta}
        \begin{bmatrix}\cos(\psi_{C,0}+\beta_C+\vartheta)\\
        \sin(\psi_{C,0}+\beta_C+\vartheta)\end{bmatrix},\\
    \psi_C(t)&=\psi_{C,0}+\kappa_Cs,\qquad V_C(t)=V_{C,0}+A_Ct,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_flow}
\end{equation}
with $\sin\vartheta/\vartheta=1$ at $\vartheta=0$. No quadrature is
needed, and the yaw rate $\kappa_CV_C(t)$ varies with speed. The flow is
not clipped at zero speed. Every new estimate replaces the obstacle
state and the parameters $A_C$ and $\beta_C$; they are constant within
one prediction and may change at the next sample. When no estimate
arrives, the last prediction is continued. The obstacle is perceived
only within the range $R_{\rm enc}$, and collision constraints are
imposed at the prediction nodes at which the two reference points are
closer than $R_{\rm enc}$, including after a later re-entry.

An anchor trajectory $(\bar{\bm x}_i,\bar{\bm u}_i)$, $i=0,\ldots,M$,
with $\bar{\bm x}_0$ the supplied estimate and
$\bar{\bm x}_{i+1}=F(\bar{\bm x}_i,\bar{\bm u}_i)$, supplies every
linearization of the sample. With
$\Delta\bm x_i=\bm x_i-\bar{\bm x}_i$,
$\Delta\bm u_i=\bm u_i-\bar{\bm u}_i$,
$A_i=D_xF(\bar{\bm x}_i,\bar{\bm u}_i)$ and
$B_i=D_uF(\bar{\bm x}_i,\bar{\bm u}_i)$, the shared model is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \Delta\bm x_{i+1}=A_i\Delta\bm x_i+B_i\Delta\bm u_i,\qquad
    \Delta\bm x_0=0,\\
    |\Delta\bm x_i|\leq\bm\tau_x,\qquad
    |\Delta\bm u_i|\leq\sigma\bm\tau_u .
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:affine_model}
\end{equation}
The trust boxes limit the correction of one solve; they are not
certified bounds on the linearization remainder. The scale
$\sigma\in[\sigma_{\min},1]$ of the input box is estimated online
(Section~\ref{subsec:trust}) and starts at $\sigma_0$. The force ratio obeys its actuator
interval, and the body velocities $v_x$, $v_y$ and $r$ obey box bounds
at the nodes and mid-hold points. The steering angle has neither a
magnitude nor a rate bound.

The horizon length $M$ is not fixed. It has $N$ primary holds, on which
collision constraints may be relaxed, and the anchor rollout is continued
until its first node $M\geq N$ that lies in the terminal set of
Section~\ref{subsec:terminal} with a margin, or to a largest length
$M_{\max}$.

\subsection{Initialization}
\label{subsec:initialization}

When the previous sample returned a plan, its inputs are shifted by one
hold and rolled out through $F$ from the new estimate; beyond the end of
the shifted plan the rollout continues under the path-guidance law
$\bm\pi$ below until the horizon ends. Predicted states of the previous
affine solution are never reused as the anchor. At the first sample, and
once more in a sample whose shifted problem is infeasible
(Section~\ref{subsec:program}), a fresh anchor is rolled out under a
guidance feedback: $\bm\pi$ without an obstacle, and a potential-field
guidance with one.

The path-guidance law steers the course toward the path through a
lookahead law and regulates the speed through the force ratio. With
$\chi$ the course relative to the path tangent, $V$ the speed,
$d_{\rm la}$ a lookahead distance, $\chi_d$ the commanded course and
$\dot\chi_d$ its rate along the motion,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \chi_d=-\arctan\frac{e_y}{d_{\rm la}},\qquad
    \dot\chi_d=-\frac{d_{\rm la}V\sin\chi}{d_{\rm la}^2+e_y^2},\\
    r_d=\operatorname{sat}_{\bar r}\Bigl[
        \frac{\kappa_{\rm r}V\cos\chi}{1-\kappa_{\rm r}e_y}
        +\dot\chi_d-k_\chi(\chi-\chi_d)\Bigr],\\
    F_d=\frac{I_{z,E}k_r(r_d-r)+l_{r,E}F_{yr}}{l_{f,E}},\qquad
    \phi_d=\min\Bigl(\frac{|F_d|}{Q_f},c_\phi\Bigr),\\
    \delta_F=\operatorname{atan2}(v_y+l_{f,E}r,v_x)+\delta_0\\
        \qquad{}+\operatorname{sgn}(F_d)\arctan
        \frac{3Q_f[1-(1-\phi_d)^{1/3}]}{C_f},\\
    \beta=\operatorname{sat}_{\bar\beta}\bigl[
        \beta_*+k_\beta(v_{x,*}-v_x)\bigr].
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:guidance_law}
\end{equation}
The yaw-rate demand $r_d$ is limited to $\bar r=c_a\mu g/V$, a fraction
of the available lateral acceleration, with $\mu$ the smaller axle
friction coefficient. The front force demand $F_d$ follows from a
yaw-rate loop and the current rear force $F_{yr}$, and the steering
angle inverts the front Fiala curve \eqref{eq:fiala_force} below the
fraction $c_\phi$ of its capacity $Q_f$; $\delta_0$ is a constant that
makes the trim an equilibrium. The law is never issued as a command.

With an obstacle, the course demand follows a potential field adapted
from \cite{ZhaiEtAl2024}: obstacle influence that depends on direction,
repulsion that depends on the relative motion, and joint course and
speed guidance. At each rollout node the ego is previewed along the path
for a time $T_{\rm p}$, at its current speed along the path and with its
lateral offset decaying as $e^{-\tau/T_{\rm la}}$, and compared with the
obstacle prediction \eqref{eq:target_flow} at the same absolute times
$\tau_j$. With the longitudinal and lateral separations
$\Delta s_j,\Delta d_j$ in path coordinates and the summed half extents
$a_j,b_j$ of the two rectangles along and across the path, including a
padding,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    w=\max_jw_j,\qquad
    w_j=\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\Delta s_j^2}{2a_j^2}
        -\frac{\Delta d_j^2}{2b_j^2}-\frac{\tau_j}{2T_{\rm la}}\Bigr),\\
    v_d=-\frac{e_y}{T_{\rm la}}+0.7\,v_*w\Bigl(
        \frac{\Delta d_\star}{b_\star}+2c\Bigr),\\
    v_s=\max\bigl(1.5\,\mathrm{m/s},\ 0.5v_*,\
        v_*[1-0.35\,w\min(2,v_{\rm cl}/v_*)]\bigr),
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:potential_field}
\end{equation}
where the star denotes the preview instant of largest influence,
$v_{\rm cl}\geq0$ is the closing speed along the path at that instant,
and $c=-\nu_{\rm p}\,\bm t_{\rm r}^{\trans}(\bm p-\bm p_C)/
\max(\normtwo{\bm p-\bm p_C},1\,\mathrm m)$ is a circulation term with
path tangent $\bm t_{\rm r}$ and passing side $\nu_{\rm p}\in\{-1,1\}$.
The commanded course is the path heading plus
$\operatorname{atan2}(v_d,v_s)$, with $v_d$ limited so that the body
stays on the road, and the speed demand is $v_s$. The side $\nu_{\rm p}$
is selected once per rollout from the lateral separation at the closest
predicted approach, the obstacle's transverse velocity and the road room
that would remain on either side after clearing the obstacle. Choosing
by road room matters with estimated states: without that term a head-on
encounter in the lane was decided by the sign of the estimated obstacle
sideslip, which is noise. The field combines attraction, a normalized
repulsion and circulation; it is not the gradient of one potential. A
yaw-rate loop, the inverse Fiala curve and a speed loop as in
\eqref{eq:guidance_law}, with limits that apply only to this rollout,
drive the nonlinear map $F$. The rollout fixes the side on which the
obstacle is passed; the programs of Section~\ref{subsec:program} refine
the trajectory locally and do not revisit that choice. A fresh anchor is
a search initialization, not a safety certificate: it can itself overlap
the obstacle or leave the road.

\subsection{Collision Constraints}
\label{subsec:geometry}

Both vehicles are rectangles. Let
$\mathcal C(t)=\{\bm m:H_C(\bm m-\bm p_C(t))\leq\bm h_C\}$ be the
obstacle rectangle, with
$H_C=[I;\,-I]R(\psi_C(t))^{\trans}$ and $\bm h_C$ its extents about
$\bm p_C$, and let $\bm p_j(\bm x)=\bm p+R(\psi)\bm o_j$,
$j=1,\ldots,4$, be the ego vertices with body-frame offsets $\bm o_j$.
The Euclidean distance between the two sets has the dual representation
\cite{ZhangLinigerBorrelli2021}
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    d(\bm x,t)=\max_{\bm\lambda\in\Lambda}\ \min_j\
        g_j(\bm x;\bm\lambda),\\
    g_j=\bm\lambda^{\trans}\bigl[H_C(\bm p_j(\bm x)-\bm p_C)
        -\bm h_C\bigr],\\
    \Lambda=\{\bm\lambda\geq0:\normtwo{H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda}\leq1\}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:distance_dual}
\end{equation}
Following \cite{LiZhangGuoLenzoGuo2023}, the multipliers are chosen at
the anchor and then held fixed while the trajectory is optimized.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:dualRows}
(i) For every $\bm\lambda\in\Lambda$ and every $\bm x$,
$d(\bm x,t)\geq\min_jg_j(\bm x;\bm\lambda)$.
(ii) If the rectangles are disjoint at $\bar{\bm x}$ and $\bm n$ is the
unit vector from a closest point of $\mathcal C$ to a closest point of
the ego rectangle, then
$\bm\lambda^\star=[\max(\bm w,0);\,\max(-\bm w,0)]$ with
$\bm w=R(\psi_C)^{\trans}\bm n$ satisfies
$H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda^\star=\bm n$ and
$\min_jg_j(\bar{\bm x};\bm\lambda^\star)=d(\bar{\bm x},t)$.
(iii) If a point of the ego rectangle lies in the interior of
$\mathcal C$, the maximum in \eqref{eq:distance_dual} is zero and
$\bm\lambda=0$ is its only maximizer.
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
(i) For $\bm m$ in the ego rectangle and $\bm m'\in\mathcal C$,
$\normtwo{\bm m-\bm m'}
\geq(H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda)^{\trans}(\bm m-\bm m')
\geq\bm\lambda^{\trans}[H_C(\bm m-\bm p_C)-\bm h_C]$, because
$\normtwo{H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda}\leq1$, $\bm\lambda\geq0$ and
$H_C(\bm m'-\bm p_C)\leq\bm h_C$. The right side is affine in $\bm m$,
so its minimum over the ego rectangle is attained at a vertex.
(ii) The direction $\bm n$ is the same for every closest pair.
$H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda^\star
=R(\psi_C)[\max(\bm w,0)-\max(-\bm w,0)]=\bm n$, and
$\bm h_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda^\star$ is the support of
$\mathcal C-\bm p_C$ in direction $\bm n$, attained at the closest point
$\bm m_C$. The line through the closest ego point with normal $\bm n$
supports the ego rectangle, hence
$\min_j\bm n^{\trans}(\bm p_j-\bm m_C)=d$. (iii) At such a point
$\bm m_0$ every component of $H_C(\bm m_0-\bm p_C)-\bm h_C$ is negative,
so $\min_jg_j\leq\bm\lambda^{\trans}[H_C(\bm m_0-\bm p_C)-\bm h_C]<0$
unless $\bm\lambda=0$.
\end{IEEEproof}

When the anchor is disjoint from the obstacle at a constrained instant,
the multipliers are those of Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(ii), computed in
closed form from the closest features of the two rectangles. When the
anchor overlaps, Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(iii) leaves only the zero
multiplier, whose row has no gradient in the ego pose. The row is then
built from another element of $\Lambda$: the unit normal $\bm n$ of the
rectangle axis, among the four axes of the two bodies, along which the
penetration is smallest. By Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(i) its value is
still a lower bound of the distance. It is negative at the anchor and
increases as the bodies separate along that axis, so the program
retains a direction of escape. With $\bm\lambda$ so chosen at the anchor
pose and the obstacle pose taken from \eqref{eq:target_flow} at the same
absolute time, each constrained instant contributes the four rows
\begin{equation}
    g_j(\bar{\bm x};\bm\lambda)+\bm n^{\trans}\Delta\bm p
    +\bm n^{\trans}R(\bar\psi)J\bm o_j\,\Delta\psi-\theta_i+\xi_i
    \geq d_{\rm m},
    \label{eq:collision_rows}
\end{equation}
which are affine in the decision variables, with
$\bm n=H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda$, a node margin $d_{\rm m}$, a slack
$\xi_i\geq0$ and a tightening $\theta_i\geq0$ that accounts for the
estimation error (Section~\ref{subsec:uncertainty}; $\theta_i=0$
without an enclosure). The constrained instants are the hold starts and
mid-hold points within $R_{\rm enc}$ and the endpoint of the horizon.
Without an enclosure, one slack per primary hold relaxes its start and
mid-hold rows, and the rows of later holds have none. With an enclosure,
every hold has a slack on its tightened rows, and beyond the primary
holds the untightened rows ($\theta_i=0$) are imposed in addition
without slack, so that the formulation reduces to the former as the
enclosure vanishes. The vertex rotation is retained to first order, with
remainder at most $\tfrac12\normtwo{\bm o_j}\Delta\psi^2$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:dualRows}(i), rows \eqref{eq:collision_rows} with
$\xi_i=\theta_i=0$ therefore imply
$d\geq d_{\rm m}-\tfrac12r_E\Delta\psi_i^2$ for the affine-predicted
pose, where $r_E=\max_j\normtwo{\bm o_j}$. With the values of
Section~\ref{sec:results} ($r_E=2.58$\,m, $|\Delta\psi|\leq0.25$\,rad,
$d_{\rm m}=0.20$\,m) the right side is at least $0.119$\,m.

\subsection{Road, Handling Envelope and Estimator Domain}
\label{subsec:envelope}

The ego drives on the road. At every predicted node after the current
one and at every mid-hold point, each rectangle vertex satisfies
\begin{equation}
    -c_{\rm R}\leq e_y\bigl(\bm p_j(\bm x)\bigr)\leq c_{\rm L},
    \qquad j=1,\ldots,4,
    \label{eq:road_rows}
\end{equation}
linearized in position and heading on the path normal at the anchor
vertex. These rows have no slack in either program.

Two further conditions tie the plan to the estimator of
Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided}. At both ends of every hold, under that
hold's force ratio $\beta_i$,
\begin{equation}
    \left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}(v_y-l_{r,E}r)/k_r^{\rm sl}\\
        \bar v_x\beta_i\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_2\leq v_x,
    \qquad k_r^{\rm sl}=\frac{3\mu_rF_{zr}}{C_r},
    \label{eq:adhesion_cone}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    |v_y|\leq v_x\tan\beta_{\rm dom}.
    \label{eq:sideslip_cone}
\end{equation}
With $\bar v_x$ replaced by $v_x$, \eqref{eq:adhesion_cone} is
$|\tan\alpha_r|\leq k_r^{\rm sl}\sqrt{1-\beta_i^2}
=\tan\alpha_{{\rm sl},r}$: the rear tire stays below the saturation
branch of \eqref{eq:fiala_force} at the capacity left by the
longitudinal force. The implementation freezes $v_x$ at its anchor value
$\bar v_x$ in the bilinear product, which makes the condition one
second-order cone, and divides the cone by $\bar v_x$ so that its
entries are of order one. This is a stable-handling envelope in the
sense of \cite{BealGerdes2013}: a saturated rear axle supplies no
restoring yaw moment. It is also an observability condition, because a
saturated force no longer determines the slip and the inversion of
\eqref{eq:force_balance} loses its information. Condition
\eqref{eq:sideslip_cone} is the cone premise of that inversion, with
the same $\beta_{\rm dom}$. Before these rows were added, a run with
estimated states at 15\,m/s combined a steering angle of $-0.40$\,rad
with a force ratio of $0.95$ and spun to a sideslip of 0.68\,rad.

\subsection{Terminal Set}
\label{subsec:terminal}

The horizon ends where the ego separates from the obstacle and can
remain on the road. At the last node $M$, with ego velocity
$\bm v=R(\psi)\bm v^E$ and obstacle velocity $\dot{\bm p}_C$,
\begin{equation}
    (\bm p-\bm p_C)^{\trans}(\bm v-\dot{\bm p}_C)\geq0,
    \label{eq:terminal_separation}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \max(w_e,0)^2\leq2a_{\rm rec}d_e,\qquad e\in\{{\rm L},{\rm R}\},
    \label{eq:road_recovery}
\end{equation}
where $w_e$ is the velocity of the center of mass toward road edge $e$
along the path normal, $d_e$ the distance of the outermost rectangle
vertex to that edge, and $a_{\rm rec}=c_{\rm rec}\mu g$ a declared
lateral deceleration. Condition \eqref{eq:road_recovery} states that the
ego can stop its lateral motion toward either edge before reaching it;
with an epigraph variable $t_e\geq\max(w_e,0)$ it is one rotated
second-order cone per edge. Membership in the road alone admitted
endpoints heading toward an edge at $43^\circ$ that left the road shortly
after the horizon. The endpoint also carries the rows
\eqref{eq:collision_rows} and \eqref{eq:road_rows}. Row
\eqref{eq:terminal_separation} is linearized at the anchor endpoint in
position, heading and body velocity and divided by the anchor distance.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:separating}
Suppose that for $t\geq t_M$ the relative velocity
$\bm v_{\rm rel}=\bm v-\dot{\bm p}_C$ is constant and
\eqref{eq:terminal_separation} holds at $t_M$. Then
$\normtwo{\bm p(t)-\bm p_C(t)}$ is nondecreasing for $t\geq t_M$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
With $\bm\Delta=\bm p-\bm p_C$,
$\tfrac{d}{dt}\normtwo{\bm\Delta}^2=2\bm\Delta^{\trans}\bm v_{\rm rel}$
and
$\tfrac{d^2}{dt^2}\normtwo{\bm\Delta}^2=2\normtwo{\bm v_{\rm rel}}^2
\geq0$. The first derivative is nonnegative at $t_M$ and nondecreasing.
\end{IEEEproof}

The anchor rollout places the end of the horizon at its first node
$M\geq N$ at which \eqref{eq:terminal_separation} holds with a
separating speed of at least $v_{\rm sep}$, that is, with right side
$v_{\rm sep}\normtwo{\bm p-\bm p_C}$, and \eqref{eq:road_recovery} holds
with $a_{\rm rec}/2$; without an obstacle only the road condition applies.
Neither terminal condition is then active at the anchor, which matters
numerically: a terminal row active at the anchor stalled the cone
solver. A rollout that never reaches such a node uses $M=M_{\max}$.

The premise of Proposition~\ref{prop:separating} is restrictive. A
braking lead, an obstacle whose constant sideslip turns it back, or the
ego's own return to the path changes the relative velocity after $t_M$
and can close the distance again; the next sample then predicts that
encounter inside its own, longer horizon. The terminal set is therefore
not controlled invariant, and no recursive-feasibility argument rests on
it. Two alternatives were tried and withdrawn. An invariant cruise core
around a freely placed straight cruise line had a radius so small that
it acted as a terminal equality, and a requirement that the obstacle be
beyond $R_{\rm enc}$ at the endpoint could not be met behind a braking
lead within $M_{\max}$ holds.

\subsection{Quadratic Control-Lyapunov Function}
\label{subsec:clf}

One obstacle-independent function measures the distance from cruise at
every sample, during avoidance and after it:
\begin{equation}
    V(\bm x)=\bm e(\bm x)^{\trans}P\,\bm e(\bm x),\qquad P\succ0 .
    \label{eq:clf}
\end{equation}
The matrix $P$ is synthesized offline for each operating point (vehicle,
speed, path curvature, sample period) and only read at run time. Let
$\bm e^+=A_e\bm e+B_e(\bm u-\bm u_*)$ be the sampled transverse model at
the trim, $Q_e=\diag(\varsigma_1^{-2},\ldots,\varsigma_5^{-2})$ with
error scales $\varsigma_i>0$, $S=P^{-1}$ and $Y=KS$ for a certificate
gain $K$. The synthesis minimizes $\rho$ by bisection subject to
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \begin{bmatrix}\rho S&(A_eS+B_eY)^{\trans}\\
        A_eS+B_eY&S\end{bmatrix}\succeq0,\qquad
    \begin{bmatrix}\bar u_j^2&Y_j\\Y_j^{\trans}&S\end{bmatrix}\succeq0,\\
    R_V^2Q_e^{-1}\preceq S\preceq c_V R_V^2Q_e^{-1},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:clf_lmi}
\end{equation}
for $j=1,2$, where $Y_j$ is the $j$th row of $Y$. The first inequality is
$(A_e+B_eK)^{\trans}P(A_e+B_eK)\preceq\rho P$. The second bounds the
certificate input by $|K_j\bm e|\leq\bar u_j$ on the sublevel set
$V\leq1$, and the third makes that set contain every error within $R_V$
error scales and keeps the weights of $P$ relative to $Q_e$ within a
ratio $c_V$ of one another. Without the input bound the fastest
contraction needs gains of several radians of steering per meter;
without the shape bound it nearly ignores some error directions. The
gain $K$ is discarded: it certifies that a decrease is attainable with
small inputs near cruise and is never applied. The result is the
smallest contraction factor $\rho^\star$ attainable under these
conditions for the linearized model. It certifies nothing for the
nonlinear vehicle away from the trim, where tire saturation and large
heading errors can make a decrease unavailable.

Every sample requests
\begin{equation}
    V\bigl(F(\bm x,\bm u_0)\bigr)\leq\rho_VV(\bm x)+\delta,
    \qquad\rho_V=e^{-2T_s/T_V},\qquad\delta\geq0,
    \label{eq:clf_row}
\end{equation}
with $\bm x$ the supplied estimate and a convergence time constant
$T_V$: without slack the error measure $\sqrt V$ shrinks by a factor
$1/e$ every $T_V$. The request must be slower than the certificate,
$\rho_V>\rho^\star$; otherwise the operating point is rejected. Only the
first input enters. In the program the successor is the affine first
node, and the error is linearized there with the analytic Jacobian
$D\bm e$ of the path chart,
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{P^{1/2}\bigl[\bm e(\bar{\bm x}_1)
        +D\bm e(\bar{\bm x}_1)\Delta\bm x_1\bigr]}^2
    \leq\rho_VV(\bm x)+\delta,
    \label{eq:clf_model}
\end{equation}
which is one second-order cone. The left side is a local model, so
$\delta=0$ in the program does not prove \eqref{eq:clf_row} for the
nonlinear map. The row acts on the estimate and carries no enclosure
(Section~\ref{subsec:uncertainty}).

\subsection{Use of the Estimation Enclosure}
\label{subsec:uncertainty}

The estimator publishes, at every sample, the ego generator matrix $G_0$
and the target parameter set of Section~\ref{subsec:enclosure}. Every
hold of the plan will be issued from such an estimate. Hold $i$ is
therefore tightened by the present enclosure propagated through that
hold only,
\begin{equation}
    G_i(\tau)=\Phi_i(\tau)G_0,\qquad\tau\in\{T_s/2,T_s\},
    \label{eq:hold_generator}
\end{equation}
with $\Phi_i$ the affine map of hold $i$ to its mid-hold point and its
end, $\tfrac12(I+A_i)$ and $A_i$; the node that closes hold $i$ is
constrained with $A_iG_0$, and the present node with $G_0$. A row
$\bm a^{\trans}\Delta\bm x\leq b$ on the ego state is tightened by the
support $\normone{\bm a^{\trans}G_i}$ of the zonotope; this applies to
the road rows \eqref{eq:road_rows}, the body-velocity bounds, the
sideslip cone \eqref{eq:sideslip_cone} and the terminal rows. The
adhesion cone \eqref{eq:adhesion_cone} acts on the estimate, because the
certified lateral-velocity radius widens exactly as the rear slip nears
saturation, and its reserve excluded states well inside the envelope.

The target is measured relative to the ego, so an error of the ego pose
moves both bodies rigidly and cancels from their distance. For the
collision rows, the rigid motion generated by the pose columns of $G_0$
about the ego position at the start of the hold is therefore subtracted
from $G_i$, leaving a relative generator $G_i^{\rm rel}$. The target set
restarts at each hold from the predicted target state at the hold start.
With radii $b_p,b_\gamma,b_V,b_A,b_\kappa$ of the published position,
course, speed, acceleration and curvature sets, nominal arc length $s$
over the elapsed time $\tau$ and $b_s=b_V\tau+\tfrac12b_A\tau^2$, the
constant-parameter family \eqref{eq:target_flow} is enclosed by
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    b_p(\tau)&=b_p+b_s+|s|b_\gamma+\tfrac12s^2b_\kappa,\\
    b_\gamma(\tau)&=\min\bigl(\pi,\ b_\gamma+|s|b_\kappa
        +(|\kappa_C|+b_\kappa)b_s\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_tube}
\end{equation}
and the heading radius $b_{\psi_C}$ adds the sideslip radius to
$b_\gamma(\tau)$. A directional version of the position bound, which
charges a speed error along the path of the target only, is used when it
is smaller. The tightening of \eqref{eq:collision_rows} is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \theta_i={}&\normone{\bm n^{\trans}G_{i,p}^{\rm rel}}
        +\sigma_C(\bm n)\\
      &+2r_E\sin\frac{\min(\pi,b_{\psi})}{2}
        +2r_C\sin\frac{b_{\psi_C}}{2},
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:collision_tightening}
\end{equation}
with $G_{i,p}^{\rm rel}$ the position rows of the relative generator,
$\sigma_C(\bm n)\leq b_p(\tau)$ the target position support,
$b_\psi$ the ego heading radius and $r_C$ the circumradius of the
obstacle rectangle; the last two terms bound the displacement of any
body point under a heading error by its chord.

For the first hold, \eqref{eq:hold_generator} and
\eqref{eq:target_tube} are the propagation of the current certified
sets through the affine model. For later holds they are an
approximation: they assume that the enclosure at the start of hold $i$
will equal the present one and that the estimate will then lie on the
plan. Neither the family of possible future estimates nor the forecast
error of the target beyond one hold is covered. The alternative that is
sound for an open-loop input sequence, $G_{i+1}=A_iG_i$ from $G_0$ over
the whole horizon, grew with the variable horizon until the road and
state rows were infeasible. The row \eqref{eq:clf_model} uses the
estimate for the same kind of reason. A worst case over the enclosure
asks that the largest successor value over the successor's enclosure
stay below $\rho_V$ times the smallest current value. Near the path
that budget is zero while the successor's enclosure is not, so no input
meets it; the second program then minimized the next value greedily, the
force ratio chattered, and a 15\,m/s head-on run did not recover in
100\,s. Estimation error enters the decrease as a disturbance instead,
and recovery of the true state can at best reach a neighborhood of the
path set by that error.

\subsection{The Two Programs}
\label{subsec:program}

Let $\mathcal A$ denote the constraints shared by both programs on
$(\Delta\bm x_{0:M},\Delta\bm u_{0:M-1},\bm\xi)$: the model
\eqref{eq:affine_model}, the force-ratio and body-velocity bounds, the
collision rows \eqref{eq:collision_rows} with $\bm\xi\geq0$, the road
rows \eqref{eq:road_rows}, the cones \eqref{eq:adhesion_cone} and rows
\eqref{eq:sideslip_cone}, and the terminal conditions
\eqref{eq:terminal_separation} and \eqref{eq:road_recovery}, each with
its tightening. The primary program is
\begin{equation}
    J_1^\star=\min\ \sum_i\xi_i
    \quad\text{subject to }\mathcal A,
    \label{eq:primary_program}
\end{equation}
and the secondary program is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \min_{\delta\geq0}\quad&
        \frac{\delta+\epsilon\,\ell(\bm x)\,\mathcal R(\Delta\bm u)}
            {\max(V(\bm x),1)}\\
    \text{s.t.}\quad&\mathcal A,\qquad\sum_i\xi_i
        \leq J_1^\star+\epsilon_{\rm lex}\max(1,J_1^\star),\\
    &\text{\eqref{eq:clf_model}},
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:secondary_program}
\end{equation}
with $\ell(\bm x)=\bm e(\bm x)^{\trans}Q_e\bm e(\bm x)$ and
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal R(\Delta\bm u)=\frac1{2M}\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}
        \normtwo{(\sigma W_u)^{-1}\Delta\bm u_i}^2,\qquad
    W_u=\diag(\bm\tau_u).
    \label{eq:tie_term}
\end{equation}
Both are second-order cone programs. If the zero correction with zero
slacks satisfies $\mathcal A$, it attains $J_1^\star=0$ and the primary
program is not passed to the solver; the secondary program is always
solved. The ordering resembles the predictive barrier construction of
\cite{WabersichZeilinger2023}, in which the minimal relaxation of a
predictive safety problem is computed before the performance problem.
The conditions under which that construction yields a control barrier
function, in particular an exact prediction model and a terminal safe
set for the complete problem, are not established here.

Minimizing a first-step slack alone leaves the later inputs
undetermined, and the next anchor inherits them. The term
$\epsilon\ell\mathcal R$ makes the plan unique at a bounded cost. Since
$0\leq\mathcal R\leq1$ on the trust box, the slack of an exact optimum
exceeds the smallest attainable one by at most $\epsilon\,\ell(\bm x)$,
which vanishes at cruise. The penalty acts on increments from the
anchor; it pulls the inputs neither toward zero nor toward $\bm\pi$. The
tolerance $\epsilon_{\rm lex}$ is $10^{-4}$\,m: with a cap of
$10^{-5}$\,m the zero-slack set is a slab a few micrometers thick, and
the cone solver repeatedly stalled on the secondary program.

When the solver certifies the primary program primal infeasible, the
input trust scale $\sigma$ is doubled on the same linearization, up to
$\sigma=1$, until the program is feasible. If the program around a
shifted anchor is still infeasible, one fresh anchor is rolled out and
treated in the same way. The secondary program is then solved on the
selected model. A sample therefore builds one linearization, or two when
the shifted problem is re-solved from the fresh rollout.

The applied command is the first input of the secondary solution,
unclipped. The solution is issued directly: its inputs are not replayed
through the nonlinear model, and no agreement test, damping or line
search follows the solve. A positive $J_1^\star$ is executed and
recorded; it is a relaxation, not a statement of safety. If the anchor
cannot be rolled out, if the primary program has no solution after the
steps above, or if the secondary program returns no converged point, no
command is issued and the run stops. Neither the previous plan nor a
primary-only point is substituted.

\subsection{Trust Scale from the Plan Innovation}
\label{subsec:trust}

The scale $\sigma$ of the input trust box is estimated from how well the
previous affine prediction agreed with the nonlinear model. For a
correction of size $s$, the largest input change from the anchor in
units of $\bm\tau_u$, the affine prediction differs from the nonlinear
rollout of the same inputs by a second-order remainder of the form
$Ls^2$, with a coefficient $L$ that depends on the operating point. Let
$\hat{\bm x}_j$ be the previous plan's affine prediction,
$\bar{\bm x}_j$ the new anchor, which is the nonlinear rollout of the
same inputs from the new estimate, and $\tilde{\bm x}_j$ a second
nonlinear rollout of the same inputs started at the plan's own
prediction of the present state. Then
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\bm x}_j-\hat{\bm x}_j
    =(\bar{\bm x}_j-\tilde{\bm x}_j)+(\tilde{\bm x}_j-\hat{\bm x}_j)
    \label{eq:innovation_split}
\end{equation}
splits the plan innovation exactly. The first term propagates the
departure of the new estimate from the prediction: estimation error,
plant mismatch and the remainder of the first hold. The second contains
no measurement; it is the model remainder of the step that was taken.
With $\varpi(\Delta\bm x)=\normtwo{\Delta\bm p}+r_E|\Delta\psi|$, which
bounds the displacement of any body point, and
$e_k=\max_j\varpi(\tilde{\bm x}_j-\hat{\bm x}_j)$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \hat L_k=\max\Bigl(
        \frac{e_k}{\max(s_{k-1},\sigma_{k-1}/2)^2},\
        \gamma_L\hat L_{k-1}\Bigr),\\
    \sigma_k=\min\Bigl(1,\ \max\Bigl(\sigma_{\min},\
        \sqrt{\tau_e/\hat L_k}\Bigr)\Bigr).
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:trust_update}
\end{equation}
The scale is the step whose predicted remainder equals a target
innovation $\tau_e$. It shrinks at once and grows by at most
$\gamma_L^{-1/2}$ per sample, and a step far inside the box is not taken
as evidence about its boundary. Only the model part $e_k$ enters. An
earlier rule that subtracted the estimator's worst-case radius from the
total innovation explained every innovation by that radius, so the scale
rose to its maximum and the encounters became infeasible. The update is
not a check of the issued solution, and $\tau_e$ is an empirical
consistency target, not a bound on the remainder or on the separation.

\subsection{Scope of the Guarantees}
\label{subsec:scope}

Three statements hold in exact arithmetic. For an executed plan whose
programs converged with $J_1^\star=0$, the affine prediction satisfies
the rows \eqref{eq:collision_rows} with
$\sum_i\xi_i\leq\epsilon_{\rm lex}$, so the predicted rectangles are
separated by at least
$d_{\rm m}-\epsilon_{\rm lex}-\tfrac12r_E\Delta\psi_i^2$ at the
constrained instants within $R_{\rm enc}$, and by the additional
tightening \eqref{eq:collision_tightening} where an enclosure is
supplied. The predicted endpoint of such a plan satisfies the linearized
terminal rows, and under the premise of
Proposition~\ref{prop:separating} the reference-point distance does not
decrease after it. Near the trim, the linear model admits an input
within $\bar{\bm u}$ that contracts $V$ by $\rho^\star<\rho_V$.

None of these is a statement about the vehicle. The constraints act on
the affine model \eqref{eq:affine_model} at nodes and mid-hold points.
The nonlinear rollout of the same inputs can violate them, and two
rectangles that are separated at consecutive constrained instants can
intersect in between. The trust boxes and the node margin $d_{\rm m}$
are not linked by a bound on the accumulated linearization remainder;
the trust update \eqref{eq:trust_update} keeps a measured remainder near
$\tau_e$ but bounds nothing. Feasibility is not recursive: the shifted
plan is an initialization, the next anchor is a new nonlinear rollout
with new multipliers and a new horizon length, and the terminal set is
not invariant, so the solution of one sample need not be feasible at the
next. A positive $J_1^\star$ is executed and may describe an overlapping
plan. Only the first hold uses a certified enclosure, and that
certificate is itself conditional on the premises of
Section~\ref{sec:estimator}. Zero slack in \eqref{eq:secondary_program}
is a statement about the local model \eqref{eq:clf_model}. The scheme
follows the real-time-iteration idea of \cite{DiehlBockSchloder2005}: it
solves convex programs on one linearization per sample instead of
iterating to convergence, and it inherits neither nominal stability nor
safety from the nonlinear problem that it approximates.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All results are synthetic. Sections~\ref{subsec:designResults} and
\ref{subsec:estimatorResults} evaluate the estimator alone, on
prescribed kinematic motions and with the kinematic measurement of
Section~\ref{subsec:egoObserver}; these trials predate the model-aided
measurement and were not repeated after it was added, which leaves that
branch of the estimator unchanged. The remaining sections evaluate the
controller with exact states and with the estimator in the loop.

\subsection{Synthesized Estimator Design}
\label{subsec:designResults}

The estimator premises are $V_E\in[5,20]$\,m/s,
$\bar\omega_E=0.30$\,rad/s; $V_C\in[10,20]$\,m/s,

\subsection{Estimator Accuracy Against a Tuned Reference}
\label{subsec:estimatorResults}

Six open-loop scenarios of 6 to 12\,s are used. In the straight
oncoming scenario the ego drives straight at 15\,m/s and the target
approaches from 95\,m at 15\,m/s. In the arc crossing scenario the ego
follows an arc of radius 100\,m and the target moves on a straight line
that meets the arc. In the arc following scenario the target is 30\,m
ahead on the same arc at 15.5\,m/s. The swerve-and-braking scenario
adds a 3.5\,m double lane change with braking to the straight oncoming
geometry, with the target at 12\,m/s. In the low-relative-speed
scenario a lead target is 30\,m ahead at 15.3\,m/s. In the weaving
scenario a weaving, accelerating ego meets a turning, accelerating
target. The target obeys the constant-acceleration, constant-sideslip
model in all of them, and the ego truth obeys the kinematic
single-track relation without mismatch. The swerve and weaving
scenarios violate the zero-jerk premise of the reference observer.
Synchronized measurements arrive at 50\,Hz with bounded uniform noise at
the declared maxima, and the maximum RK4 step is 0.005\,s.

The reference is a reproduction of the multistage observer of
\cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}, with companion-form ego and target chains
and a switched course--acceleration yaw stage. As in that paper, its ego
chain uses GNSS position and the gyroscope and does not use the GNSS
velocity that the estimator of Section~\ref{sec:estimator} measures. Its
gains are selected from 54 candidates (two gain shapes, three ego rates,
three target rates and three yaw gains) on training seeds 101--102, once
for every scenario, by minimizing the sum of the position, speed and
course errors scaled by 0.1\,m, 0.5\,m/s and $5^\circ$. In five of the
six scenarios the selected candidate lies on the boundary of this grid.

The estimator receives no error-based tuning, but its gains are
synthesized anew from the operating domain declared for each scenario.
Only the low-relative-speed scenario uses the domain of
Section~\ref{subsec:designResults}. The arc following scenario declares
$\beta_{\max}=0.03$\,rad, which gives the target gains
$[10.21,51.59,116.4]$. The straight, arc crossing and swerve scenarios
declare $\rho_{\max}=100$\,m (the swerve scenario also
$\bar\omega_E=0.40$\,rad/s and $\beta_{\rm dom}=0.06$\,rad), and the
weaving scenario declares $\rho_{\max}=80$\,m. For
$\rho_{\max}=100$\,m and 80\,m, respectively, the decay floors are 0.4
and 0.5\,s$^{-1}$, the scalar gains 1.198 and 1.498\,s$^{-1}$, and the
target gains $[6.24,19.31,27.20]$ and $[6.50,20.93,30.23]$.

Both observers run on identical truth, sensor draws and initial offsets.

The estimator has the lower mean in five of six scenarios for each
metric. It is worse in target speed at low relative speed and in
position in arc following, and marginally worse in course in arc
following, where the difference is a statistical tie (95\% paired
bootstrap interval of the reference-minus-estimator difference
$[-0.018,0.006]^\circ$). Its position advantages in the straight and

at or near zero. The advantage is large where the ego maneuvers: the
speed RMSE is 0.053 against 0.549\,m/s in the swerve-and-braking
scenario and 0.061 against 0.276\,m/s in the weaving scenario. Against a
single reference tuning selected over all scenarios, the estimator has
the lower mean in all six scenarios for all three metrics. Against that
tuning, over 36 noisy stress cells of five seeds each (the six
scenarios with 20 and 100\,Hz sampling, doubled noise, threefold initial
offsets, a dropout of the relative-position measurement and
single-track mismatch), it remains lower in speed and course in every
cell and in position in 34.

Other recorded metrics are less favorable. Against the per-scenario
tuned reference, the target-acceleration RMSE is lower in only two of
six scenarios, the two with a maneuvering ego, and the position RMSE
over the complete run, including the initial transient, is lower in
none; the speed RMSE over the complete run is lower in all six. Without
noise, where one run per scenario exists, the single reference tuning
has the lower position error in four of six scenarios, the lower speed
error in two and the lower course error in one.

The mean time after which the position and velocity errors remain within
0.5\,m and 1.0\,m/s is 0.63 to 1.13\,s. The per-scenario tuned reference
needs 0.87 to 2.16\,s in the four scenarios in which it always
converges; it converges in 14 of 20 trials of the swerve scenario and in
none of the weaving scenario. With the bounded-real shape, and a
rear-axle distance of 1.45\,m, the corresponding times were 0.47 to
0.73\,s. One estimator step takes about 2.1\,ms against 0.3\,ms for the
reference, which computes no bounds, orientation sets or measurement
histories. In a paired benchmark of the two shapes on identical draws,
with five seeds in each of ten noisy cases (nominal, two target
maneuvers, two measurement dropouts, 25\,Hz sampling, aggressive ego
motion, an oncoming target, doubled noise and threefold initial
offsets), the noise-variance shape has the lower position and velocity
RMSE in all ten (0.0238 against 0.0283\,m and 0.083 against 0.117\,m/s
in the nominal case). The curvature-response lag is about 0.6\,s for
both, and the noise-free errors of the noise-variance shape are larger.

Several qualifications apply. With exact ego inputs and identical
injection gains, the covariant chain and the companion chain agree
within 0.2\% in speed RMSE in three of the four scenarios with constant
ego motion (0.053365 and 0.053356\,m/s in the straight scenario) and
differ in the arc crossing scenario (0.049 against 0.057\,m/s). The
coordinate change is therefore not the main source of the measured
differences; the experiment does not apportion the remainder among the
ego stage, the gain selection and the validity of the models during ego
maneuvers. The noise-variance criterion was adopted after a comparison
of the bounded-real shape on the same test seeds was known, in which
the estimator led in two of six scenarios in position and three in
speed. The criterion is a predicted variance and uses no simulated
error, but the rerun is not a preregistered confirmation. The reference
is our reproduction with gains selected empirically on a bounded grid,
not the authors' implementation or sensor suite. No
enclosure-containment or outage statistics are reported for these
open-loop trials.

Table~\ref{tab:controllerParameters} lists the parameters. The vehicle
is a generic passenger car. In the closed-loop trials the estimator uses
the same vehicle parameters as the controller, not the rear-axle
distance of Section~\ref{subsec:designResults}. With these values the
synthesis \eqref{eq:clf_lmi} certifies contraction factors
$\rho^\star$ of 0.954 at 8\,m/s and 0.937 to 0.940 at 15\,m/s per
sample, on the straight and the curved path, against the requested
$\rho_V=0.975$; the corresponding time constants of $\sqrt V$ are
2.1\,s and 1.5 to 1.6\,s against $T_V=4$\,s.

Seven encounter geometries are run at reference speeds of 8 and
15\,m/s. In the head-on case the obstacle starts 24\,m ahead on the path
and approaches at 8\,m/s; in the accelerating head-on case it also
accelerates at 1\,m/s$^2$. The braking lead starts 1.45\,m ahead of the
ego body at the ego speed and brakes at 0.5\,m/s$^2$. The crossing
obstacle moves at 8\,m/s perpendicular to the path and reaches the path
when the cruising ego does. The turning crossing obstacle reaches the
same point at 10\,m/s and perpendicular to the path at that instant,
while accelerating at $A_C=1$\,m/s$^2$ and turning with
$\beta_C=0.05$\,rad, a turning radius of about 32\,m. The curved head-on
and curved crossing cases use a path of curvature $0.005$\,m$^{-1}$,
with an opposing obstacle on the same circle at 8\,m/s and an obstacle
that crosses at 10\,m/s with $A_C=0.5$\,m/s$^2$ and
$\beta_C=0.04$\,rad. In every case the bodies are initially separated,
and the ego under unmodified cruise would collide. The road has four
lanes of 3.66\,m and paved shoulders of 3.05\,m, and the path is the
center of the second lane from the right, which gives the clearances
$c_{\rm R}$ and $c_{\rm L}$ of Table~\ref{tab:controllerParameters}.

Each issued input is held for $T_s$ on the model $f$ itself, integrated
by an adaptive Runge--Kutta method with relative and absolute tolerances
$10^{-11}$ and $10^{-12}$. The plant therefore differs from the
prediction only through the integration scheme and the linearization.
There is no parameter mismatch or actuator dynamics, the obstacle
follows \eqref{eq:target_flow} exactly with fixed $A_C$ and $\beta_C$,
and the measured computation time is not applied as a delay. The body
distance is evaluated at 31 instants per hold.

Two observation modes are used. With \emph{exact states}, the
controller receives the true ego and obstacle states and no enclosure.
With \emph{estimated states}, it receives only the output of the
estimator of Section~\ref{sec:estimator}, which runs at 80\,Hz on
synthetic GNSS, accelerometer, gyroscope and relative-position samples
of the true motion with bounded uniform noise at the maxima of
Table~\ref{tab:controllerParameters}. The relative-position sensor has
a range of $R_{\rm enc}$. An obstacle is acquired after 20 consecutive
detections, whose least-squares line initializes its velocity, and its
estimate is passed to the controller only while it is detected. The
estimator starts from a 0.5\,s measurement history before the first
control sample. Its ego domain is derived from the controller's limits
(speed 1 to 18\,m/s, $\beta_{\rm dom}$, yaw rate and acceleration from
the state bounds and tire friction), and its target domain is the hull
of the scenario obstacles within sensor range, widened by 10\%; the
gains are synthesized from this domain by the programs of
Section~\ref{subsec:gainSelection} and differ from those of
Section~\ref{subsec:designResults}. The lateral-velocity measurement is
that of Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided}. The estimated-state trials are
repeated for six noise seeds.

A run fails if the bodies touch or if a sample returns no solved
command. It succeeds if, in addition, cruise is recovered: all five
transverse errors within (0.1\,m, $1^\circ$, 0.1\,m/s, 0.05\,m/s,
0.01\,rad/s) during a dwell of 1\,s that starts after the last instant
at which unmodified cruise would be in contact. Runs last at most 2000
holds (100\,s). On the circular path the opposing obstacle of the curved
head-on case meets the ego a second time within that period, so those
runs contain two encounters and confirm recovery after the second. The
campaign uses MATLAB R2026a on an AMD Ryzen 7 7800X3D and the
interior-point cone solver Clarabel through a compiled interface, with
an iteration limit of 400 per program and a time limit of 5\,s per
call. Below, a primary optimum is called zero when it does not exceed
$\epsilon_{\rm lex}$ plus the feasibility tolerance $10^{-5}$, and a
control-Lyapunov slack when it does not exceed $10^{-5}\max(V,1)$.

Table~\ref{tab:closedLoop} and Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop} summarize the
98 runs. With exact states all fourteen runs succeed: no contact, no
departure from the road, and cruise recovered between 9.35 and
13.25\,s in the single-encounter cases. With estimated states 82 of the
84 runs succeed. The other two stop without a solved command, an
8\,m/s braking lead at 2.65\,s and an 8\,m/s curved crossing at
7.10\,s, each for one seed; no run collides or leaves the road. Four
seeds succeed in all fourteen encounters and two in thirteen.

With exact states the controller uses the node margin almost completely.
In ten of the fourteen runs the smallest sampled distance is below
$d_{\rm m}=0.20$\,m, down to 0.153\,m
(Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop}(d)): the margin is consumed between the
constrained instants and by the linearization. A positive primary
optimum occurs at 38 samples of eight runs, at most 0.027\,m, and is
executed each time. A fresh anchor is rolled out at three samples of
the campaign. The steering angle reaches 0.40\,rad, the yaw rate
0.82\,rad/s, and the force ratio ranges from $-0.35$ to $0.76$. The
largest lateral deviation is 2.3 to 6.9\,m
(Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop}(e)): the maneuvers leave the lane and use the
adjacent lanes, and the body stays at least 3.85\,m from a road edge.
The horizon is $N$ holds in the median and at most 111 holds.

With estimated states the passes are wider. The median over the 84 runs
of the smallest distance is 1.66\,m, against 0.20\,m with exact states,
because the tightened rows \eqref{eq:collision_tightening} reserve the
current enclosure and the forecast of the obstacle changes from sample
to sample. The wider passes use the road: the lateral deviation reaches
10.6\,m, and the smallest distance to a road edge falls to 0.23\,m in a
15\,m/s curved head-on. The steering angle reaches 0.69\,rad, the yaw
rate 1.10\,rad/s and the force ratio $-0.97$ and $0.98$. Recovery on the
straight road is confirmed after a median of 10.9\,s and at most
16.7\,s. Over all estimated-state samples the horizon has a median of 16
holds, a 99th percentile of 79 and a maximum of 138.

The control-Lyapunov condition is met in the model more often than on
the plant. In the exact-state runs, 3064 consecutive state pairs have
$V>10^{-3}$, and 2520 of them have zero slack in
\eqref{eq:secondary_program}. In 73 of those the requested decrease
\eqref{eq:clf_row} is not achieved by the plant; in none does $V$
increase. Recovery in Table~\ref{tab:closedLoop} is an observed dwell,
not a certified decrease.

\subsection{Estimation in the Loop}
\label{subsec:loopEstimation}

Over the 84 estimated-state runs, the 95th percentile of the ego
estimation error within a run is 0.002 to 0.007\,rad in heading, 0.01 to
0.11\,m/s in lateral velocity and 0.007 to 0.022\,m in position. With
the kinematic measurement of Section~\ref{subsec:egoObserver} and a
fixed declared domain, the same encounters gave heading errors of 0.07
to 0.31\,rad and lateral-velocity errors of 1.0 to 3.9\,m/s at
15\,m/s (one seed), the ego enclosure was lost in nine of thirteen
runs, and only five of fourteen runs recovered. The force-balance
measurement of Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided} and the handling
envelope of Section~\ref{subsec:envelope} were introduced together for
this reason.

The published enclosures were audited against the recorded truth in
every frame of the fourteen runs of one seed. The ego enclosure was
valid in all 5958 frames and the target enclosure was available in
2291; neither failed to contain the truth in any frame. The medians of
the ego radii within a run were 0.06\,m in position, 0.005 to
0.008\,rad in heading and 0.08 to 0.09\,m/s in lateral velocity. The
true motion stayed inside the sideslip cone \eqref{eq:sideslip_cone}.
It exceeded the adhesion condition \eqref{eq:adhesion_cone} in three
frames of three 15\,m/s runs, by at most 0.7\%, which is possible
because that condition acts on the estimate and on the affine
prediction. The audit covers the sampled instants of one seed and is
not a proof of the premises.

The target enclosure remains wide where the controller needs it most.
The relative-position radius has a median of about 0.065\,m, but the
set of motion parameters used for prediction has median radii of 0.24
to 0.53\,rad in course and 1.9 to 4.0\,m/s in speed, and the whole
declared range for $A_C$ and $\kappa_C$. The point estimates of the
parameters are also noisy: between 0.5 and 3\,s, the root-mean-square
error within a run has a median of 0.27\,m/s$^2$ for $A_C$ (largest
0.46\,m/s$^2$) and 0.008\,rad for $\beta_C$ (largest 0.027\,rad), and
the estimate of $A_C$ can sit at the limit of its declared range for
several consecutive samples. The gain program
\eqref{eq:noise_shape_lmi} bounds the variance of the velocity error;
the noise of the acceleration state, from which $A_C$ and $\beta_C$ are
reconstructed, is not part of its objective.

Both runs stop in a sample whose horizon is long while the published

forecast obstacle is 3.7 and 5.6\,m from the true one.

The failing problems were solved again with groups of rows removed.
Without the collision rows, or without the terminal separation row
\eqref{eq:terminal_separation}, both are feasible; removing the road,
handling or road-recovery rows does not help. With all collision rows
relaxed, the slack is nonzero only at the end of the horizon (holds 58
to 85 and 102 to 105). The conflict is therefore between the forecast
obstacle near the end of a long horizon and the requirement to separate
from it there, in a part of the horizon whose rows have no slack and
whose forecast no enclosure covers. Neither failure is a violation of a
published bound.

As an ablation, the published $A_C$ and $\beta_C$ were replaced by a
least-squares fit of the constant-parameter trajectory
\eqref{eq:target_flow} to the last 2\,s of relative-position
measurements, with the tracker's position, velocity and enclosures
unchanged. The errors of $A_C$ and $\beta_C$ fell to medians of
0.02\,m/s$^2$ and 0.0005\,rad, all 84 runs succeeded, and the smallest
distance to a road edge was 1.00\,m. The fit is a second estimator
outside the certificate of Section~\ref{sec:estimator} and is not part
of the reported design.

\subsection{Computation}
\label{subsec:computation}

Four exact-state encounters were timed one at a time on the same
machine (8\,m/s head-on and turning crossing, 15\,m/s head-on and
crossing, about 800 holds). One controller call took a median of
9.2\,ms and a 95th percentile of 41\,ms, and 3.4\% of the calls
exceeded the 50\,ms hold. These timings precede the last estimator
change, which does not alter the exact-state controller. In the
campaign itself eight runs shared the machine
(Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop}(f)): the median call time was 12 to 13\,ms,
6.8\% of the exact-state calls and 7.9 to 8.6\% of the estimated-state
calls exceeded the hold, and the largest call took 1.1\,s. The
estimator took a median of 16 to 19\,ms per hold, for four estimator
samples. The slow calls are those with long horizons or a second
linearization. Because the computation time is not applied as a delay,
the outcomes describe the algorithm with instantaneous computation.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The two components stand at different levels of assurance.
Theorem~\ref{thm:cascadeISS} is a continuous-time statement about the
velocity--target cascade, and the gain selection of
Section~\ref{subsec:gainSelection} takes place inside its certificate.
With a shape chosen outside the certificate, only the scale can be
adjusted to it, and the gains come out larger than the certificate
requires; choosing the shape inside the certificate lowered the
predicted velocity noise and the certified radii together. The remaining
losses against the tuned reference in the open-loop trials are
consistent with constraints of the certificate. At low relative speed
the decay floor $\lambda_T=0.8$\,s$^{-1}$ keeps the certified gains
noisier than the reference's uncertified choice, and in arc following
the target sideslip bound $\beta_{\max}=0.03$\,rad declared for that
scenario raises $L_q$ from 0.80 to 2.29\,s$^{-2}$. Less conservative
Lipschitz multipliers, and separate metrics for the variance bound and
the dissipation inequality, are the natural refinements.

Closing the loop changed both designs, and most changes came in pairs.
The estimator's premises are statements about the true motion, and the
controller decides that motion. The kinematic premise of the velocity
measurement failed in exactly the maneuvers the controller is built
for, and the bounds published under it were then wrong, not merely
loose. Sharing the tire model and the held input replaced that premise
by one the controller can enforce, and the controller in turn accepted
two constraints, the sideslip cone and the rear-adhesion cone, whose
purpose is partly to keep the estimator informative. In the other
direction, the estimator's enclosure could not be used as a worst case
throughout the horizon or in the decrease condition: both made the
programs infeasible or the commands erratic. The implemented compromise
certifies one hold and replans, and the results show what that leaves
open. The large difference between observed errors and certified radii
matters now that the controller consumes the enclosure: the certified
course and speed radii of the obstacle are one to two orders of
magnitude above the observed errors, and the wide passes and small road
margins of the estimated-state runs are their consequence.

The two runs without a solution point at the acceleration state of the
tracker. The controller needs the obstacle's motion parameters for a
forecast over horizons of up to 138 holds, and the reconstruction of
$A_C$ from a high-gain acceleration state is the noisiest quantity the
estimator publishes. The ablation with a windowed fit shows that the
information is in the measurements: a constant-parameter stage inside
the estimator cascade, with its own certificate, or a variance bound on
the acceleration state in the gain program \eqref{eq:noise_shape_lmi},
would address the cause. On the controller side, a forecast enclosure
that grows along the horizon, used with slack beyond the first hold,
would state the uncertainty the long horizons actually face. None of
these was evaluated.

The controller has no certificate for the nonlinear vehicle, and
success in 96 of 98 runs does not supply one. The trust box and the
node margin are not related by a bound on the linearization remainder;
the innovation-based trust scale keeps the measured remainder near a
target and is a heuristic. Separation is imposed at nodes and mid-hold
points only, and the sampled distance falls below the node margin in
most exact-state runs; whole-hold separation needs a swept or interval
condition. The terminal set is not invariant, so feasibility at one
sample does not imply feasibility at the next, and the variable horizon
moves the difficulty into its length: the samples that fail, and the
slow calls, are the ones with long horizons. A positive primary optimum
is executed.

Several premises of the closed-loop trials are favorable. The plant is
the prediction model with nominal parameters, so the structural premise
of the force-balance measurement holds by construction, and the 5\%
parameter box is a design margin, not a measured mismatch. The obstacle
follows its declared model. Computation is treated as instantaneous
although 3 to 9\% of the calls exceed the hold. The steering angle is
unbounded in magnitude and rate. The road edges are declared, not
perceived, and the relative-position measurement is synthetic with a
noise bound of 0.04\,m; on simulated camera and LiDAR captures the
perception pipeline of Section~\ref{sec:perception} has so far produced
position errors above the 0.12\,m bound assumed in
Section~\ref{subsec:designResults} on most frames, and it has not been
run in the loop. The sampled realization of the observer is not covered
by the continuous-time theorem. Each of these premises has to be removed
before physical collision avoidance can be claimed.

The control-Lyapunov construction behaves as intended in one respect:
one obstacle-independent function governs avoidance and recovery, and
every completed run returns to cruise with the same function and
programs throughout. The quadratic form is certified only for the
linearized model near the trim, and the decrease is requested on an
affine model of the estimate. The recovery results are therefore
empirical.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Retaining measured acceleration and yaw rate as exogenous inputs removes
the zero-jerk and zero-yaw-acceleration assumptions of the differentiated
ego design. The direct velocity measurement in \eqref{eq:ego_observer}
yields the cascade comparison of Theorem~\ref{thm:cascadeISS}, and a
parallel yaw observer with an orientation set supplies inertial
reconstruction. Shaping the target gains by a semidefinite program that
contains the Lipschitz certificate reduces the predicted noise and the
certified radii together. In open-loop synthetic trials with operating
domains declared per scenario, the estimator has a lower mean error than
a per-scenario tuned reproduction of the reference observer in five of
six scenarios for each of position, speed and course after the initial
transient; the position error over the complete run and most
acceleration errors favor the reference.

The controller linearizes a dynamic bicycle model with modified Fiala
tires along one nonlinear rollout per sample and solves two cone
programs: the relaxation of distance-dual collision rows first, and the
violation of a decrease condition on one quadratic control-Lyapunov
function second, over a horizon that ends where the plan separates from
the obstacle and can remain on the road. Designed together, the
estimator measures the lateral velocity through the controller's tire
model, and the controller keeps the vehicle where that measurement is
informative and tightens each hold by the estimator's current
enclosure. In fourteen collision-threat encounters with exact states,
every run ends collision-free on the road with cruise recovered; with
the estimator and bounded sensor noise, 82 of 84 runs do so, none
collides, and two stop without a solved command where a long horizon
meets a noisy estimate of the obstacle's acceleration. These are
simulations on the prediction model itself. A certified estimate of the
obstacle's motion parameters and a forecast enclosure along the horizon,
a bound that links the linearization step to the collision margin,
whole-hold separation, an invariant terminal condition, actuator
constraints, real-time computation, plant mismatch and perception in
the loop remain necessary before physical collision avoidance can be
claimed.
