\begin{abstract}
We study vehicle collision avoidance under coupled longitudinal--lateral
dynamics and uncertainty in surrounding-vehicle states. The ego vehicle
coordinates steering and braking in response to collision threats, while
path and speed tracking serve as nominal driving and recovery objectives.
Evasive maneuvers complicate target-state estimation and can compromise
the availability of subsequent avoidance actions. We jointly design a
target-state estimator and a predictive safety controller. The estimator keeps the nonlinear relative motion model of
Sharma et al., which accounts for the ego vehicle's own motion, without
the differentiated coordinates that forced that observer to assume
constant ego acceleration and yaw rate; it carries an input-to-state
stability certificate and publishes an error enclosure with every
estimate. The theoretical controller minimizes predictive barrier slack
over causal nonlinear feedback policies. Prediction carries the physical
vehicles, observers, sensor histories and uncertain parameters jointly;
conditioning on the entire next sensor block retains a subset of the
predicted successor. With initial feasibility, valid uncertainty
contracts, attained primary minima and a compatible terminal family,
restricting the old policy tail and appending a terminal rule proves
recursive feasibility. The optimal slack sum is nonincreasing, and its
zero set excludes interior collision at control decision instants.
No constant-width tube or finite-time arrival at zero is asserted.
The adopted compact nonlinear Fiala controller approximates this
construction using fixed measurement-consistency ensemble inference,
condensed nonlinear sequential quadratic programming over coarse control
knots, a terminal continuation to sensing-range exit, and a secondary tire-loading
and rear-slip preference on the zero-deficit level.
In the historical October 11 affine-RTI baseline (predating this compact
nonlinear controller revision and pending rerun), all fourteen
exact-state collision-threat encounters recover cruise without collision;
with the estimator and bounded noise, 74 of 84 recover, and none collides
or leaves the road. These results do not evaluate the revised compact
controller or the full robust construction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} driving has become a major area of research,
and an active collision-avoidance system is an essential component of an
autonomous vehicle. By detecting potential hazards and initiating an
evasive response, a collision-avoidance system enables a vehicle to avoid
obstacles without driver intervention. In road traffic, other vehicles are among the most common sources of
collision risk. Consequently, the design of systems that
can actively avoid collisions with surrounding vehicles has attracted
considerable research interest \cite{LiEtAl2022Emergency,ChenChengLiZhaoWang2024}.

Collision avoidance often requires the ego vehicle to perform complex
maneuvers, such as decelerating while turning \cite{TanWuLiLiuZhang2025}.
Such maneuvers pose two challenges. First, the vehicle's longitudinal and
lateral dynamics become strongly coupled, as changes in speed alter lateral
tire forces and yaw response while yaw motion couples the longitudinal and
lateral velocities \cite{BealBoyd2019,ParkGerdes2017}. Neglecting this
coupling can lead to large prediction errors and control commands that demand
maneuvers beyond the vehicle's physical capabilities. Second, sensors
such as LiDAR can provide relative-position measurements of surrounding
vehicles, but a more comprehensive assessment of collision risk benefits
from additional state information, such as velocity, acceleration, and heading. When only relative positions are
available, estimating these states is complicated by the ego vehicle's
evasive maneuvers, which also affect the measurements
\cite{AlaiSharmaAlexanderRajamani2024,SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}.

Regarding the ego vehicle's coupled dynamics, some approaches simplify
lateral control by assuming a constant longitudinal speed, thereby
decoupling it from the longitudinal dynamics. This assumption is used in
steering control \cite{JiKhajepourMelekHuang2017}, integrated path tracking
\cite{XiongLiuYangLeng2022}, and hierarchical planning
\cite{ChenAksunGuvenc2025}, but it cannot capture braking-induced speed
changes. Other approaches use kinematic models for trajectory planning
\cite{LiZhangGuoLenzoGuo2023}, which allow speed to vary but omit tire-force
dynamics. To capture the effect of speed variations on lateral dynamics,
lateral models can be formulated using linear parameter-varying control
\cite{AtouiSenameMilanesMartinez2022} or gain-scheduled robust control
\cite{LiLamLuLi2022}, and steering decisions can be computed for prescribed
braking and throttle profiles \cite{TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013}.
Although these methods capture the effect of speed on lateral motion, speed
dependence alone does not account for the reduction in lateral tire capacity
under braking. Combined demands can be constrained through acceleration
limits \cite{ChenPengGrizzle2018}, stability envelopes
\cite{ChenChengLiZhaoWang2024}, or stability-based speed adjustment
\cite{AttiaOrjuelaBasset2012,AttiaOrjuelaBasset2014}, while
assume--guarantee contracts bound the interaction between separate controllers
\cite{XuGrizzleTabuadaAmes2018}; their guarantees depend on the prescribed
bounds holding during operation. Such constraints need not, however,
represent the full nonlinear tire response. To describe the coupled dynamics
more directly, nonlinear feedback methods coordinate longitudinal and
lateral actions using backstepping \cite{GuoHuWang2016}, Lyapunov design
\cite{CheblyTaljCharara2017}, or dynamic inversion
\cite{SeoLeeBaekHorowitzChoi2022}. Predictive controllers optimize motion
using wheel-slip inputs \cite{ZhouJiaJingLiuGuvenc2018}, combined-slip tire
models \cite{ZhangJiangLiuHuWangChen2023}, load-transfer dynamics
\cite{ThompsonDallasGohBalachandran2024}, or drift-aware planning
\cite{ZhaoChenGaoYaoGaoHua2024}. Their accuracy depends on the adopted model,
and nonlinear prediction also increases computational demand.

Regarding the estimation of surrounding-vehicle motion, one option is to
express the motion model in an inertial frame using estimated ego position and
orientation \cite{SharmaRajamani2024}, but ego localization and heading errors
then feed directly into the target track. Working in the ego sensor frame
avoids this global transformation, yet some relative-motion observers do not
account for ego rotation
\cite{JeonZemoucheRajamani2019,AlaiZemoucheRajamani2024}. To address the estimation errors that this omission can cause during
evasive maneuvers, Alai \emph{et al.}
\cite{AlaiSharmaAlexanderRajamani2024} formulated a nonlinear relative motion
model (NRMM) that incorporates ego velocity and yaw rate explicitly,
Kyong \emph{et al.} \cite{KyongAlaiTamehRajamani2026} further demonstrated
the importance of ego sideslip-angle estimation for relative-motion target
tracking during sharp turns and U-turns, and Sharma
\emph{et al.} \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026} designed a multistage high-gain
observer (HGO) around the NRMM for simultaneous ego-state estimation and target
trajectory tracking. While the NRMM captures how an evasive maneuver alters
relative motion in the sensor frame, that multistage HGO transforms both the
ego kinematics and the NRMM relative-position dynamics into third-order
companion chains by assuming zero body jerk and zero yaw acceleration, an
assumption that breaks down during rapid steering and braking. In addition, its
nominal convergence and scalar yaw $H_\infty$ analyses do not provide an
explicit, time-varying state-error bound under sensor noise, model mismatch,
and initial transients, which a safety controller needs to tighten its
collision constraints.

Together, these two challenges raise the central problem of how to
construct a collision-avoidance controller with formal safety guarantees
that respects coupled tire and actuator limits while remaining feasible
under online estimation errors. Field-based
planners such as artificial potential fields \cite{WangGaoLiSunCheng2019}
and virtual fluid-flow models \cite{ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021} blend lane keeping
with obstacle repulsion intuitively, yet a collision-free
reference path does not certify closed-loop safety once tire saturation,
input bounds, and tracking errors intervene. Control barrier functions
(CBFs) enforce set forward invariance directly through affine conditions
on the control input \cite{AmesXuGrizzleTabuada2017,XiaoBelta2022}. Under
bounded actuation, however, myopic one-step CBF quadratic programs can
lose feasibility \cite{ZengZhangLiSreenath2021}. For lane keeping and
obstacle avoidance, Bruggemann \emph{et al.}
\cite{BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022} investigate input-constrained barrier
functions using a constant-speed lateral vehicle model. Hardware actuator
limits can also lead to saturation in high-speed corner cases when they are
omitted from the controller formulation
\cite{TayalEtAl2026}. Road experiments have also recorded safety
violations under nominal CBFs during vehicle cut-ins and delays in live
traffic \cite{GunterNiceBuntingSprinkleWork2022} and under unmodeled
actuation disturbances on automated trucks
\cite{AlanTaylorHeAmesOrosz2023}. Approaches to anticipating future
hazards include enforcing discrete-time barrier constraints within MPC
\cite{ZengZhangSreenath2021} and constructing barriers from predicted
motion under a prescribed policy \cite{BlackJankovicSharmaPanagou2023}.
Predictive control barrier functions (PCBFs) use finite-horizon
constraint relaxations and terminal constructions to establish recursive
feasibility and safety recovery under their respective assumptions
\cite{WabersichZeilinger2023,HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025}. Still,
these PCBF guarantees assume exact state measurements or fixed offline
disturbance bounds. Conversely,
observer-based CBFs account for moving-obstacle estimation errors via
input-to-state safety margins \cite{TezukaNakamura2020} or bounded-error
observer sets \cite{QuanZhouFriskChung2025}, yet remain one-step filters
without multi-step prediction under coupled tire and actuator limits.
Safe MPC for uncertain road users \cite{BatkovicGuptaZanonFalcone2023}
establishes recursive feasibility through reachable-set tightening, a
safe terminal set, and step-to-step prediction consistency, but relies on
a kinematic ego model and assumes ego sensing and localization are
already solved. In coupled maneuvers with online ego and target
estimation, refreshing the estimate and re-tightening constraints at each
sample can invalidate the prior backup plan unless successive uncertainty
sets remain compatible.

For nonlinear observer design, Sharma, Zemouche and Rajamani
\cite{SharmaZemoucheRajamani2026} develop derivative-based LMI conditions
for systems with vector arguments and introduce state scaling to improve
numerical feasibility. Their analysis informs the nonlinear-increment
bounds and scaled quadratic certificate used in the estimator below.

To address these challenges, this paper develops a joint NRMM-based
state estimator and PCBF--CLF controller built around a shared dynamic
bicycle model with modified Fiala tires. The system also includes a
camera--LiDAR perception pipeline for target-position measurement. The
design and evaluation of perception are not the focus of this paper: the
pipeline has been tested offline, while the closed-loop experiments use
synthetic relative-position measurements. The system architecture is
described in Section~\ref{sec:systemDesign}. The main contributions of
this work are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
\item We reformulate the NRMM target observer of
\cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026} in covariant coordinates so that measured
ego acceleration and yaw rate enter as exogenous inputs without
differentiation, eliminating the zero-jerk and zero-yaw-acceleration
assumptions. Combined with semidefinite-programming (SDP) gain synthesis
and a tire force-balance lateral-velocity measurement, the observer
admits a continuous-time cascade input-to-state stability (ISS)
certificate and publishes explicit, time-varying error enclosures online
(Section~\ref{sec:estimator}).
\item We design a compact nonlinear PCBF controller on the coupled Fiala
bicycle model that pairs fixed measurement-consistency ensemble inference
with condensed nonlinear sequential quadratic programming over coarse
control knots and exact dual rectangle separation at control-boundary
nodes. A terminal continuation that keeps the vehicles separated until
the target leaves sensing range supports horizon-shift feasibility under an explicit
physical handoff assumption, while a secondary tire-loading and rear-slip
preference guides selection among validated zero-deficit driving plans
without altering the primary safety objective (Section~\ref{sec:control}).
\item We establish a joint nonlinear output-feedback safety formulation
that propagates both vehicles and observers under future
measurement-dependent policies. Conditioning on the entire received
sensor block permits restriction of the old policy tail. Under explicit
uncertainty, attainment and terminal-closure assumptions, this proves
recursive feasibility, nonincreasing optimal safety relaxation and
decision-instant safety of its zero set for the road-free problem.
The adopted compact Fiala controller's approximations are stated
separately (Section~\ref{subsec:robust}).
\item We benchmark the proposed estimator against the reproduced
multistage HGO of \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}, verify the
information-set safety mechanisms on a longitudinal reference problem,
and preserve the historical October 11 closed-loop baseline across 14
collision-threat encounters under both exact states and bounded sensor
noise over six random seeds, including an audit of truth containment for
the published enclosures; closed-loop re-evaluation of the revised
compact controller remains pending (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:systemDesign} introduces the task and system architecture.
Sections~\ref{sec:estimator} and~\ref{sec:control} develop the state
estimator and the predictive controller, respectively. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the
numerical evaluation, and Sections~\ref{sec:discussion}
and~\ref{sec:conclusion} discuss implementation limitations and conclude
the paper.

\section{System Design}
\label{sec:systemDesign}

Although the proposed system is not restricted to a particular road
scenario, we consider the junction scenario illustrated in
Fig.~\ref{fig:driving_environment}.
The ego vehicle initially cruises at a constant speed along a prescribed
path. A target vehicle emerges from the side road and is detected upon
entering the ego vehicle's perception range as it attempts to merge into
the ego lane. In the encounter considered here, maintaining
the prescribed path and cruising speed would lead to a collision with the
target. The collision-avoidance system must therefore enable the ego vehicle
to temporarily depart from this cruising condition, avoid the target,
and then return to the prescribed path and
cruising speed once the threat has passed.

This section presents the design of a collision-avoidance system for this
task, with the overall architecture shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}.
The perception module provides position measurements of surrounding
vehicles relative to the ego vehicle. Since perception is not the focus
of this paper and the algorithms depend on the sensors used to detect
surrounding vehicles, we do not discuss them in detail, but briefly
describe the implementation used in our simulation environment in
Section~\ref{sec:results}. The estimator provides state estimates and
corresponding estimation-error bounds for both the ego and target vehicles. The controller uses these
estimates and error bounds to compute the collision-avoidance control
input at each time step.

\subsection{Estimator Design}
\label{sec:estimator}

To analyze the poses of the ego and target vehicles in
Fig.~\ref{fig:driving_environment}, we illustrate the two vehicles
separately in Fig.~\ref{fig:ego_obstacle_frames}.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.86\textwidth]
        {ego_obstacle_dynamics_v04.pdf}
    \caption{Ego and obstacle kinematics. The ego vehicle (blue) and the
    obstacle vehicle (orange) travel on courses that may meet. The vector
    \(\bm r\) gives the target position relative to the ego vehicle,
    expressed in the ego frame. The body frames sit at the centers of mass, the yaw angles \(\psi_E\) and
    \(\psi_C\) orient them relative to the inertial \(X_I\) axis, the
    sideslip angles \(\beta_E\) and \(\beta_C\) separate the velocities
    \(V_E\) and \(V_C\) from the body axes, and the course angles
    \(\gamma_E\) and \(\gamma_C\) orient the velocities in the inertial
    frame.}
    \label{fig:ego_obstacle_frames}
\end{figure*}

Let $\{I\}$ denote the inertial frame and let $\{E\}$ and $\{C\}$
denote the ego and target vehicle frames, respectively, each centered
at the vehicle's center of mass with its $X$ axis pointing forward and
its $Y$ axis pointing to the left. For vehicle $i\in\{E,C\}$, let
$\bm p_i=[x_i,y_i]^{\trans}$ and $\bm v_i$ denote its inertial position
and velocity, $V_i=\normtwo{\bm v_i}$ its speed, $\psi_i$ its yaw,
$\beta_i$ its sideslip, and $\gamma_i=\psi_i+\beta_i$ its course
angle. Let $R(\psi_E)$ be the rotation from the ego frame to the
inertial frame. The ego-frame velocity is
$\bm v^E=[v_x,v_y]^{\trans}=R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_E$.
With $J=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$
and $\nabla_\omega\bm z=\dot{\bm z}+\omega J\bm z$, the ego kinematics are
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\bm p}_E=\bm v_E,\qquad
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm v^E=\bm a_E,\qquad
    \omega_E=\dot\psi_E,
    \label{eq:estimator_ego_kinematics}
\end{equation}
where $\bm a_E=[a_{x,E},a_{y,E}]^{\trans}=
R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\dot{\bm v}_E$ is the true acceleration expressed
in the ego frame.

The target position relative to the ego vehicle is expressed in the ego
frame by $\bm r=[r_x,r_y]^{\trans}=R_E^{\trans}(\bm p_C-\bm p_E)$,
where $R_E=R(\psi_E)$ and $r_x,r_y$ are its longitudinal and lateral
components. Let $\psi_R=\psi_C-\psi_E$ be the relative yaw and
$A_C=\dot V_C$ the signed scalar acceleration of the target vehicle.
Its relative-motion state is
\begin{equation}
    \bm X_{vn}=[r_x,\,r_y,\,V_C,\,A_C,\,\psi_R,\,\beta_C]^{\trans}.
    \label{eq:target_physical_state}
\end{equation}
For observer design, let $\bm a_C=\dot{\bm v}_C$ and express the target
velocity and acceleration in the ego frame as
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \bm r=R_E^{\trans}(\bm p_C-\bm p_E),\qquad
    \bm q=R_E^{\trans}\bm v_C,\qquad
    \bm s=R_E^{\trans}\bm a_C,\\
    \bm x_T=[\bm r^{\trans},\bm q^{\trans},\bm s^{\trans}]^{\trans}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_coordinates}
\end{equation}
For positive target speed, the target dynamics in these coordinates can
be written as
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm r=\bm q-\bm v^E,\quad
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm q=\bm s,\quad
    \nabla_{\omega_E}\bm s=\Phi(\bm q,\bm s)+\bm\nu_\Phi,\\
    \Phi=-\omega_C^2\bm q+3A_C\omega_C J\frac{\bm q}{V_C},\\
    V_C=\normtwo{\bm q},\quad
    A_C=\frac{\bm q^{\trans}\bm s}{V_C},\quad
    \omega_C=\frac{(J\bm q)^{\trans}\bm s}{V_C^2}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_chain}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_C=\dot\gamma_C$ is the target course rate and
$\bm\nu_\Phi$ is the difference between the actual acceleration dynamics
and the nominal vector field $\Phi$. The relative-position dynamics
account for the ego velocity and yaw rate while retaining the target
motion in the ego frame. The target velocity and acceleration determine
its speed and course, while recovery of its body yaw also requires
information about its sideslip. The bound on $\bm\nu_\Phi$ is specified
in the observer error analysis.

The measurements are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm y_p&=\bm p_E+\bm n_p, &\quad
    \bm a_m&=\bm a_E+\bm n_a,\\
    u_3&=\dot\psi_E+d_\omega,\\
    \bm y_R&=\bm r+\bm n_R,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:ego_measurements}
\end{equation}
where $\bm y_p$ is the GNSS position, $\bm a_m=[a_x,a_y]^{\trans}$
and $u_3$ are the IMU acceleration and yaw-rate measurements, and
$\bm y_R=[y_1,y_2]^{\trans}$ is the relative-position measurement.
The noise satisfies uniform bounds \(\normtwo{\bm n_p}\leq\bar n_p\),
\(\normtwo{\bm n_a}\leq\bar n_a\),
\(|d_\omega|\leq\bar n_g\), \(\normtwo{\bm n_R}\leq\bar n_R\). Inertial
biases are compensated upstream; no observer carries a bias state. The
estimator uses the ego measurements to estimate ego motion and combines
these estimates with the relative-position measurement to track the
target vehicle.

\subsubsection{Position--IMU Velocity Reconstruction}
\label{subsec:positionVelocity}

The GNSS receiver supplies position rather than a direct velocity
measurement. At each sensor sample, a four-state filter corrects its
inertial position and velocity predictions using the new GNSS position.
For sample period $h$, bandwidth $\omega_f$, and predicted state
$(\bm p_f^-,\bm v_f^-)$, define
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    q_f=e^{-\omega_f h},\quad \alpha_{pv}=1-q_f^2,\quad
    \beta_{pv}=(1-q_f)^2/h,\\
    \bm r_p=\bm y_p-\bm p_f^-,\quad
    \bm p_f^+=\bm p_f^-+\alpha_{pv}\bm r_p,\quad
    \bm v_f^+=\bm v_f^-+\beta_{pv}\bm r_p,\\
    \bm p_{f,k+1}^-=\bm p_{f,k}^++h\bm v_{f,k}^+
        +\tfrac12h^2\hat{\bm a}_{I,k},\quad
    \bm v_{f,k+1}^-=\bm v_{f,k}^++h\hat{\bm a}_{I,k},\\
    \hat{\bm a}_{I,k}=R(\hat\psi_{E,k})\bm a_{m,k}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:position_velocity_filter}
\end{equation}
Both posterior error poles equal $q_f$. The filter is initialized from
available prior information, and the initialization uncertainty is
included in its error enclosure. The filter propagates signed
error generators through its correction and prediction steps, including
position noise, yaw uncertainty and acceleration mismatch. Bounded-noise
position secants may further tighten the velocity enclosure without
changing the numerical estimate. These are conditional containment bounds
in real arithmetic, not a floating-point interval certificate.

Write $\bm v_m$ for the reconstructed inertial velocity supplied to the
remaining observer stages, $V_m=\normtwo{\bm v_m}$ for its magnitude,
and $\bm n_v=\bm v_m-\bm v_E$ for its error.
The continuous analysis below assumes a valid bound
$\normtwo{\bm n_v}\leq\bar n_v$ over the interval being analyzed. In the
sampled runtime the corresponding radius evolves with the measurements,
motion and yaw bounds; it is not a GNSS velocity-sensor noise constant.

\begin{assumption}
\label{asm:ego}
\(0\leq V_E|_{\min}\leq V_E\leq V_E|_{\max}\),
$v_x\geq V_E\cos\beta_{\rm dom}$, and
\(|\dot\psi_E|\leq\bar\omega_E\).
\end{assumption}

Let $l_{r,E}$ be the distance from the ego center of mass to its rear
axle. The kinematic branch uses the following relation.

\begin{assumption}
\label{asm:singleTrack}
The kinematic single-track relation holds up to a bounded mismatch,
\begin{equation}
    \dot\psi_E=\frac{V_E\sin\beta_E}{l_{r,E}}+d_{\rm st},
    \qquad|d_{\rm st}|\leq\bar d_{\rm st},
    \label{eq:single_track_mismatch}
\end{equation}
and \(|\beta_E|\leq\beta_{\rm dom}<\pi/2\).
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{asm:singleTrack} bounds the unmodeled rear-axle slip
in the kinematic branch. A model-aided measurement is introduced below
to account for larger slip during avoidance maneuvers.

\subsubsection{Ego Observers}
\label{subsec:egoObserver}

Using the reconstructed inertial velocity and measured yaw rate, define
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \phi(V,w)=\sqrt{\max\{V^2-w^2,V^2\cos^2\beta_{\rm dom}\}},\\
    \bm y_v=[\phi(V_m,c),\ c]^{\trans},\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm v}^E=\bm a_m+k_v(\bm y_v-\hat{\bm v}^E).
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:ego_observer}
\end{equation}
Here $c$ is a lateral-velocity pseudo-measurement obtained from the
model-aided force balance developed below; when that model or the held
input is unavailable, the kinematic branch uses $c=l_{r,E}u_3$. On the
true forward cone, $\phi(V_E,v_y)=v_x$, and the extension remains
defined for every measured speed. For the kinematic branch, the lateral
component is not clipped, giving
$y_{v,2}-v_y=l_{r,E}(d_\omega+d_{\rm st})$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:egoRows}
For the kinematic measurement $c=l_{r,E}u_3$, let
$\bm e_v=\hat{\bm v}^E-\bm v^E$, $k_v>0$, and
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    C_\omega(k)=\max_{V\in\{V_E|_{\min},V_E|_{\max}\}}
    \Bigl[(V\sin\beta_{\rm dom}+kl_{r,E}\tan\beta_{\rm dom})^2\\
        +(kl_{r,E}-V\cos\beta_{\rm dom})^2\Bigr]^{1/2}.
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:common_gyro_bound}
\end{equation}
Under Assumptions~\ref{asm:ego}--\ref{asm:singleTrack},
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    D^+\normtwo{\bm e_v}\leq-k_v\normtwo{\bm e_v}+\bar\delta_v,\\
    \bar\delta_v=\bar n_a+k_v\sec\beta_{\rm dom}\,\bar n_v
      +C_\omega(k_v)\bar n_g
      +k_vl_{r,E}\sec\beta_{\rm dom}\,\bar d_{\rm st}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:ego_rows}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Put $\nu=V_m-V_E$, so $|\nu|\leq\bar n_v$. Integrating the piecewise
derivatives of $\phi$ along the segment between $(V_E,v_y)$ and
$(V_m,l_{r,E}u_3)$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \bm y_v-\bm v^E=\alpha\bm e_1\nu
        +l_{r,E}\bm g(\zeta)(d_\omega+d_{\rm st}),\\
    \bm g(\zeta)=[-\zeta,1]^{\trans},\\
    \cos\beta_{\rm dom}\leq\alpha\leq\sec\beta_{\rm dom},\qquad
    |\zeta|\leq\tan\beta_{\rm dom}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:velocity_measurement_error}
\end{equation}
Continuity covers branch crossings and the origin. Substituting before
taking norms,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \dot{\bm e}_v={}&(-k_vI-u_3J)\bm e_v+\bm n_a+k_v\alpha\bm e_1\nu\\
        &+(k_vl_{r,E}\bm g(\zeta)-J\bm v^E)d_\omega
          +k_vl_{r,E}\bm g(\zeta)d_{\rm st}.
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:velocity_error_derivation}
\end{equation}
The common gyro column has components
$[v_y-k_vl_{r,E}\zeta,\ k_vl_{r,E}-v_x]^{\trans}$. Maximizing
its norm over $\zeta$ and the forward cone, then using the convexity of
its squared bound in the speed, gives \eqref{eq:common_gyro_bound}. The
rotation is skew and contributes nothing to the norm derivative. The
remaining terms give \eqref{eq:ego_rows}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The same gyro error enters both the measurement and the propagation;
bounding these contributions independently would discard their
cancellation. The lemma does not assume statistical independence, and
yaw error does not enter the conditional velocity comparison directly.
In the sampled position--IMU filter, yaw uncertainty does affect the
reconstructed velocity enclosure.

Yaw and inertial position are estimated in parallel. Let $\bm v_B$
denote the body-frame velocity pseudo-measurement, $\bm v_I=\bm v_m$ the
reconstructed inertial velocity, and $s_f=\omega_f\bar n_p$. The yaw
innovation uses the regularized direction correspondence
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    c_f=\frac{\bm v_B^{\trans}\bm v_I}
        {\normtwo{\bm v_B}\normtwo{\bm v_I}+s_f^2},\quad
    s_f^{\psi}=\frac{v_{B,x}v_{I,y}-v_{B,y}v_{I,x}}
        {\normtwo{\bm v_B}\normtwo{\bm v_I}+s_f^2},\\
    \dot{\hat\psi}_E=u_3+k_\psi
        (s_f^{\psi}\cos\hat\psi_E-c_f\sin\hat\psi_E),\\
    \dot{\hat{\bm p}}_E=\bm v_m+k_p(\bm y_p-\hat{\bm p}_E).
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:parallel_outputs}
\end{equation}
A numerical guard covers a zero denominator. The yaw correction varies
continuously with the direction information and does not switch off
according to the informative-arc flag. An orientation set $\Psi$ is
propagated separately using the gyro error bound and intersected with
valid measurement-consistent arcs. If the velocity information cannot
constrain direction, its contribution is the whole circle. Disconnected
components are kept, and the published radius is
$B_\psi=\sup_{\theta\in\Psi}d_{S^1}(\hat\psi_E,\theta)$ about the
numerical estimate. An empty intersection invalidates orientation
outputs. Heading convergence from rest is not claimed.

For the continuous position equation with a continuously available
position signal and a uniform velocity-input error bound, the position
error $\bm e_p=\hat{\bm p}_E-\bm p_E$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    D^+\normtwo{\bm e_p}\leq-k_p\normtwo{\bm e_p}+\bar n_v+k_p\bar n_p,\\
    \limsup_{t\to\infty}\normtwo{\bm e_p}\leq\bar n_p+\bar n_v/k_p.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:position_error_bound}
\end{equation}
This continuous comparison is conditional on its input bounds. The
sampled realization separately accounts for held measurements and
numerical defects; the comparison alone is not a sampled-system proof.

\subsubsection{Model-Aided Lateral Velocity}
\label{subsec:modelAided}

The kinematic measurement in \eqref{eq:ego_observer} depends on the
single-track relation in Assumption~\ref{asm:singleTrack}, whose mismatch
can increase when the rear tire develops lateral slip. As highlighted in
\cite{AlaiSharmaAlexanderRajamani2024,KyongAlaiTamehRajamani2026}, accurate
ego lateral-velocity (sideslip) information is critical for relative-motion
target tracking during turning maneuvers. To account for rear-tire slip
during evasive maneuvers, the estimator uses the ego vehicle's dynamic
bicycle model
and the control input $\bm u=[\delta_F,u_L]^{\trans}$ held by the
controller, where $\delta_F$ is the front steering angle and $u_L$ is
the longitudinal-force ratio. Let $m_E$ be the vehicle mass, $l_{f,E}$
and $l_{r,E}$ the distances from its center of mass to the front and
rear axles, and $F_{zi}$ and $\mu_i$ the normal load and friction
coefficient at axle $i\in\{f,r\}$. The slip angles and lateral tire
forces are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \alpha_f=\operatorname{atan2}(v_y+l_{f,E}\dot\psi_E,\,v_x)
        -\delta_F,\\
    \alpha_r=\operatorname{atan2}(v_y-l_{r,E}\dot\psi_E,\,v_x),\\
    F_{yf}=\Phi_f(\alpha_f;Q_f),\qquad F_{yr}=\Phi_r(\alpha_r;Q_r).
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:tire_forces}
\end{equation}
For axle \(i\in\{f,r\}\), with \(t=\tan\alpha\), \(C_i>0\) the
nominal cornering stiffness and \(Q_i=\eta_i\mu_iF_{zi}\), the modified
Fiala law of \cite[Sec.~II-A, eqs.~(7), (11) and (13)]{FahmyGhanyBaumann2018}
is
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_i(\alpha;Q_i)=
    \begin{cases}
    -C_it+\dfrac{C_i^2}{3Q_i}|t|t-\dfrac{C_i^3}{27Q_i^2}t^3,
       & |\alpha|<\alpha_{{\rm sl},i},\\[3pt]
    -Q_i\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha), & |\alpha|\geq\alpha_{{\rm sl},i},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:fiala_force}
\end{equation}
with \(\alpha_{{\rm sl},i}=\arctan(3Q_i/C_i)\),
\(\eta_i=\sqrt{1-u_L^2}\) and \(F_{xi}=u_L\mu_iF_{zi}\) for
\(|u_L|<1\). The lateral force balance at the center of mass is
\begin{equation}
    m_Ea_{y,E}=G(v_y):=F_{xf}\sin\delta_F
        +F_{yf}(\alpha_f)\cos\delta_F+F_{yr}(\alpha_r),
    \label{eq:force_balance}
\end{equation}
where $a_{y,E}$ is the true lateral acceleration in the ego frame. The inversion assumes that $G$ is non-increasing in
$v_y$ on the declared operating domain; wherever $G$ is strictly
decreasing, the lateral force balance determines $v_y$.

The numerical pseudo-measurement $c$ is obtained from the nominal
force balance with a proximal regularization. Let $G_0$ denote the
nominal force function and let
$\epsilon_F=m_E\bar n_a+\epsilon_{\rm par}\sum_j\mu_jF_{zj}$.
On $|c|\leq V_m\sin\beta_{\rm dom}$, the point solution satisfies
\begin{equation}
    G_0(c)-\frac{\epsilon_F}{\max(V_m\sin\beta_{\rm dom},\epsilon_{\rm mach})}c
    =m_Ea_y,
    \label{eq:regularized_force_inverse}
\end{equation}
with clamping at the cone endpoints when no root lies inside. The added
slope regularizes the point inversion when tire force is saturated; it is
not an additional physical tire force. A separate feasible interval is
computed alongside the point estimate. Let the cornering stiffnesses and friction
coefficients lie within a relative uncertainty $\epsilon_{\rm par}$ of
their model values, and let $G_{\min}$ and $G_{\max}$ be the sums of the
per-axle extremes of the force over the sensor bounds on $u_3$ and $V_m$
and over this parameter box. The implementation evaluates parameter and
measurement corners to bound the axle forces. This construction
requires the relevant force monotonicity and non-increasing force envelopes to hold on the declared
operating domain. Under these conditions, every admissible lateral
velocity satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    G_{\min}(v_y)\leq m_E(a_y+\bar n_a),\\
    G_{\max}(v_y)\geq m_E(a_y-\bar n_a),
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:force_interval}
\end{equation}
which is an interval $[\underline c,\overline c]$, and
$\rho_c=\max(c-\underline c,\overline c-c)$ bounds $|c-v_y|$. An
empty interval means that the measurements contradict the model or the
cone, and the ego enclosure is declared invalid. With
$\bm y_v=[\phi(V_m,c),\,c]^{\trans}$ in \eqref{eq:ego_observer}, the
mean-value argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:egoRows} gives
$\normtwo{\bm y_v-\bm v^E}\leq\sec\beta_{\rm dom}(\bar n_v+\rho_c)$ on
the cone, and the velocity row of the comparison becomes
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    D^+\normtwo{\bm e_v}\leq{}&-k_v\normtwo{\bm e_v}+\bar n_a
        +\bar n_gV_E|_{\max}\\
        &+k_v\sec\beta_{\rm dom}\,(\bar n_v+\rho_c(t)).
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:model_aided_row}
\end{equation}
The model-aided bound replaces the kinematic mismatch premise with the
force-model structure, parameter box and operating cone. The gyro
cancellation of Lemma~\ref{lem:egoRows} is not retained in
\eqref{eq:model_aided_row}. The radius $\rho_c$ is computed online;
near tire saturation, the force balance becomes less informative and
the interval can widen and become asymmetric. The controller retains
the operating-cone premise, but does not prohibit rear-tire saturation.
Dividing this interval by the speed interval gives a sideslip interval
for the yaw correspondence developed below. Without the model or a held
input, the kinematic measurement of Section~\ref{subsec:egoObserver} is
used unchanged. The force-balance bound is conditional on the model and
uncertainty assumptions and does not certify a plant with different
low-speed dynamics.

\subsubsection{Certified Course--Yaw Correspondence}
\label{subsec:courseCertificate}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:course}
Let Assumption~\ref{asm:singleTrack} hold and
\(V_m=\normtwo{\bm v_m}>\bar n_v\), put
\(\underline V=V_m-\bar n_v\), and suppose \(|l_{r,E}u_3|\leq V_m\).
Define
\begin{equation}
    \hat\beta=\arcsin\frac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m},
    \qquad
    y_F=\angle\bm v_m-\hat\beta .
    \label{eq:course_definition}
\end{equation}
Then, with
\begin{equation}
    \bar e_{\sin}
    =\frac{l_{r,E}}{\underline V}
    \Bigl(\bar n_g+\bar d_{\rm st}+\frac{|u_3|\bar n_v}{V_m}\Bigr),
    \label{eq:sine_error_bound}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \bar e_\beta=\max_{\pm}\Bigl|
    \arcsin\bigl(\Pi\bigl[\tfrac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m}\pm\bar e_{\sin}\bigr]\bigr)
    -\hat\beta\Bigr|,
    \label{eq:sideslip_error}
\end{equation}
where \(\Pi\) clips to \([-\sin\beta_{\rm dom},\sin\beta_{\rm dom}]\), one has
\(|\beta_E-\hat\beta|\leq\bar e_\beta\) and
\begin{equation}
    |y_F-\psi_E|\leq B_F:=\arcsin\frac{\bar n_v}{V_m}+\bar e_\beta .
    \label{eq:course_certificate}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
By \eqref{eq:single_track_mismatch},
\(\sin\beta_E=l_{r,E}(u_3-d_\omega-d_{\rm st})/V_E\), so
\begin{equation}
    \Bigl|\sin\beta_E-\frac{l_{r,E}u_3}{V_m}\Bigr|
    \leq l_{r,E}\Bigl[\frac{|d_\omega|+|d_{\rm st}|}{V_E}
    +|u_3|\frac{|V_m-V_E|}{V_EV_m}\Bigr],
    \label{eq:sine_derivation}
\end{equation}
and \(|V_m-V_E|\leq\bar n_v\), \(V_E\geq\underline V\) give
\eqref{eq:sine_error_bound}. Since \(|\sin\beta_E|\leq\sin\beta_{\rm dom}\),
the true sine lies in the clipped interval, on which \(\arcsin\) is
increasing, which gives \eqref{eq:sideslip_error}. For the course,
\(\bm v_E\) lies in the disk of radius \(\bar n_v\) about \(\bm v_m\);
the disk excludes the origin, so \(\bm v_E\) lies in its tangent cone and
\(|\angle\bm v_m-\gamma_E|\leq\arcsin(\bar n_v/V_m)\). Subtracting
\(\gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E\) yields \eqref{eq:course_certificate}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The correspondence is used only where these conditions hold; otherwise
it contributes the whole circle to the yaw enclosure. Neither case
touches the body-velocity innovation, which is defined at standstill as
well.

\subsubsection{Target-Vehicle Observer}
\label{subsec:targetTracker}

The declared operating set is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    a\leq\normtwo{\bm q}\leq b,\quad
    |A_C|\leq A_{\max},\quad
    \normtwo{\bm r}\leq r_{\max},\\
    |\omega_C|\leq\Omega_{\max}=\frac{b\sin\beta_{\max}}{l_{r,C}},\\
    \normtwo{\bm s}\leq S=\sqrt{A_{\max}^2+b^2\Omega_{\max}^2},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_domain}
\end{equation}
with \(a\) and \(b\) the target speed bounds \(V_C|_{\min}\) and
\(V_C|_{\max}\), $0<a<b$, and $l_{r,C}$ the distance from the target
center of mass to its rear axle. The course-rate limit $\Omega_{\max}$ is
imposed on the target motion and parameterized by $\beta_{\max}$.
Estimates may leave
\eqref{eq:target_domain} during a transient, so the observer evaluates
an extension. With $\Pi_r$ the Euclidean projection onto the ball of
radius $r$ and $\operatorname{clip}_r$ the clip to $[-r,r]$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    d=\max(\normtwo{\bm q},a),\quad
    \bm Q=\Pi_b(\bm q),\quad\tilde{\bm s}=\Pi_S(\bm s),\\
    A_e=\operatorname{clip}_{A_{\max}}
        (\bm Q^{\trans}\tilde{\bm s}/d),\\
    \Omega_e=\operatorname{clip}_{\Omega_{\max}}
        ((J\bm Q)^{\trans}\tilde{\bm s}/d^2),\\
    \Phi_e(\bm q,\bm s)=-\Omega_e^2\bm Q
        +3A_e\Omega_e J\bm q/d,
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_extension}
\end{equation}
which agrees with $\Phi$ on \eqref{eq:target_domain} and is defined on
$\R^4$.

The tracker is the covariant high-gain chain
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm r}&=\hat{\bm q}-\hat{\bm v}^E
        +l_1\omega\,(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm r}),\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm q}&=\hat{\bm s}
        +l_2\omega^2(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm r}),\\
    \nabla_{u_3}\hat{\bm s}&=\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})
        +l_3\omega^3(\bm y_R-\hat{\bm r}).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_observer}
\end{equation}
Let \(A_c\) be the integrator-chain matrix, \(C=\bm e_1^{\trans}\) and
\(A_l=A_c-\bm lC\). The shape $\bm l$ and the bandwidth $\omega>0$ are
chosen to satisfy the dissipation condition developed below. For
$\bm e_T=\hat{\bm x}_T-\bm x_T$, put $D=\diag(1,\omega^{-1},\omega^{-2})$
and, for $P\succ0$, define
\begin{equation}
    \bm\epsilon=(D\otimes I_2)\bm e_T,
    \qquad
    W_T=\sqrt{\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(P\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon}.
    \label{eq:target_metric}
\end{equation}
The derivative-based treatment of nonlinear increments follows the
framework of \cite[Lemma~1 and Theorem~1]{SharmaZemoucheRajamani2026};
the normalization in \eqref{eq:target_metric} also draws on the state
scaling in \cite[Sec.~3, Eqs.~(33)--(35)]{SharmaZemoucheRajamani2026}.
The extension \eqref{eq:target_extension} and its explicit derivative
bounds below are derived for the present NRMM coordinates.
Write $\Delta\Phi_e=\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})-\Phi_e(\bm q,\bm s)$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:lipschitz}
With
\(c_A=S/a\), \(c_\Omega=S\max(a^{-2},2b^{-2})\),
\(c_{Q\Omega}=S\max(a^{-1},2b^{-1})\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    L_q=\Omega_{\max}^2+2\Omega_{\max}c_{Q\Omega}
        +3\Omega_{\max}\Bigl(\frac{A_{\max}}a+c_A\Bigr)\\
        +3A_{\max}c_\Omega,\\
    L_s=\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}
        0&2\Omega_{\max}\\3\Omega_{\max}&3A_{\max}/a
        \end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_2,
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:lipschitz_constants}
\end{equation}
\(\Phi_e\) satisfies, for all \((\bm q,\bm s),(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})\),
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \normtwo{\Phi_e(\bm q,\bm s)-\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})}\\
    \leq L_q\normtwo{\bm q-\hat{\bm q}}+L_s\normtwo{\bm s-\hat{\bm s}} .
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:global_lipschitz}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Projections and clips are nonexpansive, and $a>0$ makes $\Phi_e$ locally
Lipschitz and piecewise differentiable, so it suffices to bound its
almost-everywhere Jacobians and integrate along the segment joining the
two arguments. Differentiating in the radial and tangential basis at
fixed \(\bm s\), with \(V_q=\normtwo{\bm q}\), \(\bm Q\) the projected
velocity and \(A_e\), \(\Omega_e\) the clipped reconstructions, gives
\(|D_qA_e|\leq S/\max(V_q,a)\) and \(|D_q\Omega_e|\leq S/\max(V_q,a)^2\) for
\(V_q\leq b\), and \(|D_qA_e|\leq bS/V_q^2\), \(|D_q\Omega_e|\leq2bS/V_q^3\)
for \(V_q\geq b\), whose suprema over \(V_q>0\) are \(c_A\) and
\(c_\Omega\), while \(\normtwo{\bm Q}|D_q\Omega_e|\leq c_{Q\Omega}\).
Using $\|D_q\bm Q\|_2\leq1$, $\normtwo{\bm q/d}\leq1$ and
$\|D_q(\bm q/d)\|_2\leq1/a$ in \eqref{eq:target_extension} gives $L_q$.
At fixed $\bm q\ne0$ the Jacobian with respect to $\tilde{\bm s}$, in
the basis aligned with $\bm q$, is bounded entrywise in absolute value
by the matrix in \eqref{eq:lipschitz_constants}, so its induced norm is
at most $L_s$; composing with $\tilde{\bm s}=\Pi_S(\bm s)$ preserves the
bound because $\|D_s\Pi_S\|_2\leq1$ almost everywhere, and continuity
covers $\bm q=0$.
\end{IEEEproof}

The following sufficient condition uses the derivative-based LMI design
principle of \cite[Theorem~1]{SharmaZemoucheRajamani2026}, with the
nonlinear increments bounded by \eqref{eq:global_lipschitz}. Its two
norm multipliers yield the NRMM condition proved here; they do not
reproduce the vector-sector multipliers of that paper's Eq.~(13).

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:dissipation}
Let $P\succ0$, \(\bm b_P=P\bm e_3\), \(t_q,t_s>0\), and
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    M={}&-\omega(A_l^{\trans}P+PA_l)
       -t_q\frac{L_q^2}{\omega^2}\bm e_2\bm e_2^{\trans}\\
       &-t_sL_s^2\bm e_3\bm e_3^{\trans}
       -(t_q^{-1}+t_s^{-1})\bm b_P\bm b_P^{\trans}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:dissipation_matrix}
\end{equation}
If \(M\succeq2\lambda_TP\) then \(D^+W_T\leq-\lambda_TW_T\) in the
absence of exogenous error inputs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
In the coordinate \(\bm\epsilon\) the injection block becomes
\(\omega A_l\otimes I_2\), and the common rotation \(-u_3(I_3\otimes J)\)
is skew and contributes nothing to
\(\tfrac{d}{dt}\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(P\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon\). The
injection contributes
$\omega\bm\epsilon^{\trans}[(A_l^{\trans}P+PA_l)\otimes I_2]\bm\epsilon$.
Splitting $\Delta\Phi_e/\omega^2$ into its velocity and acceleration
increments, \eqref{eq:global_lipschitz} bounds them by
$(L_q/\omega)\normtwo{\bm\epsilon_2}$ and $L_s\normtwo{\bm\epsilon_3}$,
and each enters the derivative through
$2\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(\bm b_P\otimes I_2)$. Applying
\(2\bm z^{\trans}\bm d\leq\normtwo{\bm z}^2/t+t\normtwo{\bm d}^2\) once
per channel gives
\(\tfrac{d}{dt}W_T^2\leq-\bm\epsilon^{\trans}(M\otimes I_2)\bm\epsilon
\leq-2\lambda_TW_T^2\), and the square root gives the comparison,
including $W_T=0$ by continuity of the homogeneous error dynamics.
\end{IEEEproof}

\subsubsection{Cascade ISS}
\label{subsec:cascade}

For a target with time-varying motion, write the course curvature as
$\kappa=\omega_C/V_C$. Differentiating the target velocity gives the
residual in \eqref{eq:target_chain} as
$\bm\nu_\Phi=\dot A_C\bm q/V_C+V_C^2\dot\kappa J\bm q/V_C$. If
$|\dot A_C|\leq\overline{\dot A}_C$ and
$|\dot\kappa|\leq\overline{\dot\kappa}$, then
$\normtwo{\bm\nu_\Phi}\leq\bar\nu_\Phi
=(\overline{\dot A}_C^2+b^4\overline{\dot\kappa}^2)^{1/2}$.
Subtracting the true chain from \eqref{eq:target_observer},
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \dot{\bm\epsilon}={}&
        [\omega A_l\otimes I_2-u_3(I_3\otimes J)]\bm\epsilon\\
        &+\omega^{-2}(\bm e_3\otimes I_2)
            (\Delta\Phi_e-\bm\nu_\Phi)\\
        &-(\bm e_1\otimes I_2)\bm e_v
            -d_\omega(D\otimes J)\bm x_T\\
        &+\omega(\bm l\otimes I_2)\bm n_R.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_error_dynamics}
\end{equation}
With the reconstructed-velocity error treated as a bounded input, the
core couplings run forward from the ego stage into the tracker. The
position--IMU filter depends on the yaw estimate, so this triangular
comparison does not establish ISS of the enlarged interconnection.
Cauchy--Schwarz in the metric \eqref{eq:target_metric} bounds the four
input channels by
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    g_{Tv}=\sqrt{P_{11}},\qquad
    g_R=\omega\sqrt{\bm l^{\trans}P\bm l},\\
    g_\omega=\sqrt{\bm h^{\trans}|P|\bm h},\qquad
    g_\Phi=\sqrt{P_{33}}/\omega^2,\\
    \bm h=[r_{\max},\ b/\omega,\ S/\omega^2]^{\trans},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:target_disturbance_coefficients}
\end{equation}
with $|P|$ entrywise, so that
$D^+W_T\leq-\lambda_TW_T+g_{Tv}\normtwo{\bm e_v}
+g_\omega\bar n_g+g_R\bar n_R+g_\Phi\bar\nu_\Phi$. Together with
Lemma~\ref{lem:egoRows} this is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \bm\chi=[\normtwo{\bm e_v},\ W_T]^{\trans},\qquad
    D^+\bm\chi\leq H\bm\chi+\bm d,\\
    H=\begin{bmatrix}-k_v&0\\g_{Tv}&-\lambda_T\end{bmatrix},\qquad
    \bm d=\begin{bmatrix}\bar\delta_v\\
    g_\omega\bar n_g+g_R\bar n_R+g_\Phi\bar\nu_\Phi\end{bmatrix}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:cascade_comparison}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Cascade ISS]
\label{thm:cascadeISS}
Let Assumptions~\ref{asm:ego}--\ref{asm:singleTrack}, the hypotheses of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:egoRows}, \ref{lem:lipschitz} and \ref{lem:dissipation},
the true target domain \eqref{eq:target_domain} and the declared
continuous sensor and model-error bounds hold, with $k_v,\lambda_T>0$.
Then $H$ is Metzler and Hurwitz, and $\bm c=-H^{-\trans}\bm 1>0$ gives
the linear copositive ISS function $V_c=\bm c^{\trans}\bm\chi$. For any
$\bm z_0\geq\bm\chi(0)$,
\begin{equation}
    \bm\chi(t)\leq\bm z(t)
    =e^{Ht}\bm z_0+\int_0^t e^{H(t-\tau)}\bm d\,d\tau,
    \label{eq:comparison_solution}
\end{equation}
and $\limsup_{t\to\infty}\bm\chi(t)\leq-H^{-1}\bm d$. The physical
component bounds are
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm e_{T,i}}\leq c_{T,i}W_T,\qquad
    \bm c_T=\diag(1,\omega,\omega^2)\sqrt{\diag(P^{-1})}.
    \label{eq:component_extraction}
\end{equation}
For the fixed design, the core error
$\bm e=[\bm e_v^{\trans},\bm e_T^{\trans}]^{\trans}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm e(t)}\leq C_e e^{-\alpha_e t}\normtwo{\bm e(0)}
        +\Gamma_e\|\bm\eta\|_{\infty,[0,t]},
    \label{eq:core_exponential_iss}
\end{equation}
with constants $C_e,\alpha_e,\Gamma_e>0$ and the disturbance vector
$\bm\eta=[\bm n_a^{\trans},\bm n_v^{\trans},d_\omega,d_{\rm st},
\bm n_R^{\trans},\bm\nu_\Phi^{\trans}]^{\trans}$, where
$\|\bm\eta\|_{\infty,[0,t]}$ is the essential supremum of
$\normtwo{\bm\eta(\tau)}$ over $0\leq\tau\leq t$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
$H$ is lower triangular with a negative diagonal and nonnegative
off-diagonal entries, so $e^{Ht}\geq0$ and $-H^{-1}\geq0$, which give
\eqref{eq:comparison_solution}, the ultimate bound and $\bm c>0$. Since
$\bm c^{\trans}H=-\bm1^{\trans}$,
$D^+V_c\leq-V_c/c_{\max}+\bm c^{\trans}\bm d$ with
$c_{\max}=\max_i c_i$. The same comparisons hold with instantaneous
disturbance magnitudes in place of their uniform bounds; for fixed
gains their weighted forcing is at most $K\normtwo{\bm\eta(t)}$ for some
$K>0$. Since $P\succ0$ and $\omega>0$ is fixed, there are
$m_c,M_c>0$ with $m_c\normtwo{\bm e}\leq V_c\leq M_c\normtwo{\bm e}$,
and integrating the scalar comparison proves
\eqref{eq:core_exponential_iss} with $C_e=M_c/m_c$,
$\alpha_e=1/c_{\max}$ and $\Gamma_e=Kc_{\max}/m_c$. The bound also rules
out finite escape under the declared premises, and with all
disturbances zero the core error converges exponentially.
Equation~\eqref{eq:component_extraction} is the ellipsoid support in
$P\otimes I_2$ after undoing the position normalization.
\end{IEEEproof}

The core has two ego-velocity states and six states for the target. Its
certificate needs neither informative yaw measurements nor a yaw
innovation chart. The shared gyro couples the two core stages as well;
\eqref{eq:cascade_comparison} uses a triangular norm bound and does not
claim every cross-stage cancellation. Orientation and position remain
separately certified outputs.

\subsubsection{Gain Selection}
\label{subsec:gainSelection}

With $T_{\rm dom}=r_{\max}/(V_E|_{\max}+V_C|_{\max})$, the scalar
gains follow a transit-time preference,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \min_{k>0}k\quad\text{s.t.}\quad e^{-kT_{\rm dom}}\leq0.05,\\
    k_\psi=k_v=k_p=\frac{\ln20}{T_{\rm dom}},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:scalar_gain_rule}
\end{equation}
which is not an optimum of the common-error bound in
\eqref{eq:ego_rows}: increasing $k_v$ changes the combined gyro
coefficient and need not improve that bound.

The target gains are decision variables of a semidefinite program that
contains the certificate. In physical coordinates ($\omega=1$) let
$\bm L\in\R^3$ be the gain vector, $A_L=A_c-\bm LC$ and $\bm Y=P\bm L$,
and put $\Xi=A_c^{\trans}P+PA_c-\bm YC-C^{\trans}\bm Y^{\trans}$, which
equals $A_L^{\trans}P+PA_L$. The required decay rate of the target
metric is the floor $\lambda_T=1/T_{\rm dom}$, one $e$-folding per
transit time. With $\Delta_\Phi=\diag(0,t_qL_q^2,t_sL_s^2)$, solve
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \min_{P\succ0,\,\bm Y,\,t_q,t_s>0,\,\gamma}\ \gamma
    \quad\text{s.t.}\\
    \begin{bmatrix}
    \Xi+2\lambda_TP+\Delta_\Phi&P\bm e_3&P\bm e_3\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&-t_q&0\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&0&-t_s
    \end{bmatrix}\prec0,\\
    \Xi+\bm e_2\bm e_2^{\trans}\prec0,\qquad
    \begin{bmatrix}\gamma&\bm Y^{\trans}\\\bm Y&P\end{bmatrix}\succeq0 .
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:noise_shape_lmi}
\end{equation}
The first inequality is the Schur form of Lemma~\ref{lem:dissipation}
at $\omega=1$, so $\bm L=P^{-1}\bm Y$ satisfies the dissipation
inequality at the decay floor. The second makes $P$ an upper bound of
the observability Gramian of $(A_L,\bm e_2^{\trans})$
\cite{BoydElGhaouiFeronBalakrishnan1994}: for the linear error equation
$\dot{\bm e}=A_L\bm e+\bm Ln$ driven by white output noise $n$ of unit
intensity, the stationary variance of the velocity error is at most
$\bm L^{\trans}P\bm L\leq\gamma$. All conditions are linear in
$(P,\bm Y,t_q,t_s,\gamma)$. One metric serves both the Gramian and the
dissipation inequality, so $\gamma$ is an upper bound on the smallest
variance attainable among certified gains, not that minimum; the
variance is a performance preference that ignores the nonlinear term
and the sampled realization, and it is not an error bound.

The returned gains are written as
$\bm L=[l_1\omega_0,\,l_2\omega_0^2,\,l_3\omega_0^3]^{\trans}$ with
$\omega_0=\min_i|\operatorname{Re}\lambda_i(A_L)|$ the slowest
closed-loop rate, which fixes a shape $\bm l$ of unit slowest rate. For
each candidate bandwidth $\omega>1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ the certificate
metric is solved anew: minimize $\operatorname{tr}P$ over $P\succeq I$
and $t_q,t_s>0$ subject to
\begin{equation}
    \begin{bmatrix}
    Z&P\bm e_3&P\bm e_3\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&-t_q&0\\
    \bm e_3^{\trans}P&0&-t_s
    \end{bmatrix}\preceq0,
    \label{eq:free_metric_lmi}
\end{equation}
where $Z=\omega(A_l^{\trans}P+PA_l)+2\lambda_TP
+\diag(0,t_qL_q^2/\omega^2,t_sL_s^2)$, whose Schur complement is
Lemma~\ref{lem:dissipation}. The normalization fixes the homogeneous
scale and leaves the metric free; the recovered matrix is checked in
the original inequality with numerical reserves. A bracketed scalar
search then minimizes the largest of the three ultimate bounds
$c_{T,i}(-H^{-1}\bm d)_2$ of \eqref{eq:component_extraction}, each
divided by its domain scale $r_{\max}$, $b$ or $S$. This is a
feasible local selection of the bandwidth, not a joint optimum, and
because the search may move the bandwidth away from $\omega_0$, the
variance bound $\gamma$ applies to the gains of
\eqref{eq:noise_shape_lmi}, not to the rescaled ones. The programs are
solved with YALMIP \cite{Lofberg2004} and SeDuMi \cite{Sturm1999}.
Neither simulated errors nor the sensor sample period enter the design.

\subsubsection{Error Enclosure and the Published Sets}
\label{subsec:enclosure}

The controller requires an error enclosure at each sample, including
the transient. The comparison solution in
\eqref{eq:comparison_solution} provides a bound for all \(t\).

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:enclosure}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:cascadeISS} and any
\(\bm z_0\geq\bm\chi(0)\),
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm r(t)-\hat{\bm r}(t)}\leq B_r(t):=c_{T,1}z_2(t),
    \quad c_{T,1}=\sqrt{(P^{-1})_{11}},
    \label{eq:relative_position_bound}
\end{equation}
and \(\limsup_{t\to\infty}B_r\leq c_{T,1}(-H^{-1}\bm d)_2\). An
initial metric bound follows from componentwise initial bounds
\(\bm b_{T0}\) as
\(W_T(0)\leq\bigl((D\bm b_{T0})^{\trans}|P|(D\bm b_{T0})\bigr)^{1/2}\).
Moreover the comparison solution is bounded uniformly in time,
\begin{equation}
    \bm z(t)\leq\bar{\bm z}:=\sup_{\tau\geq0}\bm z(\tau)<\infty ,
    \label{eq:uniform_bound}
\end{equation}
so every radius derived from it is bounded by a constant that depends
only on the design, the declared bounds and \(\bm z_0\).
\end{corollary}

The uniform bound \eqref{eq:uniform_bound} follows from $e^{Ht}\to0$
and convergence of the comparison integral. The safety interface uses
the valid transient enclosure at the current time, without assuming a constant future radius or convergence of future
observer errors.

Inertial reconstruction of the relative vector costs one further yaw
term,
\begin{equation}
    \normtwo{\bm p_C-\bm p_E-R(\hat\psi_E)\hat{\bm r}}
    \leq B_r+2\bar R\sin\frac{\min(B_\psi,\pi)}2 ,
    \label{eq:inertial_relative_bound}
\end{equation}
\(\bar R=\min(r_{\max},\normtwo{\hat{\bm r}})\), and the analogous
rotation bounds convert the remaining components of
\eqref{eq:component_extraction} into the componentwise ego and target
position, velocity and acceleration enclosures \(\bm b_E(t)\),
\(\bm b_T(t)\). A target body-yaw enclosure additionally requires a
valid relation between its sideslip and course curvature. These bounds
certify the current estimate; they are not predictions of future observer
output. When the
required bounds are valid, the controller receives ego and target sets
at each sample $k$. The ego set is a
zonotope
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal E_k=\{G_0\bm\zeta:\norminf{\bm\zeta}\leq1\}
    \label{eq:ego_set}
\end{equation}
about the estimated controller state, whose generator matrix $G_0$
keeps the correlation between the yaw error and the body velocity
inferred from reconstructed inertial velocity. The target set $\mathcal Q_k$ consists
of a ball of radius $B_r$ for the relative position (an inertial
ball once \eqref{eq:inertial_relative_bound} and the ego position radius
are added), an interval for the course, and intervals for the speed, the
scalar acceleration $A_C$ and the curvature $\kappa_C$, each intersected
with the declared domain \eqref{eq:target_domain}. The last two follow
from the reconstructions $A_C$ and $\omega_C/V_C$ of \eqref{eq:target_chain}
and the component bounds \eqref{eq:component_extraction}. When prior
measurements are available, the target enclosures can be tightened by
intersecting them with bounds obtained from bounded-noise position
secants and interpolation. Under the constant-acceleration and
constant-curvature model, the controller can also retain a
history-conditioned joint parameter set for target speed, scalar
acceleration, curvature and course, preserving their dependence rather
than replacing it with independent intervals. These refinements do not
change the observer's point estimate and remain conditional on the
specified sensor and motion bounds. The digital runtime accounts for
held measurements, relative-position prediction and outages in a
separate finite-time enclosure. The continuous theorem does
not establish exponential stability of the sampled fourth-order
Runge--Kutta (RK4) realization, nor a directed-rounding certificate for
the computed bounds.

\subsection{Control Design}
\label{sec:control}

The adopted controller combines fixed measurement-consistency inference
over a retained physical ensemble with condensed nonlinear predictive
control barrier function (PCBF) optimization of held actuator commands,
following the predictive barrier formulation of
\cite{HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025} and related predictive safety
filtering ideas \cite{WabersichZeilinger2023}. Sparse nonlinear
optimization over few control knots across a substantial preview with warm
initialization is motivated by \cite{ThompsonDallasGohBalachandran2024},
adapted here to a time-coordinate held-input vehicle model that includes
rest. The controller enforces terminal membership by checking safe
continuation to sensing-range exit, retains a complete held-input plan for
validation at the next measurement, and applies collision constraints at
control-boundary state nodes. On the zero-deficit level, predicted tire
loading and rear slip guide the choice of driving sequence without
altering the primary safety objective or terminal membership condition.
Section~\ref{subsec:robust} develops the idealized joint nonlinear
observer--feedback theory and proves recursive feasibility under its
declared contracts; the subsections below present the adopted compact
nonlinear algorithm and the explicit limits of its guarantees. The
obstacle vehicle of this section is the target of
Section~\ref{sec:estimator}.

\subsubsection{Prediction Model and Physical Contract}
\label{subsec:predictionModel}

The ego vehicle has speed \(V_E\) and yaw \(\psi_E\), the obstacle speed
\(V_C\) and yaw \(\psi_C\). One ego model serves the whole system, a
dynamic bicycle with modified Fiala tires: the controller predicts with
it, and the estimator inverts its lateral force balance to measure the
lateral velocity (Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided}). With
\(\bm p_E\in\R^2\) and \(\bm v_E\in\R^2\) the inertial position and
velocity, \(\bm v^E=[v_x\;\;v_y]^{\trans}=R(\psi_E)^{\trans}\bm v_E\) the
body velocity, and
\begin{equation}
    R(\psi_E)=\begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\psi_E&-\sin\psi_E\\
        \sin\psi_E&\cos\psi_E
    \end{bmatrix}
    \label{eq:planar_rotation}
\end{equation}
the body-to-inertial rotation, the position kinematics are
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\bm p}_E=\bm v_E=R(\psi_E)\bm v^E .
    \label{eq:position_kinematics}
\end{equation}
The force and moment balances in the body frame read
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    m_E\dot v_x&=F_{xf}^{b}+F_{xr}-F_R(v_x)
        +m_Ev_y\dot\psi_E,\\
    m_E\dot v_y&=F_{yf}^{b}+F_{yr}-m_Ev_x\dot\psi_E,\\
    I_{z,E}\ddot\psi_E&=l_{f,E}F_{yf}^{b}-l_{r,E}F_{yr},
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:bicycle_model}
\end{equation}
where $[F_{xf}^{b},F_{yf}^{b}]^{\trans}
=R(\delta_F)[F_{xf},F_{yf}]^{\trans}$ rotates the front wheel forces into
the body frame and $F_R$ is the passive road load \eqref{eq:road_load}.
The slip angles and lateral tire forces are given by
\eqref{eq:tire_forces} and \eqref{eq:fiala_force}. One signed
longitudinal-force ratio \(u_L\in[-1,1]\) acts on both axles: negative
values brake, positive values drive. The static loads are
\(F_{zf}=m_Egl_{r,E}/L\) and \(F_{zr}=m_Egl_{f,E}/L\) with
\(L=l_{f,E}+l_{r,E}\), the longitudinal forces are
\(F_{xi}=u_L\mu_iF_{zi}\), and the lateral force capacities are
\(Q_i=\mu_iF_{zi}\sqrt{1-u_L^2}\) for \(i\in\{f,r\}\). We use the
symmetric branch condition and \cite[eq.~(13)]{FahmyGhanyBaumann2018};
the additional arctangent printed in \cite[eq.~(12)]{FahmyGhanyBaumann2018}
is inconsistent with it. At \(|u_L|=1\) we set \(Q_i=0\) and
\(\Phi_i=0\) without evaluating the singular polynomial. Tire saturation
is part of the physical model; admitted flows must remain in a domain on
which the declared vehicle and observer dynamics are well posed. Speed,
sideslip and course are
\begin{equation}
    V_E=\normtwo{\bm v_E},
    \qquad
    \beta_E=\operatorname{atan2}(v_y,v_x),
    \qquad
    \gamma_E=\psi_E+\beta_E .
    \label{eq:yaw_from_course}
\end{equation}

For the controller, write $\psi=\psi_E$ and
$\bm p=[x_E,y_E]^{\trans}$. The physical ego state and held actuator
input at control instants $t_k=kh$ ($h=T_s>0$) are
\begin{equation}
    \bm x=[x_E,\,y_E,\,\psi,\,v_x,\,v_y,\,\omega_E]^{\trans},\qquad
    \bm u=[\delta_F,\,u_L]^{\trans},
    \label{eq:controller_state}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_E=\dot\psi$ is the yaw rate. Equations
\eqref{eq:position_kinematics}, \eqref{eq:bicycle_model},
\eqref{eq:tire_forces} and \eqref{eq:fiala_force} define
$\dot{\bm x}=f(\bm x,\bm u)$ above the blend speed $v_2=4$\,m/s. Below
$v_1=2.5$\,m/s the body accelerations follow the low-speed kinematic map
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    m_E\dot v_x=\bigl[u_{L,+}+u_{L,-}\tanh(v_x/v_{\rm h})\bigr]\\
        \quad{}\times\bigl(\mu_fF_{zf}\cos\delta_F+\mu_rF_{zr}\bigr)\\
        \quad{}-F_R(v_x)+m_Ev_y\omega_E,\\
    \dot v_y=\frac{l_{r,E}\omega_{\rm k}-v_y}{T_{\rm k}},\qquad
    \dot\omega_E=\frac{\omega_{\rm k}-\omega_E}{T_{\rm k}},\\
    \omega_{\rm k}=\frac{v_x\tan\delta_F}{L},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:kinematic_model}
\end{equation}
with $u_{L,+}=\max(u_L,0)$, $u_{L,-}=\min(u_L,0)$, brake-hold speed
$v_{\rm h}$ and lag $T_{\rm k}>0$. Between $v_1$ and $v_2$, the three
body derivatives are blended using $w=s^2(3-2s)$ with
$s=\operatorname{clip}((v_x-v_1)/(v_2-v_1),0,1)$, while position and yaw
kinematics are unchanged. A braked vehicle slower than $0.2\,v_{\rm h}$
at a control boundary is reset to rest; any such hybrid reset is applied
only at control boundaries and consistently across prediction,
conditioning, and replay. The passive road load is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    F_R(v)={}&\tfrac12\rho_{\rm air}C_dA_fv|v|\\
       &+m_Eg(q_0+q_1|v|+q_4|v|^4)\tanh(v/v_{\rm tr}),
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:road_load}
\end{equation}
with air density $\rho_{\rm air}$, drag coefficient $C_d$, frontal area
$A_f$, nonnegative rolling coefficients $q_0,q_1,q_4$ and
$v_{\rm tr}>0$.

The body sideslip $\beta_E$ is the angle of the center-of-mass velocity,
not either tire slip angle $\alpha_f,\alpha_r$. In low-speed turning,
$v_y\simeq l_{r,E}\omega_E$ can give nonzero body sideslip while rear
tire slip is near zero. The shared observer sideslip cone
\begin{equation}
    |v_y|\leq v_x\tan\beta_{\rm dom}
    \label{eq:sideslip_cone}
\end{equation}
is an estimation-domain premise rather than a tire-force capacity limit;
enlarging it changes the estimator premise and recomputed error bounds
without increasing tire capacity. On the dynamic or blended branch,
well-posedness of the front-tire slip map requires
$v_x\cos\delta_F+(v_y+l_{f,E}\omega_E)\sin\delta_F>0$, equivalent to
$|\alpha_f|<\pi/2$ on the declared steering domain.

The reference path has constant curvature $\kappa_{\rm r}$ and prescribed
cruise speed $v_*$. On the path chart with phase $s_p$, signed lateral
offset $e_y$ and heading error $e_\psi$,
$\dot s_p=(v_x\cos e_\psi-v_y\sin e_\psi)/(1-\kappa_{\rm r}e_y)$,
$\dot e_y=v_x\sin e_\psi+v_y\cos e_\psi$, and
$\dot e_\psi=\omega_E-\kappa_{\rm r}\dot s_p$ on $1-\kappa_{\rm r}e_y>0$.
Deviation from the steady cruise trim $(\bm x_*,\bm u_*)$ is measured by
the transverse error
\begin{equation}
    \bm e(\bm x)=[e_y,\ e_\psi,\ v_x-v_{x,*},\ v_y-v_{y,*},\
        \omega_E-\omega_*]^{\trans},
    \label{eq:transverse_error}
\end{equation}
leaving longitudinal phase free.

The obstacle motion has constant tangential acceleration $A_C$ and
constant sideslip $\beta_C$ during forward motion, with an absorbing stop
at rest. With $\kappa_C=\sin\beta_C/l_{r,C}$, $V_C(t)=\max(0,V_{C,0}+A_Ct)$,
arc length $s(t)=\int_0^t V_C(\tau)\,d\tau$ and
$\vartheta(t)=\tfrac12\kappa_Cs(t)$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm p_C(t)&=\bm p_{C,0}+s\,\frac{\sin\vartheta}{\vartheta}
        \begin{bmatrix}\cos(\psi_{C,0}+\beta_C+\vartheta)\\
        \sin(\psi_{C,0}+\beta_C+\vartheta)\end{bmatrix},\\
    \psi_C(t)&=\psi_{C,0}+\kappa_Cs,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:target_flow}
\end{equation}
with $\sin\vartheta/\vartheta=1$ at $\vartheta=0$. For $A_C<0$, the
target stops at $V_C=0$ and retains its stopped pose while keeping its
latent parameters $(A_C,\beta_C)$ fixed; stopping is absorbing, and
inference retains the physical track through missed returns and range
exits.

Let $\widetilde F_h$ denote the numerical held-input flow over one
control period $h$, applying the rest reset only at the control boundary.
Nonlinear validation uses a fixed fine RK4 mesh ($K_v=8$ substeps per
hold by default) together with the continuous cubic RK4 interpolant for
dense physical-domain checks inside each hold, while local SQP
sensitivities propagate on a coarser mesh. Neither mesh supplies a
certified integration-error bound.

\subsubsection{Fixed Measurement-Consistency Inference}
\label{subsec:uncertainty}

Let the augmented physical Markov state be
\begin{equation}
    \bm\chi=(\bm p_E,R_E,\bm v^E,\omega_E,\bm r,R_{\rm rel},
        V_C,A_C,\beta_C,\bm\vartheta,\bm\sigma,\bm\eta),
    \label{eq:markov_state}
\end{equation}
where $\bm r=R_E^{\trans}(\bm p_C-\bm p_E)$,
$R_{\rm rel}=R_E^{\trans}R_C$, $\bm\vartheta$ collects persistent
physical parameters ($\bm\vartheta^+=\bm\vartheta$), $\bm\sigma$ collects
physical modes, and $\bm\eta$ collects persistent sensor biases or
disturbance memory. With $\bm e(\beta)=[\cos\beta,\sin\beta]^{\trans}$,
the relative pose obeys
$\dot{\bm r}=V_CR_{\rm rel}\bm e(\beta_C)-\bm v^E-\omega_EJ\bm r$ and
$\dot R_{\rm rel}=(\kappa_CV_C-\omega_E)JR_{\rm rel}$ during forward
target motion, reducing to $\dot{\bm r}=-\bm v^E-\omega_EJ\bm r$ and
$\dot R_{\rm rel}=-\omega_EJR_{\rm rel}$ after target absorption.

Observations arrive at sensor period $h_s$ with $h=Lh_s$: position-only
GNSS, gyro yaw rate, gravity-compensated center-of-mass body acceleration
evaluated under the preceding applied input $\bm u^-$, and relative
target position when available,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \bm y_p=\bm p_E+\bm\eta_p+\bm n_p,\qquad
    u_3=\omega_E+\eta_r+d_\omega,\\
    \bm a_m=f_v(\bm v^E,\omega_E,\bm u^-;\bm\vartheta)
        +\omega_EJ\bm v^E+\bm\eta_a+\bm n_a,\\
    \bm y_R=\bm r+\bm\eta_R+\bm n_R.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:raw_measurements}
\end{equation}
A derived velocity is not treated as an independent GNSS measurement.
Write $(\bm\chi^+,Y,\gamma)\in\mathscr R_h(\bm\chi,\bm u)$ when one
admissible realization generates the physical hold $\gamma$, every sensor
reading and detection flag in block $Y$, and endpoint $\bm\chi^+$. The
exact measurement-consistent set update is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal I_{k+1}=\{\bm\chi^+:\exists\bm\chi\in\mathcal I_k,\gamma,\;
        (\bm\chi^+,Y_{k+1},\gamma)\in\mathscr R_h(\bm\chi,\bm u_k)\},
    \label{eq:exact_consistency_update}
\end{equation}
conditioned using the input $\bm u_k$ that actually generated $Y_{k+1}$.
Under a valid contract and initial containment,
\eqref{eq:exact_consistency_update} retains the true successor.

The numerical controller maintains a finite retained ensemble
$\widehat{\mathcal I}_k=\{\bm\chi_k^s\}_{s=1}^{S_k}$ with fixed
inference equations (no online observer-gain optimization or learned
memory update). Each member is propagated through every sensor period
using the same fine RK4 resolution as nonlinear validation (without
applying the absorbing rest reset before the control boundary) and
survives only if every received residual satisfies its declared sensor
bound. No surviving member is replaced by an independently fitted point,
and parameters are not redrawn. An empty ensemble signals an
information-contract failure rather than physical infeasibility, and a
finite ensemble does not enclose states lying between its members.

For a tracked target with perception range $R_{\rm enc}$, define
$d_k^-=\min_s\normtwo{\bm p_{C,k}^s-\bm p_{E,k}^s}$. Once entry has been
recorded (by a return, $d_k^-\le R_{\rm enc}$, or explicit initial
supply), the target contributes collision and terminal separation
constraints at step $k$ if and only if $d_k^-\le R_{\rm enc}$. When
$d_k^->R_{\rm enc}$ and exit is confirmed, the target-free plan must
still satisfy the physical-domain and actuator constraints. The posterior
continues propagating the target. Re-entry restores the encounter
constraints before a new command is issued. Planning selects one representative member
\begin{equation}
    s_0\in\arg\min_s
        \normtwo{\bm x_k^s-\frac1{S_k}\sum_{a=1}^{S_k}\bm x_k^a}^2
    \label{eq:representative_member}
\end{equation}
to generate the single geometric initialization trajectory and reported
tracking cost. Every retained member is propagated under the same
proposed input sequence inside both the nonlinear oracle and fine
acceptance validation; future open-loop commands are reoptimized as new
measurements arrive.

\subsubsection{Preview Horizon, Control Knots, and Dual Geometry}
\label{subsec:geometry}

With speed bound $v_k^+=\max_s\normtwo{\bm v_k^{E,s}}$, the requested
preview time and hold count are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    T_k^{\rm req}=\max\bigl\{T_{\min},\,2h,\,v_k^+/a_p+T_s,\,
        T_{\rm space}\bigr\},\\
    N_k=\max\left\{N_{k-1}-1,\left\lceil
        \frac{\Delta T\lceil T_k^{\rm req}/\Delta T\rceil}{h}
        \right\rceil\right\},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:preview_rule}
\end{equation}
where $a_p>0$ is a preview scale (not a certified braking deceleration)
and, for an active target with $v_*>0$,
$T_{\rm space}=2(a_E+\max_s a_C^s)/\max(v_k^+,v_*)$ with footprint radii
$a=\sqrt{\ell^2+w^2}+\normtwo{\bm o}$ sizes the passing lookahead
without replacing rectangle geometry ($T_{\rm space}=0$ otherwise). At
initialization the $N_{k-1}-1$ term is zero; on continuation the preview
may shrink by one hold per step as $T_k^{\rm req}$ decreases, and an
accepted plan always spans $N_k$ holds.

The $N_k$ holds are partitioned into $M\le N_k$ control intervals with
integer boundaries $0=c_0<c_1<\cdots<c_M=N_k$ and lengths
$b_i=c_i-c_{i-1}$, where the first intervals have $b_i=1$ (in particular
$b_1=1$) and the remaining holds are distributed evenly. Independent
decision variables are endpoint commands $\bm v_i\in\R^2$,
$i=1,\ldots,M$, with $\bm v_0=\bm u^-$. Held commands inside interval
$i$ are linearly interpolated,
\begin{equation}
    \bm u_{c_{i-1}+j}=\bm v_{i-1}+\frac{j}{b_i}(\bm v_i-\bm v_{i-1}),
    \qquad j=1,\ldots,b_i,
    \label{eq:knot_interpolation}
\end{equation}
and each $\bm u_j$ is held constant over $h$, so $\bm v_1$ is the next
applied command. Convex bounds on $\bm v_i$ imply the same bounds on all
interpolated commands, and any explicitly specified finite slew bound
$|v_{i,\delta}-v_{i-1,\delta}|\le b_ih\dot\delta_{\max}$ (and
analogously for $u_L$) bounds every physical hold increment; omitted
bounds are not imposed. Physical states are condensed by forward
substitution $\bm x_0^s=\bm x_k^s$,
$\bm x_{j+1}^s=\widetilde F_h(\bm x_j^s,\bm u_{j+1},\bm\vartheta^s)$.

Both vehicles are rectangles. Let
$\mathcal C(t)=\{\bm m:H_C(\bm m-\bm p_C(t))\leq\bm h_C\}$ be the
obstacle rectangle, with $H_C=[I;\,-I]R(\psi_C(t))^{\trans}$ and
$\bm h_C$ its directional extents about $\bm p_C$, and let
$\bm p_a(\bm x)=\bm p+R(\psi)\bm o_a$, $a=1,\ldots,4$, be the ego
vertices with body-frame offsets $\bm o_a$. The Euclidean distance
between the two rectangles has the dual representation
\cite{ZhangLinigerBorrelli2021}
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    d(\bm x,t)=\max_{\bm\lambda\in\Lambda}\ \min_a\
        g_a(\bm x;\bm\lambda),\\
    g_a=\bm\lambda^{\trans}\bigl[H_C(\bm p_a(\bm x)-\bm p_C)
        -\bm h_C\bigr],\\
    \Lambda=\{\bm\lambda\geq0:\normtwo{H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda}\leq1\}.
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:distance_dual}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:dualRows}
(i) For every $\bm\lambda\in\Lambda$ and every $\bm x$,
$d(\bm x,t)\geq\min_ag_a(\bm x;\bm\lambda)$.
(ii) If the rectangles are disjoint at $\bar{\bm x}$ and $\bm n$ is the
unit vector from a closest point of $\mathcal C$ to a closest point of
the ego rectangle, then
$\bm\lambda^\star=[\max(\bm w,0);\,\max(-\bm w,0)]$ with
$\bm w=R(\psi_C)^{\trans}\bm n$ satisfies
$H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda^\star=\bm n$ and
$\min_ag_a(\bar{\bm x};\bm\lambda^\star)=d(\bar{\bm x},t)$.
(iii) If a point of the ego rectangle lies in the interior of
$\mathcal C$, the maximum in \eqref{eq:distance_dual} is zero and
$\bm\lambda=0$ is its only maximizer.
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
(i) For $\bm m$ in the ego rectangle and $\bm m'\in\mathcal C$,
$\normtwo{\bm m-\bm m'}
\geq(H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda)^{\trans}(\bm m-\bm m')
\geq\bm\lambda^{\trans}[H_C(\bm m-\bm p_C)-\bm h_C]$, because
$\normtwo{H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda}\leq1$, $\bm\lambda\geq0$ and
$H_C(\bm m'-\bm p_C)\leq\bm h_C$. The right side is affine in $\bm m$,
so its minimum over the ego rectangle is attained at a vertex.
(ii) The direction $\bm n$ is the same for every closest pair.
$H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda^\star
=R(\psi_C)[\max(\bm w,0)-\max(-\bm w,0)]=\bm n$, and
$\bm h_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda^\star$ is the support of
$\mathcal C-\bm p_C$ in direction $\bm n$, attained at the closest point
$\bm m_C$. The line through the closest ego point with normal $\bm n$
supports the ego rectangle, hence
$\min_a\bm n^{\trans}(\bm p_a-\bm m_C)=d$. (iii) At such a point
$\bm m_0$ every component of $H_C(\bm m_0-\bm p_C)-\bm h_C$ is negative,
so $\min_ag_a\leq\bm\lambda^{\trans}[H_C(\bm m_0-\bm p_C)-\bm h_C]<0$
unless $\bm\lambda=0$.
\end{IEEEproof}

For the predictive deficit, we use the signed unit-normal extension
\begin{equation}
    d_\pm(\bm x,\bm q)=\max_{\bm\lambda\ge0,\;\normtwo{H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda}=1}
        \min_a g_a(\bm x;\bm\lambda),
    \label{eq:signed_distance_dual}
\end{equation}
which equals Euclidean distance for separated rectangles and is strictly
negative in penetration, preserving a separating gradient even at
centered overlap. The inner dual maximization is eliminated
algebraically and exactly: closest vertex--edge pairs give its value
outside collision (optimizing $\bm n=H_C^{\trans}\bm\lambda$ continuously
on the unit circle), while Minkowski-polygon facet normals give its exact
signed depth inside collision. Positive separation is never replaced by a
fixed four-axis clearance bound, bounding circles, smooth-max surrogate,
or extra fixed planning buffer; the resulting value is piecewise smooth
at feature changes.

\subsubsection{Terminal Continuation to Sensing-Range Exit}
\label{subsec:terminal}

The terminal set $Z_f$ covers the current encounter. The first
control node at which the two reference points are strictly farther
apart than $R_{\rm enc}$ completes the encounter. Equality is in range.
A predicted exit completes a terminal witness. Online release requires
the observation update to confirm exit for the surviving members.
Nominal control then governs path and speed recovery.

At an endpoint with the target still in range, all members share the
terminal command
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \bm u_f=[0,\,u_{L,f}]^{\trans},\\
    u_{L,f}=\operatorname{clip}_{[u_{L,\min},u_{L,\max}]}
        \frac{F_R(v_*)}{\mu_f F_{zf}+\mu_r F_{zr}} .
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:terminal_command}
\end{equation}
Membership requires this input to be reachable from the preceding
command. Apply it in the nonlinear vehicle model for up to
$B=\max(1,\lceil T_{\rm preview,\min}/h\rceil)$ holds, checking model
admissibility and the signed rectangle distance
\eqref{eq:signed_distance_dual} at the control nodes through the first
exit. An endpoint already outside range needs zero continuation holds.

For a target still in range after these holds, write $\bm p_0,\psi_0,v$
for the ego handoff position, heading, and positive longitudinal speed.
Under the same command the simplified continuation uses
\begin{equation}
    \bm p_{E,j}=\bm p_0+jhv
        [\cos\psi_0,\sin\psi_0]^{\trans},\quad \psi_{E,j}=\psi_0 .
\end{equation}
The target follows \eqref{eq:target_flow}, including its absorbing stop.
Set $J=\min\{j\ge0:\normtwo{\bm p_{C,j}-\bm p_{E,j}}>R_{\rm enc}\}$,
with $J=\infty$ when the set is empty. The terminal core requires finite
$J$ and, at each $j=0,\ldots,J$,
\begin{equation}
    d_\pm(\bm x_j,\bm q_j)>0,\qquad
    d_\pm(\bm x_j,\bm q_j)\ge d_{\rm safe}.
    \label{eq:terminal_core}
\end{equation}
The dual compares the two footprints at the same node. The vehicles may
cross the same location at different times when their simultaneous
clearance satisfies \eqref{eq:terminal_core}.

\begin{assumption}[Physical handoff]
\label{asm:handoff}
From a core member with an active encounter, one hold of the full
Fiala flow under $\bm u_f$ is admissible, maintains the required node
clearance and either reaches exit or preserves membership in the core.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Terminal closure for the current encounter]
\label{prop:invariance}
Under Assumption~\ref{asm:handoff} and the declared target flow,
applying $\bm u_f$ from an active member of $Z_f$ either completes
the encounter or gives another member of $Z_f$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
In the simplified model a hold before exit consumes one checked node
and reduces $J$ by one. A nonlinear bridge ending at exit likewise
retains its checked suffix. For a bridge of $B$ holds reaching the core,
one hold leaves $B-1$ checked holds. Assumption~\ref{asm:handoff}
supplies the additional admissible hold at their far end. This gives a
bridge ending at exit or in the core as required.
\end{IEEEproof}
The full-model handoff is a design assumption. Each numerical candidate
is checked for the retained hypotheses. A new detection after actual
release requires fresh admission.

\subsubsection{Condensed PCBF Program and Zero-Deficit Tire-Loading Preference}
\label{subsec:program}

For the node-only collision contract, define the stage deficit over hold
$j\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$ by
\begin{equation}
    C_j(\bm v)=\max_{s=1,\ldots,S}\max_{r\in\{j,j+1\}}
        \bigl\{d_{\rm safe}-d_\pm(\bm x_r^s,\bm q_r^s)\bigr\},
    \label{eq:stage_deficit}
\end{equation}
covering both control-boundary nodes of each hold (their union covers the
root and terminal nodes, with no interior collision sample or motion
reserve). The primary condensed PCBF problem over $2M+N$ decision
variables is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \min_{\bm v,\bm\xi\ge0}\quad&\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\xi_j\\
    \text{s.t.}\quad&C_j(\bm v)\le\xi_j,\quad j=0,\ldots,N-1,\\
    &\bm v\in\mathcal U_N,\quad v_{x,j}^s\ge0,\quad
        (\bm x_N^s,\bm q_N^s,\bm u_N)\in Z_f\;\forall s,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:primary_program}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal U_N$ enforces physical command bounds and any configured
increment limits, terminal membership is hard with no safety slack, and
the forward-motion and Fiala front-wheel domain conditions are checked
across the sampled trajectory. A driving sequence must also satisfy
$\normtwo{[v_x^s,v_y^s]^{\trans}}>0$ after the initial node at sampled
states of each executable hold (including post-reset endpoints). Letting
$\tilde v_{x,j}^s$ be the pre-reset longitudinal speed at boundary $j$,
restoration imposes $\min\{0.2\,v_{\rm h}-\tilde v_{x,j}^s,-u_{L,j}\}\le0$
to exclude the interior of the stop-reset branch.

Reference tracking along the fixed cruise path and speed is measured by
\begin{equation}
    J=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{M}hb_i\bm e_i^{\trans}W\bm e_i
      +\frac{w_u}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{\normtwo{\bm v_i-\bm v_{i-1}}^2}{b_i},
    \label{eq:tracking_cost}
\end{equation}
evaluated on the representative member $s_0$ with defaults
$W=\diag(0.02,0.2,2,0.1,0.1)$ and $w_u=0.1$; the second term equals the
sum of squared command increments over all interpolated holds. This cost
permits passing excursions while retaining the fixed cruise reference and
makes no general convergence or liveness claim.

A feasible zero-deficit driving sequence attains the lower bound zero of
\eqref{eq:primary_program}. To guide selection on this zero-deficit level
without altering the primary safety objective or terminal membership,
define at the end of hold $j$ for each retained hypothesis $s$ the
dimensionless rear-slip residual
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    a_{j+1}^s=\frac{C_r^s}{\mu_r^sF_{zr}}\,
        \operatorname{atan2}\Bigl(&
            v_{y,j+1}^s-l_{r,E}\omega_{E,j+1}^s,\\
            &\sqrt{(v_{x,j+1}^s)^2+v_{\rm floor}^2}\Bigr),
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:rear_slip_residual}
\end{equation}
where $v_{\rm floor}$ is the scheduling speed floor regularizing slip
near rest and $\mu_r^sF_{zr}/C_r^s$ sets the angular scale from member
$s$'s rear tire parameters. Define the secondary tire-loading and rear-slip
cost over the $N$ held inputs $U=(\bm u_0,\ldots,\bm u_{N-1})$ by
\begin{equation}
    J_{\rm tire}(U)=\frac h2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
        \left[u_{L,j}^2+u_{L,j}^4
            +\frac1S\sum_{s=1}^S (a_{j+1}^s)^2\right].
    \label{eq:secondary_program}
\end{equation}
Here $u_{L,j}^2$ measures squared longitudinal force utilization; since
$Q_r^2/(\mu_rF_{zr})^2=1-u_{L,j}^2$, the quartic term emphasizes the loss
of lateral tire capacity under large traction or braking. All terms are
even under lateral reflection and act only within the available actuator
range. Between two validated zero-deficit plans, a proposed replacement
is accepted only if it strictly reduces $J_{\rm tire}$ and satisfies
$J_{\rm new}\le J_{\rm incumbent}$ up to numerical tolerance. This rule
preserves a feasible incumbent when tire-loading preference and reference
tracking favor different plans; it provides a local preference and makes
no claim of global secondary optimality or yaw stability.

When no retained plan is immediately accepted, one planner-generated
geometric guide trajectory on representative member $s_0$ supplies the
initial actuator sequence. At each integration step, its tangential
acceleration is proportional to the sum of transport and separation
gradients of the predicted encounter, so lateral contributions cancel in
a reflection-symmetric straight head-on encounter without imposing a
fixed passing direction; when target-free, trim-corrected route guidance
reproduces an admissible steady turn $\bm u_*$ at trim. Steering
increments are scaled in units of the bicycle's friction-limited turn
angle, and when needed the initial knot vector is projected onto the
linear actuator and terminal handoff rows. From this starting point, a
damped Gauss--Newton step for $J_{\rm tire}$ using residuals
$(u_{L,j},u_{L,j}^2,a_{j+1}^s/\sqrt S)$ under the same interpolation and
physical bounds can advance the iterate when nonlinear replay strictly
reduces $J_{\rm tire}$ without increasing total positive collision
deficit or maximum positive model-domain and terminal violation beyond
numerical tolerance. Condensed SQP then minimizes the summed stage
deficits in \eqref{eq:primary_program} under the hard domain, motion, and
terminal constraints. Its first validated zero-deficit trial supplies an
incumbent. Within the same call budget, one further damped Gauss--Newton
proposal on the linearized zero-deficit level (imposing linearized stage,
forward-motion, and terminal constraints) may replace that incumbent
after full nonlinear validation and the two cost comparisons above.

\subsubsection{Node Acceptance, Plan Retention, and Shift Feasibility}
\label{subsec:recursive}

Before any candidate sequence can supply a command, its $N$ interpolated
held inputs are replayed on every retained hypothesis using the fine RK4
flow ($K_v=8$ substeps per hold) and checked for physical input bounds,
configured increment bounds, model-domain admissibility, and exact
node-only dual separation $d_\pm(\bm x_j^s,\bm q_j^s)\ge d_{\rm safe}$
for $j=0,\ldots,N$ and along the terminal bridge through its last
in-range or first out-of-range node. There is no intersample clearance requirement or collision
reserve; a trajectory may satisfy every node inequality while
intersecting between nodes, and the algorithm makes no safety claim at
intermediate times. The complete accepted fine held-input sequence is
stored.

After applying $\bm u_{0|k}$, set $r=N_{k+1}-(N_k-1)\ge0$ and form the
shifted executable candidate
\begin{equation}
    U_{k+1}^{\rm shift}=
        \bigl(\bm u_{1|k},\ldots,\bm u_{N_k-1|k},
        \bm w_1,\ldots,\bm w_r\bigr).
    \label{eq:shifted_plan}
\end{equation}
Starting from the old last command, each $\bm w_j$ moves toward $\bm u_f$
within the actuator increment bounds. Active terminal entry makes
$\bm u_f$ reachable in one hold. After exit the extension uses the
admissible transition toward nominal control.
This fine held-input suffix remains directly representable even when it
no longer interpolates the new coarse control knots. An optional new
guide input spliced onto a feasible driving continuation is accepted only
after full validation. During an active encounter, when both the guide
splice and the shifted plan can be checked within the call budget, the
shifted plan replaces the guide splice if it strictly reduces
$J_{\rm tire}$ while satisfying $J_{\rm new}\le J_{\rm incumbent}$ up to
numerical tolerance; if only one plan passes validation, that plan
supplies the next command.

\begin{proposition}[Shift feasibility on the admitted information domain]
\label{prop:recursive}
Suppose an initial plan is feasible, the posterior contains the true
member and is contained in the predicted successors, the physical flow
and timing agree, requested horizons fit the numerical allocation,
Assumption~\ref{asm:handoff} holds, the target-free extension is
admissible after exit, and no new encounter requires admission. Then the shifted plan
\eqref{eq:shifted_plan} is feasible at step $k+1$ and satisfies
\begin{equation}
    V_{N_{k+1}}(\mathcal I_{k+1})\le V_{N_k}(\mathcal I_k)-\xi_{0|k}^\star
    \label{eq:compact_descent}
\end{equation}
on a shift-closed policy domain; in particular, a validated zero-deficit
plan supplies a zero-deficit successor even if the optimizer returns no
improvement.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Conditioning can discard physical members, and every surviving member
inherits its checked trajectory suffix. Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}
supplies each appended hold while an encounter is active. The assumed
admissible extension covers the remaining holds after exit. Induction
gives a common executable extension of $r$ holds. The shifted plan
inherits the remaining stage deficits
$(\xi_{1|k}^\star,\ldots,\xi_{N_k-1|k}^\star)$ followed by $r$ zeros,
yielding \eqref{eq:compact_descent} and preserving zero deficit.
\end{IEEEproof}

Releasing target constraints upon range exit enlarges the feasible set,
whereas re-entry increases collision obligations and requires a separate
initial-admission check. Proposition~\ref{prop:recursive} gives continued
admissible motion under its stated assumptions; it supplies neither a
uniform positive minimum speed nor a proof of lane return, and numerical
rechecks over a finite ensemble do not constitute a robust physical-model
or timing certificate.

\subsubsection{Joint Nonlinear Observer--Feedback Safety}
\label{subsec:robust}

The theoretical predictor carries both physical vehicles, both observers
and future measurements under causal control rules. It starts from a
valid current enclosure; it does not presume future observer convergence.
The horizon is fixed during an encounter. Collision statements below
concern the control decision instants. Road constraints are omitted, and
a compatible terminal family is an existence assumption.

\paragraph{Physical and information state}
Let sensor times be $t_j=jh_s$ and control times $t_{kL}$, with
$T_s=Lh_s$, $h_s>0$ and $L\in\mathbb N_{>0}$. The input is held for
$T_s$. Use the nonlinear Fiala vehicle and the constant-acceleration,
constant-sideslip target, including its declared stopping continuation.
There is no additional residual force in this construction; the
uncertain tire parameters remain constant along each physical branch.
Additional disturbances require a common extension of the physical,
measurement, observer and terminal assumptions.

Retain the augmented state
\begin{equation}
 \bm z=(\bm x_E,\bm x_C^{\rm phys},\bm\vartheta_E,\bm\zeta_E,
       \hat{\bm x}_T,\hat{\bm y},\bm m,\bm u^-,\mathcal H).
 \label{eq:joint_augmented_state}
\end{equation}
Here $\bm x_C^{\rm phys}$ includes the target parameters and mode;
$\bm\zeta_E$ includes all ego-estimator states;
$\hat{\bm x}_T=(\hat{\bm r},\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})$;
$\hat{\bm y}$ is the radar predictor; $\bm m$ contains held readings;
and $\mathcal H$ is the absolute observable history, clock and policy
memory. A decision state is posterior to its boundary measurements.
Observer states and the current history are known, while the physical
state and parameters satisfy a joint enclosure
\begin{equation}
 \bm z_k\in\mathcal Q_k,\qquad \mathcal I_0=\mathcal Q_0.
 \label{eq:current_containment}
\end{equation}
The present enclosure must be valid for the sampled sensing contract.
The continuous-measurement ISS result alone does not establish that
premise. Correlations in the initial set may be retained; a product of
sound marginal sets is permissible but more conservative.

Require every admitted physical and observer flow to exist uniquely
over each sensor interval. On a moving chart, sufficient conditions
include forward speed bounded away from zero, slip angles away from
their tangent singularities, positive compact tire parameters and
locally Lipschitz resistance. Fiala saturation is retained. A low-speed
extension requires its own well-posed dynamics and acceleration
measurement. No branch may be discarded because its flow is undefined.

A policy has the form $\bm u_i=\pi_i(\mathcal H_i)$. Indistinguishable
histories receive the same command; the current policy therefore gives
one common first input. Distinct future observations may yield distinct
commands. Unknown physical parameters cannot be arguments of the policy
except through observable information.

\paragraph{Exact sensor-period transition}
On interval $j$, hold the gyro sample $\omega_j=u_{3,j}$ and write
$a_\ell=l_\ell\omega^\ell$ for the effective gains in
\eqref{eq:target_observer}. Its sampled predictor realization is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \dot{\hat{\bm r}}&=\hat{\bm q}-\hat{\bm v}^E-\omega_jJ\hat{\bm r}
                         +a_1(\hat{\bm y}-\hat{\bm r}),\\
 \dot{\hat{\bm q}}&=\hat{\bm s}-\omega_jJ\hat{\bm q}
                         +a_2(\hat{\bm y}-\hat{\bm r}),\\
 \dot{\hat{\bm s}}&=\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})
                   -\omega_jJ\hat{\bm s}+a_3(\hat{\bm y}-\hat{\bm r}),\\
 \dot{\hat{\bm y}}&=\hat{\bm q}-\hat{\bm v}^E-\omega_jJ\hat{\bm y},
 \qquad \hat{\bm y}(t_j^+)=\bm y_{R,j}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:joint_target_predictor}
\end{equation}
The target estimate is continuous at a radar reset. Denote the
ego-estimator reset, intersample flow and outputs by $P_E,f_O,H_E$,
respectively; they retain the position-only velocity-filter and yaw
memory of Section~\ref{sec:estimator}.

At a posterior control boundary, select $\bm u=\pi(\mathcal H)$.
Flow both vehicles and both observers for $h_s$, holding $\bm u$ and
the sensor record. Generate the endpoint readings from those physical
states and one joint noise vector. The IMU uses the input already
acting immediately before that endpoint. Apply $P_E$, reset the radar
predictor on detection, and append every reading and detection flag to
$\mathcal H$. Repeat this flow/reset operation $L$ times with the same
input. Thus, with $\mathcal F^{\bm u}$ the sensor-period flow and
$\mathcal P^{\bm u}_{\nu_j,d_j}$ its endpoint measurement/reset map,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \bm z^+&=\mathcal G_{h}(\bm z,\bm\nu;\pi)\\
 &=\bigl(\mathcal P^{\bm u}_{\nu_L,d_L}\circ\mathcal F^{\bm u}\bigr)
   \circ\cdots\circ
   \bigl(\mathcal P^{\bm u}_{\nu_1,d_1}\circ\mathcal F^{\bm u}\bigr)(\bm z),
 \quad h=T_s .
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:joint_sampling}
\end{equation}
All flows here are exact mathematical flows. Each boundary measurement
is used once. A noise realization is shared by every observer using
that reading; each persistent parameter affects the vehicle and its
resulting measurements on the same branch. Past estimation error
initializes $\mathcal I_k$ and is not reinjected as a fresh disturbance.

\paragraph{Coupled nonlinear errors}
Use estimate-minus-truth errors
$\bm e_r,\bm e_q,\bm e_s$,
$\bm e_y=\hat{\bm y}-\bm r$ and
$\bm e_v=\hat{\bm v}^E-\bm v^E$.
Put $w=\omega_j-\omega_E(t)$ and
$\bm\lambda=\bm e_y-\bm e_r$. Subtracting the physical chain gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \dot{\bm e}_r&=\bm e_q-\bm e_v-\omega_jJ\bm e_r
                          -wJ\bm r+a_1\bm\lambda,\\
 \dot{\bm e}_q&=\bm e_s-\omega_jJ\bm e_q-wJ\bm q+a_2\bm\lambda,\\
 \dot{\bm e}_s&=\Phi_e(\hat{\bm q},\hat{\bm s})-\bm j_{\rm phys}
                          -\omega_jJ\bm e_s-wJ\bm s+a_3\bm\lambda,\\
 \dot{\bm e}_y&=\bm e_q-\bm e_v-\omega_jJ\bm e_y-wJ\bm r .
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:joint_target_errors}
\end{equation}
Here $\bm j_{\rm phys}=\Phi(\bm q,\bm s)$ during positive-speed
target motion. The common ego-velocity and gyro errors cancel in the
predictor innovation. Following detection, for $0\le\tau<h_s$,
\begin{equation}
 \bm\lambda(t_j+\tau)=e^{-a_1\tau}R(-\omega_j\tau)
           [\bm n_{R,j}-\bm e_r(t_j)].
 \label{eq:joint_innovation_decay}
\end{equation}
Indeed $\dot{\bm\lambda}=-(a_1I+\omega_jJ)\bm\lambda$ and
$\bm e_y(t_j^+)=\bm n_{R,j}$. This identity does not assert
contraction of the full estimation error. The held-gyro error also
contains $\omega_E(t_j)-\omega_E(t)$. At a target stopping event,
$\bm s^+=0$, so $\bm e_s^+=\bm e_s^-+\bm s^-$.
The extension discrepancy
$\Phi_e(\bm q,\bm s)-\bm j_{\rm phys}$ must be retained outside
\eqref{eq:target_domain}; it is not another independent disturbance.

For the ego output error $\bm e_E=H_E(\bm\zeta_E)-W(\bm x_E)$,
$W=(\bm p_E,\psi_E,\bm v^E)$, its exact reset is
$H_E(P_E(\bm\zeta_E^-,\bm y,\bm u^-))-W(\bm x_E)$.
Its flow increment is the difference of the two exact output
increments. Thus $\bm e_v$ in \eqref{eq:joint_target_errors} comes
from the same joint physical and measurement realization.

For a dynamically consistent comparison branch, denote differences by
$\Delta$. The relative dynamics satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta\dot{\bm r}
   &=\Delta\bm q-\Delta\bm v^E-\omega_EJ\Delta\bm r
                                      -\Delta\omega_EJ\bar{\bm r},\\
 \Delta\dot\psi_R&=\Delta(\kappa_CV_C)-\Delta\omega_E .
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:joint_relative_errors}
\end{equation}
The vehicle state difference follows the difference of its two
nonlinear force laws. For $\bm u=K_i(c)$, where $c$ contains available
estimates and memory, the input difference is $K_i(c)-K_i(\bar c)$.
In particular,
$\Delta\hat{\bm r}=\Delta\bm r+\Delta\bm e_r$.
Equations \eqref{eq:joint_target_errors}--\eqref{eq:joint_relative_errors}
therefore retain how a future target observation changes ego motion.

\paragraph{Reachable sets and whole-block conditioning}
For a fixed causal rule define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T_\pi(S)=
 \{\mathcal G_h(\bm z,\bm\nu;\pi):
               \bm z\in S,\ \bm\nu\in{\rm Adm}(\bm z)\}.
 \label{eq:joint_nonlinear_enclosure}
\end{equation}
The admissible block contains the declared sensor modes, bounded noises
and any temporal noise memory. Set $\mathcal R_0=\mathcal I_k$ and
$\mathcal R_{i+1}=\mathcal T_{\pi_i}(\mathcal R_i)$.
These exact sets retain their observable-history tags. A fibre
$\mathcal R_i^\eta$ contains the tuples with one history $\eta$ and
receives one common command. A union over fibres is the unconditional
prediction.

For a relative-error projection $D_i$, the exact error set is
$D_i(\mathcal R_i)$. In particular,
\begin{equation}
 B_{i+1,a}=\sup_{\substack{\bm z\in\mathcal R_i\\
                          \bm\nu\in{\rm Adm}(\bm z)}}
 \bigl\|D_{i+1,a}(\mathcal G_h(\bm z,\bm\nu;\pi_i))\bigr\|.
 \label{eq:joint_error_bound}
\end{equation}
A bounded finite-horizon operating region makes these bounds finite,
not necessarily small. A fibre with radar reading $\bm y_R$
has relative-position diameter at most $2\bar n_R$, while different
fibres may have different centers. This conditional estimation bound
does not replace physical target dispersion by a smaller fictitious set.

A mathematical feedback criterion is to group states by an observable
statistic $c$ into $S_c$. Let $L_c(\bm u)$ be the worst next
relative-position error in its exact successor under held $\bm u$,
and define
\begin{equation}
 U_c(B)=\{\bm u\in\mathcal U:
       H(S_c,\bm u),\ L_c(\bm u)\le B\}.
 \label{eq:joint_feedback_selector}
\end{equation}
Here $H$ requires well-defined flows and hard admissibility on all
branches. An admissible selector $K(c)\in U_c(B)$ certifies that
bound. If such selectors exist but $\bigcap_cU_c(B)=\varnothing$,
feedback meets a bound no common command can meet. Nonemptiness is
a substantive authority/information condition, not a consequence of
bounded sensor noise.

After applying the first common command, let $\mathcal A_{k+1}$ select
tuples with the \emph{entire} received sensor block, including
intermediate readings, detection flags, known observer states and input
history. Condition by
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_{k+1}=\mathcal R_{1|k}\cap
                  \mathcal A_{k+1}\cap\mathcal Q_{k+1}.
 \label{eq:information_set}
\end{equation}
The last intersection is optional when no additional sound observer
enclosure is available.

\begin{lemma}[Joint containment and conditioning]
\label{lem:nested}
Under initial containment and unchanged physical and observation
contracts, exact joint prediction and \eqref{eq:information_set} give
\begin{equation}
 \bm z_{k+1}\in\mathcal I_{k+1}\subseteq\mathcal R_{1|k}.
 \label{eq:nesting}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Induct over the $L$ physical flows and sensor resets. The true
parameters, states and noise generate an allowed successor at each
step, and the applied first command agrees across the current history
fibre. Its endpoint tuple bears the entire actual sensor block and
belongs to any valid additional enclosure. Conditioning retains that
tuple and only removes members of the predicted successor.
\end{IEEEproof}
An empty posterior signals a violated premise. A new target or a
changed uncertainty contract requires fresh initial feasibility.
Guaranteed detection within $R_{\rm sen}$ makes a miss an exit
certificate at its sample; other exit rules must use the same
observable history and be preserved under restriction. Nominal exit
alone cannot end obligations for active branches.

The center innovation remains a difference on the same physical
history. With $\bm\epsilon_k=\bm x_k-\hat{\bm x}_k$ and no extra defect,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \bm\delta_k={}&-\bm\epsilon_{k+1}
  +F_{\bm\vartheta}(\hat{\bm x}_k+\bm\epsilon_k,\bm u_k)\\
 &-F_{\bm\vartheta_0}(\hat{\bm x}_k,\bm u_k).
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:innovation}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Exact geometry and terminal closure}
Let $\sigma_{\mathcal B}$ be the support of a body-fixed rectangular
footprint, including its reference-point offset, and
$\bm d_p=\bm p_E-\bm p_C$. For $\|\bm n\|=1$ the exact gap is
\begin{equation}
 g_{\bm n}(\bm z)=\bm n^\trans\bm d_p
 -\sigma_{\mathcal B_E}(-R_E^\trans\bm n)
 -\sigma_{\mathcal B_C}(R_C^\trans\bm n).
 \label{eq:directional_margin}
\end{equation}
With $\bm b_n=R_E^\trans\bm n$ this equals
$-\bm b_n^\trans\bm r-\sigma_{\mathcal B_E}(-\bm b_n)
-\sigma_{\mathcal B_C}(R(-\psi_R)\bm b_n)$.
Take the infimum over this joint relative-pose image, retaining the
dependence of $\bm b_n$ on true ego heading. Define
\begin{equation}
 C(S;\bm n)=\max\{0,-\inf_{\bm z\in S_{\rm active}}g_{\bm n}(\bm z)\}.
 \label{eq:robust_row}
\end{equation}
For no active tuples set $C=0$. A common stage normal is a sufficient
robust certificate, optimized continuously on the unit circle. It
remains valid on subsets. Zero gap excludes interior overlap; contact
at equality is allowed. Strict clearance requires an explicit positive
threshold. No intersample clearance conclusion is made here.

Assume a nonempty downward-closed terminal family $\mathcal B$ of
history-tagged sets, including unions of histories. Every member has
an admissible current zero-slack certificate. For each $S\in\mathcal B$
there is an admissible causal rule $\pi_f$ such that
\begin{equation}
 H(S,\pi_f),\quad C(S;\bm n_f)=0,\quad
                   \mathcal T_{\pi_f}(S)\in\mathcal B.
 \label{eq:terminal_information}
\end{equation}
Its successor has a zero-slack witness by membership. The terminal
rule may depend on observable history, but not on an unknown true
state. This is an existence assumption under the same dynamics and
uncertainty contract; no particular braking construction is required.
The candidate of Section~\ref{subsec:terminal} is separate from this
premise.

\paragraph{Nonlinear PCBF and recursive feasibility}
Fix $1\le N\le M$ for the encounter. Optimize causal rules
$\pi_{0:M-1}$, their joint sets, unit normals $\bm n_{0:M}$ and
nonnegative common slacks:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R_0&=\mathcal I_k,&
 \mathcal T_{\pi_i}(\mathcal R_i)&\subseteq\mathcal R_{i+1},\\
 H(\mathcal R_i,\pi_i),&&C(\mathcal R_i;\bm n_i)&\le\xi_i,\\
 \xi_i&=0\quad(i\ge N),&
                  \mathcal R_M&\in\mathcal B .
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:robust_image_certificate}
\end{equation}
The image and hard conditions apply for $0\le i<M$, and the collision
condition for $0\le i\le M$. $N=M$ softens every decision stage;
$N<M$ retains a hard tail. The endpoint has zero deficit by the
terminal assumption. Define
\begin{equation}
 V_{\rm P}^\star(\mathcal I_k)=\min\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\xi_{i|k}.
 \label{eq:joint_primary}
\end{equation}
Select an attained primary minimizer and apply its first common
command. No performance objective is required.

The policy class must admit restriction to surviving absolute histories
and concatenation with the terminal rule. Retain any prefix, observer
and reference memory needed to evaluate the old tail. Recentered
coordinates require an invertible relabeling and transport of policies
and certificates. Assume attainment for each encountered feasible
problem; a compact nonempty feasible sublevel, closed feasibility and
lower-semicontinuous cost are sufficient. Arbitrary history-dependent
policies alone do not establish those properties.

\begin{theorem}[Joint recursive feasibility and zero-deficit invariance]
\label{thm:robust}
Assume initial feasibility, the joint-flow and containment premises,
whole-block conditioning \eqref{eq:information_set}, terminal closure
\eqref{eq:terminal_information}, and policy/certificate closure under
restriction and terminal appending. Applying the first command of an
attained primary minimizer gives
\begin{equation}
 0\le V_{\rm P}^\star(\mathcal I_{k+1})
 \le V_{\rm P}^\star(\mathcal I_k)-\xi_{0|k}^\star
 \le V_{\rm P}^\star(\mathcal I_k).
 \label{eq:information_descent}
\end{equation}
The next problem is feasible while the encounter is active. An attained
zero slack sum remains zero, and the true vehicle interiors do not
overlap at the active decision instants.
\end{theorem}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:nested} gives
$S_0=\mathcal I_{k+1}\subseteq\mathcal R_{1|k}^\star$.
Restrict $\pi_{1|k}^\star,\ldots,\pi_{M-1|k}^\star$ to the surviving
histories. For a fixed rule the exact successor is inclusion-monotone:
each realization from a subset is still admitted from the superset.
Induction therefore gives
\[
 S_j\subseteq\mathcal R_{j+1|k}^\star,\qquad 0\le j<M .
\]
Hard constraints, old normals and slacks remain valid on these sets.
Downward closure implies $S_{M-1}\in\mathcal B$. Append its causal
terminal rule; the successor $S_M\in\mathcal B$ has a zero-slack
witness. For $M=1$ the argument starts directly from
$S_0\subseteq\mathcal R_{1|k}^\star\in\mathcal B$.

The shifted prefix is $(\xi_{1|k}^\star,\ldots,\xi_{N-1|k}^\star,0)$.
Its final zero is supplied by the old hard tail or the terminal
extension. Thus the next feasible cost equals the old cost minus
$\xi_{0|k}^\star$, giving \eqref{eq:information_descent}.
Nonnegativity preserves zero. The true current tuple remains in the
root set, so its zero-slack certificate separates the footprint
interiors. Induction closes the argument.
\end{IEEEproof}
This specializes the predictive-slack shift argument of
\cite{HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025} to the joint physical/observation
system. The slack sum, not every future slack component, is
nonincreasing. Positive slack is not a safety guarantee, and
nonincrease does not prove finite-time arrival at zero. No uniform
smallness or stepwise contraction of the uncertainty sets is required.

Sound outer sets also suffice if the retained sets can enclose the
restricted images with the same certificates and terminal closure.
A compulsory looser replacement need not do so. A feasible-plan variant
stores $J_k$ and accepts the shifted plan or a feasible replacement with
$J_{k+1}\le J_k-\xi_{0|k}$; this certifies the stored cost rather than
the optimal value.

A validated secondary preference among zero-deficit plans (such as
\eqref{eq:secondary_program}) leaves the attained primary optimum
unchanged and is not a premise of Theorem~\ref{thm:robust}. The compact
nonlinear implementation and its derivations are maintained in
\path{controller/CONTROLLER_THEORY.md}.

\subsubsection{Scope of the Guarantees}
\label{subsec:scope}

The exact information update \eqref{eq:exact_consistency_update} has the
containment property under its stated contract, the feasible distance
dual \eqref{eq:signed_distance_dual} certifies geometric separation at
individual control-boundary nodes, and knot interpolation
\eqref{eq:knot_interpolation} enforces every configured input increment.
Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} concerns terminal closure through sensing-range exit
under Assumption~\ref{asm:handoff}, Proposition~\ref{prop:recursive}
gives shift feasibility on the admitted information domain, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:robust} proves recursive feasibility and zero-value
safety for the road-free information-set problem under its exact
joint-flow, whole-block conditioning, policy-closure and terminal
contracts.

These facts do not convert a finite retained ensemble, numerical RK4
integration, node-only geometry checks, finite preview, or local SQP
into a universal continuous-time safety or completeness certificate. A
finite ensemble does not enclose states between its members, node-only
checks make no intersample collision claim, and
Assumption~\ref{asm:handoff} is an explicit handoff premise rather than a
proved nonlinear Fiala theorem. Moreover, even individual physical
feasibility is weaker than output-feedback feasibility because
indistinguishable histories must share one command; re-entry after
confirmed range exit requires separate admission, and solver time limits
do not certify hard actuator deadlines or compensate unmodeled execution
latency.

\subsubsection{Computation}
\label{subsec:computation}

The compact nonlinear controller compiles its flow and causal sensitivity
graphs from configuration size hints and the preview rule
\eqref{eq:preview_rule}, keeping unused horizon slots as identities so
horizon updates within the allocation avoid rebuilding the graph (a
larger preview grows the allocation rather than truncating it). The
fine-RK4 measurement-consistency inference kernel is cached independently
of the optimization graph and reused across sensor blocks. Local SQP
sensitivities propagate $S_{j+1}=F_xS_j+F_uD_{\bm v}\bm u_j$ on the
coarser mesh exploiting causal sparsity, while fine-validation rollouts
cache and reuse identical suffixes of shifted plans. A start-up warm-up
primes the compiled kernels and straight-road trim before timed samples.
Mean solve time, full controller-call time, startup compilation, and
worst-case latency remain distinct quantities.

\subsection{Estimator Accuracy Against a Tuned Reference}
\label{subsec:estimatorResults}

Six open-loop scenarios of 6 to 12\,s are used. In the straight
oncoming scenario the ego drives straight at 15\,m/s and the target
approaches from 95\,m at 15\,m/s. In the arc crossing scenario the ego
follows an arc of radius 100\,m and the target moves on a straight line
that meets the arc. In the arc following scenario the target is 30\,m
ahead on the same arc at 15.5\,m/s. The swerve-and-braking scenario
adds a 3.5\,m double lane change with braking to the straight oncoming
geometry, with the target at 12\,m/s. In the low-relative-speed
scenario a lead target is 30\,m ahead at 15.3\,m/s. In the weaving
scenario a weaving, accelerating ego meets a turning, accelerating
target. The target obeys the constant-acceleration, constant-sideslip
model in all of them, and the ego truth obeys the kinematic
single-track relation without mismatch. The swerve and weaving
scenarios violate the zero-jerk premise of the reference observer.
Synchronized measurements arrive at 50\,Hz with bounded uniform noise at
the declared maxima, and the maximum RK4 step is 0.005\,s.

The reference is a reproduction of the multistage observer of
\cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}, with companion-form ego and target chains
and a switched course--acceleration yaw stage. As in that paper, its ego
chain uses GNSS position and the gyroscope and does not use the GNSS
velocity that the estimator of Section~\ref{sec:estimator} measures. Its
gains are selected from 54 candidates (two gain shapes, three ego rates,
three target rates and three yaw gains) on training seeds 101--102, once
for every scenario, by minimizing the sum of the position, speed and
course errors scaled by 0.1\,m, 0.5\,m/s and $5^\circ$. In five of the
six scenarios the selected candidate lies on the boundary of this grid.

The estimator receives no error-based tuning; its gains are synthesized
anew from the operating domain declared for each scenario. Only the
low-relative-speed scenario uses the domain of
Section~\ref{subsec:designResults}. The arc following scenario declares
$\beta_{\max}=0.03$\,rad, which gives the target gains
$[10.21,51.59,116.4]$. The straight, arc crossing and swerve scenarios
declare $r_{\max}=100$\,m (the swerve scenario also
$\bar\omega_E=0.40$\,rad/s and $\beta_{\rm dom}=0.06$\,rad), and the
weaving scenario declares $r_{\max}=80$\,m. For $r_{\max}=100$\,m
and 80\,m, respectively, the decay floors are 0.4 and 0.5\,s$^{-1}$, the
scalar gains 1.198 and 1.498\,s$^{-1}$, and the target gains
$[6.24,19.31,27.20]$ and $[6.50,20.93,30.23]$.

Both observers run on identical truth, sensor draws and initial offsets.

in speed; the criterion is a predicted variance and uses no simulated
error, but the rerun is not a preregistered confirmation. The reference
is our reproduction with gains selected empirically on a bounded grid,
not the authors' implementation or sensor suite. No
enclosure-containment or outage statistics are reported for these
open-loop trials.

Table~\ref{tab:controllerParameters} lists the historical baseline
parameters. The vehicle
is a generic passenger car. In the closed-loop trials the estimator uses
the same vehicle parameters as the controller, not the rear-axle
distance of Section~\ref{subsec:designResults}. With these values the
historical offline CLF LMI synthesis certifies, for the cruise law, contraction
factors $\rho^\star$ of 0.9744 to 0.9748 per sample against the
requested $\rho_V=0.9753$, that is, time constants of $\sqrt V$ of 3.85
to 3.92\,s against $T_V=4$\,s, on sets $\Omega$ whose half-extents are
1.49\,m (8\,m/s, straight), 1.59\,m (8\,m/s, curved) and 2.0\,m
(15\,m/s) of lateral offset, 0.114 to 0.144\,rad of heading, 0.49 to
0.74\,m/s of speed, 0.19 to 0.27\,m/s of lateral velocity and
0.20\,rad/s of yaw rate; the samples that built the enclosures reach
front slip angles of 0.080\,rad. The independent check on fresh samples
found worst contractions of 0.941 to 0.963 and no violated input bound
or state row.

Seven encounter geometries are run at reference speeds of 8 and
15\,m/s. In the head-on case the obstacle starts 24\,m ahead on the path
and approaches at 8\,m/s; in the accelerating head-on case it also
accelerates at 1\,m/s$^2$. The braking lead starts 1.45\,m ahead of the
ego body at the ego speed and brakes at 0.5\,m/s$^2$ until it stops. The
crossing obstacle moves at 8\,m/s perpendicular to the path and reaches
the path when the cruising ego does. The turning crossing obstacle
reaches the same point at 10\,m/s and perpendicular to the path at that
instant, while accelerating at $A_C=1$\,m/s$^2$ and turning with
$\beta_C=0.05$\,rad, a turning radius of about 32\,m. The curved head-on
and curved crossing cases use a path of curvature $0.005$\,m$^{-1}$,
with an opposing obstacle on the same circle at 8\,m/s and an obstacle
that crosses at 10\,m/s with $A_C=0.5$\,m/s$^2$ and $\beta_C=0.04$\,rad.
In every case the bodies are initially separated, and the ego under
unmodified cruise would collide. The road has four lanes of 3.66\,m and
paved shoulders of 3.05\,m, and the path is the center of the second
lane from the right, which gives the clearances $c_{\rm R}$ and
$c_{\rm L}$ of Table~\ref{tab:controllerParameters}.

Each issued input is held for $T_s$ on the model $f$ itself, including
its low-speed regime, integrated by an adaptive Runge--Kutta method with
relative and absolute tolerances $10^{-11}$ and $10^{-12}$. The plant
therefore differs from the prediction only through the integration
scheme and the linearization, and it is not snapped to rest below
$v_{\rm h}/5$. There is no parameter mismatch or actuator dynamics, the
obstacle follows \eqref{eq:target_flow} exactly with fixed $A_C$ and
$\beta_C$, and the measured computation time is not applied as a delay.
The body distance is evaluated at 31 instants per hold.

Two observation modes are used. With \emph{exact states}, the
controller receives the true ego and obstacle states and no enclosure.
With \emph{estimated states}, it receives only the output of the
estimator of Section~\ref{sec:estimator}, which runs at 80\,Hz on
synthetic GNSS, accelerometer, gyroscope and relative-position samples
of the true motion with bounded uniform noise at the maxima of
Table~\ref{tab:controllerParameters}. The relative-position sensor has
a range of $R_{\rm enc}$. An obstacle is acquired after 20 consecutive
detections, whose least-squares line initializes its velocity, and its
estimate is passed to the controller only while it is detected. The
estimator starts from a 0.5\,s measurement history before the first
control sample. Its ego domain is derived from the controller's limits
(speed 1 to 18\,m/s, $\beta_{\rm dom}$, yaw rate and acceleration from
the state bounds and tire friction), and its target domain is the hull
of the scenario obstacles within sensor range, widened by 10\%; the
gains are synthesized from this domain by the programs of
Section~\ref{subsec:gainSelection} and differ from those of
Section~\ref{subsec:designResults}. The lateral-velocity measurement is
that of Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided}. The estimated-state trials are
repeated for six noise seeds.

A run fails if the bodies touch or if a sample returns no solved
command. It succeeds if, in addition, cruise is recovered: all five
transverse errors within (0.1\,m, $1^\circ$, 0.1\,m/s, 0.05\,m/s,
0.01\,rad/s) during a dwell of 1\,s that starts after the last instant
at which unmodified cruise would be in contact. Runs last at most 2000
holds (100\,s). On the circular path the opposing obstacle of the curved
head-on case meets the ego a second time within that period, so those
runs contain two encounters and confirm recovery after the second. The
campaign uses MATLAB R2026a on an AMD Ryzen 7 7800X3D and the
interior-point cone solver Clarabel through a compiled interface; before
each run the controller's warm-up action compiles every code path. Six
runs shared the machine. Below, a primary optimum is called zero when
it does not exceed $\epsilon_{\rm lex}$ plus the feasibility tolerance
$10^{-5}$, and a CLF slack when it does not exceed
$10^{-5}\max(V,1)$.

Table~\ref{tab:closedLoop} and Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop} summarize the
98 runs. With exact states all fourteen runs succeed: no contact, no
departure from the road, and cruise recovered between 8.45 and
14.70\,s in the single-encounter cases. With estimated states 74 of the
84 runs succeed. The other ten stop without a solved command, between
0.25 and 5.95\,s into the run (Section~\ref{subsec:failureResults}); no
run collides or leaves the road. Two seeds succeed in thirteen
encounters and four in twelve; seven of the ten failures are curved
head-ons.

With exact states the controller uses the node margin almost
completely. In six of the fourteen runs the smallest sampled distance
is below the historical 0.20\,m design clearance, down to 0.136\,m
(Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop}(d)): the margin is consumed between the
constrained instants and by the plant's departure from the sampled
model. The primary optimum is positive at one sample of one run, by
$1.2\cdot10^{-4}$\,m. The steering angle reaches 0.52\,rad, the yaw
rate 0.99\,rad/s and the sideslip 0.19\,rad, inside the cone of
0.25\,rad; the force ratio ranges from $-1.00$ to $0.97$, and the speed
stays above 4.9\,m/s, so the low-speed regime of the model is not
entered. The largest lateral deviation is 3.8 to 8.4\,m
(Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop}(e)): the maneuvers leave the lane and use the
adjacent lanes, and the body stays at least 2.67\,m from a road edge.
The horizon is $N$ holds in the median, 40 holds at the 95th percentile
and at most 117 holds; the first sample of a run plans 24 to 25 holds.

With estimated states the passes are wider. The median over the 84 runs
of the smallest distance is 1.80\,m, against 0.28\,m with exact states,
and no run comes within that historical clearance (least 0.21\,m), because the
historical per-hold tightened collision rows reserve the current
enclosure and the forecast of the obstacle changes from sample to
sample. The wider passes use the road: the lateral deviation reaches
11.0\,m, and the smallest distance to a road edge falls to 0.18\,m in a
15\,m/s curved crossing. The steering angle reaches 1.04\,rad, at
4.1\,m/s in an 8\,m/s turning crossing, the yaw rate 1.19\,rad/s and
the sideslip 0.21\,rad; the force ratio ranges from $-0.85$ to $1.00$.
Ten runs slow into the blended regime between $v_1$ and $v_2$, to
3.2\,m/s at the least, and none into the kinematic regime. Recovery is
confirmed after a median of 10.3\,s and at most 22.8\,s in the
single-encounter cases. Over all estimated-state samples the horizon
has a median of 16 holds, a 95th percentile of 74 and a maximum of 184.

The primary optimum is positive at 1109 of the 23083 estimated-state
samples, in 75 runs, and exceeds 1\,m at 665 of them. With an
enclosure it is the summed relaxation of the tightened rows over the
whole horizon, and the historical per-hold target tube over a long
horizon can exceed the available room; the untightened rows beyond the
primary holds are hard. The issued plans themselves clear the obstacle
by $d_{\rm m}$ at every constrained instant beyond the primary holds, by
construction, and within the primary holds they fall short of $d_{\rm m}$
at 13 samples of six runs, by at most 0.22\,m; with exact states at 16
samples of five runs, by at most 0.5\,mm.

The CLF condition is met in the model more often than on the plant. In
the exact-state runs, 3702 consecutive state pairs have $V>10^{-3}$,
and 3078 of them have zero slack in the historical secondary CLF program and
the full step accepted. In 217 of those the requested discrete CLF decrease
is not achieved by the plant, and in 60 $V$
increases; all 60 are in the 15\,m/s curved head-on run at $V<0.04$,
where the ego cruises on the curve between its two encounters with a
speed error within $\pm0.15$\,m/s, a lateral offset within 0.11\,m, and
zero modeled slack unattained at 58 of 601 samples. The tie term of
that historical secondary program fixes the later inputs of the plan with a
weight that vanishes on the path, and the next anchor inherits those
inputs; the run at 8\,m/s on the same curve cruises without a visible
error, and the four estimated-state runs on the curve that recover hold
their speed within $\pm0.05$\,m/s. In the estimated-state runs, 15955
pairs have zero slack and the full step; 1965 miss the requested
decrease and 792 increase. Recovery in Table~\ref{tab:closedLoop} is an
observed dwell, not a certified decrease.

\subsection{The Shifted Plan and the Terminal Set in the Loop}
\label{subsec:feasibilityResults}

Proposition~\ref{prop:recursive} says that the shifted plan is accepted
whenever the plant is the sampled model and the forecast does not
change. With exact states the plant is the same differential equation
under another integrator and the forecast is the truth: the shifted
plan was accepted at all 5522 samples that had one, the remaining 16
samples being the first sample of each run and the two second
encounters on the circle, at which the obstacle re-enters the range and
a fresh rollout is used. The full step was accepted at 5477 samples and
a shorter one at 61; the zero step was never needed, no sample was
linearized twice, and no fresh rollout was needed for an infeasible
shifted problem. With estimated states the shifted plan was accepted at
22919 of 22966 samples (99.8\%). At the other 47 it failed a collision
row (32), the handling envelope (7), or the terminal set (8, one
endpoint no longer mild and seven whose continuation met the moved
forecast); the zero step was taken at 66 samples, a second
linearization at 6, and a fresh rollout after an infeasible shifted
problem at 28, all in 15\,m/s braking leads; five further samples
started from a fresh rollout because the obstacle re-entered the range.
The 47 are the two mechanisms of Section~\ref{subsec:robust} at work:
rows that the innovation moved, which a tube would have absorbed, and
a forecast that moved the terminal set, which nesting would have
prevented.

The extension of the shifted plan appended guidance holds through
states outside $\mathcal X_f$ at 275 exact-state and 521
estimated-state samples, braking holds at 20 and 26, and found no hold
that keeps the endpoint in $\mathcal X_f$ at 18 and 14, after which the
plan stayed one hold shorter; no run ran out of plan. The continuation
of the issued plan's endpoint ended with the obstacle's exit at 678
exact-state and 4697 estimated-state samples and with the parked check
at 1517 and 6889; the rest had no obstacle in range. So in most samples
with an obstacle the certificate behind the horizon is a stop: the ego
could brake to rest at any time and the obstacle's forecast would pass
the parked car.

\subsection{Estimation in the Loop}
\label{subsec:loopEstimation}

Over the 84 estimated-state runs, the 95th percentile of the ego
estimation error within a run is 0.002 to 0.010\,rad in heading, 0.009
to 0.16\,m/s in lateral velocity and 0.007 to 0.023\,m in position.
With the kinematic measurement of Section~\ref{subsec:egoObserver} and
a fixed declared domain, the same encounters gave heading errors of
0.07 to 0.31\,rad and lateral-velocity errors of 1.0 to 3.9\,m/s at
15\,m/s (one seed, on the controller of October 5), the ego enclosure
was lost in nine of thirteen runs, and only five of fourteen runs
recovered. The force-balance measurement of
Section~\ref{subsec:modelAided} and the historical handling-envelope
constraints were introduced together for this reason.

The published enclosures were audited against the recorded truth in
every sample of the fourteen runs of one seed, by replaying the
estimator on the recorded plant trajectory; the replay reproduced the
recorded estimates to $5\cdot10^{-15}$. The ego enclosure was valid and
available in all 4001 samples and the target enclosure in 2018 of them,
and neither failed to contain the truth. The medians of the ego radii
within a run were 0.062 to 0.064\,m in position, 0.005 to 0.018\,rad in
heading and 0.08 to 0.15\,m/s in lateral velocity. The true motion
stayed inside the sideslip cone \eqref{eq:sideslip_cone} and below the
rear-tire saturation threshold in every sample of that earlier version, including
the two runs of the seed that slow into the blended regime, where the
plant's lateral forces are no longer those of the Fiala model that the
force-balance measurement inverts. The audit covers the sampled
instants of one seed and is not a proof of the premises.

The target enclosure remains wide where the controller needs it most.
The relative-position radius has a median of about 0.064\,m, but the
set of motion parameters used for prediction has median radii of 0.20
to 0.59\,rad in course and 1.4 to 4.3\,m/s in speed, and the whole
declared range for $A_C$ and $\kappa_C$. The point estimates of the
parameters are also noisy: between 0.5 and 3\,s, the root-mean-square
error within a run has a median of 0.26\,m/s$^2$ for $A_C$ (largest
0.61\,m/s$^2$) and 0.008\,rad for $\beta_C$ (largest 0.042\,rad). The
gain program \eqref{eq:noise_shape_lmi} bounds the variance of the
velocity error; the noise of the acceleration state, from which $A_C$
and $\beta_C$ are reconstructed, is not part of its objective. These
widths are the ones that Section~\ref{subsec:robust} measures the
robust problem against.

the forecast until the obstacle has left the range instead of until the
plan ends, with the published parameters of this estimator.

\subsection{Computation}
\label{subsec:computationResults}

One controller call took a median of 5.5\,ms and a 95th percentile of
19\,ms with exact states, and 7.3 and 36\,ms with estimated states, in
the campaign itself, with six runs sharing the machine
(Fig.~\ref{fig:closedLoop}(f)); 1.1\% of the exact-state calls and 2.3\%
of the estimated-state calls exceeded the 50\,ms hold, and the largest
calls took 0.11 and 0.30\,s. The estimator took a median of 12\,ms per
hold, for four estimator samples. The time grows with the plan length:
the slow calls are those with long horizons, up to 184 holds, and the
28 samples that solved a fresh rollout after an infeasible shifted
problem. In a separate benchmark with exact states, two runs sharing the
machine and the obstacle appearing after 2\,s of target-free driving,
the sample at which the obstacle appears took 26\,ms in the median and
72\,ms at most, and all samples 6.5\,ms in the median and 23\,ms at the
95th percentile. The historical block-assembly and row-pruning optimizations halved
these times against the previous implementation of the same problem,
whose 95th percentile with estimated states was 136\,ms; the solver
tolerances account for about 6\% of the time at the median and 15\% at
the 95th percentile, and cost 0.1\,s of recovery time at the median.
Because the computation time is not applied as a delay, the outcomes
describe the algorithm with instantaneous computation.

\subsection{Verification of the Information-Set Construction}
\label{subsec:informationChecks}

A separate longitudinal reference implements the physical core of
Section~\ref{subsec:robust} with a constant-speed moving target and
forward-only ego braking with absorbing rest. The hold is 0.2\,s, the
horizon has four holds with two slack holds, the braking alphabet is
$\{0,2,4\}$\,m/s$^2$, and the slew limit is 10\,m/s$^3$. All 81 input
sequences are enumerated, first minimizing robust deficit and then a
speed/braking cost. Four-coordinate interval sets contain ego and target
position and speed. Bounded observation offsets alternate deterministically;
no simulated truth enters the optimizer. Whole-hold gaps are minimized
analytically at endpoints and any relative-speed crossing.

In twelve safe holds, all primary values remain zero, the minimum true
gap is 0.56\,m, ten raw observation boxes are not nested in the old
prediction, and two observations are missing. Posterior conditioning
preserves every shifted witness. One-hold horizons and uncertain parked
states also retain valid continuations; an input-limited case is
infeasible. An initially overlapping example starts with a 0.4\,m robust
deficit and a $-0.2$\,m true gap before recovering; it is not safe from
initialization. A separate trajectory has a 4\,mm minimum gap at the
start, midpoint and endpoint but a $-1$\,mm whole-hold minimum. It
illustrates why sampled geometry cannot certify the continuous hold.

A pointwise-bound check uses
$P=\diag(1,4)$, $A=[-1,-20;\,5,-1]$ and zero disturbance injection.
Although $A^\trans P+PA+I=\diag(-1,-7)\prec0$, the initial error
$(0,1)^\trans$ evolves as
$e^{-t}(-2\sin 10t,\cos 10t)^\trans$ and has norm 1.70927 at
$t=\pi/20$. Thus an energy inequality with unit attenuation and a
declared unit disturbance bound does not by itself establish a unit
Euclidean error ball. The metric bound of radius 2 does contain this
trajectory. This checks the inference from energy gain to pointwise
containment, not the vehicle experiment of \cite{QuanZhouFriskChung2025}.

The reference and edge cases pass eleven behavior tests; the associated
observer-enclosure, information-set and rectangle-support suite passes
64 tests in total. These are ordinary floating-point specializations,
not directed-rounding certificates or new full-vehicle performance
results. Source and settings are in
\path{scripts/verifyUnifiedObserverSafety.m}, with compact results in
\path{report/UNIFIED_OBSERVER_SAFETY_20261025.json}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The paper examines how target-state estimation and predictive control
can be combined for vehicle collision avoidance, with particular
attention to the conditions supporting the safety analysis.

\subsubsection*{The estimator during the maneuver}
The point of keeping Sharma's relative motion model while giving up its
differentiated coordinates was to stay valid during the ego's own
maneuver, and the open-loop comparison bears this out where it counts:
in the swerve-and-braking and weaving scenarios, which violate the
zero-jerk premise of the reference observer, the speed error is an
order of magnitude lower, and the reference fails to converge in a
third and in all of the trials. The gain selection takes place inside
the certificate rather than beside it. With a shape chosen outside the
certificate only the scale can be adjusted to it, and the gains come
out larger than the certificate requires; choosing the shape inside
lowered the predicted velocity noise and the certified radii together.
The remaining losses against the tuned reference are consistent with
constraints of the certificate: at low relative speed the decay floor
keeps the certified gains noisier than the reference's uncertified
choice, and in arc following the sideslip bound declared for that
scenario raises $L_q$ from 0.80 to 2.29\,s$^{-2}$.

\subsubsection*{Closing the loop changed both designs}
Most changes came in pairs, because the estimator's premises are
statements about the true motion and the controller decides that
motion. The kinematic premise of the velocity measurement failed in
exactly the maneuvers the controller exists for, and the bounds
published under it were then wrong, not merely loose. Sharing the tire
model and the held input replaced that premise by one the controller
can enforce. The current controller retains the sideslip domain
and allows Fiala saturation; the force-balance interval accounts for
its loss of information. The earlier audited controller also imposed
a rear-nonsaturation cone, so its recorded results should not be
interpreted as validation of the revised constraint set. In the other direction, the estimator's enclosure
could not be used as a worst case throughout the horizon or in the
decrease condition: both made the programs infeasible or the commands
erratic. The baseline compromise tightens one affine hold and replans; it does
not certify the nonlinear hold without remainder bounds. The adopted
compact nonlinear controller of Section~\ref{sec:control} instead pairs
fixed measurement-consistency ensemble inference with condensed nonlinear
PCBF optimization and full fine-RK4 replay across every retained member.
The theoretical construction of Section~\ref{subsec:robust} instead propagates both
vehicles, their observers and future control responses on common
measurement branches. Conditioning the retained successor preserves the
old policy continuation. This retains information-feedback effects that
a prescribed open-loop input sequence omits. It does not establish a
uniformly narrow tube or initial feasibility for every encounter.

\subsubsection*{The terminal set and the ten failures}
In the historical October 11 baseline, the braking-to-rest terminal set
was built from the obstacle's own motion model and one braking law, was
invariant under that law, and supported exact-information shift
feasibility. In the loop the statement held
where its premises held: the shifted plan was accepted at every
exact-state sample and at 99.8\% of the estimated-state samples. The
cost is that the forecast is now trusted until the obstacle leaves the
range, which for a stop in the lane means several seconds beyond the
plan, and the published course and curvature of an obstacle acquired
half a second earlier are not good enough for that on the curved road:
eight of the ten failures are of this kind, and an earlier design with a
weaker, non-invariant terminal condition recovered 82 of 84. The
published parameter sets contained the truth in every audited sample;
the controller forecasts with their centers. These historical failures
concern the October 11 braking-terminal baseline; the adopted compact
controller of Section~\ref{subsec:terminal} checks continuation to first
range exit, with up to $B$ nonlinear bridge holds evaluated
across the retained ensemble under Assumption~\ref{asm:handoff}, which
remains pending closed-loop campaign re-evaluation. Propagating
the published target marginal through the full horizon recovered 31 of 84
encounters in an earlier ablation, with one collision; that experiment
does not test the complete information-set formulation.

Theorem~\ref{thm:robust} closes the shift argument by conditioning the
retained first successor and carrying the old input tail and its
certificates. A common terminal continuation covers every admitted
branch, including the parked pose set. Neither fixed-width tubes nor
nested raw observer balls are needed. The two cited observer-based
barrier designs motivate valid error compensation; they do not replace
this predictive feasibility argument or its actuator and terminal
premises. Exact images define a conditional mathematical construction.
Efficient sound image and terminal-membership algorithms for the full
Fiala/NRMM system remain implementation work. Shared relative geometry
can reduce conservatism, but every remaining uncertainty must be covered.

\subsubsection*{The CLF and secondary preference}
In the historical October 11 baseline, one obstacle-independent function
governs avoidance and recovery, every
completed run returns to cruise with the same function and programs
throughout, and the certificate of the cruise law rests on the sampled
nonlinear model rather than on its linearization at the trim, at the
cost of an enclosure hypothesis that is sampled, not proved. The
decrease requested of the issued input is a request on a local model,
and the plant misses it in 7\% of the zero-slack samples with exact
states. The one run in which $V$ increases, at 15\,m/s on the curve,
points at the tie term: near the path its weight vanishes, the later
inputs of the plan are then fixed by nothing but the solver's choice,
and the next anchor inherits them. A floor of one unit of the error
scales on that weight, applied to the shifted plan, removed the
oscillation in the revision of the controller that followed the
campaign of this section (cruise exact at both speeds on both paths, 14
of 14 and 73 of 84 encounters recovered); the results reported here
predate it. The adopted compact nonlinear controller of
Section~\ref{subsec:program} replaces the per-step CLF inequality with
the quadratic tracking cost \eqref{eq:tracking_cost} and guides selection
on the zero-deficit level using the tire-loading and rear-slip preference
\eqref{eq:secondary_program}, which remains to be evaluated in a rerun of
the closed-loop campaign.

\subsubsection*{Premises of the trials}
Several premises are favorable. The plant is the prediction model with
nominal parameters, so the structural premise of the force-balance
measurement holds by construction above 4\,m/s, and the 5\% parameter
box is a design margin, not a measured mismatch. The obstacle follows
its declared model. Computation is treated as instantaneous although 1
to 2\% of the calls exceed the hold. The steering angle is unbounded in
magnitude and rate in the historical trials, and the estimated-state
runs use up to 1.04\,rad.
The road edges are declared, not perceived, and the relative-position
measurement is synthetic with a noise bound of 0.04\,m; on simulated
camera and LiDAR captures the perception pipeline has so far produced
position errors
above the 0.12\,m bound assumed in Section~\ref{subsec:designResults} on
most frames, and it has not been run in the loop.
Both the historical braking-terminal premise and the revised moving
handoff assumption (Assumption~\ref{asm:handoff}) carry no derived
Fiala mismatch bound, and node-only collision checks leave intersample
separation uncertified. The
sampled realization of the observer is not covered by the
continuous-time theorem. Each of these premises has to be removed
before physical collision avoidance can be claimed.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work developed a joint estimator--controller design for vehicle
collision avoidance under coupled vehicle dynamics and target-state
uncertainty. The estimator keeps the ego-motion-aware
relative motion model of Sharma \emph{et al.} but takes the measured
acceleration and yaw rate as inputs instead of differentiating them
away, so it needs neither zero jerk nor zero yaw acceleration; it
carries a cascade ISS certificate, chooses its gains inside that
certificate, measures the lateral velocity through the controller's
tire model and held input, and publishes an enclosure with every
estimate. The adopted compact nonlinear controller combines fixed
measurement-consistency ensemble inference with condensed nonlinear PCBF
optimization over coarse control knots on the Fiala bicycle model, exact
dual rectangle separation at control-boundary nodes, a terminal
continuation to sensing-range exit
that supports shift feasibility under Assumption~\ref{asm:handoff}, and
a secondary tire-loading and rear-slip preference \eqref{eq:secondary_program}
that guides selection on the zero-deficit level without altering the
primary safety objective.

Theorem~\ref{thm:robust} provides a unified conditional link from
observer containment to decision-instant safety. A valid current
enclosure initializes joint nonlinear prediction of the physical
vehicles, observers and measurement-dependent policies. The entire next
sensor block conditions the retained first successor. Restriction to
surviving histories and a compatible terminal extension give the shift
proof. Nonzero estimation error and varying tube widths are allowed.
Attained primary minimizers give optimal-relaxation descent and an
invariant zero set, without a secondary performance objective. Initial
feasibility, well-defined joint flows, admissible policy restriction and
terminal closure remain explicit premises. This theorem neither
requires constant tube width nor proves finite-time arrival at zero;
road boundaries and intersample collision are outside its scope.

In the historical October 11 baseline (predating the revised compact
nonlinear controller and pending rerun), in fourteen collision-threat encounters with exact states every run
ends collision-free on the road with cruise recovered and the shifted
plan feasible at every sample. With the estimator and bounded sensor
noise, 74 of 84 runs do so, none collides or leaves the road, and ten
stop without a solved command, eight of them within two seconds of the
start, where the published course and curvature of the obstacle place
its forecast meters from its motion. The published enclosures contained
the truth in every audited sample. These are simulations on the
prediction model itself. That historical implementation used per-hold affine
tightening and center-based braking terminal rows, so those results are evidence
about that earlier approximation rather than the revised compact controller.
The longitudinal reference verifies selected
containment and shift mechanisms separately. Re-evaluating the adopted
compact controller across the closed-loop campaign, implementing the full
certificate construction, and evaluating physical model, perception and
intersample separation contracts are future engineering work. They do not
leave a missing step in the stated mathematical recursive-feasibility proof.
