\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} driving has become a major area of research,
and an active collision-avoidance system is an essential component of an
autonomous vehicle. By detecting potential hazards and initiating an
evasive response, a collision-avoidance system enables a vehicle to avoid
obstacles without driver intervention. In road traffic, other vehicles are among the most common sources of
collision risk. Consequently, the design of systems that
can actively avoid collisions with surrounding vehicles has attracted
considerable research interest \cite{LiEtAl2022Emergency,ChenChengLiZhaoWang2024}.

Collision avoidance often requires the ego vehicle to perform complex
maneuvers, such as decelerating while turning \cite{TanWuLiLiuZhang2025}.
Such maneuvers pose two challenges. First, the vehicle's longitudinal and
lateral dynamics become strongly coupled, as changes in speed alter lateral
tire forces and yaw response while yaw motion couples the longitudinal and
lateral velocities \cite{BealBoyd2019,ParkGerdes2017}. Neglecting this
coupling can lead to large prediction errors and control commands that demand
maneuvers beyond the vehicle's physical capabilities. Second, sensors
such as LiDAR can provide relative-position measurements of surrounding
vehicles, but a more comprehensive assessment of collision risk benefits
from additional state information, such as velocity, acceleration, and heading. When only relative positions are
available, estimating these states is complicated by the ego vehicle's
evasive maneuvers, which also affect the measurements
\cite{AlaiSharmaAlexanderRajamani2024,SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}.

Regarding the ego vehicle's coupled dynamics, some approaches simplify
lateral control by assuming a constant longitudinal speed, thereby
decoupling it from the longitudinal dynamics. This assumption is used in
steering control \cite{JiKhajepourMelekHuang2017}, integrated path tracking
\cite{XiongLiuYangLeng2022}, and hierarchical planning
\cite{ChenAksunGuvenc2025}, but it cannot capture braking-induced speed
changes. Other approaches use kinematic models for trajectory planning
\cite{LiZhangGuoLenzoGuo2023}, which allow speed to vary but omit tire-force
dynamics. To capture the effect of speed variations on lateral dynamics,
lateral models can be formulated using linear parameter-varying control
\cite{AtouiSenameMilanesMartinez2022} or gain-scheduled robust control
\cite{LiLamLuLi2022}, and steering decisions can be computed for prescribed
braking and throttle profiles \cite{TurriCarvalhoTsengJohanssonBorrelli2013}.
Although these methods capture the effect of speed on lateral motion, speed
dependence alone does not account for the reduction in lateral tire capacity
under braking. Combined demands can be constrained through acceleration
limits \cite{ChenPengGrizzle2018}, stability envelopes
\cite{ChenChengLiZhaoWang2024}, or stability-based speed adjustment
\cite{AttiaOrjuelaBasset2012,AttiaOrjuelaBasset2014}, while
assume--guarantee contracts bound the interaction between separate controllers
\cite{XuGrizzleTabuadaAmes2018}; their guarantees depend on the prescribed
bounds holding during operation. Such constraints need not, however,
represent the full nonlinear tire response. To describe the coupled dynamics
more directly, nonlinear feedback methods coordinate longitudinal and
lateral actions using backstepping \cite{GuoHuWang2016}, Lyapunov design
\cite{CheblyTaljCharara2017}, or dynamic inversion
\cite{SeoLeeBaekHorowitzChoi2022}. Predictive controllers optimize motion
using wheel-slip inputs \cite{ZhouJiaJingLiuGuvenc2018}, combined-slip tire
models \cite{ZhangJiangLiuHuWangChen2023}, load-transfer dynamics
\cite{ThompsonDallasGohBalachandran2024}, or drift-aware planning
\cite{ZhaoChenGaoYaoGaoHua2024}. Their accuracy depends on the adopted model,
and nonlinear prediction also increases computational demand.

Regarding the estimation of surrounding-vehicle motion, one option is to
express the motion model in an inertial frame using estimated ego position and
orientation \cite{SharmaRajamani2024}, but ego localization and heading errors
then feed directly into the target track. Working in the ego sensor frame
avoids this global transformation, yet some relative-motion observers do not
account for ego rotation
\cite{JeonZemoucheRajamani2019,AlaiZemoucheRajamani2024}. To address the estimation errors that this omission can cause during
evasive maneuvers, Alai \emph{et al.}
\cite{AlaiSharmaAlexanderRajamani2024} formulated a nonlinear relative motion
model (NRMM) that incorporates ego velocity and yaw rate explicitly,
Kyong \emph{et al.} \cite{KyongAlaiTamehRajamani2026} further demonstrated
the importance of ego sideslip-angle estimation for relative-motion target
tracking during sharp turns and U-turns, and Sharma
\emph{et al.} \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026} designed a multistage high-gain
observer (HGO) around the NRMM for simultaneous ego-state estimation and target
trajectory tracking. While the NRMM captures how an evasive maneuver alters
relative motion in the sensor frame, that multistage HGO transforms both the
ego kinematics and the NRMM relative-position dynamics into third-order
companion chains by assuming zero body jerk and zero yaw acceleration, an
assumption that breaks down during rapid steering and braking. In addition, its
nominal convergence and scalar yaw $H_\infty$ analyses do not provide an
explicit, time-varying state-error bound under sensor noise, model mismatch,
and initial transients, which a safety controller needs to tighten its
collision constraints.

Together, these two challenges raise the central problem of how to
construct a collision-avoidance controller with formal safety guarantees
that respects coupled tire and actuator limits while remaining feasible
under online estimation errors. Field-based
planners such as artificial potential fields \cite{WangGaoLiSunCheng2019}
and virtual fluid-flow models \cite{ChengLiLiuLiGuo2021} blend lane keeping
with obstacle repulsion intuitively, yet a collision-free
reference path does not certify closed-loop safety once tire saturation,
input bounds, and tracking errors intervene. Control barrier functions
(CBFs) enforce set forward invariance directly through affine conditions
on the control input \cite{AmesXuGrizzleTabuada2017,XiaoBelta2022}. Under
bounded actuation, however, myopic one-step CBF quadratic programs can
lose feasibility \cite{ZengZhangLiSreenath2021}. For lane keeping and
obstacle avoidance, Bruggemann \emph{et al.}
\cite{BruggemannSteevesKrstic2022} investigate input-constrained barrier
functions using a constant-speed lateral vehicle model. Hardware actuator
limits can also lead to saturation in high-speed corner cases when they are
omitted from the controller formulation
\cite{TayalEtAl2026}. Road experiments have also recorded safety
violations under nominal CBFs during vehicle cut-ins and delays in live
traffic \cite{GunterNiceBuntingSprinkleWork2022} and under unmodeled
actuation disturbances on automated trucks
\cite{AlanTaylorHeAmesOrosz2023}. Approaches to anticipating future
hazards include enforcing discrete-time barrier constraints within MPC
\cite{ZengZhangSreenath2021} and constructing barriers from predicted
motion under a prescribed policy \cite{BlackJankovicSharmaPanagou2023}.
Predictive control barrier functions (PCBFs) use finite-horizon
constraint relaxations and terminal constructions to establish recursive
feasibility and safety recovery under their respective assumptions
\cite{WabersichZeilinger2023,HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025}. Still,
these PCBF guarantees assume exact state measurements or fixed offline
disturbance bounds. Conversely,
observer-based CBFs account for moving-obstacle estimation errors via
input-to-state safety margins \cite{TezukaNakamura2020} or bounded-error
observer sets \cite{QuanZhouFriskChung2025}, yet remain one-step filters
without multi-step prediction under coupled tire and actuator limits.
Safe MPC for uncertain road users \cite{BatkovicGuptaZanonFalcone2023}
establishes recursive feasibility through reachable-set tightening, a
safe terminal set, and step-to-step prediction consistency, but relies on
a kinematic ego model and assumes ego sensing and localization are
already solved. In coupled maneuvers with online ego and target
estimation, refreshing the estimate and re-tightening constraints at each
sample can invalidate the prior backup plan unless successive uncertainty
sets remain compatible.

For nonlinear observer design, Sharma, Zemouche and Rajamani
\cite{SharmaZemoucheRajamani2026} develop derivative-based LMI conditions
for systems with vector arguments and introduce state scaling to improve
numerical feasibility. Their analysis informs the nonlinear-increment
bounds and scaled quadratic certificate used in the estimator below.

To address these challenges, this paper develops a joint NRMM-based
state estimator and PCBF--CLF controller built around a shared dynamic
bicycle model with modified Fiala tires. The system also includes a
camera--LiDAR perception pipeline for target-position measurement. The
design and evaluation of perception are not the focus of this paper: the
pipeline has been tested offline, while the closed-loop experiments use
synthetic relative-position measurements. The system architecture is
described in Section~\ref{sec:systemDesign}. The main contributions of
this work are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
\item We reformulate the NRMM target observer of
\cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026} in covariant coordinates so that measured
ego acceleration and yaw rate enter as exogenous inputs without
differentiation, eliminating the zero-jerk and zero-yaw-acceleration
assumptions. Combined with semidefinite-programming (SDP) gain synthesis
and a tire force-balance lateral-velocity measurement, the observer
admits a continuous-time cascade input-to-state stability (ISS)
certificate and publishes explicit, time-varying error enclosures online
(Section~\ref{sec:estimator}).
\item We design a compact nonlinear PCBF controller on the coupled Fiala
bicycle model that pairs fixed measurement-consistency ensemble inference
with condensed nonlinear sequential quadratic programming over coarse
control knots and exact dual rectangle separation at control-boundary
nodes. A terminal continuation that keeps the vehicles separated until
the target leaves sensing range supports horizon-shift feasibility under an explicit
physical handoff assumption, while a secondary tire-loading and rear-slip
preference guides selection among validated zero-deficit driving plans
without altering the primary safety objective (Section~\ref{sec:control}).
\item We establish a joint nonlinear output-feedback safety formulation
that propagates both vehicles and observers under future
measurement-dependent policies. Conditioning on the entire received
sensor block permits restriction of the old policy tail. Under explicit
uncertainty, attainment and terminal-closure assumptions, this proves
recursive feasibility, nonincreasing optimal safety relaxation and
decision-instant safety of its zero set for the road-free problem.
The adopted compact Fiala controller's approximations are stated
separately (Section~\ref{subsec:robust}).
\item We benchmark the proposed estimator against the reproduced
multistage HGO of \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026}, verify the
information-set safety mechanisms on a longitudinal reference problem,
and preserve the historical October 11 closed-loop baseline across 14
collision-threat encounters under both exact states and bounded sensor
noise over six random seeds, including an audit of truth containment for
the published enclosures; closed-loop re-evaluation of the revised
compact controller remains pending (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:systemDesign} introduces the task and system architecture.
Sections~\ref{sec:estimator} and~\ref{sec:control} develop the state
estimator and the predictive controller, respectively. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the
numerical evaluation, and Sections~\ref{sec:discussion}
and~\ref{sec:conclusion} discuss implementation limitations and conclude
the paper.

\section{Vehicle Dynamics}\label{sec:systemDesign}
The ego vehicle follows a prescribed path at speed $V_{\rm ref}>0$.
During an encounter it measures the target's position in its own body
frame. Let $\vct p_E,\vct p_T$ be the inertial reference positions
and $\mtx R_E,\mtx R_T$ the body-to-inertial rotations. The radar
observes $\vct\rho$ and the relative orientation is $\mtx Q$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relativepose}
 \vct\rho=\mtx R_E^\top(\vct p_T-\vct p_E),\qquad
 \mtx Q=\mtx R_E^\top\mtx R_T .
\end{equation}
Together these quantities determine whether the rectangles intersect.
We seek one input, based on the measurements to date, that keeps them
apart and follows the cruise command whenever safety permits.
\subsection{Ego-Vehicle Model}
Let $\vct x_E=[\vct p_E^\top,\psi_E,\vct v^\top,r]^\top$ and
$\vct u=[\delta,b]^\top$, where $\vct p_E\in\mathbb R^2$ is the inertial
position, $\psi_E$ is the body heading, $\vct v=[v_x,v_y]^\top$ is the
body velocity, $r$ is the yaw rate, $\delta$ is the front steering angle,
and $b\in[-1,1]$ is the longitudinal force demand. With
$\vct e(\theta)=[\cos\theta,\sin\theta]^\top$,
$\mtx J=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ and
$\mtx R(\theta)=[\vct e(\theta),\mtx J\vct e(\theta)]$, the planar
single-track model gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ego}
\begin{aligned}
 \dot{\vct p}_E&=\mtx R_E\vct v,\qquad \dot\psi_E=r,\\
 m(\dot{\vct v}+r\mtx J\vct v)
   &=\mtx R(\delta)\vct F_f+\vct F_r-F_d(v_x)\vct e(0),\\
 I_z\dot r&=l_f[\mtx R(\delta)\vct F_f]_2-l_r[\vct F_r]_2 ,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mtx R_E=\mtx R(\psi_E)$, $m$ and $I_z$ are mass and yaw
inertia, $l_f,l_r$ are the axle distances, $F_d$ is the longitudinal
road load, and $\vct F_f,\vct F_r$ are forces in wheel axes. For axle
$i\in\{f,r\}$ let $K_i=\mu_i F_{zi}$ and $D_i=K_i\sqrt{1-b^2}$, where
$\mu_i$ is the friction coefficient and $F_{zi}$ is the static normal
load. The modified Fiala law gives $F_{xi}=bK_i$ and, for $D_i>0$,
\begin{equation*}
 F_{yi}=-D_i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha_i)
       [1-(1-z_i)_+^3],\qquad z_i=\frac{C_i|\tan\alpha_i|}{3D_i},
\end{equation*}
where $x_+=\max(x,0)$, $C_i$ is the cornering stiffness, and
\begin{equation*}
 \alpha_f=\operatorname{atan2}(v_y+l_fr,v_x)-\delta,\quad
 \alpha_r=\operatorname{atan2}(v_y-l_rr,v_x).
\end{equation*}
For $D_i=0$ we set $F_{yi}=0$. We consider forward rolling motion with
positive wheel-longitudinal velocities. The physical parameters
$\vct\vartheta$ are constant along each trajectory. Admissible inputs
respect the actuator limits and yield unique flows. Any actuator state
is included in $\vct x_E$.

\subsection{Target Motion Model}
Let $\vct x_T=[\vct p_T^\top,V,\gamma,A,\kappa]^\top$, where
$\vct p_T$ is the target position, $V$ is speed, $\gamma$ is course,
$A$ is tangential acceleration, and $\kappa$ is curvature. During the
encounter, $A$ and the sideslip $\beta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ are constant.
With rear axle distance $l_T$, the body heading is $\psi_T=\gamma-\beta$
and $\kappa=\sin\beta/l_T$. For $V>0$ the dynamics are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 \dot{\vct p}_T=V\vct e(\gamma),\quad
 \dot V=A,\quad \dot\gamma=\kappa V,\quad \dot A=\dot\kappa=0.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Target Flow}
At a moving epoch $a$, let $V_a=V(a)$, $b_T=p_T(a)$ and
$d=e^{\mathrm i\gamma(a)}$, identifying planar vectors with complex
numbers in the orbit equations. Integrating $\dot V=A$ and
$d\gamma=\kappa\,ds$ gives the traveled distance and reference-point
displacement in initial course axes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clock}
 s(\tau)=V_a\tau+\tfrac12 A\tau^2,\qquad
 F_\kappa(s)=\int_0^s e^{\mathrm i\kappa l}\,dl .
\end{equation}
The reference point has position $b_T+dF_\kappa(s)$. A body point $q$
also rotates through the heading $\gamma(a)+\kappa s-\beta$, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit}
 T_q(a+\tau)=b_T+d\,G_q(s),\quad
 G_q(s)=F_\kappa(s)+e^{\mathrm i(\kappa s-\beta)}q .
\end{equation}
For straight motion $F_0(s)=s$. If $A<0$ and $\tau\ge -V_a/A$, the
absorbing stop holds $s=-V_a^2/(2A)$ in \eqref{eq:orbit}, fixing both
position and heading.

\subsubsection{Relative Dynamics}
To express the target motion in the radar frame, let
$\vct w=\mtx R_E^\top\dot{\vct p}_T$ and
$\vct f=\mtx R_E^\top\ddot{\vct p}_T$. For a vector in ego axes
write $\nabla_r\vct w=\dot{\vct w}+r\mtx J\vct w$. With
$\vct d_B=\vct w/V$ and $\Omega=\kappa V$, \eqref{eq:target} gives
$\vct f=A\vct d_B+V\Omega\mtx J\vct d_B$.
Differentiating with $\nabla_r\vct d_B=\Omega\mtx J\vct d_B$ yields
the nonlinear relative motion model (NRMM) \cite{SharmaAlaiRajamani2026},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nrmm}
\begin{aligned}
 \nabla_r\vct\rho&=\vct w-\vct v,\qquad \nabla_r\vct w=\vct f,\\
 \nabla_r\vct f&=-\Omega^2\vct w+3A\Omega\mtx J\vct d_B .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Conversely, let $V=\|\vct w\|>0$, $A=\vct d_B^\top\vct f$ and
$N=(\mtx J\vct d_B)^\top\vct f$. Substituting $\Omega=N/V$ into
\eqref{eq:nrmm} gives
\begin{equation*}
 \dot V=A,\qquad \dot A=0,\qquad
 \dot N=2A\Omega
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{d}{dt}\frac{N}{V^2}=0 .
\end{equation*}
Thus $\kappa=N/V^2$ and $\beta=\arcsin(l_T\kappa)$ recover the same
target orbit and body heading. Vary the future input at a fixed initial
state. By \eqref{eq:target} and \eqref{eq:relativepose} the variations satisfy
\begin{equation*}
 \delta_{\vct u}\vct p_T=0,\qquad
 \delta_{\vct u}\vct\rho=-\mtx R_E^\top\delta_{\vct u}\vct p_E
                  -\mtx J\vct\rho\,\delta_{\vct u}\psi_E .
\end{equation*}
The target orbit is autonomous. Its measured position depends on the
ego motion, so each orbit estimate must retain the ego trajectory that
produced it.

\section{Observer Design}\label{sec:estimator}
\subsection{Set-Membership Observer}
During an in-range encounter the ego sensors and radar provide
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outputs}
\begin{aligned}
 \vct y_p&=\vct p_E+\vct\eta_p+\vct n_p,&
 y_r&=r+\eta_r+n_r,\\
 \vct y_a&=\dot{\vct v}+r\mtx J\vct v+\vct\eta_a+\vct n_a,&
 \vct y_\rho&=\vct\rho+\vct\eta_\rho+\vct n_\rho ,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\eta$ is persistent bias and $n$ belongs to the prescribed
joint error family. Evaluate the acceleration under the applied input.
Let $z$ specify the initial ego state, the physical parameters
$\vct\vartheta$, the target tuple $(b_T,d,V_a,A,\kappa)$, the biases
and any error memory needed to describe an admissible history. Take a
compact prior $\Theta_a^-$ containing the true $z$.

\begin{definition}[Joint set-membership observer]\label{def:observer}
Let $\mathcal Y_{[a,t]}(z)$ be the output histories admitted by
\eqref{eq:ego}, \eqref{eq:target} and \eqref{eq:outputs} under the
applied input. For the received history $y_{[a,t]}$, define the
observer state and its target-position prediction by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observer}
\begin{aligned}
 \Theta_t&=\{z\in\Theta_a^-:y_{[a,t]}\in\mathcal Y_{[a,t]}(z)\},\\
 \mathcal T_{q,t}(h)&=\{T_{q,z}(t+h):z\in\Theta_t\},\qquad h\ge0 ,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $T_{q,z}$ is the material-point flow \eqref{eq:orbit}.
\end{definition}
At $h=0$ the second line gives the current position set. At $h>0$ it
propagates the same members. Every member consistent with the longer
measurement history also satisfies the shorter one \cite{BertsekasRhodes1971}.
Applying the same target flow at the same physical time gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditioning}
 \Theta_{t+\Delta}\subseteq\Theta_t,\qquad
 \mathcal T_{q,t+\Delta}(h)\subseteq\mathcal T_{q,t}(h+\Delta).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Geometric Measurement Constraints}
\subsubsection{Curvature from Position}
Fix an ego trajectory and radar bias in \eqref{eq:observer}.
At any observed time $t_j$ transform the radar reading to inertial axes.
With $F_j=F_\kappa(s(t_j-a))$, \eqref{eq:outputs} and \eqref{eq:orbit}
give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disks}
\begin{aligned}
 \vct Y_j &= \vct p_E(t_j)+\mtx R_E(t_j)(\vct y_\rho(t_j)-\vct\eta_\rho),\\
 Y_j &= b_T+dF_j+\varepsilon_j,\qquad |\varepsilon_j|\le\epsilon_j .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $Y_j$ is the complex coordinate of $\vct Y_j$. First consider exact
positions. Three distinct fixes in forward order on an arc of less than
one revolution determine its signed curvature. With
$\vct D_{ij}=\vct Y_j-\vct Y_i$, the signed triangle area and circumradius give
\begin{equation*}
 \kappa=\frac{2\det(\vct D_{01},\vct D_{02})}
              {\|\vct D_{01}\|\,\|\vct D_{02}\|\,\|\vct D_{12}\|}.
\end{equation*}
Distinct collinear fixes give $\kappa=0$. This relation uses positions
alone. Their observation times now determine how far the target could
have traveled on that orbit.

\subsubsection{Speed and Acceleration}
Fix a candidate $\kappa$ and a forward moving arc with
$|\kappa|\Delta s\le\pi$. Write $l$ for its true chord length and
$\hat l=|Y_j-Y_i|$ for the measured length. Subtracting the two
equations in \eqref{eq:disks} and taking moduli gives
\begin{equation*}
 |l-\hat l|\le\epsilon_i+\epsilon_j,\qquad
 l=\begin{cases}
 2\sin(|\kappa|\Delta s/2)/|\kappa|,&\kappa\ne0,\\
 \Delta s,&\kappa=0 .
 \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
Intersect the measured interval with the range of this chord law and
call its endpoints $l^-,l^+$. On a nonempty interval the inverse is
$L_\kappa(l)=2\arcsin(|\kappa|l/2)/|\kappa|$ for $\kappa\ne0$
and $L_0(l)=l$. The clock in \eqref{eq:clock} then gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clockstrip}
 L_\kappa(l^-)\le\Delta[V_a+(\bar t-a)A]\le L_\kappa(l^+),
\end{equation}
where $\Delta=t_j-t_i>0$ and $\bar t=(t_j+t_i)/2$.
Each pair therefore constrains the same two unknowns $(V_a,A)$.

\subsubsection{From the Orbit to the Footprint}
Given a clock and curvature, \eqref{eq:disks} leaves the initial course
$d$ and translation $b_T$ constrained by
\begin{equation*}
 |d|=1,\qquad b_T\in\bigcap_j\mathbb B(Y_j-dF_j,\epsilon_j),
\end{equation*}
where $\mathbb B$ is a closed disk. For two exact fixes with $F_1\ne F_0$,
subtraction gives $d=(Y_1-Y_0)/(F_1-F_0)$ and $b_T=Y_0-dF_0$.
Substitution into \eqref{eq:orbit} gives the position of each corner,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chord}
 T_q(a+\tau)=Y_0+(Y_1-Y_0)\frac{G_q(s(\tau))-F_0}{F_1-F_0}.
\end{equation}
Under bounded errors the observer retains every joint member satisfying
the full output history. The chord inequalities constrain these members
even when the measurements do not determine a unique orbit.

\subsubsection{Joint Polytope}
For example, an exact straight-line chord of length $l$ from $a$ to
$a+\tau$ gives $V_a+\tau A/2=l/\tau$. Both $V_a$ and $A$ may still
vary, but their measured combination is fixed. To retain such
relations, let $\vct\lambda\in B$ be the displacement from a fixed reference in
a joint parameter chart, with $B$ a bounded box. For a scalar output
$y=\bar y+\vct a^\top\vct\lambda+e$, let $r_y$ bound the sum of
measurement and expansion errors on the chart. The resulting strip is
\begin{equation*}
 y-\bar y-r_y\le \vct a^\top\vct\lambda\le y-\bar y+r_y .
\end{equation*}
Collecting such rows, including valid clock strips in the same
coordinates, gives the outer enclosure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polytope}
 P_t=\{\vct\lambda\in B:\mtx H\vct\lambda\le\vct b\}.
\end{equation}
Each row holds for every compatible member of its chart. Appending
observation rows thus preserves containment of the true member and
restricts the same joint variables used in prediction.

\section{Controller Design}\label{sec:control}
\subsection{Rectangle Separation}
For one member $z\in\Theta_t$ and a future input $\vct u$, evaluate the
two footprints at the same time $t+h$. Let $B_0=[-L,L]\times[-W,W]$
be the centered rectangle and $B_i=\vct o_i+B_0$ the footprint about
vehicle $i$'s reference point. The displacement between centers in ego
axes is $\vct c=\vct\rho+\mtx Q\vct o_T-\vct o_E$ by \eqref{eq:relativepose}.
Since $-B_0=B_0$, contact or overlap is equivalent to
\begin{equation*}
 B_0\cap(\vct c+\mtx Q B_0)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \vct c\in Z(\mtx Q):=B_0\oplus\mtx Q B_0 .
\end{equation*}
For a compact set $S$ write $h_S(\vct n)=\sup_{\vct x\in S}\vct n^\top\vct x$
and $h_0=h_{B_0}$. The support of the sum $Z(\mtx Q)$ gives the signed
clearance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance}
\begin{aligned}
 g(\vct c,\mtx Q)
 &=\max_{\|\vct n\|=1}
   \{\vct n^\top\vct c-h_0(\vct n)-h_0(\mtx Q^\top\vct n)\},\\
 h_0(\vct n)&=L|n_1|+W|n_2| .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For $\vct c$ outside $Z$, the normal from its projection onto $Z$ to
$\vct c$ attains the distance. In the interior, the least supporting-plane
slack is the radius of the largest disk about $\vct c$ contained in $Z$,
so $g$ is negative. At contact $g=0$. For $B_0=\{\vct x:\mtx D\vct x
\le\vct k\}$ each support in \eqref{eq:distance} has the equivalent
dual form \cite{ZhangLinigerBorrelli2021}
\begin{equation*}
 h_0(\vct n)=\min_{\vct\ell\ge0,\ \mtx D^\top\vct\ell=\vct n}
                  \vct k^\top\vct\ell .
\end{equation*}

The same candidate input $\vct u$ must serve every member of the
observer set \eqref{eq:observer}. Evaluating \eqref{eq:distance} on
each member and taking the worst clearance gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
 G_t(h;\vct u)=\inf_{z\in\Theta_t}
 \underbrace{g(\vct c_z(t+h),\mtx Q_z(t+h))}_{g_{t,z}(h;\vct u)} .
\end{equation}
Each member in \eqref{eq:gap} admits its own normal in \eqref{eq:distance}.

\subsection{Directional Clearance Bound}\label{subsec:robust}
For the four ego corners $\vct E_i$ and four target corners $\vct T_j$
in inertial axes, the gap along a unit direction $\vct n$ is
\begin{equation*}
 \min_j\vct n^\top\vct T_j-\max_i\vct n^\top\vct E_i
 =\min_{i,j}\vct n^\top(\vct T_j-\vct E_i).
\end{equation*}
Thus it suffices to bound the corner \emph{differences}. On the joint
chart \eqref{eq:polytope} write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cornererror}
 \vct T_j-\vct E_i=\bar{\vct D}_{ij}
       +\mtx M_{ij}\vct\lambda+\vct e_{ij},\qquad
       \|\vct e_{ij}\|\le r_{ij}.
\end{equation}
Here $\mtx M_{ij}=\mtx M_{T,j}-\mtx M_{E,i}$ is the difference of
the two corner variations with respect to the same $\vct\lambda$.
Any common translation cancels.

\begin{lemma}[Directional clearance]\label{lem:directional}
Suppose $P_t$ is nonempty and \eqref{eq:cornererror} encloses all
compatible members. The robust clearance \eqref{eq:gap} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:certificate}
\begin{aligned}
 G_t(h;\vct u)&\ge\max_{\|\vct n\|=1}\min_{i,j} L_{ij}(\vct n),\\
 L_{ij}(\vct n)&=\vct n^\top\bar{\vct D}_{ij}
              -h_{P_t}(-\mtx M_{ij}^\top\vct n)-r_{ij}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For each support direction $\vct c'$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
 h_{P_t}(\vct c')=
 \min_{\vct\mu\ge0}\{\vct b^\top\vct\mu+
                         h_B(\vct c'-\mtx H^\top\vct\mu)\}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a unit $\vct n$, the smallest possible projected affine variation
in \eqref{eq:cornererror} is
\begin{equation*}
 \inf_{\vct\lambda\in P_t}\vct n^\top\mtx M_{ij}\vct\lambda
 =-h_{P_t}(-\mtx M_{ij}^\top\vct n).
\end{equation*}
The remainder contributes at least $-r_{ij}$. Each compatible member
therefore satisfies $\min_{i,j}\vct n^\top(\vct T_j-\vct E_i)
\ge\min_{i,j}L_{ij}(\vct n)$. Maximizing over $\vct n$ gives
\eqref{eq:certificate}. To evaluate its support, take any
$\vct\mu\ge0$ and $\vct\lambda\in P_t$. Then
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
 {\vct c'}^\top\vct\lambda
 &=\vct\mu^\top\mtx H\vct\lambda+
       (\vct c'-\mtx H^\top\vct\mu)^\top\vct\lambda\\
 &\le \vct b^\top\vct\mu+h_B(\vct c'-\mtx H^\top\vct\mu).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The right side is the support LP's dual objective after eliminating the
box multipliers. Strong LP duality gives \eqref{eq:dual} for every
nonempty bounded $P_t$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Terminal Set}
At the end of the prediction interval the controller needs a common
input that preserves a prescribed clearance $d_*>0$ thereafter.
Choose the constant terminal input $\vct u_f=[0,b_f]^\top$ with
$|b_f|<1$. At handoff let $\vct r_0=\vct p_T-\vct p_E$ and let
$\zeta$ collect the remaining physical state, parameters and actuator
state, omitting the common translation. Under $\vct u_f$ denote the
inertial reference-point displacements by $\vct w_E(h),\vct w_T(h)$.
These displacements and the rotations $\mtx R_E(h),\mtx R_T(h)$ depend
only on $\zeta$. The relative displacements for which the clearance
falls below $d_*$ at some future time form the set $\mathcal F(\zeta)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forbidden}
\begin{aligned}
 Z_h&=\mtx R_E(h)B_E\oplus[-\mtx R_T(h)B_T],\\
 K_h(\zeta)&=\vct w_E(h)-\vct w_T(h)+Z_h,\\
 \mathcal F(\zeta)&=\bigcup_{h\ge0}
                  \bigl(K_h(\zeta)\oplus\mathbb B^\circ_{d_*}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb B^\circ_{d_*}$ is the open disk of radius $d_*$ about
zero. Both terms in $Z_h$ use the same elapsed time.

\begin{definition}[Terminal set]\label{def:terminal}
Let $\mathcal A_f$ be the states admitting the handoff to $\vct u_f$ and
an admissible forward flow under $\vct u_f$. We define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminalset}
 \mathcal K_f=\{(\vct r_0,\zeta)\in\mathcal A_f:
                         \vct r_0\notin\mathcal F(\zeta)\}.
\end{equation}
A terminal information set is admissible if it is nonempty and
contained in $\mathcal K_f$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Terminal invariance]\label{lem:terminal}
Every state in $\mathcal K_f$ has clearance at least $d_*$ for all
future times under $\vct u_f$, and $\mathcal K_f$ is positively
invariant under that input.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At time $h$ the set of differences between ego and target material
points is $K_h(\zeta)-\vct r_0$. By \eqref{eq:forbidden}, exclusion
from $\mathcal F(\zeta)$ is equivalent to
$\operatorname{dist}(\vct r_0,K_h(\zeta))\ge d_*$ for every $h\ge0$.
After elapsed time $s$ the relative position and displacement sets satisfy
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
 \vct r_s&=\vct r_0+\vct w_T(s)-\vct w_E(s),\\
 K_{s+h}(\zeta)&=\vct w_E(s)-\vct w_T(s)+K_h(\zeta_s).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Thus $\operatorname{dist}(\vct r_s,K_h(\zeta_s))
=\operatorname{dist}(\vct r_0,K_{s+h}(\zeta))\ge d_*$.
Forward admissibility passes to the remaining flow, proving
$(\vct r_s,\zeta_s)\in\mathcal K_f$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Predictive Barrier Synthesis}
\subsubsection{Minimum Safety Deficit}
For a common input on $[t,t+T]$, let $X_t(h;\vct u)$ collect the
joint physical states reached from $\Theta_t$ at $t+h$, expressed in
the handoff coordinates of Definition~\ref{def:terminal}. Among inputs
admissible for every member, let $\mathcal U_t(T)$ consist of those
satisfying
\begin{equation*}
 G_t(h;\vct u)>0\quad(0\le h\le T),\qquad
 X_t(T;\vct u)\subseteq\mathcal K_f .
\end{equation*}
Strict separation is required throughout. A slack $\xi(h)$ measures
any shortfall from the larger design clearance $d_*$. The predictive
barrier value \cite{HuangWangMargellosGoulart2025} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barrier}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal V_t(T)=
 \inf_{\vct u\in\mathcal U_t(T),\,\xi}\quad
       &\int_0^T\xi(h)\,dh\\
 \text{subject to}\quad
       &G_t(h;\vct u)+\xi(h)\ge d_*,\\
       &\xi(h)\ge0,\qquad 0\le h\le T .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For fixed $\vct u$, minimizing over $\xi$ pointwise gives the least
slack $c_t$ and its total $D_t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deficit}
 c_t(h;\vct u)=(d_*-G_t(h;\vct u))_+,\quad
 D_t(\vct u)=\int_0^T c_t(h;\vct u)\,dh .
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathcal V_t(T)=\inf_{\vct u\in\mathcal U_t(T)}D_t(\vct u)$,
with value $+\infty$ for an empty feasible family. For a certified
lower bound $\ell_t\le G_t$ from \eqref{eq:certificate}, requiring
$\ell_t>0$ and using $(d_*-\ell_t)_+$ gives a sufficient clearance
condition and an upper bound on the deficit. By Lemma~\ref{lem:terminal},
a common input reaching $\mathcal K_f$ early can be extended to $T$ at
zero additional deficit.

\subsubsection{Cruise Tracking at Minimum Deficit}
To choose among inputs with the same minimum deficit, we derive the
nominal command from the path-tracking error.
Let $s_\Gamma$ be the arc coordinate on the prescribed path, $\theta$
the tangent angle, $k_\Gamma$ the path curvature, and $e_y$ the lateral
error. For course $\chi=\psi_E+\operatorname{atan2}(v_y,v_x)$ the
transverse kinematics on a regular path chart are
\begin{equation*}
 \dot s_\Gamma=\frac{\|\vct v\|\cos(\chi-\theta)}
                       {1-k_\Gamma e_y},\qquad
 \dot e_y=\|\vct v\|\sin(\chi-\theta).
\end{equation*}
At a realizable constant-curvature trim, $\|\vct v^\star\|=V_{\rm ref}>0$,
$r^\star=k_\Gamma V_{\rm ref}$ and
$\psi^\star_{\rm rel}=-\operatorname{atan2}(v_y^\star,v_x^\star)$.
In a local heading chart the transverse error is
\begin{equation*}
 \vct e_c=\bigl[e_y,\ \psi_E-\theta-\psi^\star_{\rm rel},\
               (\vct v-\vct v^\star)^\top,\ r-r^\star\bigr]^\top .
\end{equation*}
Linearize its dynamics to obtain $(\mtx A_c,\mtx B_c)$. For an
admissible local gain $\mtx K$ choose $\mtx P\succ0$ and $\alpha_c>0$ satisfying
\begin{equation*}
 (\mtx A_c+\mtx B_c\mtx K)^\top\mtx P+
 \mtx P(\mtx A_c+\mtx B_c\mtx K)\preceq-2\alpha_c\mtx P .
\end{equation*}
Differentiating $V_c=\vct e_c^\top\mtx P\vct e_c$ under
$\vct u^\star+\mtx K\vct e_c$ gives
$\dot V_c\le-2\alpha_c V_c+o(\|\vct e_c\|^2)$. For varying curvature
the corresponding inequality along a realizable reference includes
$\dot{\mtx P}$ when the metric is time varying. Denote the nominal
command forecast from the available ego information by $\bar{\vct u}_t$.

When the primary minimum is attained, we choose the control by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:secondary}
\begin{aligned}
 \min_{\vct u\in\mathcal U_t(T)}\quad&
 \int_0^T (\vct u-\bar{\vct u}_t)^\top\mtx W
                     (\vct u-\bar{\vct u}_t)\,dh\\
 \text{subject to}\quad&D_t(\vct u)=\mathcal V_t(T),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mtx W\succ0$. If $\bar{\vct u}_t$ attains the primary minimum,
its cost in \eqref{eq:secondary} is zero. We apply the selected input
until the next control decision.

\subsection{Recursive Feasibility}
\begin{theorem}[Feasibility and deficit decrease]\label{thm:shift}
Suppose the prior contains the true member, the encounter and measurement
models hold, and a feasible input exists at admission. Let the observer
satisfy \eqref{eq:observer} and the admissible input class be closed
under restriction and terminal concatenation. If a prefix of length
$0<\Delta\le T$ of $\vct u\in\mathcal U_t(T)$ is executed, then for
every $T'>0$ with $T'\ge T-\Delta$ there exists
$\vct u'\in\mathcal U_{t+\Delta}(T')$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:descent}
 D_{t+\Delta}(\vct u')\le D_t(\vct u)
                  -\int_0^\Delta c_t(h;\vct u)\,dh .
\end{equation}
Feasible replacements satisfying \eqref{eq:descent} preserve strict
separation on successive executed prefixes covering the encounter. If
the initial plan has zero deficit, their physical clearance is at least
$d_*$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $S=T-\Delta$. Keep the remaining input up to $S$ and extend it
with the terminal law,
\begin{equation*}
 \vct u'(h)=\begin{cases}
    \vct u(h+\Delta),&0\le h<S,\\
    \vct u_f,&S\le h\le T'.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
On $[0,S]$, uniqueness gives identical footprints for each surviving
member at matching physical times. Since
$\Theta_{t+\Delta}\subseteq\Theta_t$ by \eqref{eq:conditioning},
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
 G_{t+\Delta}(h;\vct u')
 &=\inf_{z\in\Theta_{t+\Delta}}g_{t,z}(h+\Delta;\vct u)\\
 &\ge \inf_{z\in\Theta_t}g_{t,z}(h+\Delta;\vct u)
 =G_t(h+\Delta;\vct u).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
At $S$, $X_{t+\Delta}(S;\vct u')\subseteq X_t(T;\vct u)\subseteq
\mathcal K_f$. Lemma~\ref{lem:terminal} preserves this membership
through $T'$ with clearance at least $d_*$. Thus $\vct u'$ is feasible,
and its deficit obeys
\begin{equation*}
 c_{t+\Delta}(h;\vct u')\le
 \begin{cases}
 c_t(h+\Delta;\vct u),&0\le h<S,\\
 0,&S\le h\le T'.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
Integration gives
\begin{equation*}
 D_{t+\Delta}(\vct u')\le\int_0^S c_t(h+\Delta;\vct u)\,dh
                         =\int_\Delta^T c_t(h;\vct u)\,dh ,
\end{equation*}
which proves \eqref{eq:descent}. For an attained old optimum the same
inequality holds for the new optimal value. Induction gives strict
separation along the true member. With zero initial budget the deficit
remains zero. A strict shortfall below $d_*$ on the true continuous
footprint motion would give a positive integral in \eqref{eq:deficit}.
\end{proof}

For the enclosure \eqref{eq:polytope}, retain the same chart, normal
and corner enclosures on the suffix. If new observations append rows
$(\mtx H_{\rm new},\vct b_{\rm new})$, the old multiplier in
\eqref{eq:dual} extends by zero,
\begin{equation*}
 \mtx H'=\begin{bmatrix}\mtx H\\ \mtx H_{\rm new}\end{bmatrix},\quad
 \vct b'=\begin{bmatrix}\vct b\\ \vct b_{\rm new}\end{bmatrix},\quad
 \vct\mu'=\begin{bmatrix}\vct\mu\\0\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
Then $(\mtx H')^\top\vct\mu'=\mtx H^\top\vct\mu$ and
$(\vct b')^\top\vct\mu'=\vct b^\top\vct\mu$. Tightening the box can
only reduce its support, so the old clearance bound still certifies the
same suffix budget. A change of chart requires certifying that budget
and terminal membership again.
Nominal control resumes once all compatible positions have left sensing
range. Admission to a subsequent encounter requires feasibility for the
new information set.

\subsection{Explicit Clearance and Terminal Certificates}
\subsubsection{Uncertain Translation}
Fix the relative orientation $\mtx Q$ in \eqref{eq:distance} and write
$Z=Z(\mtx Q)$. If the possible center displacements form a compact
convex set $C$ disjoint from $Z$, a closest pair of $C$ and $Z$
has a common supporting normal. In this case \eqref{eq:gap} reduces to
\begin{equation*}
 \operatorname{dist}(C,Z)=
 \max_{\|\vct n\|=1}\{-h_C(-\vct n)-h_Z(\vct n)\}.
\end{equation*}

\subsubsection{Multipliers from a Radar Chord}
Any nonnegative multiplier in \eqref{eq:dual} already yields an upper
support bound. To obtain one from a radar chord, let $Y$ be the
observed target position at the prediction epoch and
$d_0$ a reference course vector in complex coordinates. Set $p'=p_T-Y$
and $v'=e^{\mathrm i\gamma}-d_0$. For a past observation $Y_j$, let
$H_j$ be its signed reference chord from the current epoch and $\eta_j$
a bound on the chord error. For true arc parameters $(s',\kappa')$ and
reference parameters $(s,\kappa)$, \eqref{eq:clock} gives
\begin{equation*}
 |F_{\kappa'}(s')-F_\kappa(s)|
 \le |s'-s|+\tfrac12 s^2|\kappa'-\kappa|.
\end{equation*}
With $z_j=Y_j-Y-H_jd_0$ and $r_j=\epsilon_j+\eta_j$, the unit modulus
of the true course and \eqref{eq:disks} give the four real strip rows
$\|p'+H_jv'-z_j\|_\infty\le r_j$. For a future corner with reference
coefficient $G$ in \eqref{eq:orbit}, its affine error is $p'+Gv'$.
The remainder norm is bounded by the variation in $G$ over the chart.

For $H_j\ne0$, choosing $\omega=G/H_j$ expresses the future error in
terms of the past observation,
\begin{equation*}
 p'+Gv'=(1-\omega)p'+\omega(p'+H_jv').
\end{equation*}
Let $\mtx C_\omega$ represent multiplication by $\omega$ in real
coordinates. Taking the support of each term gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chorddual}
\begin{aligned}
 \vct n^\top(p'+Gv')&\le h_{B_p}(\mtx C_{1-\omega}^\top\vct n)
                    +\vct\nu^\top z_j+r_j\|\vct\nu\|_1,\\
 \vct\nu&=\mtx C_\omega^\top\vct n ,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $p'\in B_p$. The positive and negative parts of $\vct\nu$ are
nonnegative multipliers in \eqref{eq:dual}. For a fixed ego trajectory,
evaluating \eqref{eq:chorddual} at $-\vct n$ bounds the target
contribution subtracted in \eqref{eq:certificate}. When the ego pose
varies on the chart, \eqref{eq:cornererror} includes its variation
in the same $\vct\lambda$.

\subsubsection{Terminal Membership in Straight and Turning Encounters}
For collinear straight motion, let the ego travel ahead at trim speed
$V_*>0$ and let the target have initial speed $V_0$ and $A<0$. For
initial bumper gap $g_0$, integrating the positive closing speed gives
\begin{equation*}
 g_{\min}=g_0-\int_0^\infty(V_0+Ah-V_*)_+\,dh
         =g_0-\frac{(V_0-V_*)_+^2}{2|A|}.
\end{equation*}
Hence $g_{\min}\ge d_*$ certifies \eqref{eq:terminalset} for these
admissible motions, including when their spatial paths overlap.

For $\kappa\ne0$, \eqref{eq:orbit} bounds the future target footprint
by the disk of center $C=b_T+\mathrm i d/\kappa$ and radius
$R_{\rm orb}=1/|\kappa|+\max_{q\in B_T}|q|$. Fix a unit direction
$\vct n$ and put $H_n=\vct n^\top C+R_{\rm orb}$, using real
coordinates for the projection. Suppose each ego corner under $\vct u_f$
satisfies $\vct n^\top\vct E_i(h)\ge m_0+\alpha h-B_f$ for all
$h\ge0$, with $\alpha>0$, $B_f\ge0$. Straight trim along $\vct n$
satisfies this inequality with $\alpha=V_*$ and $B_f=0$. Setting
$H_*=(H_n+d_*-m_0+B_f)_+/\alpha$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff}
 \min_{i,j}\vct n^\top(\vct E_i(h)-\vct T_j(h))\ge d_*,
 \qquad h\ge H_* .
\end{equation}
Under these hypotheses, \eqref{eq:terminalset} therefore requires
clearance verification only on $[0,H_*]$ in addition to forward
admissibility.
