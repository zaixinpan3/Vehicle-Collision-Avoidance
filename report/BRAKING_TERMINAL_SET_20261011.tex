\paragraph{Status and scope.}
This report implements and measures the terminal set specified by the user
on October 11, 2026, in place of the CLF-tube terminal set of commit
\texttt{423e3e8} (unchanged at \texttt{710a228}, the baseline here). Past the
plan's endpoint both vehicles continue in the constant tangential
acceleration and sideslip family: the target with its own $(A_C,\beta_C)$,
the ego with the terminal law
\[
\kappa_f(z)=\bigl(\max(-a_b,-V_E/\Delta T),\ \beta_E\bigr),
\]
braking at $a_b$ with its sideslip held until it stops at a sample instant;
the terminal set keeps the states whose joint continuation stays
collision-free while the target is in range, with one closed-form check
after the ego has parked. The theory and the implementation are in
\path{controller/TERMINAL_SAFE_SET.md}; this report records the choices that
the specification leaves open or that the controller's declared model
forces, the checks, the pilot runs that shaped the anchor extension, the
paired campaign, and the decisions that remain the user's.

\paragraph{Result.}
The construction is exact where it is closed form: the continuation agrees
with an RK4 integration of the law to $10^{-9}$~m, the parked check with a
dense sampling of the target's track to $1.8\cdot10^{-7}$~m in 2917 random
cases, and $X_f$ is invariant under the law down to rest. In the paired
campaign against the CLF-tube set (the same 98 encounters) it recovers 9 of
14 exact encounters against 14 and 60 of 84 noisy ones against 53, with no
collision in either arm. It is the first terminal set to recover noisy
braking leads (8 of 12; every earlier variant lost all 12 at or near the
first frame), and it plans 16 holds at the first frame instead of 109, with
the 95th-percentile controller time per frame at 0.023~s against 0.174~s
(exact). Its losses have three mechanisms: $X_f$ always contains stopping, so
a plan that cannot certify cruising yields, and the declared model cannot
follow a yield to rest (four exact encounters end at 1.3--3.6~m/s); the law
is not a hold of the declared model, and in three noisy 15-m/s curved
crossings no declared-model hold keeps the endpoint in $X_f$ until the plan
runs out (hypothesis H3); and braking during a swerve meets the rear-adhesion
envelope in five noisy encounters. Fourteen noisy failures are numerical, as
are eighteen of the baseline's. The remaining design decisions are in
Section~\ref{sec:decisions}.

\section{The construction as implemented}
\paragraph{Terminal law and one-step map.}
The plan's state is the Fiala bicycle's $x=(p,\psi,v_x,v_y,r)$, reduced to the
family by $V_E=\lVert(v_x,v_y)\rVert$, $\beta_E=\operatorname{atan2}(v_y,v_x)$
and the course curvature $\kappa_E=r/V_E$. The speed falls by $a_bh$ per hold
($h=\Delta T=0.05$~s, $a_b=5$~m/s$^2$,
\path{terminal.brakingDecelerationMetersPerSecondSquared}) and by what is
left in the last one, so the ego stops at node $J=\lceil V_E/(a_bh)\rceil$
(61 nodes from 15~m/s). Both vehicles move on arcs of constant curvature with
a piecewise linear speed, so the joint one-step map $\phi$ and every
continuation pose are explicit functions of the endpoint
(\texttt{terminalSafeSet.continuation}); the unit test compares them with a
fine RK4 integration of the same kinematics (agreement to $10^{-9}$~m).

\paragraph{The set.}
$X_f$ holds an endpoint when the law is admissible there (the sideslip cone
of the problem, and $a_b$ with the held arc's lateral acceleration $rV_E$
inside the friction circle $\mu g$), every node, hold midpoint and the parked
pose of the continuation keeps the ego rectangle on the road, every node and
midpoint before the target's first sampled exit from the 50-m range clears
the target's forecast rectangle by the 0.2-m margin, and, if the target has
not left by the stop, the target's remaining track until it leaves the range
about the parked ego clears the parked rectangle by the same margin.

\paragraph{Invariance, maximality, recursive feasibility.}
The continuation of $\phi(z)$ is the remainder of the continuation of $z$ at
the same absolute times, so $X_f$ (with the absorbing end of the encounter) is
forward invariant under $\kappa_f$, and it is the largest set that $\kappa_f$
keeps safe. With exact information on the declared model (H1) and an
unchanged forecast (H2), the shifted plan keeps its endpoint at the same
absolute time and is therefore accepted at the next sample while it has holds
left; the step $\alpha=0$ of the step-size rule is that candidate. What keeps
the plan from running out is an extension by holds of the declared model that
keep the endpoint in $X_f$; whether one exists is hypothesis H3, checked
online and not proven, because the terminal law is not a hold of the declared
model (Section~\ref{sec:model}).

\section{Choices the specification leaves open}
\label{sec:choices}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The ego's curvature.} Sharma's relation $\kappa=\sin\beta/l_r$
presumes a rear axle without slip. For the Fiala state it is wrong at speed:
in the trim of a 4~m/s$^2$ left turn at 15~m/s the sideslip at the centre of
mass is $-0.0093$~rad, so that relation would continue the turn as a right
turn ($-0.0056$ against $0.0178$~1/m), about 6~m off the actual arc at the
end of the 22.5-m stop. The continuation holds the measured course curvature
$r/V_E$ with the sideslip, the same family with an effective
$l_r=V_E\sin\beta_E/r$.
\item \emph{Rectangles instead of a centre distance.} Every collision row of
the controller is a rectangle distance, and a centre distance $d$ covering two
4.8-by-1.9-m rectangles (5.4~m with the margin) would exclude a target
passing a parked ego in the next lane (3.66~m). The braking phase uses the
rectangle distance at the continuation's nodes and hold midpoints, the
instants at which the plan's own rows are sampled. After the stop, the
point-to-arc formula becomes vertex paths against edges: on a line each
vertex sweeps a segment, on a circle an arc about the circle's centre, and
the arc-to-segment distance has the candidates of the point-to-arc distance
(the foot point when the arc passes it, otherwise an end) plus the line
against the circle. Because the rectangles are apart at the stop and the
distance is continuous along the track, the least vertex-to-edge distance
over the swept stretch is the exact least distance.
\item \emph{The track's end under signed speed.} The repository's contract
(and the simulated truth) has signed speed: a target whose speed reaches
zero turns back and runs on beyond its starting point. The swept stretch is
therefore $[0,b]$ when the target leaves first, and $[a,\sigma^*]$ when it
turns at $\sigma^*=-V^2/(2A)$ inside the range and leaves at $a$ behind; the
specification's ``ends where the target stops or turns back'' holds for a
target that stays stopped, a different (hybrid) contract.
\item \emph{Road and admissibility.} $X_f\subset X$ requires the
continuation to stay on the road; it is checked at every continuation pose,
independently of the target. $\kappa_f\in U$ is read as the sideslip cone and
the friction circle at the endpoint (both only relax along the
continuation).
\item \emph{Terminal rows.} Problem (8)'s ``$\lVert e\rVert\ge d$ at the
$J_b$ continuation points'' becomes fixed-dual rectangle rows at the
continuation's nodes and midpoints before the exit, the parked pose's rows
against the target pose nearest it on its track (the derivative of a
minimum is that of its minimizer), road rows along the continuation, and,
where the anchor endpoint is outside it, the friction circle as a bound on
$|rV_E|$, linearized on the endpoint
correction through central differences of the closed form with the schedule
held. Conditions with more than 5~m to spare are left out; each row keeps up
to 5~cm of the margin it has at the anchor (Section~\ref{sec:pilot}); the exact set decides
acceptance. Where a pose is an exact contact (the parked witness of a track
crossing the parked rectangle), the ordinary-distance dual is not defined and
the witness is moved 1~mm or 1~cm along the track. The campaign's first
pass showed both needs: without the friction row the optimizer could not see
an inadmissible endpoint and returned a zero correction, and the contact
raised an assertion in two encounters. A friction row present at every
sample (second pass) then left the CLF stage's cone program without a
numerical result on the boundary of the circle in a hard recovery turn of
\path{clfNominalRecoveryTest} (15~m/s, curved road, 2.5~rad initial heading
error), so the row enters only where the endpoint is outside the circle.
\end{itemize}

\section{What the declared model forces}
\label{sec:model}
The terminal law is a certificate evaluated at the declared model's state,
not a hold of that model: the Fiala bicycle with steering and braking-ratio
inputs cannot brake while holding its sideslip and course curvature, its
domain ends at \texttt{scheduleSpeedFloor} (1~m/s), and its one RK4 step per
50-ms hold loses stability below about 2~m/s (the lateral eigenvalue is about
$-116/v_x$ s$^{-1}$; RK4 needs $|\lambda h|<2.78$). The shift with an unchanged
endpoint needs no hold of the law, but once the plan has shrunk to
\texttt{horizonSteps} every sample has to append a hold of the declared model
whose endpoint is in $X_f$.

\section{Pilot runs that shaped the anchor extension}
\label{sec:pilot}
Exact-state encounters (seed 0, 2000 frames, the campaign's settings) were run
on the working tree while the extension was designed; their raw outputs were
disposable and are summarized here.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Append the law's realization whenever the shifted plan is short}
(previous steering, braking ratio $-a_b/(\mu g)=-0.60$). The braking tail is
executed: later holds reach the first hold nearly unchanged, because the CLF
stage acts on the first successor and the correction of each hold is bounded
by the input trust region ($\pm0.125\,s$ in braking ratio, $s\le1$). The
exact 15-m/s braking lead fell to the 1-m/s floor while the lead still ran at
10~m/s, and the plan ran out (\texttt{anchorUnavailable}); the 8-m/s curved
crossing braked from 8 to about 1.3~m/s in 1.4~s.
\item \emph{Cruise hold if it keeps the endpoint in $X_f$, else the hold
nearest to it on the segment toward the realization} (bisection). The curved
crossing, the accelerating head-on and the curved head-on recovered; both
braking leads and the 8-m/s turning crossing did not. The endpoint sat on the
boundary of $X_f$ (least margin 0--9~mm): the exact runs integrate the plant
with \texttt{ode45}, not the declared RK4 map, and the appended hold departs
from the law, so the shifted endpoint fell out of $X_f$ (H1 and H3 fail by
millimetres) and the step-size search had to repair it.
\item \emph{Searching toward the hardest braking the rear adhesion admits}
first made it worse (the 8-m/s crossing failed as well): the extra braking
reaches the first hold.
\item \emph{Final form.} Toward the law's realization first and the hardest
admissible braking only if it fails; the terminal rows of the convex problem
keep up to 5~cm of the margin each condition has at the anchor (never tighter
than the anchor, so the zero correction stays feasible and the exact set
still decides acceptance). Shifted candidates fell out of $X_f$ in 2 frames
of the four pilot runs that completed (13 of 5025 exact frames in the
campaign), but the turning crossings still braked to the floor (the 8-m/s one
from 8 to 2.8~m/s in 1.45~s, its plan's endpoint at 1.24~m/s), where no
acceptable step exists. A latent bug surfaced there and was fixed: when the
full step's rollout leaves the model's domain, the re-linearization was built
on an empty anchor (\texttt{MATLAB:badsubscript}); the search now stops.
\end{enumerate}
The mechanism common to the failures is that $X_f$ always contains stopping:
when cruising is not certified the plan yields, and the declared model cannot
follow a yield to a stop.


\section{Checks}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Continuation.} \path{terminalSafeSetTest} compares the closed-form
poses at every node and hold midpoint with a fine RK4 integration of the
constant-$(A,\beta)$ kinematics under $\kappa_f$ (400 steps per half hold):
agreement to $10^{-9}$~m, and the speed reaches zero at the last node.
\item \emph{Invariance.} From a member, the successor under $\phi$ is a
member at every hold down to rest; its remaining stop and exit times are the
original ones less the elapsed holds, and its continuation poses are the
remainder of the original ones (to $10^{-9}$~m).
\item \emph{Parked check.} 2917 random cases (seed 11; 1141 sweeping their
whole circle, 1307 with speed and acceleration of opposite signs) compared
with the least rectangle distance over 200001 samples of the swept stretch
(spacing at most 1.05~mm): the closed form is never above the sampled minimum
(at worst $-6.3\cdot10^{-13}$~m), at most $1.8\cdot10^{-7}$~m below it, and the
witness pose reproduces it to $1.6\cdot10^{-12}$~m
(\path{parked-clearance-check.json}). An earlier version of this check
filtered sampled poses by a fixed 8-m reference distance and reported
spurious gaps of up to 0.47~m on whole circles; the minimizer lay farther
than 8~m from the parked ego.
\item \emph{Encounters, admissibility, rows, holds.} Unit cases for a lead
far and near, a stationary target beyond and within the stopping distance,
an oncoming target in the ego lane and in the next, a lead braking hard
(turns back into the parked ego) and gently (leaves the range first), a
target leaving the range during the braking, a circling target; the road,
the friction circle and the sideslip cone; the linearized rows (zero
correction feasible, the 5-cm reserve, first-order prediction of the binding
clearance to 5~mm); the anchor's cruise and braking holds.
\item \emph{Suite.} The full MATLAB suite passes, 606 of 606 tests on the committed code (\path{tests.txt}; 604 before, plus two retired terminal settings that the configuration must reject).
\end{itemize}

\section{Campaign}
The 98 encounters of the earlier reports (seven geometries at 8 and 15~m/s;
seed 0 with exact states and the \texttt{ode45} plant, six seeds with the
estimator in the loop) were run for the baseline and for this design. The
braking arm was run three times while the terminal rows were corrected:
without the friction row and the contact guard (exact 9 of 14, noisy 51 of
84; two encounters stopped on the contact assertion and were rerun after the
guard), with a friction row at every sample (9 and 50; a target-free recovery
test then failed, Section~\ref{sec:choices}), and with the committed code
(9 and 60), reported below. The baseline arm ran beside the first two passes
with identical outcomes and frame counts in all 98 encounters; the third pass
reran the braking arm alone with the same three sessions.

\paragraph{Failures.}
\path{failures.txt} lists every unrecovered encounter with the speed at its
last solved frame and the reasons of the step-size search.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Yield to low speed} (exact braking leads at 8 and 15~m/s, the
8-m/s crossing and turning crossing): the ego slows to 1.3--3.6~m/s
following the lead or waiting for the crosser, where the declared model has
no acceptable step (the 15-m/s lead's plan runs out at 1.3~m/s). The exact
15-m/s turning crossing ends at 6.6~m/s with the parked ego met by the
target's track.
\item \emph{The plan runs out} (three noisy 15-m/s curved crossings, at about
12~m/s after 1.4--1.8~s): for the last eight to ten solved frames no
declared-model hold kept the endpoint in $X_f$, the plan shrank one hold per
frame, and the shift became empty or unacceptable (H3 fails).
\item \emph{Braking in a swerve} (five noisy 8-m/s encounters: two turning
crossings, two accelerating head-ons and a head-on): full steps are rejected
on the rear-adhesion envelope (\texttt{handlingEnvelope}) or the law's
admissibility until no step is acceptable.
\item \emph{Numerical} (fourteen noisy encounters): five 8-m/s curved
crossings within their first 8 frames and three 8-m/s curved head-ons within
15, two 8-m/s curved head-ons after 79~s, two 8-m/s braking leads after
about 4~s (\texttt{pcbfNoNumericalResult}, 12), a 15-m/s braking lead after
5.5~s and an 8-m/s accelerating head-on (\texttt{clfNoNumericalResult}, 2).
The baseline has 18 such failures.
\item \emph{Other} (two noisy): a 15-m/s braking lead at 14.9~m/s after
3.2~s and an 8-m/s curved head-on after 79~s, both
\texttt{nonlinearAcceptanceFailed} with full steps accepted in their last
solved frames.
\end{itemize}
Eleven of the baseline's noisy braking leads fail within their first two
frames (numerical or time limit) and the twelfth at frame 14; with this set the first plan is 16 holds long at the
median.

\paragraph{Sessions.}
MATLAB's licensing service refused most session starts when one was started
per encounter (error 5001), and a long-running local service host had to be
restarted once; the campaign therefore runs three long-lived sessions per
arm. Every session that ran the controller, in both arms, reported a heap
corruption at exit (\texttt{free(): chunks in smallbin corrupted}) after its
last result had been written, and some then hung until stopped; this
predates the change.

\section{Decisions for the user}
\label{sec:decisions}
The construction is implemented as specified where the controller allows
it; the measured differences come from three properties of the rest of the
controller, each a design decision rather than a defect of the set.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The declared model cannot stop.} $X_f$ always contains
stopping, so when cruising is not certified the plan yields; the Fiala
RK4 model ends at 1~m/s and is unstable below about 2~m/s at one step per
hold. A low-speed regime of the declared model (a kinematic bicycle blended
in below a few m/s, or more integration steps per hold) would let the plan
follow a yield to rest, and below that speed the terminal law would be a
hold of the declared model, which would turn H3 into a property there.
\item \emph{Nothing asks the plan to pass.} The CLF stage acts on the first
successor and the horizon stays at \texttt{horizonSteps} (8 or 16 holds)
because $X_f$ is reached at once; the CLF-tube set forced plans of about 110
holds that contained the whole pass. A minimum horizon longer than the soft
prefix, or a progress or CLF term over the whole plan, would let the
optimizer plan passes inside the same terminal set.
\item \emph{The target contract after zero speed.} Under the signed-speed
contract a target that brakes to zero within range turns back into a
parked ego; a hybrid stop rule (predictor and simulated truth) would admit
stopping behind it.
\end{enumerate}
Smaller choices made here that the user may revisit: the braking rate
$a_b=5$~m/s$^2$; the friction-circle condition (binding in hard swerves at
15~m/s on the curve); holding the measured course curvature instead of
$\sin\beta/l_r$; the 5-cm reserve of the terminal rows; and keeping this set
as the production terminal set (it replaces the CLF-tube set in this commit;
the CLF-tube set is at \texttt{423e3e8}).
