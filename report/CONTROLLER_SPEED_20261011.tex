\paragraph{Status and scope.}
On October 11, 2026 the user asked for the controller to be made as fast as
possible. Control may be somewhat worse, and some extreme scenarios may fail,
but most ordinary scenarios should still succeed. The request followed two
measurements at commit \texttt{c2df2ea} (the low-speed model).
\begin{itemize}
\item Over ten exact encounters, building the problem took 44 percent of the
  controller's time, the two conic solves 26 percent and the step-size
  rule's checks 24 percent. The time grew with the plan length.
\item The frame at which an oncoming target appeared took 114--155~ms with
  everything warm, and about 1.1~s when it was also the session's first
  call.
\end{itemize}
This report records the changes, the checks that the rewritten parts leave
the problem and the decisions unchanged, a benchmark in which the target
appears after the ego has been driving, and a paired campaign against
\texttt{c2df2ea}. One rule tried along the way lost encounters and was
reverted (Section~\ref{sec:reverted}).

\paragraph{Result.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Time.} In the 98-encounter campaign with the estimator in the
  loop, the controller's time per frame falls from 15.8 to 8.2~ms at the
  median, from 136 to 41~ms at the 95th percentile and from 1.65 to 0.37~s
  at the maximum. Frames over 100~ms fall from 1510 to 81.
\item \emph{Outcomes.} The campaign recovers 14 of 14 exact encounters (13
  before) and 74 of 84 noisy ones (72), with no collision.
\item \emph{Target onset.} In a benchmark where the target appears after 2~s
  of target-free driving, the onset frame takes 26~ms at the median and
  72~ms at most (88 and 225~ms before). The session's first call, now made
  by a warm-up action at start-up, takes 0.73~s (0.95~s).
\item \emph{Cost in control quality.} Noisy encounters that both versions
  recover are confirmed recovered 0.10~s later at the median (later in 46
  of 70). This comes from the looser solver tolerances. With the old
  tolerances and every other change, recovery is confirmed as early as at
  \texttt{c2df2ea}, with the same outcome counts, but the time per frame is
  higher (95th percentile 50.6 against 43.3~ms, measured side by side).
\item \emph{Remaining losses.} They are unchanged and predate this step. The
  main one is the noisy curved head-ons (5 of 12 recovered, 3 before),
  whose safety stage is primal infeasible early in the encounter.
\end{itemize}

\section{Changes}
\subsection{The same problem, built faster}
The changes in this group leave the convex problem's feasible set and
optimum unchanged, and the step-size rule's decisions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Dense blocks.} Each inequality row is a small dense block on
  the few variables it involves: a node's state, or a hold's start state,
  input and slack. The sparse matrices are assembled in one call. The target
  forecast at all nodes and midpoints is one call, and so is the lane
  projection of all ego corners. Building the matrices row by row had cost
  time quadratic in the plan length.
\item \emph{Rows the boxes imply.} A row $a^\top z\le b$ whose largest
  value over the variables' boxes is below $b$ cannot bind, and it is left
  out. The boxes are the state box of the trust region and the input box at
  \texttt{trustMaximumScale}, which contains every box that the trust
  expansion uses. Most road rows, state-limit rows and collision rows far
  from the target go this way.
\item \emph{Collision duals not computed.} The test is the reference
  distance, less both vehicles' reaches, the margin and the largest
  enclosure tightening. Where this exceeds the most the pose can move over
  the boxes, the collision row cannot bind and its distance dual is not
  computed. The terminal set skips its continuation's duals and road rows
  by the same test on the continuation's sensitivity.
\item \emph{Trust expansion.} A larger trust scale reuses the formulated
  problem and changes only the input increments' bounds.
\item \emph{Step-size rule.} Every hold of a candidate is checked at once
  (state limits, handling, road, collision), with the first failing hold and
  the reason that a hold-by-hold scan would give. Exact rectangle distances
  are computed only where a lower bound from the reference distance is
  below the margin.
\item \emph{Trim.} The cruise trim is solved by Newton's method with the
  analytic tangent. \texttt{fsolve} remains only for the case where Newton's
  method does not converge.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Changes that alter results}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Solver tolerances.} The conic solves stop at $10^{-6}$ in
  feasibility and gap ($10^{-8}$ and $10^{-7}$ before). A stalled solve
  is accepted at $10^{-4}$ (\texttt{solver.reducedTolerance}, Clarabel's
  AlmostSolved; $10^{-5}$, the feasibility tolerance, before). A solve's
  point is a search direction, which the step-size rule checks on the
  nonlinear model before anything is issued, and the lexicographic and CLF
  ties are $10^{-4}$.
\item \emph{Target onset.} When a target appears that the previous plan did
  not know of (the previous sample had no target), the sample starts from a
  fresh potential-field rollout. It no longer starts from the shifted
  target-free plan. Before, that plan was extended by cruise holds until its
  endpoint reached the terminal set (62 holds in the profiled frame). It was
  then linearized, solved and found infeasible, and only then replaced by
  the rollout, which was linearized and solved again. The rule also fires
  when a target reappears after a complete scan without it. In the campaign
  that happened only when curved-road targets re-entered the range long
  after the encounter (7 frames, all after frame 1000). The simulated
  radar misses no detection, so a dropout during a maneuver is untested.
\item \emph{Warm-up.} \texttt{collisionAvoidanceController("warmUp", cfg)}
  runs one synthetic encounter and resets: lane following, then a head-on
  target that appears and is tracked. Every code path is then compiled and
  the kernels are loaded before the first sample. The validation driver
  calls it before each encounter.
\end{itemize}

\section{Checks}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Unit tests.} Full suite: 613 of 613 (\path{tests.txt}; 612
  before). One
  test is new, and two needed attention.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item New: at a target's onset the sample starts from the rollout, and
    the next sample uses the shifted plan.
  \item The enclosure test of \path{observerPredictiveControlTest} now
    places the target 20~m ahead instead of the scenario's 24~m. At 24~m
    the tightened collision rows cannot bind, the problem leaves them out,
    and the optimizations with the small and the large enclosure are
    identical. Before, the test passed only because the redundant rows
    changed the solver's path. At 20~m the rows bind, and both versions'
    first inputs differ between the two enclosures.
  \item The 15-m/s curved-road recovery from a displaced and reversed state
    (\path{clfNominalRecoveryTest}) failed once the implied rows were left
    out. At frame 7 the CLF stage stalled (Clarabel InsufficientProgress,
    primal residual $2.5\cdot10^{-5}$) and returned no point. The reduced
    tolerance accepts that solve.
  \end{itemize}
\item \emph{Formulation.} The anchors were 60 plans captured from six
  encounters, each formulated with and without synthetic enclosures (120
  problems). On them the block assembly reproduced the previous matrices to
  $4.4\cdot10^{-16}$. This check preceded leaving the implied rows out.
\item \emph{Step-size rule.} On 2880 candidate plans the batched check gave
  the same decision and reason as the hold-by-hold scan in every case.
\item \emph{Solver output.} The previous and the new solver ran on the same
  inputs and configuration in 210 frames captured from seven encounters.
  They had the same solve status and plan length in every frame, and equal
  objective values. Where the optimum is not unique the points differ, by up
  to $5.2\cdot10^{-3}$ in an input. In two frames the step-size rule then
  accepted a different step (1/32 instead of 1/16, and 1/16 instead of 1/8).
\end{itemize}

\section{Benchmark: a target that appears}
\paragraph{Setup.}
The 14 encounters are the campaign's seven geometries at 8 and 15~m/s,
with exact states and the declared model with RK4 holds as the plant. The
ego first drives 2~s (40 frames) on its lane without a target. Then the
target appears at the scenario's $t=0$ state. An encounter is recovered when
the transverse errors stay within the campaign's tolerances for 1~s after
the scenario's impact time, and it stops 400 frames after the onset. Before
the encounters the harness runs the warm-up encounter itself, so that it
also applies to \texttt{c2df2ea}; the cold start is its first call. Both
versions ran at the same time in two MATLAB sessions. Another session's job
was using about five of the sixteen cores.

\paragraph{Failures.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Both versions fail the 15-m/s accelerating head-on at the onset
  frame. The fresh rollout itself meets the target, and no step of its
  correction is accepted (\texttt{nonlinearAcceptanceFailed}).
\item \texttt{c2df2ea} also fails the 8-m/s braking lead
  (\texttt{clfNoNumericalResult} after 49 frames) and the 8-m/s crossing
  (\texttt{anchorUnavailable} after 27). It does not recover the 15-m/s
  braking lead in 400 frames.
\item This step fails the 8-m/s turning crossing (\texttt{anchorUnavailable}
  after 26 frames). From 0.9~s after the onset no hold keeps the endpoint
  in the set, and the plan runs out at 1.25~s. This is the extension
  hypothesis H3 failing, as in \texttt{c2df2ea}'s 8-m/s crossing.
\end{itemize}
Of this step's 34 frames over 50~ms, 32 are in the 15-m/s braking lead,
whose plans are 44 holds long at the median and up to 117 while it passes
the lead.

\paragraph{Outcomes.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Exact.} The 8-m/s curved crossing, whose plan ran out at
  \texttt{c2df2ea}, is now recovered.
\item \emph{Noisy, newly recovered (4).} Two seed-3 braking leads and two
  15-m/s curved head-ons.
\item \emph{Noisy, newly lost (2).} The seed-3 8-m/s head-on (frame 12)
  and the seed-5 8-m/s braking lead (frame 120), both
  \texttt{nonlinearAcceptanceFailed}. Both failing frames were re-solved
  offline from the saved inputs. The \texttt{c2df2ea} code also finds no
  acceptable step there, with the same safety slack (7.15~m and 35.6~m in
  the first round), and the old tolerances change nothing. The noisy
  trajectories leave \texttt{c2df2ea}'s within the first frames, because of
  solver-level differences: points where the optimum is not unique, and the
  tolerance. These two reach states that neither version can handle, and
  four others avoid states where \texttt{c2df2ea} fails.
\end{itemize}
\path{comparison.txt}, \path{campaign-runs.csv}, \path{failures.txt} and
\path{timing.txt} list every encounter.

\paragraph{Recovery time.}
Of the 70 noisy encounters that both arms recover, this step confirms
recovery later in 46, earlier in 13 and at the same frame in 11. The median
paired difference is $+0.10$~s. In the 13 exact encounters both recover it
is $+0.05$~s (7 later, 4 earlier, 2 the same). The largest lateral
deviation does not change systematically: the median paired difference is
0.00~m (noisy, 38 larger and 32 smaller).

\paragraph{The tolerances.}
To find the cause, this step was run once more with the old tolerances
($10^{-8}$ and $10^{-7}$; the reduced tolerance stays $10^{-4}$), side by
side with an identical rerun of this step. The rerun reproduced every
encounter's outcome and frame count.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Outcomes.} The old tolerances recover the same counts: 14 of 14
  exact and 74 of 84 noisy, with three noisy encounters gained and three
  lost against this step.
\item \emph{Recovery time.} Against \texttt{c2df2ea} the median paired
  difference is 0.00~s (noisy: 20 later, 22 earlier, 29 the same). Against
  this step it is $-0.10$~s (45 earlier, 13 later).
\item \emph{Time per frame}, noisy, the old tolerances against this step:
  median 9.5 against 8.9~ms, 95th percentile 50.6 against 43.3~ms, 99th
  percentile 79.1 against 75.2~ms. Frames over 50~ms number 1066 against
  839 (of 20640 and 23083 frames). Exact: median 7.4 against 6.9~ms, 95th
  percentile 24.9 against 21.4~ms.
\end{itemize}
So the looser tolerances buy about 6 percent at the median and 15 percent
at the 95th percentile, and cost about 0.1~s of recovery confirmation at the
median (0.25~s in the mean). They are kept, because the user accepted
somewhat worse control for speed. \path{tolerance/} and \path{recovery.txt}
have the comparison.

\paragraph{Slow frames.}
The time per frame still grows with the plan length: a line fitted to the
noisy arm's frames without a restart has 0.41~ms per hold and 4.2~ms at
zero. In the noisy arm, 63 of the 81 frames over 100~ms are long plans.
The other 18 are restarts: the shifted problem is infeasible at every trust
scale and is solved again from a fresh rollout (28 such frames, median
193~ms, at most 372~ms, all in 15-m/s braking leads). At \texttt{c2df2ea}
the 26 restart frames took 425~ms at the median and up to 1.65~s.

\section{A rule that was reverted}
\label{sec:reverted}
The first version of this step had one more rule, meant to avoid long
extensions. A shifted plan whose endpoint had left the terminal set got at
most \texttt{horizonSteps} cruise holds to return to it, and was otherwise
replaced by a fresh rollout before any solve.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Campaign.} The arm was run against the same \texttt{c2df2ea}
  arm. The rule fired in 120 frames of 37 noisy encounters, and 13 of
  those encounters failed. The arm recovered 68 of 84 noisy encounters (72
  for \texttt{c2df2ea}), with 7 newly lost and 3 newly recovered. The exact
  arm was identical to the final one.
\item \emph{Mechanism.} Two of the failing frames were re-solved offline.
  In both, the fresh rollout's problem was primal infeasible at every trust
  scale (Clarabel PrimalInfeasible), and nothing fell back to the shifted
  plan, which \texttt{c2df2ea} had extended and solved. In two of the seven
  lost encounters the steering also changed sign at the restart.
\end{itemize}
The rule was reverted. The shifted plan is again extended by cruise holds
until its endpoint is in the set, at most to the maximum horizon
(\texttt{maximumHorizonSteps}). The fresh rollout at a target's onset
was kept, because no maneuver is in progress then. The rule gained little
time. Relative to \texttt{c2df2ea} under the same load, the noisy 95th
percentile was 0.29 with it and 0.30 without it. \path{rejected-rule/} has
its comparison.

\section{Decisions for the user}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Tolerances.} $10^{-6}$ is kept for speed. Restoring $10^{-8}$
  and $10^{-7}$ (\texttt{solver.constraintTolerance},
  \texttt{solver.optimalityTolerance}) brings the recovery confirmation
  back to \texttt{c2df2ea}'s and gives up 6--15 percent of the time per
  frame.
\item \emph{Long plans.} The horizon ends where the plan reaches the
  terminal set, so its length, and the time with it, is not bounded below
  \texttt{maximumHorizonSteps}. A cap on the plan length, or a time budget
  per frame with a rule for what happens when it runs out, would bound the
  time. Either changes the algorithm.
\item \emph{The main remaining loss.} With the estimator in the loop, 7 of
  12 curved head-ons fail, mostly within 5--15 frames on
  \texttt{pcbfNoNumericalResult}. Three of them and a braking lead were
  re-solved offline from the saved inputs. In each, the safety stage is
  primal infeasible (certified by the solver) at every trust scale, from the
  shifted plan and from the fresh rollout alike: a hard row cannot be met,
  not a numerical breakdown. It predates this step and is the next
  candidate.
\end{itemize}

  \texttt{runSpeedBenchmark(<source root>, <output.json>)}, for a

  \texttt{c2df2ea}; copies of the working tree, with and without the reverted
