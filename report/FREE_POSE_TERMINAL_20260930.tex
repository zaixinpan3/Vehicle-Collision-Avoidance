\section{Result and precise scope}
All fourteen unchanged collision-threatening scenarios now complete their
160-hold, eight-second closed-loop simulations. Independent Python rectangle
checks confirm strictly positive sampled clearance and zero predicted safety
slack in every issued plan. The minimum replay body clearance is
\textbf{0.093552 m}; the collision margin remains 0.006 m. No road constraints,
scenario changes, target-departure assumption, additional controller mode or
runtime backup selector were introduced.

The immediately preceding recorded implementation at
\path{bd0fcfca4938409e60bfb786ab1d6d07ad31c5d0} completed ten cases. Its four
curved cases failed at initialization. The new implementation completes those
four as well. Configuration, target trajectory, given-path collision baseline
and random-seed records match exactly between the two campaigns.

The maximum \emph{running} controller-call wall time is \textbf{0.216044 s},
excluding initialization, versus the previous recorded 0.950391 s. Mean and
median running times are 30.743 and 22.038 ms. However, 484 of 2,226 running
calls exceed the 50 ms control period. Maximum initialization time is
1.022066 s. This is a measured improvement, not a real-time deadline guarantee.

The successful criterion here is collision avoidance and original hard
constraint admission, not asymptotic path recovery. The retained
first-feasible stopping rule can choose a displaced straight continuation:
the largest final lateral error is \textbf{30.641 m}, compared with 3.354 m
among the ten previously completed cases. This material tracking tradeoff is
reported alongside safety and timing, rather than hidden by a nominal recovery
controller. Original-path tracking remains a soft search preference.

\section{The structural defect}
Removing explicit road-edge inequalities did not remove the old terminal
requirement to rejoin the given path and cruise on it indefinitely. Free
longitudinal phase relaxed arrival position, but preserved the entire cruise
orbit. In the curved fixtures that orbit has radius 200 m. Changing phase
cannot separate intersecting whole-orbit envelopes, so the old curved
separation row had a negative constant margin and zero phase sensitivity.
Smaller trust regions or more optimizer iterations cannot repair that row.

There are two distinct effects. Comparing complete spatial sweeps is a
conservative sufficient collision test. In addition, the curved head-on target
actually keeps returning around the same circle in the opposite direction
under the unchanged motion model. Merely replacing spatial separation with a
finite time-aligned scan would not justify permanent cruising on that circle.
The controller must be allowed to choose a different admissible continuation.
No scenario-specific claim that the target will depart is needed.

A related computational restriction was unnecessary pose regulation at the
end of the completion tail. Even with free longitudinal phase, lateral
position and heading still had to settle into a very small lane-centered
ellipsoid. These are not physical actuator or tire requirements when road
constraints are absent. They caused long completion rollouts and difficult
endpoint correction. Finally, the same reference object served both terminal
membership and the nominal lane CLF. A terminal change would have silently
changed the tracking objective unless those references were separated.

\section{One invariant core, freely placed}
Let the augmented state be $y=(p,\psi,v_x,v_y,r,u^{-})\in\mathbb R^8$,
where $u^{-}$ supplies input memory for slew limits. The defining nominal
transition is the existing nonlinear Fiala bicycle held-input RK4 map,
$F_h(y,u)=(f_h(x,u),u)$.

For a planar rigid transform $g=(p_g,\theta_g)$, let $T_g$ rotate and translate
position, add $\theta_g$ to yaw, and leave body velocities and inputs unchanged.
With no spatially varying dynamics or road constraints,
\[
F_h(T_g y,u)=T_g F_h(y,u).
\]
Thus an admissible invariant neighborhood around straight cruise can be
transported to every world position and heading using exactly the same metric,
feedback gain, contraction bound, input limits and slew proof. This is one
family generated by symmetry, not a bank of controller modes.

The existing eight-dimensional core construction bounds the nonlinear RK4
closed-loop error map $\Phi$ using $P=R^TR\succ0$, radius $r_B$, contraction
$\gamma<1$ and center defect $d$:
\[
\sup_{\|Re\|\le r_B}\|R D\Phi(e)R^{-1}\|\le\gamma,
\quad\|R\Phi(0)\|\le d,
\quad\gamma r_B+d<r_B.
\]
It also checks actuator magnitude, slew and model-domain bounds. These
conditions concern the declared numerical map. The implementation retains
its interval-enclosure arithmetic and numerical limitations; a finite sample
of closed-loop states is not substituted for the contraction construction.

\subsection{Analytical elimination of three pose coordinates}
Partition the moving-frame error as $e=(a,z)$, with pose error $a\in\mathbb R^3$
and intrinsic error
\[
z=(v_x-v_{\mathrm{ref}},v_y,r,u^{-}-u^r)\in\mathbb R^5.
\]
Completing the square in the original metric gives
\begin{align}
G&=-P_{aa}^{-1}P_{az}, &
S&=P_{zz}-P_{za}P_{aa}^{-1}P_{az},\\
e^TPe&=(a-Gz)^TP_{aa}(a-Gz)+z^TSz.
\end{align}
Every local pose error can be realized by reference translation and rotation.
Therefore ego-only membership in the union of transported cores has the exact
reduced condition
\[
\boxed{\|\operatorname{chol}(S)z\|_2\le r_B.}
\]
The minimizing pose error is $a=Gz$, and its reference placement is recovered
without optimization variables:
\[
\theta_g=\psi-a_3,\qquad p_g=p-R(\theta_g)a_{1:2}.
\]
The local yaw chart is sufficient for core admission. The implementation uses
this five-dimensional cone and its intrinsic endpoint correction, then checks
the original full eight-dimensional membership at the reconstructed pose.
Input memory remains in both formulations. There is no coordinate relaxation
in the ego-only membership test, no pose grid and no fixed terminal destination.

Target separation still has to be checked for the reconstructed pose. Choosing
the minimizing pose is a sufficient selection; it does not search every pose
that might satisfy membership and target separation simultaneously. The
construction is consequently not a complete viability kernel.

\subsection{Known-target separation without a departure prior}
Straight target motion uses the existing extrema of relative quadratic
progress, now including the derivative of arbitrary ego heading. For a
turning target, its entire future body orbit lies inside a disk of center
$c_T$ and outer radius $R_T$. The chosen ego reference moves along the ray
$p_g+v_g t$, $t\ge0$. A sufficient margin is
\[
\min_{t\ge0}\|p_g+v_gt-c_T\|-R_T-b_E-\epsilon_p-d_{\min},
\]
where $b_E$ encloses the ego rectangle and $\epsilon_p$ bounds its terminal
position deviation at the certified numerical sample times. The minimizer is
\[
t_* = \max\{0,-(p_g-c_T)^Tv_g/\|v_g\|^2\}.
\]
The ray begins at the completion endpoint. Before that endpoint, collision
constraints continue to compare the actual predicted rectangles at matching
absolute times. The ego can therefore cross a target's orbit during a safe
interval and subsequently leave it, even if the target circles forever.
The infinite future is not truncated or ignored.

Where the active branch is differentiable, its pose gradient is composed with
the analytic fitted-pose Jacobian. This gives SCvx sensitivity to terminal
position, heading, body velocities and input memory. Nonlinear evaluation
rechecks the full separation condition after each candidate change. The new
row is no longer the old phase-independent negative constant.

\subsection{Recursive feasibility and a single execution rule}
A feasible solution consists of the MPC prefix, a hard finite completion,
and a selected placed core whose indefinite continuation passes target
separation. Under the nominal successor assumption, remove the first input
and retain the remaining sequence. When an appended input is needed, the
same core's feedback supplies it; fixed-placement invariance supplies
successor membership and admissibility. The already certified future
separation covers this suffix. Input memory includes the appended slew
constraint. This proves existence of a successor feasible candidate under
the fixed model, clock, target and constraint assumptions.

The selected pose is held fixed for this shift argument. A new search may
select another pose only after rechecking all original constraints. A shifted
sequence is an initialization of the same MPC problem and passes the common
nonlinear admission test. There is no solver-failure branch that issues an
unverified old control, nor a separate nominal-versus-backup selector. The
first fully admissible initialization or search candidate returns immediately;
positive restoration slacks never authorize execution.

\section{Implementation and validation}
The change is confined to three production files:
\begin{itemize}
\item \path{terminalContinuation.m}: Schur metric, analytic fitted pose and
its Jacobian, straight-ray target-orbit bound and straight-target pose derivative.
\item \path{solvePredictiveControl.m}: intrinsic terminal cone/correction,
consistent nonlinear pose reconstruction, current-problem warm-start admission,
and seed completion using the same transported core. Flow initialization,
its input-proximal regularization and collision linearization remain shared.
\item \path{collisionAvoidanceController.m}: separate the original-path
nominal reference from the straight terminal core; carry the selected pose
in state format 57. No new configuration switch was added.
\end{itemize}
The nine-file core-source budget is unchanged. No road, tire, actuator,
collision-buffer, target-motion or solver-budget setting was relaxed. The
existing seed preferences remain initialization heuristics; they are not new
hard constraints or independent safety guarantees.

\section{Limits and research basis}
A physically avoidable scenario can still be missed by local optimization,
a finite seed budget, a finite completion length or the sufficient terminal
certificate. Passing these fourteen fixtures does not prove global solver
completeness. The mathematical argument concerns the nominal fixed-epoch RK4
model, while ODE replay is independent finite-sample evidence. Neither
provides a continuous-time uncertain-plant certificate.

The first-feasible policy protects the requested avoidance priority and avoids
extra optimization after admission, but it does not enforce lane-CLF decay.
For example, curved head-on at 15 m/s finishes 30.641 m from the path, and
straight crossing at 15 m/s finishes 20.328 m away. Safety success must not
be reported as nominal recovery or road-driving suitability. A different
tracking requirement would need to be expressed within the same optimization
problem, with its computational and feasibility consequences evaluated.

Artificial terminal reference selection is related to Limon, Alvarado, Alamo
and Camacho, \emph{MPC for tracking piecewise constant references for constrained
linear systems}, Automatica 44(9), 2382--2387 (2008),
\href{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2008.01.023}{doi:10.1016/j.automatica.2008.01.023}.
Only its indexed publisher abstract was consulted. Luque, Chanfreut, Limon and
Maestre, \emph{Model predictive control for tracking with implicit invariant
sets}, Automatica 179, 112436 (2025),
\href{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2025.112436}{doi:10.1016/j.automatica.2025.112436},
provides linear-system implicit-terminal and shift arguments in Theorems 2--3.
Those theorems are not claimed to certify the nonlinear Fiala model. The
rigid-symmetry transport and Schur elimination above are project derivations
applied to its existing augmented nonlinear core.
