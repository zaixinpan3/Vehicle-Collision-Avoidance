### Direct horizon bounds and their limits

A horizon selected once per decision needs a certificate if it is to exclude
insufficient terminal-reaching length as a cause of infeasibility. Constant
target tangential acceleration and sideslip do not supply that certificate.
They specify target evolution; they do not establish joint constrained
reachability under one admissible output-feedback law.

There are three distinct questions:

1. Whether a safe plan reaches $Z_f$ within $N$ holds in (D4).
2. Whether the executed plan preserves a feasible safe continuation after the
   next received sensor block.
3. Whether the particular linearization, bounds and numerical solver of one
   SQP-RTI QP return a feasible step.

A sufficient length for the first question does not establish the other two.
With a shift-compatible terminal certificate, the exact terminal-reaching
feasibility domains satisfy $\mathcal F_N\subseteq\mathcal F_{N+1}$, since a
feasible plan can be padded by $\alpha_f$. In contrast, remove the terminal
condition and let $\mathcal P_N$ denote feasibility of only the prefix's hard
constraints. Then $\mathcal P_{N+1}\subseteq\mathcal P_N$: truncation proves the
inclusion. A short prefix may admit a command that leads to future failure,
but increasing the horizon at that later state need not restore feasibility.
These are statements about the exact problems with the same contracts and
policy class, not monotonicity of QPs with different nominal trajectories.

**A sufficient direct bound.** Suppose a certified domain $D$ contains
$\mathcal I$ and a fixed admissible finite-memory policy $\alpha_b$ is available.
Assume every complete hold under this policy obeys $\mathsf H$ and the clearance
condition, every successor remains in $D$, and there are a nonnegative function
$V$ and a uniform constant $\varepsilon>0$ such that

$$
\{s\in D:V(s)=0\}\subseteq Z_f,\qquad
V(s^+)\le \max\{V(s)-\varepsilon,0\}
\quad\text{for every }(s^+,Y,\gamma)\in\mathscr G_{\alpha_b}(s).
\tag{D6}
$$

The condition includes successors from $V=0$; early-arriving branches must
remain terminal while other branches are still approaching. Neither a
hidden-state policy choice nor a branch-dependent unobserved switching time
is allowed. One common $\alpha_b$ processes all admissible measurements using
the declared memory coordinates. A more general backup can be used only if
its switching and policy representation obey the same causal requirements.

If $\overline V(\mathcal I)=\sup_{s\in\mathcal I}V(s)<\infty$, then

$$
\overline N(\mathcal I)
=\max\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{\overline V(\mathcal I)}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil\right\}
\tag{D7}
$$

is sufficient for (D4), allowing terminal padding if a larger minimum horizon
is used. Indeed, induction gives
$V(s_j)\le\max\{V(s_0)-j\varepsilon,0\}$ for every admissible branch.
At $j=\overline N$, every branch lies in $Z_f$, and all preceding holds are safe
by the backup certificate. This proves policy feasibility for exact reachable
sets. If (D4) restricts the enclosure family, the required hold, successor and
terminal certificates must also be representable in that family; an enclosure
that protrudes outside $Z_f$ can otherwise make its transcription infeasible.
Computing an upper bound on $\sup V$, together with
a certified lower bound on $\varepsilon$, is sufficient; optimistic numerical
rounding is not. If the resulting length exceeds a resource cap, report that
the certificate cannot be represented within that cap rather than silently
clipping the bound and preserving its guarantee.

This is a sufficient construction, not a claim that $V$, $\varepsilon$, $D$, or
such a backup have already been constructed for this vehicle. Exponential
convergence to an exact terminal point does not imply finite-time arrival.
A bounded initial set alone does not imply a uniform finite arrival time.
No restriction to braking or stopped targets follows from (D6); any admitted
motion must be included when checking it. The optional component below does
not establish a general bound for entering that component from arbitrary
initial information.

An offline integer-rank certificate is an alternative: store an upper bound
on safe terminal-reaching holds together with the compatible causal policy
and its validity set. A lookup is justified only when it covers the complete
current information set with a common implementable policy. Taking the maximum
of independently designed hidden-state controllers' ranks is insufficient.
A missing certificate means that no bound has been established, not that the
physical encounter is necessarily impossible.

**Reuse after initialization.** If an actually executed, certified feasible
plan has $L$ remaining stages at the current decision, conditioning as in (D5)
leaves a certified tail of length $L-1$ at the next decision. Set
$N^+=\max\{N_{\min},L-1\}$ and append terminal policies if needed; no search over
window lengths is required. A newly accepted plan may change the stored length
only if its terminal inclusion and complete continuation are established.
An empty tail starts inside $Z_f$. The initial certificate is the difficult
step; shifting does not manufacture one.

**RTI qualification.** In exact arithmetic, a feasible nominal point of the
current finite NLP makes $\Delta w=0$ feasible for its ordinary SQP linearization
if all increment bounds contain zero, no incompatible strict tightening is
added, and the same feasible lifted states and witnesses are retained. This
explains how a valid shifted anchor can avoid artificial QP infeasibility.
It does not prove that a nonzero QP step satisfies the original nonlinear
constraints. Nor does existence of a nonlinear feasible point imply feasibility
of a QP at an arbitrary seed: $1-z^2\le0$ has feasible points $z=\pm1$, but its
linearization at $z=0$ is $1\le0$. Increasing a prediction length does not repair
such a contradictory local row. This need not rely on a zero gradient:
with $-2\le z\le2$ and $1-z^2\le0$, the seed $\bar z=1/4$ requires
$\Delta z\ge15/8$ from the linearized inequality, while the upper bound
requires $\Delta z\le7/4$. The nonlinear point $z=3/2$ is feasible.
Finite solver tolerances, new hypothesis representations and changed nominal
trajectories also require explicit checks.

The current unverified finite-scenario RTI deployment supplies neither (D6)
nor a retained certified terminal-reaching anchor. A sampled conflict-time
heuristic, repeated trials of longer horizons, or a fixed conservative-looking
number cannot be labeled (D7). The requested guarantee therefore remains an
unimplemented certificate requirement, rather than an enabled runtime option.
