\section{Changes}
At the user's direction every LQR element is removed. The design is
optimization under a CLF constraint; it has no feedback law.

\paragraph{CLF matrix.} \texttt{nonlinearBicycleModel.cruise} previously took
$P$ in $V=e^\top Pe$ from \texttt{dlqr}, with two input weights in
\texttt{cfg.clf}. $P$ is now synthesized by a linear matrix inequality, solved
with YALMIP/SeDuMi from \path{solver/}. For every $e$ of the sampled local
transverse model some input must give the full decrease
$V^+-V\le-e^\top Qe$. With $S=P^{-1}$ and $Y=KS$ this is
\[
\begin{bmatrix}S&(AS+BY)^\top&SQ^{1/2}\\AS+BY&S&0\\Q^{1/2}S&0&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq0,
\]
and among the feasible $P$ the least steep relative to $Q$ is chosen
(maximize $\gamma$ with $S\succeq\gamma Q^{-1}$). $K$ only certifies that the
decrease can be attained; it is discarded. The controller still requests
$\eta=0.5$ of the certified decrease. The two input weights are deleted. Each
operating point is solved once, in 0.1--1~s, and cached.

\paragraph{Ancillary feedback.} The stagewise Riccati gains are deleted. Under
an observer enclosure the ego uncertainty tube now propagates open loop,
$G_{i+1}=A_iG_i$. The shifted plan is rolled out with its own inputs, without a
feedback correction. The controller state no longer stores gains, and its
version is 76.

\paragraph{Tests and scripts.} The nonlinear decrease checks in
\texttt{nominalClfTest} and \texttt{validateNominalClf} used the LQR gain as a
witness. They now use the input that minimizes the nonlinear successor value. A
new test checks that no gain is stored and that $P\succeq Q$. The LQR option of
\path{scripts/studyTerminalHorizonReduction.m} is removed.

\section{A first choice of $P$ was wrong}
The first version certified only the requested fraction $\eta e^\top Qe$. The
least steep such $P$ satisfies $P\succeq\eta Q$ with equality in one direction:
it lies on the edge of feasibility. Near the path the decrease then needed
inputs outside the trust region. Four \texttt{clfNominalRecoveryTest} cases
(0.1~m lateral error) kept a CLF slack of $1.2\times10^{-3}$ (limit $10^{-5}$).
The exact 8-m/s brakingLead run stopped at hold 119. Certifying the full
decrease restores the reserve that the LQR identity had provided:
$P/Q$ has eigenvalues 1.02--32 against 1--25 for the former LQR matrix.

\section{The noisy collision}
The noisy 15-m/s brakingLead run collides at hold 185. Before this change it
stopped at hold 63 without a solution. In the first-version run, which shows the
same collision at hold 102, the estimator placed the target's acceleration at
$+0.1$~m/s$^2$ against a true $-0.5$~m/s$^2$. The target position error was
0.45--0.48~m, above the 0.20~m planning margin. The estimated gap fell below
0.2~m from 4.65~s, while the slack on the observer tightening grew from 0.3 to
1.7~m (\path{collisionDiag.m.txt}).

Two counterfactual runs of this case separate the two changes:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
variant & outcome & holds\\
\midrule
new $P$ (first version), Riccati gains kept & no solution, no collision & 62\\
LQR $P$, open-loop tube & collision & 180\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The collision follows the open-loop tube, not the CLF matrix. The open-loop
tube is larger. In the noisy formulation the observer tightening is soft, so
the larger tightening is absorbed by slack rather than reported as infeasible,
and the controller continues into contact. The counterfactual logs are
\path{counterfactual-onlyP.log} and \path{counterfactual-onlyTube.log}.

\section{Open decision}
With the Riccati gains removed, soft observer tightening turns into a
collision in this case. The options are: make the observer tightening hard
(report no solution instead), restore some feedback to contract the tube, or
record the collision as a known risk. The user has not yet chosen.
