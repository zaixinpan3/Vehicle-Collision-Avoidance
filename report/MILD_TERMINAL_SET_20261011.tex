\paragraph{Status and scope.}
The user specified the terminal set's ingredients on October 11, 2026. The
set holds only mild states, which are written into its definition. Inside
it the ego is the simplest vehicle under the simplest law, not necessarily
the Fiala bicycle, because a terminal state should involve no aggressive
maneuver. In a mild state the mismatch between this simple model and the
vehicle's dynamics need not be considered. This report implements that set
on top of commit \texttt{f94a0e2} (braking ends at rest). It records the two
changes to the plan's extension that the set required, the checks, and a
paired campaign against \texttt{f94a0e2}. The vehicle model's 1-m/s floor is
unchanged here; a low-speed regime is the next step.

\paragraph{Result.}
The exact campaign recovers 13 of 14 encounters (9 before) and the noisy
campaign 73 of 84 (54 before), the best of every variant so far, with no
collision. Noisy braking leads recover 11 of 12 (3 before): the plan now
passes the lead instead of following it to the speed floor. Plans brake
in their tail in 40 frames instead of 1449. The plan at the first frame is
24--25 holds long instead of 16, and the controller's 95th-percentile time
per frame rises from 0.046 to 0.108~s (noisy). The losses are numerical:
noisy curved head-ons fall from 7 to 3 of 12, mostly because the
conic solver of the safety stage returns no result early in the encounter.

\section{The set}
\paragraph{Mildness.}
A state is mild when its sideslip lies in the problem's cone and the held
arc's lateral acceleration is at most
$a_m=2$~m/s$^2$
(\texttt{terminal.lateralAccelerationMaximumMetersPerSecondSquared}):
$|rV|\le a_m$. The terminal set holds only mild endpoints, so an endpoint
in the middle of a swerve is not terminal and the plan has to settle first.

\paragraph{Simplest vehicle and law.}
Inside the set the ego is a kinematic bicycle with its steering held,
braking at $a_b=4$~m/s$^2$ to rest (5~m/s$^2$ before). With the steering
held, the kinematic bicycle keeps its sideslip and its course curvature. The
closed-form continuation of \texttt{f94a0e2} is therefore exactly this
vehicle's motion, and the target keeps its forecast as before. Braking at
4~m/s$^2$ with 2~m/s$^2$ of lateral acceleration uses 54 percent of the
friction ($\mu g=8.3$~m/s$^2$), and the configuration requires
$\sqrt{a_b^2+a_m^2}<\mu g$. Along the continuation the sideslip is constant
and $|rV|=|\kappa|V^2$ only falls, so mildness is kept.

\paragraph{The user's assumption.}
In a mild state the declared model is taken to follow the kinematic model.
No bound on the mismatch is computed. Under this assumption the law's hold
is a hold of the declared model, so the extension hypothesis H3 of the
recursive-feasibility statement follows
(\path{controller/TERMINAL_SAFE_SET.md}, Section 6). The implementation does
not rely on the assumption: every appended hold is a hold of the declared
model and is kept only when its endpoint is checked to be in the set.

\section{What the set required of the plan}
The 15-m/s curved-road recovery test from displaced and reversed states
(\path{clfNominalRecoveryTest}) failed with the set alone. It exposed two
mechanisms, each fixed and then checked over 1500 frames from both of the
test's initial states.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{An endpoint pinned on the boundary.} The mildness condition
  entered the convex problem only where the endpoint violated it. An
  endpoint inside the set was pushed out by every full step, which the
  exact check rejected; the step size fell to 0--1/16, and the vehicle
  executed its old tail. The mildness row now enters within 1~m/s$^2$ of
  the bound and keeps up to 0.1~m/s$^2$ of the margin the endpoint has, like
  the collision rows' 5-cm reserve.
\item \emph{Mildness kept by braking.} A plan shorter than
  \texttt{horizonSteps} is extended by a hold that keeps the endpoint in the
  set. When the cruise hold did not, the only such hold was braking,
  because braking lowers $|rV|$ quadratically. Two cases showed it. During a
  20-m lane recovery at 15~m/s, the endpoint 0.8~s ahead was still in the
  turn, and the plan braked from 15 to about 4~m/s. In steady cornering,
  the unregulated tail of the plan drifted (the CLF stage acts on the first
  successor; the drift exists at \texttt{f94a0e2} as well), its cruise hold
  stopped being mild, and the braking that kept it mild drifted it further.
  The extension now first follows the startup guidance (potential guidance
  with a target, path guidance without one) through states outside the
  set until the set is reached again. Each guided hold meets the hard rows
  and clears the target at its midpoint and endpoint. The result is a longer
  plan that completes the maneuver. Only if guidance does not reach the set
  does the extension fall back to a staying hold, searched toward holding
  the previous steering: at the cruise braking ratio first, then braking at
  $a_b$, then braking as hard as the rear adhesion admits.
\end{enumerate}
With both changes, both of the test's initial states return to the lane at
15~m/s (the 20-m displacement without braking) and cruise steadily on the
curve to frame 1500, with no braking in the plan's tail.

\section{Checks}
\begin{itemize}
\item Full suite: 608 of 608 (\path{tests.txt}). The terminal-set tests use
  4~m/s$^2$ (stop after 2~s and 8~m from 8~m/s). They check that an arc with
  2.4~m/s$^2$ of lateral acceleration is not mild although it is well
  inside the friction, and that one with 1.6~m/s$^2$ is. They also check
  that the configuration rejects a braking rate and a lateral acceleration
  that together exceed the friction.
\item Recovery probes (both initial states of
  \path{clfNominalRecoveryTest} at 15~m/s on the curve, 1500 frames): no
  failure, no braking in the tail in steady cruise.
\end{itemize}

The guided extension was used in 637 of 25566 frames (one hold at the
median, at most 123). Encounters that both arms recover (8 exact, 48 noisy)
are confirmed sooner: median 9.6 against 10.8~s (exact) and 9.7 against
11.3~s (noisy). Their median largest lateral deviation is 5.5 against
5.0~m (exact) and 7.3 against 5.9~m (noisy).

\paragraph{Braking leads.}
Every recovered braking lead passes the lead. The largest lateral position
is 3.8--7.6~m, the least speed 6.3--14.6~m/s, and 2.3~m/s in one 8-m/s
encounter. Under \texttt{f94a0e2} the plan followed the lead in its lane to
the speed floor. A mild endpoint behind a slowing lead is still terminal,
but a plan that completes the pass is now within reach of the extension, and
the planner takes it.

\paragraph{Failures.}
\path{failures.txt} lists them.
\begin{itemize}
\item Noisy curved head-ons: nine fail. Eight fail within 5--45 frames on
  \texttt{pcbfNoNumericalResult} (the safety stage's conic program), all
  eight of the arm's such failures; one fails on
  \texttt{nonlinearAcceptanceFailed} after 11 frames. In two 15-m/s runs the
  last acceptance reason is \texttt{continuationLeavesRoad}: the 28-m stop at
  4~m/s$^2$ with the curvature held is longer than the 22.5-m stop at
  5~m/s$^2$.
\item The exact 8-m/s curved crossing runs out of plan after 27 frames: no
  hold kept the endpoint in the set in its last 8 frames.
\item One noisy 8-m/s accelerating head-on fails on
  \texttt{clfNoNumericalResult}, and one 15-m/s braking lead on
  \texttt{nonlinearAcceptanceFailed} after 75 frames.
\end{itemize}

\section{Next}
The vehicle model still ends at 1~m/s, so a stop behind a stopped vehicle
is a terminal state that the vehicle cannot reach. The next step gives the
declared model a kinematic regime at low speed (user-approved October 11).
