\section{Question and experimental scope}
The preceding fourteen-case experiment stopped after eight seconds. Its largest
final lateral error, 24.739862 m in the 15 m/s curved head-on case, was the
observed plant error at that simulation time, not the MPC terminal error.
That finite endpoint alone could not establish eventual recovery or failure
to recover. The requested behavior is collision avoidance followed by return
to constant-speed motion along the given path, with no prescribed recovery
speed or settling-time requirement.

This experiment extends the same controller and fixtures from commit
\path{03f9b33ba47598cb1cdffa2a81167b2596f90f6e}.
No controller objective, constraint, stopping rule, input bound, target flow,
or road definition was changed. The seven original collision threats are
tested at 8 and 15 m/s. The control hold remains 50 ms, the prediction collision
margin is 6 mm, observations are exact, and road constraints remain disabled.
ODE45 with relative tolerance $10^{-11}$ and absolute tolerance $10^{-12}$
provides the actual successor. Each hold retains 31 independent geometry
samples. The controller uses its unchanged RK4 prediction. No random draws
are made.

\section{Observed recovery criterion and reproducible continuation}
The five transverse errors are lateral position, heading relative to the
nominal trim, longitudinal speed, lateral velocity and yaw rate relative to
the nominal trim. The observation box is
\[
|e|\le
\begin{bmatrix}
0.10\ \mathrm{m}&1\ \mathrm{degree}&0.10\ \mathrm{m/s}&
0.05\ \mathrm{m/s}&0.01\ \mathrm{rad/s}
\end{bmatrix}^{\!T}.
\]
All five sampled errors must remain inside this box for five seconds, starting
no earlier than simulation time eight seconds. These thresholds define a
reported experimental observation, not new control constraints or a proof
of asymptotic stability. Merely crossing the path does not satisfy the test.
There is no imposed maximum settling time in the controller.

The initial extension allows up to 300 simulated seconds per case and stops
early only on confirmed sampled recovery or an actual execution failure.
Low-speed straight-road cases with continuing recovery transients are then
resumed from their saved state, with an observation cap of 1200 seconds.
Reaching the observation cap without recovery is explicitly inconclusive
about the infinite-time limit; it is not labeled physical infeasibility or
proof of divergence. A MAT continuation file preserves the measured ego
state, target, complete controller state, trace and dwell counter. Extending
the cumulative frame count resumes that state instead of cold-starting the
controller. Scenario, configuration, transition and recovery settings must
match. Continuations must use the same controller implementation.

Four independent single-thread MATLAB processes evaluate the initial cases;
additional processes resume completed low-speed cases while other cases run.
Wall-clock solve times are recorded, but this run is not an isolated runtime
benchmark. The initial single-case 60-second pilot was run separately.

\section{Why completing the CLF stage does not prove recovery}
The evaluated relaxed CLF condition is
\[
W_{k+1}-W_k\le-\alpha W_k+\rho_k,\qquad
\alpha=0.01,\quad \rho_k\ge0.
\]
Positive slack can offset or exceed the dissipative term. The acceptance rule
compares a new candidate's slack with an incumbent candidate at the same
current state. It does not require the slack sequence across successive
states to vanish, nor does it require actual $W_k$ to decrease.
One accepted local secondary update therefore establishes the declared
numerical stopping contract, not eventual nominal recovery.

For comparison, if the displayed inequality holds and $\rho_k\to0$, then
\[
W_k\le(1-\alpha)^k W_0+
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-\alpha)^{k-1-j}\rho_j\to0.
\]
This conditional implication is elementary; its premise is not established
by the current acceptance rule. Requiring a particular fast recovery time is
unnecessary for this distinction. The experiment does not replace the
controller or impose a stronger online rule.

\section{A long-time inconsistency in the curved head-on fixture}
The analytic given path continues the radius-200 m circle indefinitely;
\texttt{curve.length} only describes the displayed arc. The curved head-on
target also follows that same circle forever in the opposite direction.
Its center computed from the declared target flow is $(0,200)$ m.
For the ideal given-path constant-speed baseline, center encounters repeat at
\[
t_n=1.6+n\frac{2\pi\,200}{v_E+8},\qquad n=0,1,\ldots.
\]
At $v_E=15$ m/s these include 1.6, 56.236394, 110.872788 and 165.509182 s;
the period is 54.636394 s. At 8 m/s the period is 78.539816 s.
The independently evaluated target and ideal ego centers coincide to
floating-point accuracy at these times. A different ego phase shifts the
encounter times without eliminating repeated encounters between opposing
vehicles sharing a closed circle.

The obstruction also applies to asymptotic recovery, rather than only exact
cruising after a finite time. If all nominal errors tend to zero, the ego
radius tends to 200 m and the relative angular speed tends to a strictly
positive value. The unwrapped relative phase must therefore cross multiples
of $2\pi$ infinitely often. At these crossings the center separation tends
to zero; the fixed positive vehicle widths then force overlap. Vanishing
recovery error and collision avoidance for all future time cannot both hold
under this particular target contract.

Consequently, perpetual collision-free nominal cruising after a last
avoidance maneuver is incompatible with these two original fixture
definitions. This is a scenario-contract issue, separate from the observed
large recovery errors and relaxed CLF limitation. A finite recovery dwell
would still not certify permanent nominal behavior in this fixture. No
unannounced single-encounter assumption, target departure, straight exit,
or scenario modification was used to obtain the reported results.
