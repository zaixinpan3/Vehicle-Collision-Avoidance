\subsection{Isolated circular cruising without a target}
Two additional controls start exactly on the same nominal circular trim, with the target removed. These are isolation experiments, not members of the fourteen collision-threat fixtures.
At 15 m/s the 60-second final lateral error is -190.160992 m, heading error -0.065201 rad, and yaw-rate error -0.024367 rad/s; initial CLF is 0 and final CLF is 2.10738e+06.
At 8 m/s the 60-second final lateral error is -255.905605 m, heading error -0.924779 rad, and yaw-rate error -0.034084 rad/s; initial CLF is 0 and final CLF is 5.05521e+06.
At 8 m/s the first 3.4 seconds follow the nominal trim. The initial plan has 78 holds, including a straight continuation after the first 68 nominal holds. At 3.5 seconds only nine holds remain and actual yaw rate has fallen to 0.003049 rad/s, compared with the nominal 0.04 rad/s. After the plan reaches the minimum eight holds, yaw rate stays near 0.007 rad/s while lateral error grows. This isolates a controller departure from nominal behavior independently of obstacle recurrence.

Code inspection locates the straight terminal construction in \texttt{collisionAvoidanceController} as \texttt{terminalContinuation.build(cfg,0)}. The seed initially tracks the curved nominal reference, then closes with straight intrinsic dynamics. The shifted plan shortens to the configured prefix; subsequent admitted local CLF updates do not preserve the curved trim. This trace identifies the onset and mechanism; it does not prove that every possible optimizer with a straight terminal family must fail, or that an arbitrary finite observation establishes the infinite-time limit.
