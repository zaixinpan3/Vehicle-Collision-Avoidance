QP failure diagnosis, 2026-11-04

The failed local QPs are primarily limited by the implemented terminal approximation and numerical coefficient step box. The evidence does not establish physical collision inevitability and does not implicate the constant-acceleration/constant-sideslip target contract.

Method: reconstruct each archived failure boundary using the exact raw sensor blocks and prior commands. All replayed commands agree exactly with the archive. Export the QP from a new read-only linearizeStep operation. Test feasibility with independent linear programs; remove one constraint group at a time; minimize a shared nonnegative slack applied ONLY to terminal rows (units: metres). Keep physical input bounds, slew constraints, shooting equalities and other inequalities in those tests. LP residuals for the key successful ablations are about 1e-9 or smaller. Some unrelaxed LP variants return -9; these are reported as numerical non-results, not proof of infeasibility. The positive optimal elastic costs and feasible constraint-removal results support the conclusions.

Three 8 m/s initial-speed head-on cases fail at 0.60-0.65 s. Their minimal linearized terminal deficits are 0.00798668, 0.03362938 and 0.00659724 m (straight, accelerating, curved). Removing terminal rows restores feasibility for all three. Releasing ONLY the brake feedforward coefficient increment bounds also restores feasibility; physical input constraints remain present. The uniform numerical increment box is +/-0.25. The shifted plan introduces a newly adjustable stage with nominal brake coefficient -0.6, so the box limits it to -0.85. Feasible no-trust QP solutions use approximately -0.89240, -0.91233 and -0.89198 in that stage, within the physical command range. This is a conflict caused by the fixed backup tail, horizon shift and uniform numerical step bound, rather than a required actuator-rate restriction.

Removing the numerical box is not a verified nonlinear fix. Fresh nonlinear prediction of those full QP steps leaves terminal residuals 0.00362249, 0.00490512 and 0.00364624 m. Collision, command and state residuals are zero to numerical precision in those checks. A 0.95 step fraction makes the terminal residual larger because it reduces the required braking correction. These are offline diagnostics, not an acceptance step added to deployment.

The two 25 m/s cases fail at initialization. Terminal-only phase-I deficits are 43.7262 m (stopped lead) and 52.0562 m (braking lead). Even without the numerical step box they remain 42.7161 and 51.0461 m. Removing terminal rows restores local feasibility while keeping other constraints. At the nominal terminal node only 0.2 s ahead, ego speed is still about 23.95 m/s. The stopping-travel terminal condition demands a large remaining reserve; it is more restrictive than merely avoiding collision over the predicted hold and does not represent every possible steering bypass.

A short horizon and local geometry aggravate the design. Four 50 ms stages provide only 0.2 s of control preview. In straight head-on continuation, the lateral component of the terminal distance gradient is about 5.16e-4, versus a longitudinal magnitude nearly one: a first-order QP near straight travel sees little lateral-separation benefit. This is a geometric explanation, not proof that a nonlinear steering maneuver would succeed. Horizon-only changes are insufficient in the tested warm starts: with shifted seeds, the straight 8 m/s case's terminal deficit rises from 0.0080 m at N=4 to 0.5246 m at N=8 and 1.9126 m at N=16 as the continuing target approaches. Triangular left/right steering seeds also produced no feasible result in the tested cases. These limited seed experiments are not a global policy search.

Recursive feasibility is not inherited from the exact theorem: this deployment has a finite sampled terminal preview, unverified nonlinear SQP steps and only a nominal future-noise case. The shifted old policy therefore need not satisfy the next local problem. The finite preview is not invariant under another hold of an approaching target. This is a structural explanation of the missing guarantee, not a measured attribution of these particular failures to sensor noise.

Recommended design work, not applied in this diagnostic task: replace the blanket coefficient step bound with physically and numerically justified limits; reconsider the fixed braking tail and terminal approximation so that admissible steering continuations can be represented; evaluate preview length and initialization together. Do not silently drop collision constraints, soften hard safety into a performance tradeoff, or add target-motion exclusions. Any revised design needs closed-loop validation beyond a recovered linear QP.

Online controller behavior is unchanged; only read-only diagnostic operations and output fields were added. Full repository tests were not run.
