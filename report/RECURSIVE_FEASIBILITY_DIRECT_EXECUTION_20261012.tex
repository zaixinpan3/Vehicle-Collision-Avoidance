\section{Technical decisions}
Recursive feasibility is an existence statement about a mathematical
optimization problem. Numerical failure in an experiment is accepted and
is not a counterexample to that existence statement. The executable
controller now issues the completed CLF-program inputs directly. There is
no post-solve step-size rule, acceptance relinearization, or nonlinear
constraint veto. The optimized affine slacks are retained rather than
replaced by deficits measured on a different nonlinear plan. A nonlinear
rollout remains prediction and trust data only. Uncomputed nonlinear
margins are reported as NaN, and nonlinear validation is false.

The terminal state should be settled. The former test bounded the held
arc's lateral acceleration and used the estimator's 0.25~rad sideslip
cone. It admitted substantial lateral drift with small yaw rate. The
terminal sideslip limit is now an independent 0.05~rad (about 2.9 degrees)
design setting. Rear adhesion is also checked under the terminal braking
ratio, and that ratio must be within the actuator bounds. The new linear
sideslip and fixed-braking rear rows enter the optimization. These changes
do not establish equality of the Fiala/blended model and the closed-form
terminal law, or admissibility under every finite slew setting; the
mathematical terminal witness must include those conditions.

The archived implementation audit of October 12 identified a solver-return
limitation in addition to mathematical limitations. The user's subsequent
scope decision removes solver return from the required theorem; it does
not remove the need for a feasible terminal witness or sound reachable
sets. Historical audit entries are not rewritten.

\section{Completed mathematical construction}
The proof is in \path{controller/ROBUST_RECURSIVE_FEASIBILITY.md} and the
revised manuscript's subsection on certified uncertainty and revised
forecasts. Let $\mathcal T(\mathcal J,u)$ be the exact reachable joint set
under a held input and declared defects. The joint state includes input
memory and the obstacle's motion parameters. A sound observer enclosure
$\mathcal Q_k^J$ is retained by
\[
 \mathcal I_{k+1}=\mathcal T(\mathcal I_k,u_k)\cap\mathcal Q_{k+1}^J.
\]
Truth containment follows by induction and implies
$\mathcal I_{k+1}\subseteq\mathcal T(\mathcal I_k,u_k)$ without assuming
zero error, no innovation, or shrinking raw observer balls. The obstacle's
projected future families are nested by its flow semigroup, including the
rest rule. A propagated acceleration state, rather than an unchanged
pre-stop interval, enters the update after the acceleration reset.

The terminal collection consists of information sets sharing a common
admissible zero-slack braking continuation, with hard and collision rows
on every reachable member and a safe parked phase. The continuation family
is closed under deleting its first hold. A subset can use the same
witness, so the collection is downward closed. Its one-hold successor has
the tail as its witness, giving robust invariance, including actuator and
slew constraints. A finite certificate needs a bound by which all ego
members stop, plus a parked-set invariance and clearance check. Generic
persistent pose defects do not supply parked invariance automatically.

The robust problem imposes universal hold constraints on propagated sets,
relaxes only the primary holds, and ends in that zero-slack collection.
After the first input, the old inputs shifted by one hold give new reachable
sets contained in the old ones at the same absolute times. The old terminal
set contains the shifted terminal set; append its first common witness
input. That hold has zero slack and preserves the horizon length. The
candidate's primary budget is the old budget minus its first hold's slack.
This proves feasibility for any feasible executed plan. For an executed
primary minimizer it proves
\[
 V^*(\mathcal I_{k+1})\le V^*(\mathcal I_k)-\xi^*_{0|k},
\]
with summable first-hold deficits and an invariant zero set. A secondary
performance problem may select within the primary minimizers. An arbitrary
feasible plan or a numerical/local approximation does not inherit the
optimal-value conclusion without a certified suboptimality budget.

This specializes the shifted-candidate argument of Huang, Wang, Margellos
and Goulart (2025), Lemma~III.5, equations~(9)--(11),
\url{https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08400v1}, to a joint information state.
It does not assert that the executable per-hold affine formulation already
implements the set intersection, monotone nonlinear reachability operator,
or setwise terminal certificate. A theorem for consecutive convex
restrictions additionally needs a shifted feasible witness in the new
restriction. The remainder of a vertex rotation and of nonlinear dynamics
cannot be omitted to claim an exact inner certificate.

\section{Deterministic verification}
\path{scripts/verifyInformationSetRecursiveFeasibility.m} checks a scalar
specialization: forward straight kinematics, a stationary target, 0.2~s
holds, a common 4~m/s$^2$ braking continuation, summed rectangle half-length
4.8~m and required clearance 0.2~m. Initial position and speed interval
widths are 0.8~m and 0.6~m/s. No random seed is used. The monotone interval
map encloses the whole hold. The script verifies truth containment,
posterior inclusion, shifted-set inclusion, hard-tail zero slack and safe
terminal braking/parked intervals. Results are recorded in
\path{RECURSIVE_FEASIBILITY_DIRECT_EXECUTION_20261012/verification.json}.

Both new raw measurement sets are not nested; their posterior intersections
are. In the first case a raw reset loses the terminal certificate, whereas
intersection preserves it. In the second case the candidate budget loses
exactly the applied hold's 0.2~m deficit. The positive-slack case deliberately starts with overlapping rectangles;
it checks budget algebra and is not a collision-free encounter. These are
checks of a concrete mathematical specialization, not a Fiala-model campaign or a numerical
certificate for every joint uncertainty set.

\section{Validation and scope}
The new tests check unmodified optimizer inputs, prediction-only metadata,
settled terminal sideslip, rear adhesion during terminal braking, the
current continuation trace schema, and the two interval examples. The
108-test interface regression passed. The scalar verification's two tests
passed. The full suite passed 624 tests (zero failed or incomplete). After the
final near-rest sideslip condition and its test were added, all 128 affected
regression tests passed. Seven changed source files were checked with the
MATLAB Code Analyzer; its only remaining diagnostic was a settings-file
warning that caused it to use default analyzer settings. No source
analyzer warning remained in the new interval verifier. The manuscript and this standalone
report are compiled as validation; repository PDFs remain generated files.

The October 11 campaign numbers remain explicitly identified as baseline
results. That campaign has not been repeated for direct execution or the
new terminal limits. No new physical-vehicle, continuous-time, or nonlinear
robust safety claim follows from the unit tests or scalar examples.
