\section{Decision and scope}
This experiment implements the selected route of repeating linearization
within the same control hold. The controller still minimizes PCBF slack and
then the slack of the same nominal cost-to-go CLF. It adds neither an
executable backup nor a second recovery controller. The target retains known
constant tangential acceleration and constant sideslip. Road constraints
remain disabled, and no physical steering magnitude or slew limit is added.
Numerical correction boxes remain part of the local search.

The success criterion is a collision-free encounter followed by a nominal
recovery dwell, with no recovery-speed requirement. Fourteen deterministic
encounters use reference speeds of 8 and 15 m/s. The baseline given-path
cruise trajectory collides in every fixture. The initial 60 s observation cap is an
experimental stopping limit, not a success deadline; one case is continued
with an unchanged controller to a 75 s cap and recovers at 64.05 s. The previous artificial
eight-second recovery gate is removed: recovery eligibility starts after the
last baseline contact within the initial observation window has finished.

\section{Algorithm}
The first anchor shifts previous inputs and rolls them through the nonlinear
Fiala bicycle model from the measured state, or uses a moving-target flow
rollout for initialization. Each local model solves the full lexicographic
PCBF/CLF pair. Let $x^{a}$ and $x^{n}$ denote its affine prediction and the
nonlinear RK4 propagation of the returned inputs. The accuracy measures are
\begin{align*}
 E_p&=\max_{i\in I_p}\{\|p_i^a-p_i^n\|_2+r_E|\psi_i^a-\psi_i^n|\},\\
 E_v&=\max_i\|[v_{x,i}^a-v_{x,i}^n,\ v_{y,i}^a-v_{y,i}^n,\ r_i^a-r_i^n]
                     \mathbin{/}[5,3,1.5]\|_\infty,\\
 E_V&=|V(x_1^n,u_0)-\widehat V(x_1^a,u_0)|/\max\{1,V(x_0,u^-)\}.
\end{align*}
Here $r_E$ bounds the ego body radius, and $I_p$ contains nodes supporting
active collision or terminal pose geometry, including the endpoint of the
last active collision hold. Unconstrained post-encounter pose drift is
reported separately; it does not suppress otherwise admissible recovery
steps. Velocity agreement is measured on the entire horizon and CLF agreement
always uses the actual first successor. Tolerances are 0.01 m, 0.01, and
0.01, respectively.

An evaluable nonlinear propagation of the complete optimized input sequence
becomes the next linearization anchor. When the maximum tolerance ratio $r$
exceeds one, the next input correction scale contracts by
$\max\{0.1,\min(0.8,0.8/\sqrt r)\}$. Only an invalid nonlinear rollout
uses up to nine halved search steps to recover an evaluable anchor. Search
controls never supply the issued command directly.

Accuracy alone is not sufficient to stop an iteration. An accurate result
with positive CLF slack whose first-input correction reaches the numerical
box boundary (within $10^{-3}$) also repeats both optimization stages on the
new nonlinear anchor. This releases an artificial restriction without
changing the CLF or introducing a target-dependent recovery mode. Otherwise
the next frame can repeatedly shift to a bad second-input reference and
never reach an available descent input.

The best accurate complete PCBF/CLF pair evaluated in the current frame is
retained, prioritizing PCBF slack within its tie tolerance and then actual
nonlinear first-successor CLF slack. A subsequent failed or inaccurate
iteration cannot delete it. This is retention of optimization iterates, not
execution of a previous frame's plan or a backup controller. Without any
accurate complete pair the frame supplies no command. There are at most eight
refinement rounds and a shared soft solver budget of 5 s. No hard execution
deadline or general convergence theorem is claimed. The stored trust scale
can grow by at most 1.5 between frames when agreement permits.

A failed primary solver status does not define a valid PCBF optimum merely
because its returned array is finite. An infeasible or numerically stalled
fresh/relinearized primary may use one bounded expansion to twice the nominal
input box. This retains the current anchor and avoids repeatedly discarding
refinement progress. The one fresh-flow retry allowance is shared across the
whole frame. Failure to obtain an accurate completed pair supplies no command
and interrupts this simulation; there is no backup execution path.

For rectangles, the ordinary distance dual is solved equivalently by its
closest-feature normal and nonnegative target-frame multipliers. This removes
many tiny conic solves and the prior distant-geometry numerical exception.
The dual is still ordinary nonnegative distance: overlapping rectangles keep
zero distance and zero normal. No signed penetration normal or alternative
collision objective is substituted. The previous 27.633427417605 m geometry
with a 1.6756-micrometer conic gap now attains primal--dual equality to roundoff.

\section{What accuracy control does not establish}
These tests assess local prediction agreement, not every original nonlinear
constraint. In particular, positive PCBF slack remains executable under the
existing relaxed semantics and does not certify collision freedom. The
midpoint collision pose is still an endpoint interpolant. A small node
prediction discrepancy is not a proof of intersample clearance, and the CLF
approximation tolerance is not an exact nonlinear Lyapunov decrease theorem.
The fixed encounter-range contract and terminal architecture are unchanged.

A replay initialized at the \emph{old} failing 15 m/s turning-crossing state
at 1.05 s demonstrates this limitation. 8 refinement rounds
were evaluated; round 8 supplied the retained result with
active-node body discrepancy 0.002801 m. Its PCBF optimum is
0.100000, and an independent ODE45 rollout of those frozen future
inputs still contains body overlap. Relinearization
makes that local prediction more accurate; it does not globally restore an
overlapping ordinary-distance problem. This replay must not be presented as
a repaired safe plan. The closed-loop experiments below instead start at the
original scenario initial states and can correct model error earlier.

\section{Closed-loop results}
Every issued input is applied to an independent ODE45 plant propagation with
relative tolerance $10^{-11}$, absolute tolerance $10^{-12}$ and 31 output
samples per 0.05 s hold. A separate Python implementation recomputes rectangle
clearance, the exact target flow and the recovery dwell. The dwell tolerances
are 0.10 m lateral error, $1^\circ$ heading error, 0.10 m/s speed error,
0.05 m/s lateral velocity and 0.01 rad/s yaw rate, satisfied at every
sampled frame endpoint for 1 s. This establishes observed recovery,
not asymptotic stability or continuous-time physical safety.

\section{Remaining crossing collision: temporal sampling}
The revised 15 m/s straight crossing still overlaps despite the improvement
in prediction accuracy. Contact is sampled at 1.460000 and 1.461667 s;
maximum sampled separating-axis penetration is 0.015806 m. The vehicle
subsequently recovers, which does not turn this case into a safety pass.

At the 1.45 s hold, the retained node prediction has a body displacement
error of only $8.04\,10^{-5}$ m. The independent plant clearance is
0.112638 m at 1.45 s, 0.096993 m at the 1.475 s midpoint, and 0.292250 m
at 1.50 s. The overlap occurs \emph{between} the first two constraint times.
The small midpoint discrepancy from the configured 0.10 m buffer is distinct
from this larger intersample loss. The reported PCBF optimum is
$6.8013\,10^{-5}$; it is a small relaxed value, not a zero-slack nonlinear
certificate. The node accuracy test does not measure continuous clearance.

An exact replay of all thirty controller calls from 0 through 1.45 s returns
identical applied inputs (maximum difference zero). The saved affine
interpolant itself has body overlap between the constraint times, while its
start/midpoint/end distances are approximately 0.112638, 0.099931 and
0.2923 m. Thus this temporal violation is already present in the optimizer's
own interpolated trajectory; it is not inferred solely from a discrepancy
between the optimizer and the plant.

For a valid time-Lipschitz bound $L$ on body distance and consecutive
constraint times separated by $\Delta t$, endpoint bounds alone imply only
\[
 d(t)\ge\min\{d(t_j),d(t_{j+1})\}-L\Delta t/2.
\]
Here the collision-constraint spacing is 0.025 s. The implementation neither
reserves this interval-motion bound nor enforces a common separating
half-space for an entire interval. Repeatedly improving the node dynamics
linearization cannot by itself close that temporal gap. This trial therefore
does not demonstrate that relinearization alone solves all fourteen cases.
The interval constraint formulation and buffer are deliberately not silently
changed to turn this route-specific experiment into a different method.

\section{Measured computation}
Across 4415 issued frames, the median controller call is 0.148 s,
the 95th percentile is 1.733 s, and the maximum issued-frame call is 2.952 s.
3497 calls exceed 0.1 s. 2568 frames use one completed refinement
round; the maximum is 8 rounds and 17 large conic solver
calls. The desired 0.1 s bound is therefore not achieved. Timings include
controller initialization and cold-start effects, and exclude the external
plant replay and Python audit. Measured computation delays are not applied
to the simulated plant: commands take effect at the prescribed 50-ms holds. They are desktop measurements, not a real-time
execution guarantee. Campaign cases run sequentially in one single-threaded
MATLAB process on an AMD Ryzen 7 7800X3D host; no additional MATLAB
controller tests run concurrently. The desktop is not an isolated real-time
platform.

\section{Validation and reproducibility}
262 distinct focused MATLAB unit tests pass. They cover ordinary-distance
geometry, the Fiala controller, the unified CLF, configuration, generated
kernels and the nine-file core-source budget. New regressions exercise
within-hold refinement of both stages, independent nonlinear anchor and
prediction propagation, preservation of progress across failed primaries,
no issued inaccurate plan at the iteration limit, and the exact distant
rectangle geometry. The tests include a direct nonlinear-value comparison showing that continued
iteration improves CLF dissipation after an already accurate but bound-limited
result, and a recovery test without an arbitrary minimum duration. The initial
expanded configuration required regeneration
of the MATLAB Coder kernels; all three builds completed successfully.
Generated solver artifacts are excluded from the project commit.
The code analyzer ran using default settings because its configured legacy
settings file was absent; an unused-variable hint, sparse-assignment
performance hints and a stale suppression remain. No lint-clean claim is made.
Exact messages and MATLAB R2026a Update 3 version information are recorded in
\texttt{final-diagnostics.json}.

Prototype runs were diagnostic, not hidden final passes. An initial full-pose
agreement rule unnecessarily delayed unconstrained recovery. A later prototype
exposed finite failed primary points resetting the braking-lead search.
The next complete fourteen-case campaign yielded ten collision-free recoveries,
one interrupted 8 m/s curved crossing, one unsuccessful 15 m/s braking-lead
cold start, and two 15 m/s curved cases drifting farther from the path at
60 s. These are not presented as final successes.

The braking cold start alternated between tiny damped anchor movements and
forced primary-box expansion: eight ratios remained
$93.8,174.1,109.1,62.2,30.7,8.31,13.3,5.36$. Full nonlinear rebasing reaches
an accurate pair in two rounds. Continuing the CLF boundary correction then
reduces its slack from about 0.209 to $10^{-11}$ by round six. In the drifting
curved traces, the first steering correction repeatedly saturated its local
box. At 60 s of curved head-on, the anchor steering was $-0.02969$ rad and
the returned steering $0.01404$ rad. The nominal CLF construction input
$0.09608$ rad reduced the same nonlinear value, but lay beyond that one local
box. Iterating after an accurate boundary-limited solution addresses this
reference-propagation obstruction.

The interrupted 8 m/s curved crossing also re-entered the target encounter
range at 10 s (body distance 29.53 m). Both shifted and fresh anchors predicted overlapping completion-tail
rectangles. The corresponding ordinary-distance multipliers are zero,
creating respectively 44 and 12 hard rows of the form $0\le-0.10$.
No trust radius can satisfy those constant rows. The terminal geometric
margins were positive (7.29 and 7.86 m), so attributing this failure to the
terminal-separation certificate would be incorrect. The actual obstruction
is the hard-tail ordinary-distance plateau, triggered by those particular
initializations. The final campaign
reported here starts from the original initial states; it is not a resumed or
silently altered version of those failed traces.
