\section{Scope and source interpretation}
The selected motion hypothesis is constant target tangential acceleration $A$
and constant target sideslip $\beta$. No sideslip-rate state is added. This
matches Sharma, Alai and Rajamani, \emph{Simultaneous ego-vehicle state
estimation and vehicle trajectory tracking using a multistage high gain
observer}, Transportation Research Part C 182 (2026), 105411, Sec.\ 2.3,
Eqs.\ (16)--(18), DOI 10.1016/j.trc.2025.105411. The existing controller already implements this model.
Consequently this task changes the flow initialization, rather than claiming
to replace a previously different target predictor. The observer is unchanged.

Cheng, Li, Liu, Li and Guo, \emph{Virtual Fluid-Flow-Model-Based Lane-Keeping
Integrated With Collision Avoidance Control System Design}, IEEE TITS 22(10),
6232--6241 (2021), DOI 10.1109/TITS.2020.2990211, Eqs.\ (34)--(36), motivate
the Gaussian lateral profile. The time-dependent envelope fitting and its
composition with transported flow below are project extensions, not results
claimed by Cheng's static-obstacle experiment.

\section{Target motion and coordinate frame}
In inertial coordinates, or a frame fixed at the prediction origin,
\[
 \dot p=V\begin{bmatrix}\cos(\psi+\beta)\\\sin(\psi+\beta)\end{bmatrix},\quad
 \dot V=A,\quad \dot\psi=V\sin\beta/l_r,\quad \dot A=\dot\beta=0.
\]
Thus $\kappa=\sin\beta/l_r$ is constant, while yaw rate need not be constant.
Writing $s(t)=V_0t+At^2/2$, $a=\kappa s/2$, and
$\operatorname{sinc}_0(a)=\sin(a)/a$ with its continuous value at zero gives
\[
 p(t)=p_0+s(t)\operatorname{sinc}_0(a)
 \begin{bmatrix}\cos(\psi_0+\beta+a)\\\sin(\psi_0+\beta+a)\end{bmatrix},\quad
 \psi(t)=\psi_0+\kappa s(t),\quad V(t)=V_0+At.
\]
No numerical quadrature or future ego-motion input is needed for this
constant-$\beta$ case. The ego-motion terms in the moving relative-frame NRMM
are coordinate transport terms; the controller already transforms its inputs
to inertial coordinates. Initialization and optimization use the same fixed
epoch and absolute-time target flow.

The pre-existing signed-velocity convention is retained. In particular, this
change does not introduce a stop clamp, which would change constant
acceleration after the zero-speed event. The suggested nonzero constant
$\dot\beta$ model and associated steering/friction clipping are outside the
selected hypothesis and this implementation.

\section{Time-aligned Gaussian envelope}
On the finite seed horizon, use nominal arrival clock
$s_E^0(t)=s_0+v_{\rm ref}t$. At the same sampled times, project the predicted
target body center onto the given path to obtain $(s_C(t),d_C(t))$. The
sampling interval is half a controller hold, currently 25 ms. Let $R$ be the
existing sum of body half-diagonals, offset norms and node buffer. Define
\[
 r_i=s_E^0(t_i)-s_C(t_i),\qquad
 \mathcal I=\{i:|r_i|<R\},\qquad h_i=\sqrt{R^2-r_i^2}\ (i\in\mathcal I).
\]
When no nominal time-aligned center pair enters the radius, the Gaussian
amplitude is zero. Otherwise $t_c$ minimizes $r_i^2+d_C(t_i)^2$. Set the
temporal width $T$ to the larger of the existing nominal lookahead and half
the longitudinal interaction time span. With
$g_i=\exp[-(t_i-t_c)^2/(2T^2)]$, the two scalar envelope amplitudes are
\[
 a_\sigma=\max\left(0,\max_{i\in\mathcal I}
       \frac{\sigma d_C(t_i)+h_i}{g_i}\right),\qquad \sigma\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
Choose the smaller $a_\sigma$ (left under a numerical tie) and define
\[
 d_G(t)=\sigma a_\sigma e^{-(t-t_c)^2/(2T^2)},\qquad
 \dot d_G(t)=-\frac{t-t_c}{T^2}d_G(t).
\]
The two amplitudes are a scalar side selection for one seed, not a maneuver
bank or two optimized controllers. The construction uses the complete finite
prediction even when the initial target is outside the encounter range.
Since $t_c$ lies inside the interaction interval and $T$ is at least half
its span, $g_i\ge e^{-2}$ on $\mathcal I$. The fitted amplitude cannot blow
up through a vanishing Gaussian denominator on that finite interval.
It never divides by relative closing speed. Equal-speed motion therefore has
a finite guide or zero disturbance according to the actual timed geometry.

For a straight path the fitted geometric Gaussian covers the sampled
circular exclusion envelope on the selected side. This statement concerns
the reference curve with its nominal clock only. On a curved path the Frenet
envelope is an approximation. The nominal clock also differs from actual
vehicle progress, so neither case certifies the bicycle trajectory.

\section{One transported-flow nonlinear rollout}
The Gaussian supplies an anticipatory guide to the existing cylinder field:
\[
 v_0=v_{\rm ref}\,\mathbf t+
       [\dot d_G-0.5(d_E-d_G)]\,\mathbf n.
\]
For actual rollout position $p$, target center $c$, $q=(p-c)/R$,
$r^2=\max(1,q^Tq)$, and $J$ the planar quarter-turn matrix, use
\begin{align*}
 w&=(v_0-\dot c)/R,\\
 M(q)&=(1+r^{-2})I-2qq^Tr^{-4},\\
 v_F&=\dot c+R\left[M(q)w+\gamma Jq/r^2\right],\\
 \gamma&=-0.6\sigma\max(\|v_0-\dot c\|,0.5v_{\rm ref})/R.
\end{align*}
The center velocity includes rotation of any target body offset. Target and
ego are evaluated at matching absolute times throughout this rollout. The
unchanged heading/speed feedback, input shaping, and nonlinear Fiala RK4 map
produce the actual state/input initialization. The temporal Gaussian stops
guiding after its longitudinal interaction window; the existing terminal
construction supplies the remainder of the seed.

The Gaussian is not directly issued as a command. The controller retains
one PCBF primary solve, one CLF secondary solve, and its existing bounded flow
retry logic. Li ordinary-distance multipliers, hard completion constraints,
CLF, trust boxes, node buffer, and execution semantics are unchanged. No
nonlinear execution check, backup controller, observer modification, or
road-boundary constraint is introduced.

\section{Validation and experimental results}
The baseline is commit \texttt{75b19ab}, with production collision geometry
from \texttt{a073453}. Each paired scenario runs 160 holds of 50 ms (8 s).
There are seven encounters at each of 8 and 15 m/s. Prefix lengths are 8 and
16; the unchanged completion adds 60 holds. The sampled node buffer is
0.10 m; the physical outcome criterion is strictly positive body distance.
Observations are exact, road constraints are absent, and no random samples
are drawn. Every initial given-path cruise baseline collides with its target.
The same exact target epochs and configuration are used in both versions.

Plant replay uses ODE45 with relative/absolute tolerances $10^{-11}/10^{-12}$
and 31 samples per hold. The replay successor feeds the next controller call.
A separate Python implementation recomputes all target poses and rectangle
distances, agreeing with MATLAB within $10^{-9}$ m. The geometry audit does not
assert nonlinear feasibility of the affine optimization. Historical aggregate
admission criteria in the audit utility require online residuals that this
controller deliberately does not compute; those criteria are not relabeled
as passed.

\paragraph{Focused tests.} The final distinct results are 109 passed, zero
failed or incomplete, covering timed/moving flow, nonlinear target dynamics,
two-stage orchestration, ordinary distance duals, and the nine-source-file
budget. One initial positive-amplitude assertion used a fixture that did not
actually intersect; after correcting the fixture to a prescribed meeting,
all 16 flow tests passed. The remaining 93 tests passed in the preceding
invocation. Code Analyzer reports only findings in unchanged portions of the
controller and none in the new tests. Static whitespace checks pass.

\paragraph{Seed audit.} The exact production initialization functions were
extracted into temporary diagnostic helpers. Minimum RK4 node distance improves
in all 14 initial rollouts. All new initial seeds have positive node distance,
with minimum 0.217079 m; the old 15 m/s curved-head-on seed overlaps, while
the new one has 3.326594 m minimum node distance. Five warmed timing samples
per seed give a maximum new per-case median of 4.906 ms. These are seed-only
measurements, not controller-call timings or safety certificates. A
Gaussian-tracking-only prototype was also considered before retaining the
composition with transported flow; its weaker bicycle rollouts were not used
for the production campaign.

\paragraph{Paired eight-second outcomes.} The baseline completes 10/14
scenarios; all ten completed runs have positive sampled clearance. Two other
runs stop on PCBF no-result failures, and two stop on the existing geometry
dual precision check. One interrupted baseline run has already collided.
The new initialization completes 14/14 in this window, with 13/14 avoiding
contact. The 15 m/s turning-crossing collision remains.

\paragraph{Nominal recovery extension.} Successful current runs are resumed
with a 60 s cumulative cap until all five transverse errors remain within
$[0.1\,\mathrm m,1^\circ,0.1\,\mathrm{m/s},0.05\,\mathrm{m/s},
0.01\,\mathrm{rad/s}]$ for 1 s, starting no earlier than 8 s. Thirteen
scenarios confirm recovery by 19.05 s, including the collision-containing
turning-crossing run. Consequently \textbf{12/14 complete collision avoidance
and nominal recovery}. The remaining 8 m/s braking-lead run stops at
10.35 s on the existing distance-dual precision requirement before confirming
recovery. Its observed minimum body distance is positive (0.138015 m).

\paragraph{Remaining collision mechanism.} New contact samples occur from
1.511667 to 1.538333 s, inside the 1.50 s hold. The maximum sampled SAT overlap
depth is 0.152024 m at 1.523333 s. Positive primary slack begins at 1.10 s.
At the collision hold, both shifted and fresh-flow primary problems return
approximately 0.10 slack with solver flag 1; the CLF stage also returns flag 1.

A separate probe rebuilds the fresh seed at the recorded 1.10 s state and
previous input. Its ninth midpoint, at 1.525 s, has overlapping rectangles.
The ordinary-distance dual returns normal norm $7.9853\times10^{-10}$ and
maximum Jacobian magnitude $1.95785\times10^{-9}$. The cold replay returns a
finite solution with primary slack 0.100000051. Thus a near-zero-gradient
collision row again absorbs the node buffer as slack. Improving initial
rollouts has not removed this ordinary-distance degeneracy or the existing
execution of positive-slack results. This probe rebuilds the intrinsic
terminal core from the same configuration; it is not claimed to reproduce
every warm-start numerical detail.

\paragraph{Remaining numerical interruption.} At 10.35 s in 8 m/s braking
lead, the existing ordinary-distance conic solver stops with flag $-7$ and a
reported primal--dual gap $1.68\times10^{-6}$ m, exceeding its $10^{-6}$ m
check. This is a geometry numerical-accuracy interruption, not evidence of
physical collision or proof of MPC infeasibility. The collision-dual code and
its thresholds were not changed in this study.

\paragraph{Runtime.} MATLAB is run with \texttt{-singleCompThread}; compared
controller campaigns do not run concurrently. During the initial continuous
8 s campaign, the maximum running call (excluding each scenario's initial
call) is 0.889515 s. The recovery extension runs in a fresh MATLAB process:
its first resumed 8 m/s head-on call at 8.00 s takes 0.976457 s, including
process-resume initialization overhead. The table retains that actual measured
call. It must not be mislabeled as warmed steady-state solve time. No 0.1 s
real-time bound is achieved or claimed.

\section{Limitations}
Better reference geometry does not prove local convex feasibility or nonlinear
closed-loop safety. A late reinitialization can still start too near a threat,
and fixed ordinary-distance multipliers still lose their directional gradient
at strict overlap. The independent replay measures sampled rectangle distance;
it is not a continuous-time robustness proof. Reported durations are measured
controller calls, not trajectory durations or isolated optimizer times.
