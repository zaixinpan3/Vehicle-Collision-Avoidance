\paragraph{Status and scope.}
On October 11, 2026 the user judged the conic tolerance of $10^{-6}$, set
earlier that day (commit \texttt{ea88059}), to be more precise than
necessary. This report tests looser tolerances. They fail, and the report
traces the failure to a defect of the CLF stage that \texttt{ea88059} had
exposed, fixes it, and tests the tolerance again. The controller keeps
$10^{-6}$.

\paragraph{Result.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Looser tolerances on \texttt{ea88059}.} $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-3}$
  were tried, with the safety-slack cap and the stall acceptance at ten times
  the tolerance. They recover 7 of 14 exact encounters (every 15-m/s one
  fails) and 34 and 26 of 84 noisy ones, against 14 and 74. At $10^{-4}$
  four encounters collide. At 15~m/s the vehicle never settles.
\item \emph{Cause.} Near the path the CLF stage did not fix the plan's later
  inputs. The only term for them, the tie
  $\varepsilon\,\mathrm{lossLower}\,R(dU)$, vanishes there. The solver
  returned those inputs anywhere in the trust box, and the next sample
  inherited them as its anchor. In \texttt{ea88059}, leaving out the rows
  that cannot bind changed where the solver put them, and at $10^{-6}$ too
  the 15-m/s cruise oscillated: speed error down to $-0.14$~m/s, CLF slack
  in about a third of the frames. The campaign did not see this, because
  each encounter stops 1~s after recovery is confirmed. At
  \texttt{c2df2ea} the solver's choice had been steady by accident.
\item \emph{Fix.} On the previous accepted plan the tie's loss is at least
  one unit of the CLF error scales. Cruise is then exact at 8 and 15~m/s,
  on straight and curved roads. On a fresh rollout the tie is not floored:
  a floored tie there held the first solution of the exact 15-m/s
  accelerating head-on so close to the seed that no step was accepted.
\item \emph{With the fix.} The campaign recovers 14 of 14 exact
  encounters (14 before) and 73 of 84 noisy ones (74), at the same time per
  frame. In the onset benchmark, 1 frame is over 50~ms (34 before) and the
  slowest takes 54~ms (113~ms).
\item \emph{The tolerance again.} With the fix, $10^{-4}$ recovers 14 of
  14 and 72 of 84 encounters. Recoveries settle 0.5--0.7~s later at the
  median, with no measurable gain in time: a solve takes about one
  interior-point iteration fewer. $10^{-3}$ leaves a standing speed error of
  0.3~m/s. So $10^{-6}$ stays.
\end{itemize}

\section{Looser tolerances on \texttt{ea88059}}
The arms were copies of \texttt{ea88059} that differed only in the solver
settings. Feasibility and gap tolerances were $10^{-6}$, $10^{-4}$ or
$10^{-3}$. With them, the lexicographic slack cap and the stall acceptance
were set to ten times the tolerance, at least $10^{-4}$ (in the
configuration, \path{solver.lexicographicTieTolerance} and
\path{solver.reducedTolerance}). The cap must be thicker than the solver's
precision: with a cap
of $10^{-5}$ at $10^{-8}$ the CLF stage had stalled before (architecture
document). The 98 encounters ran with two MATLAB sessions per arm, side by
side.

\section{The cause}
\paragraph{A limit cycle.}
The 15-m/s exact head-on at $10^{-4}$ shows the pattern.
\begin{itemize}
\item For about 30 frames, $V$ falls by the required 2.5 percent per
  frame, as the affine model predicts, and the speed error goes from $-0.24$
  to $-0.11$~m/s.
\item Over 7 frames, the correction that the first hold needs relative to
  the anchor grows from 0.41 to 0.99 of the trust box.
\item The decrease then becomes infeasible: the CLF slack is $7\cdot10^{-3}$
  to $3.5\cdot10^{-2}$, and the speed error grows back to $-0.24$~m/s.
\end{itemize}
The cycle repeats about every 2~s.

\paragraph{The plan's later inputs are free.}
Only the first successor enters the CLF row. Near the path, neither that row
nor the constraints fix the inputs after the first hold. The tie fixes them,
and its weight $\varepsilon\,\mathrm{lossLower}$, with
$\mathrm{lossLower}=\|e/\text{scales}\|^2$, vanishes as the errors vanish.
The solver therefore returns those inputs where its barrier terms put them,
anywhere in the trust box, and the next sample inherits them as its anchor's
first input. A cruise probe at $10^{-6}$ shows this: 15~m/s on a straight
road without a target, from a speed error of $-0.25$~m/s, on the declared
model. The anchor's first braking ratio jumped between $-0.17$ and $+0.13$,
while the cruise trim is $0.018$. The table below starts from the
reference instead, for 600 frames.

Keeping the rows that \texttt{ea88059} leaves out, which cannot bind,
restores \texttt{c2df2ea}'s behavior. Restoring the old tolerances does not.
The feasible set and the optimum are the same with and without those rows,
but the optimum is not unique, and the rows' barrier terms had chosen a
steady point. On the 15-m/s curve \texttt{ea88059} wandered by up to 6~cm
and 0.066~m/s, with CLF slack in 27 frames; \texttt{c2df2ea} wandered by
2~cm and 0.03~m/s. At 8~m/s both versions are steady. The campaign stops
each encounter 1~s after recovery is confirmed, so it measured none of
this. The looser tolerances show the defect sooner: with the implied rows
kept, $10^{-4}$ leaves a standing speed error of up to $-0.07$~m/s and
$10^{-3}$ CLF slack in 497 of 600 frames.

\section{The fix}
On the previous accepted plan, the shifted plan, the tie's loss is
$\max(\mathrm{lossLower},1)$: at least one unit of the CLF error scales.
The code marks that anchor with \path{problem.inheritedAnchor}. Its largest effect on the scaled CLF slack is then
$\varepsilon=10^{-4}$, and it keeps the later inputs at the inherited plan
unless the CLF row or a constraint needs them. On a fresh rollout (startup,
target onset, restart) the tie is not floored. The rollout is a search seed,
which may meet the target: a tie floored at 0.1 or 1 held the first solution
of the exact 15-m/s accelerating head-on so close to the seed that the
step-size rule accepted no step. With 0 or 0.01 that frame is solved. With
the floor on every anchor, the campaign lost that exact encounter and
recovered 77 of 84 noisy ones (4 more than the final version, none fewer;
\path{global-floor/}).

\section{The tolerance with the fix}
The fixed controller at $10^{-4}$ (cap and stall acceptance at
$10^{-3}$) was run against the same arm at $10^{-6}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Outcomes.} Exact 14 of 14 and noisy 72 of 84 (73), with 4
  encounters gained and 5 lost.
\item \emph{Recovery.} Recoveries settle later: median $+0.73$~s exact
  (12 of 14 later) and $+0.48$~s noisy (58 of 68 later).
\item \emph{Iterations.} A solve takes 8 interior-point iterations at
  the median instead of 9, and 13 instead of 15 at the 95th percentile
  (noisy, \path{solverstats.txt}). The solver's share of the controller
  time is 0.16 against 0.17.
\item \emph{Time.} Measured side by side with the tie floored on every
  anchor, the controller is not faster (noisy, $10^{-4}$ against
  $10^{-6}$).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Median 7.5 against 7.7~ms; 95th percentile 43.7 against 43.2~ms;
    99th percentile 81.4 against 76.3~ms.
  \item Frames over 100~ms: 118 against 79, because the coarser solves
    left more long plans (1528 against 1090 frames over 80 holds).
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
$10^{-3}$ is too coarse even with the fix: the 15-m/s cruise probe keeps a
standing $-0.31$~m/s error, with CLF slack in 554 of 600 frames.
\path{tolerance-1e-4/} and \path{global-floor-tolerance/} hold the
comparisons.

\section{Checks}
\begin{itemize}
\item Full suite: 617 of 617 (\path{tests.txt}; 613 before). The new test
  \path{clfNominalRecoveryTest/cruiseStaysOnTheReference} runs 300 frames
  from the reference at 8 and 15~m/s, on straight and curved roads. Over
  the last 200 frames the lateral and speed errors must stay within 1~cm
  and 0.01~m/s, the heading and yaw-rate errors within 0.002, the
  lateral-velocity error within 0.01~m/s, and the CLF slack must stay
  zero. Without the floor both 15-m/s cases fail; with it all four pass.
\end{itemize}

\section{Decisions for the user}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Tolerance.} $10^{-6}$ is kept. The solver takes about a sixth
  of the controller's time and converges in about nine iterations, so a
  looser tolerance has little left to save. The cost of a coarse solve is
  real: later or no settling.
\item \emph{Validation.} The campaign's 1-s recovery dwell cannot see a
  slow oscillation after recovery. The new test covers cruise. A longer
  dwell, or a stretch of cruise after each encounter, would cover the
  encounters as well.
\item \emph{Remaining losses.} Noisy curved head-ons (4 of 12 recovered):
  the safety stage is primal infeasible early in the encounter. They are
  unchanged by this step.
\end{itemize}
