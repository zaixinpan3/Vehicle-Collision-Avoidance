\section{Request}
The user asked to explore a terminal set for a finite encounter: the safety
task ends when the target leaves the perception range, modelled as an
absorbing mode of a hybrid system, and the terminal set is the set of states
from which a backup controller completes the encounter safely, so that the
prediction horizon need not cover the whole avoidance. The proposal also
suggested a persistent-separation set from a collision-cone condition, and
asked whether the backup should only accept already separating states or
also continue avoiding. The user asked for an implementation and a
simulation test if the design is reasonable.

\section{Assessment}
\begin{itemize}
\item The CLF-tube terminal set of October 6 already is a safe-exit set: its
  backup is the CLF terminal controller (return to the given path at the
  reference speed), and the tube must miss the target until it leaves the
  range. What the proposal adds is the finite-encounter formulation without
  the 60-s sliding window (hypothesis H4), and a backup that may keep
  avoiding.
\item \emph{Finite encounter.} Requiring the target to leave the range
  removes H4, and $S\cup\{\dagger\}$ is invariant with an absorbing end state.
  It also makes every state non-terminal for a target that stays in range: a
  parallel target at 49.9~m (two unit tests) and the first frames of the
  braking lead, whose first estimate is a lead at the ego's speed ($A=0$ until
  0.5~s). A fixed encounter window keeps both properties: the encounter ends at
  the earlier of the target's exit and a fixed time 60~s after the encounter's
  start. Because that end does not move, the shift argument holds within the
  encounter without H4; a target still present then starts a new encounter.
\item \emph{Backup controller.} The return-to-path backup makes no state
  terminal while a slower vehicle stays in the given lane; this is the
  braking lead's startup failure in all 12 noisy runs. A family of lane-hold
  backups (the same CLF and tube about each lane centre of the road) lets the
  terminal set include overtaking states. The tube certificate selects a backup
  whose tube stays clear, which is the role the proposal gave the collision
  cone.
\item \emph{Persistent separation.} $\mathcal S_{\mathrm{sep}}$ is the
  separating terminal set of October 4--6, withdrawn because it is not
  invariant under the ego's return to the path. It was not implemented; the
  tube check covers both cases the proposal distinguished.
\item \emph{Return to nominal.} A lane backup kept alone would never be left:
  the shifted endpoint keeps its lane and appended holds keep it there (the
  conflict that withdrew the shoulder terminal on October 4). A plan ending
  in another lane is therefore extended by path guidance to the nominal set
  whenever that extension meets every hard row.
\end{itemize}
The lanes are modes taken from the road description; this relaxes the
October 6 requirement that the terminal set use no mode.

\section{Addendum: proven separation instead of a preset window}
The user asked why the window is 60~s and whether a preset time is needed.
It is not: 60~s was the engineering cap of October 6, with no derivation, and
the safety task itself has no duration. The encounter now ends at the first of
two computed events, the target's exit or a proven separation, and
\texttt{terminal.horizonSeconds} only bounds how far the check computes; a
state that reaches it with neither event is not terminal (variant F, which
replaces D in the source).

\paragraph{Proven separation.} On a straight road the distance between two
path boxes is at least their gap along the road and their gap across it.
After a grid time the ego band across the road only shrinks, and along the
road it moves at the trim's station rate and widens by at most
$T\sigma(r)$ more. A straight-line target ($\beta=0$) keeps a gap across the
road open if its lateral velocity never closes it ($sV\ge0$ and $sA\ge0$ on
the side away from the ego, and opposite signs on the other) and a gap along it
open if its station rate never closes it ($cV\ge v$ and $cA\ge0$ ahead,
$cV\le v$ and $cA\le0$ behind). A circling target stays in the disk of its
circle widened by its body reach. These hold for all later times, also
through a stop. A separation proven for a state holds for its successors
under the backup at the same absolute time, so invariance and the shift
argument hold within an encounter without any window. On a curve only the
exit ends an encounter.

\paragraph{Implementation.} \path{controller/terminalSafeSet.m}:
\texttt{permanent} (the certificate above, tested at every grid point of a
chunk), \texttt{localTargetMotion} (the forecast's tangential acceleration and,
for $\beta\ne0$, the circle's centre and widened radius), the target's course
and speed on the grid, and the computation bound \texttt{horizonSteps}; the
check ends at the first exit or proven separation after confirming the grid
points before it and the separation's own point, and returns
\texttt{noExitOrSeparation} when it reaches the bound with neither. The fixed
window (\texttt{windowSteps}, \texttt{clearToWindowEnd},
\texttt{encounterWindowEnded}) and the encounter start in the controller state
are removed. The issued plan and the trace record how the encounter ends
(\texttt{terminalEnd}). \texttt{terminalSafeSetTest} now checks that a
parallel target, a faster lead and a target circling beside the road end the
encounter by proven separation, that a slower lead in the ego lane meets the
tube, and that a lead closing only after the 60-s bound is not terminal. The
full suite (592 tests) passes on F (\path{tests.txt}).

\section{Second addendum: the collision cone and the startup side}
The user asked whether the same-direction, same-speed case could be judged
from the relative velocity's position with respect to the collision cone, and
then asked for a terminal set designed on the collision cone, with a
simulation test (variant G, the committed design).

\paragraph{Design.} The cone is not a test of the next instant but of the
whole future under constant relative motion, so it is the right form for the
separation certificate, with three changes that keep the certificate
invariant: it is taken with respect to the backup's converged motion (the
lane trim at the station rate $v$), not the ego's present velocity; the
transient is absorbed by widening the ego box by the tube's remaining drift
$T\sigma(r)$; and the target's constant acceleration $A$ makes the relative
motion a parabola. On a straight road, in the frame moving at $v$, the
difference of the box centres is
$q(u)=q(0)+(cV_s-v,\,sV_s)\,u+(cA,\,sA)\,u^2/2$, and the boxes meet at some
$u\ge0$ iff $q(u)$ enters the sum box $|q_s|\le H_s$, $|q_d|\le H_d$
(half-widths added, with the buffer). The relative motions for which this
happens form the collision cone of the two boxes; separation is proven iff the
relative motion is outside it. The test is exact for the forecast: the least
normalized box distance $\max(|q_s|/H_s,|q_d|/H_d)$ over $u\ge0$ is attained
at $u=0$, at a vertex or zero of $q_s$ or $q_d$, or where the two normalized
distances are equal, and it is evaluated at all of these (at most 11
candidates, \texttt{localParabolaMeetsBox}, vectorized over the grid). The
sign conditions of the first addendum are the one-coordinate special case; a
slow diagonal crosser whose crossing ends after the 60-s bound is now
terminal. A condition on the present relative velocity alone (``the distance
grows now'') is neither necessary nor sufficient: a target drifting away
across the road while the along-road gap closes is safe although the distance
shrinks, and a faster lead that brakes is safe now and met later. The circling
target keeps its disk test; on a curve only the exit ends an encounter.

For the same-direction, same-speed case the cone gives the verdict of the
first addendum (seed 20261003, 15~m/s: relative velocity $(-0.10,+0.043)$~m/s,
left lane inside the cone, right lane outside), so the braking leads' startup
is decided by the side of the startup rollout, and the cone is used there as
well: a potential-field rollout that reaches no terminal state is run once
more toward the lane the cone prefers (\texttt{terminalSafeSet.coneLane}: the
nearest lane whose settled backup at the present station is already in its
set, not the nominal lane, none when the nearest such lanes lie on both
sides), driven by the backup's path guidance; without such a lane the
potential field runs again toward the other side. A retry under the
potential field alone was tried first and parked the ego at $-6.6$~m at
15~m/s, 3~m past the right lane centre and outside that backup's certified
region (\path{scripts/whyRetryFails.m}). The choice is guidance; the
endpoint is certified like any other. The four first frames of the first
addendum now solve standalone in 0.4 to 3.3~s with 44- to 48-hold plans
ending in the right lane (\path{scripts/smokeStartup.m}).

\section{Fourth addendum: a closed-form terminal check}
\paragraph{Request.} The user set three requirements for the terminal set: it
exists only to make the shifted plan feasible (the closed loop does not
normally execute the terminal controller), so it must need no heavy real-time
computation, must not be so conservative that the problem loses its solution,
and should be mathematically clean. Measured against the design of the third
addendum (variant H): the membership test followed a 5-ms grid for up to 60~s
whenever the node itself certified nothing (12.9~ms per call for a slower
lead far ahead, 2.6~ms for the braking lead's first estimate, repeated at
every node of a 512-hold startup rollout); the station drift bound used a
first-order heading term that widened the cone test's box by 14.5~m along the
road at the certified level; and 60~s was a preset constant.

\paragraph{Design (variant I2, the committed design).} Three changes, none of
which alters the set's definition (Section 4 of the theory note), only how
membership is decided and how tightly the tube is bounded.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Closed form first.} The node's own grid point is evaluated alone:
  the tube and the forecast at that one time, the exit test and the cone test.
  A relative motion outside the cone, or a target already out of range, is
  certified without a grid.
\item \emph{A span bounded by the forecast's geometry.} Only when the node
  itself certifies nothing does the check follow the grid, and
  \texttt{terminalSafeSet.scanBound} limits the span from the forecast rather
  than by a constant. On a straight road with a straight-line target the
  relative motion is the parabola of the cone test: if it enters the box of
  the settled tube (level 0) at $u_{\mathrm{hit}}$, the tube meets the target
  then unless the target has left the range before, and the first time at
  which the reference point is surely out of range is computed from the same
  parabola (a quartic); a node whose forecast meets the settled tube before
  any exit is rejected at once. If the parabola never enters the settled box,
  it leaves the whole tube's box (widened by the total drift) for good at
  $u_{\mathrm{out}}$, where the cone certifies at the latest; the span is
  $u_{\mathrm{out}}$, or the tube's settling time
  \[ T\log\bigl(\max(T\sigma(r_0),\,a_1 r_0)/\text{buffer}\bigr) \]
  for a co-moving target inside the widened box. A circling target's span ends when the tube's box
  has passed its disk; on a curve, where there is no cone, the span is the
  settling time plus the range transit time $2R/v$. The constant
  \texttt{terminal.horizonSeconds} is removed from the configuration.
\item \emph{A tighter drift bound.} Expanding the tangential speed about the
  trim, the heading error enters a straight road's station rate only at
  second order:
  \[ |v_t-v_t^*|\le|\delta v_x|+|\delta v_y|\,(|\sin\psi^*|+|e_\psi|)
     +|v_x^*|\,(|\sin\psi^*|\,|e_\psi|+e_\psi^2/2)+|v_y^*|\,|e_\psi| \]
  replaces $|\delta v_x|+|\delta v_y|+(|v_x^*|+|v_y^*|)|e_\psi|$. At 8~m/s and
  $V=\bar c$ the total drift $T\sigma$ falls from 14.5~m to 4.6~m; the bound
  keeps $\sigma(\lambda r)\le\lambda\sigma(r)$, so the remaining-drift
  argument of Section 4.1 is unchanged.
\end{enumerate}
A first attempt (variant I) bounded the span by the settling time alone. That
is wrong where no cone exists: on curves and for circling targets the exit is
the only end, and the settling time of a nearly settled tube is a few seconds,
so exits at 5--15~s were cut off; noisy 54 of 84 with a new failure
(8~m/s turning crossing of seed 2) and no gain in compute. The
geometry-bounded span replaces it.

\paragraph{One CLF for a speed interval.} The braking lead's first estimate
(a lead at nearly the ego's speed in the ego lane) is rejected by every
variant because the terminal controller returns to the reference speed; at
the lead's speed the same state is certified at once (probe of October 9).
A terminal controller whose cruise speed is a continuous parameter in an
interval needs one CLF certified over that interval. The LMIs of
\texttt{synthesizeClfMatrices} with a common $S$ and one certificate gain per
grid speed (\path{scripts/clfSpeedIntervalStudy.m},
\path{clf-speed-interval.txt}) are feasible for 4--8~m/s (certified time
constant 3.94~s against the required 4.00) and 7.5--15~m/s (2.26~s), with
tube scales within a few percent of the per-speed ones ($a_1\approx3.0$~m),
and infeasible for 4--15~m/s (4.31~s). Curvature 0.005 gives the same
picture. This is a feasibility study, not a certificate check, and nothing
of it is in the controller.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} The committed check decides membership in closed
form wherever the forecast allows, bounds the remaining cases by their own
geometry, carries no preset time, and bounds the tube three times more
tightly along the road. It changes no outcome for the better: the remaining
failures are solver stalls on long plans and the braking lead's startup,
which only a terminal controller with an adjustable cruise speed can reach.
