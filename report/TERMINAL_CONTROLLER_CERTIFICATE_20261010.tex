\section{Request}
The user objected to the terminal controller as it stood (the same
optimization problem without collision rows, with the CLF row without
slack): with such a controller it is hard to prove that the vehicle does not
leave the terminal set. The user then set the terminal set's requirements
(the set only has to be safe so that recursive feasibility holds; no heavy
real-time computation; no excess conservatism; a provable invariance under
the terminal controller, with assumptions allowed) and asked for an
implementation whose main work is to try to close the mathematical gap.

\section{The gap}
The terminal set $S$ of \path{controller/TERMINAL_SAFE_SET.md} is invariant
under any controller whose closed loop satisfies $V(x^+)\le\rho V(x)$,
$\rho=e^{-2h/T}$, because the CLF tube is nested along such a motion and the
encounter's end events only come earlier. Which input the terminal
controller chooses is irrelevant; what matters is that an input satisfying
the constraint exists at every state of $S$. That existence was hypothesis
H3, checked online by a candidate search (path guidance, the linear
minimizer, a 45-point grid) and never proven: the offline LMI certified a
linear gain $K$ on the \emph{linearized} sampled model only (fastest
contraction $\rho^*$, time constant 2.1~s at 8~m/s and 1.5~s at 15~m/s
against the required 4~s), and discarded $K$.

\section{First route: a Lipschitz budget for the nonlinear remainder}
The natural way to lift the linear certificate is the triangle inequality
in the $P$-norm, $F=\mathrm{chol}(P)$: with $\phi(e)=e(x^+)-(A+BK)e$ the
one-hold remainder and $L(c)=\sup\{\|F\phi(e)\|/\|Fe\| : e'Pe\le c\}$,
\[
\|Fe^+\|\le(\sqrt{\rho^*}+L(c))\,\|Fe\|,\qquad
\sqrt{\rho^*}+L(c)\le\sqrt{\rho}\ \Rightarrow\ V(x^+)\le\rho V(x)\ \text{on}\ \{V\le c\}.
\]
This was implemented (certificate sampling in \texttt{terminalSafeSet},
a level bisection in the synthesis script) and measured
(\path{lipschitz-remainder.txt}, 2000 directions on the boundary of each
level, the gain recovered from the stored $P$). It fails by two orders of
magnitude. The budget $\sqrt{\rho}-\sqrt{\rho^*}$ is 0.011 at 8~m/s and
0.020 at 15~m/s per 50-ms hold, while
\[
L(1)=0.70,\quad L(0.4)=0.57,\quad L(0.1)=0.29,\quad L(0.025)=0.14
\qquad(8~\text{m/s, curvature }0.005),
\]
that is $L\approx0.9\sqrt{c}$: a second-order remainder with a large
constant. Under $u^*+Ke$ the sampled $V(x^+)/V(x)$ exceeds 1 at every level
down to $c=0.025$ (1.05--1.07 at 8~m/s, up to 1.36 at 15~m/s on the curve).
Two causes, both measured in \path{jacobian-variation.txt}:
\begin{itemize}
\item The modified Fiala tire leaves its linear range at slips of a few
  hundredths of a radian ($F_y=C\alpha-\tfrac{C^2}{3\mu F_z}|\alpha|\alpha+\dots$,
  relative deviation $4.7|\alpha|$ for the default tire), and the former $P$
  let the lateral velocity reach $a_4\sqrt{\bar c}=1.4$~m/s and the yaw rate
  0.76~rad/s at the certified level: slips far beyond the linear range.
  Over that set the Jacobian entries of the lateral-velocity and yaw-rate
  rows vary by $\pm50$--$180\%$ of their trim values, and the steering column
  by $\pm50$--$65\%$.
\item The RK4 hold of 50~ms is not the exponential of the continuous
  Jacobian in the fast yaw mode at 8~m/s (entry (5,5): 0.264 against 0.188),
  so the former $A$ was not even the declared model's linearization.
\end{itemize}
Fitting the budget would need $c\approx1.5\cdot10^{-4}$, a lateral extent of
about a centimetre. The route was abandoned.

\section{Second route: a robust LMI over an enclosure of the Jacobians}
Let $g(e,\delta u)$ be the transverse error after one hold from $x(e)$ with
input $u^*+\delta u$ and $G=[\partial g/\partial e,\ \partial g/\partial\delta u]$
its Jacobian, available exactly from the RK4 variational equations and the
error chart (\texttt{terminalSafeSet.jacobians}). On a convex set $\Omega$
containing the trim,
\[
g(e,Ke)=\bar G\begin{bmatrix}e\\Ke\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\bar G=\int_0^1 G(te,tKe)\,dt\in\mathrm{conv}\{G(e',Ke'):e'\in\Omega\}.
\]
If the hull lies in a zonotope $Z=\{G_0+\sum_k\delta_kD_k+R:|\delta_k|\le1,\|R\|\le\varepsilon\}$,
the discrete Lyapunov inequality at the vertices of $Z$ (residual by
Petersen's lemma) proves $V(g(e,Ke))\le\rho V(e)$ on $\Omega$. With
$S=P^{-1}$, $Y=KS$ this is an LMI; the set is $\Omega=\{V\le1\}$, kept inside
a certification box $|e_i|\le\beta_i$ (rows $S_{ii}\le\beta_i^2$) and under
the input box ($|K_je|\le\bar u_j$ on $\Omega$), and the fill
$t$ in $S\succeq t\,\mathrm{diag}(\beta)^2$ is maximized. The enclosure is
the trim's Jacobian, the leading six principal directions of the sampled
deviations with their coefficient bounds, and the residual norm, in
box-scaled coordinates, each widened by 1.25.

Three versions were needed (\path{robust-lmi-product-box.txt},
\path{robust-lmi-scaled-product-box.txt}, \path{robust-lmi-along-controller.txt}):
\begin{itemize}
\item Enclosing over the product of the state box and the input box returns
  only the trivial solution ($S\approx10^{-6}$), at every box tried. The
  product contains states with the largest lateral velocity and yaw rate
  steered the wrong way, where the front slip reaches 0.15~rad and the tire's
  stiffness derivative is nearly zero: the LMI then has to contract the
  lateral position without a working front tire.
\item In unscaled coordinates the residual term of Petersen's lemma couples
  to the condition number of $S$ (metres against radians per second), which
  alone forces $S\to0$. Scaling by the box removes this; the product
  enclosure still fails because the steering box ($\pm0.075$~rad) saturates
  the front tire at every state.
\item Enclosing along the controller's own inputs, $u=u^*+Ke$ over
  $\Omega$, is feasible with non-trivial sets. The enclosure then depends on
  $(P,K)$, so the design iterates: plain LMI, then enclosures along the
  current controller over the current set, four rounds; the fill oscillates
  (0.01--0.09) rather than converging.
\end{itemize}
A certificate that refers to its own set needs a last step: $K$ is fixed
(the robust iteration with the largest fill), the Jacobians are enclosed
along $K$ over that iteration's set $\Omega_0$, and $P$ is solved for with
$K$ fixed and $\Omega\subseteq\Omega_0$ ($S'\preceq S_0$); if the LMI has no
solution the sampling set is shrunk by 0.7 and the step repeated (its
Jacobians approach the trim's, where $K$ contracts). Picking the plain LMI's
gain as the starting point fails at every shrink (it has no robustness
margin at all); the robust iterations' gains succeed at the first or second
attempt. The LMIs are solved with a contraction $0.1\%$ stricter than
$\rho$, and the vertex inequalities are re-verified by eigenvalues at $\rho$
with the solved multipliers; the smallest contraction they certify is
recorded. The partial-hold maps are enclosed over the same samples to give
the hold factor $\mu$, the largest $e^{s/T}\sqrt{V(x(s))/V(x)}$ inside a
hold, by which the tube's half-axes are inflated.

Box entries are lateral (m), heading (rad), speed (m/s), lateral velocity (m/s), yaw rate (rad/s); the required contraction is 0.975 per hold ($T=4$~s).

The lateral extent saturates near 0.6~m at 8~m/s and 1.0--1.65~m at 15~m/s
whatever the box: with the input box of 0.075~rad the robust LMI cannot
contract a larger lateral error at $T=4$~s against the enclosed variation of
the steering effectiveness (the first principal direction, coefficient
bound 0.12--0.38 in scaled units) and of the tire rows. The default box
$[1~\text{m},\,0.15~\text{rad},\,0.75~\text{m/s},\,0.15~\text{m/s},\,0.08~\text{rad/s}]$
is kept; it gives the largest heading and speed extents. Two levers were
measured on the straight road (\path{certificate-levers.txt}): a slower
required decay ($T=6$ or 8~s instead of 4~s, which the optimizer would share)
and a wider steering box (0.1~rad instead of 0.075). Neither moves the set
materially: lateral 0.54--0.78~m at 8~m/s and 0.82--1.00~m at 15~m/s, heading
0.047--0.061~rad. The size is set by the tire, not by the decay rate: the
modified Fiala tire's stiffness derivative falls to 70\% at a slip of
0.033~rad, and a heading error of 0.05~rad steered at 0.075~rad already puts
the front tire there, so the enclosure's first principal direction (the
steering effectiveness and the tire rows) is what the robust LMI pays for.
A less conservative enclosure (correlated structure instead of a zonotope,
or an interval enclosure of the tire characteristic) is the way to a larger
certified set, not a different $T$. Against the former
linear certificate the set is about three times smaller laterally (0.62
against 1.9~m at 8~m/s) and in heading (0.053 against 0.18~rad), and far
smaller in lateral velocity and yaw rate (0.11~m/s and 0.067~rad/s against
1.4~m/s and 0.76~rad/s), which is where the tire's nonlinearity lives. The
state-row level of the new $P$ is 16--209, so $\bar c=1$ everywhere. On an
independent sample (another seed, 800 points) the worst contraction under
$u^*+Ke$ is 0.945--0.962 against the required 0.975, and the sampled hold
factor is below the certified one.

\section{Implementation}
\begin{itemize}
\item \path{scripts/synthesizeClfMatrices.m}: the robust design above;
  entries now carry $K$, the trim's RK4 Jacobians, the certified level (1),
  the hold factor, the fill, the extents and the certificate's record
  (box, input box, components, coefficient bounds, residual, margin, sample
  counts and seed, iterations, LMI eigenvalue margin, largest inputs and
  slips, sampled worst contraction and hold factor). Existing entries are
  re-synthesized from their keys; entries whose key no longer reproduces
  (the retired settings) are replaced.
\item \path{config/collisionAvoidanceControllerConfig.m}:
  \texttt{clf.certificationRegionScale} and \texttt{clf.shapeRatio} are
  replaced by the five certification-box settings; rejected as unknown.
\item \path{controller/terminalSafeSet.m}: \texttt{terminalInput} is
  $u=u^*+Ke(x)$, one nonlinear hold, with the decrease, input box and rows
  evaluated as a guard; the candidate search is removed. New
  \texttt{jacobians} (the sampled error map's Jacobians) and
  \texttt{certificate} (the sampled check). The tube's half-axes carry
  $\mu$, and \texttt{levelMaximum} is the least of \texttt{terminal.levelMaximum},
  the certified level and the state-row level divided by $\mu^2$ (so hold
  midpoints meet the rows).
\item \path{controller/nonlinearBicycleModel.m}: \texttt{cruise} reads
  $K$, the Jacobians, the level and the hold factor; an entry without them
  is rejected (\texttt{collisionAvoidanceController:staleClfMatrix}).
\item Tests: \texttt{nominalClfTest} verifies the stored certificate
  (input bound, box, fill, contraction, LMI margin, hold factor) and repeats
  the sampled check with another seed on the certified set;
  \texttt{terminalSafeSetTest} uses the new \texttt{terminalInput};
  \texttt{collisionAvoidanceControllerConfigTest} rejects the retired
  settings.
\item Documents: \path{controller/TERMINAL_SAFE_SET.md} (Section 2 rewritten,
  H3 restated as the enclosure hypothesis, hold factor, limitations),
  \path{controller/NOMINAL_CLF.md}, \path{controller/PCBF_CLF_ARCHITECTURE.md},
  \path{controller/CONTROLLER_FILES.md}, \path{README.md}, \path{CLAUDE.md}.
\end{itemize}
The run-time cost of the terminal controller is one matrix product and one
RK4 hold; the membership test is unchanged.

\section{What is proven and what is assumed}
Under H1 (declared sampled model, exact state) and H2 (the target follows
its forecast), the appended holds of the terminal controller satisfy
$V(x^+)\le\rho V(x)$, the input box and the problem's rows on $S$, by the
certificate; invariance of $S$ and recursive feasibility within an encounter
follow as before. The certificate's one numerical hypothesis is H3: the
Jacobians along the controller over $\Omega$ lie in the recorded zonotope.
It rests on 1200 boundary and 600 interior samples (recorded seed), a margin
of 1.25 on every coefficient bound and residual, and the independent sampled
check; it is not an interval enclosure. The former H3 (existence of an
admissible decreasing input, checked online) is gone, and so is the
assumption that the continuous decrease holds inside a hold (now the hold
factor). The ode45 plant of the experiments differs from the declared RK4
model (H1), as before.

\section{Campaign}
The paired campaign (14 exact and 84 noisy encounters; seeds 0, 20261003 and
1--5; 8 and 15~m/s; seven geometries; both variants interleaved in one pool
of eight processes) compares the baseline, commit \texttt{deb4dac} with its
linear certificate, against the working tree with the certified terminal
controller (\path{comparison.txt}, \path{campaign-runs.csv},
\path{encounter-ends.txt}). No collision in either variant (least replay
clearance 0.155~m exact and 0.240~m noisy against the 0.2-m buffer at the
sampled rows).

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
 & exact recovered & noisy recovered & horizon with a target (median / p95) & controller s with a target (median / p95) \\
\midrule
baseline (deb4dac) & 14 of 14 & 54 of 84 & 47 / 187 holds & 0.065 / 0.391 \\
certified terminal controller & 12 of 14 & 39 of 84 & 81 / 201 holds & 0.110 / 0.406 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The certified variant recovers 15 noisy runs fewer and no run more; 14 of
the 15 new failures and both exact failures stop on
\texttt{clfNoNumericalResult}, the CLF stage's conic solve returning no
numerical result (once in 84 for the baseline). Twelve of the fifteen stop at
the first frame; the exact curved head-ons and three noisy ones stop at
their second encounter on the circle (frames 1096--1596), where the
baseline had completed. The mechanism is the size of the certified set: a
plan must end inside it, so plans are longer (first-frame horizon median
124 against 90 holds; 81 against 47 holds whenever a target is present,
the first curved head-on starts with 135 holds against 94), and the CLF
stage's conic program on those plans fails numerically more often, the
weakness the previous report already recorded on 222-hold plans. The
terminal controller itself costs nothing measurable: the median controller
time without a target is unchanged (0.015 against 0.018~s). All twelve noisy
braking leads stop at the first frame in both variants, as before. The
encounters that both variants complete end the same way (exit or the cone);
with longer plans the end is reached sooner after the endpoint (99th
percentile 4.4 against 8.1~s).

The result is the trade this certificate makes: the former set was not a
proof, and the proven set is small enough to lengthen plans beyond what the
conic solver handles reliably. The remedies are on the optimizer's side
(the numerical robustness of long CLF-stage solves, or a horizon that need
not grow until the endpoint reaches the set) or in a tighter enclosure
(Section 5); none was attempted here.
