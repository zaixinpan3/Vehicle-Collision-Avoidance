\section{Request}
After the terminal controller had been certified on the nonlinear model
(\path{report/TERMINAL_CONTROLLER_CERTIFICATE_20261010.tex}, commit
\texttt{8faf395}), the certified set was three times smaller than the former
unproven one (0.6~m of lateral error at 8~m/s against 1.9~m), the plans
became longer and the conic solver failed more often. The user asked for a
brainstorm of mathematically elegant ways to construct the terminal set and
then asked to try the elegant ones. This report records what was tried, what
each construction certifies, and which ones went into the code. After the
first adoption (commit \texttt{e2046e0}: force-level controller with the
secant enclosure) the user decided to keep the controller's input as it was,
steering angle and braking ratio, and to go with the first idea alone; the
production certificate of this commit is the secant enclosure with the
self-consistent growth on the steering-level controller $u=u^*+Ke$
(Section~\ref{sec:production}).

\section{Starting point}
The certificate of commit \texttt{8faf395} encloses, in box-scaled
coordinates, the pointwise one-hold Jacobians $G(e,Ke)$ of the sampled error
map along the controller over the certified set, by a zonotope (six principal
directions with coefficient bounds, a residual, all widened by 1.25), and
solves the robust discrete Lyapunov LMI at its vertices. Two things limited
it. First, the pointwise Jacobians of a saturating tire spread like the
tangents of its force curve; at the slips the set reaches the first
coefficient bound was 0.11--0.16. Second, the design iterated the enclosure
and the LMI and ended with a step at fixed gain whose set had to lie inside
the set the enclosure was built on; that step was fragile (it returned the
trivial set and shrank the set by 0.7 until it passed), so the final set was
roughly the size of the second-to-last iteration's.

\section{Constructions tried}
All experiments use the study synthesizer
\path{scripts/alternativeCertificate.m} of this folder, which repeats the
production pipeline with options, and its driver \texttt{runAlternatives.m}
(variants are named by tokens: \texttt{secant}, \texttt{force},
\texttt{sat}, \texttt{poly}, \texttt{T$n$} for the time constant,
\texttt{wide} for the box $[2~\text{m},0.2~\text{rad},1~\text{m/s},0.3~\text{m/s},0.15~\text{rad/s}]$
(the default box is $[1,0.15,0.75,0.15,0.08]$), \texttt{comp$n$} for the
number of principal directions, \texttt{iter$n$}, \texttt{gs$\delta$} for the
gain band and \texttt{grow$\gamma$} for the growth factor). Every row of the
tables is a verified certificate: the vertex inequalities hold at the
required contraction with the recorded enclosure (relative margin
$\ge -10^{-6}$), and an independent sample with another seed confirms the
contraction and the problem's rows. Results are in \path{variants.txt}
(self-consistent scheme) and \path{variants-first-scheme.txt} (the
first scheme with the fixed-gain final step); the JSON lines beside them
hold every number, the gains and the enclosures.

\subsection{Secant enclosure: ray-averaged Jacobians}
The mean-value identity the certificate rests on is exact for the ray
average, not for the pointwise Jacobians: with $g(0,0)=0$,
\[
g(e,w)=\bar G(e,w)\,[e;w],\qquad \bar G(e,w)=\int_0^1 G(te,tw)\,dt .
\]
The certificate needs the Lyapunov inequality only for the matrices
$\bar G(e,Ke)$, $e\in\Omega$, whose convex hull is in general much smaller
than the hull of the pointwise Jacobians. For the modified Fiala curve,
normalized as $F=1-(1-s)^3$, the tangent slope is $(1-s)^2$ and the secant
slope $(1-(1-s)^3)/(3s)$: at the force peak the tangent is 0 and the secant
still $1/3$; at $s=0.5$ they are 0.25 and 0.58. The study computes $\bar G$ by
Gauss--Legendre quadrature along the ray (8 nodes; the integrand has a kink
where a slip angle crosses zero, so the convergence is algebraic,
\path{scripts/quadratureCheck.m}: relative residual of the identity
$3.6\cdot10^{-4}$ with 4 nodes, $1.2\cdot10^{-4}$ with 8, $1.9\cdot10^{-5}$
with 24) and carries the quadrature's residual exactly: the rank-one matrix
that makes the identity hold for a sample has norm
$\|\text{gap}\|/\|[e;w]\|$, and its maximum over the samples is added to the
enclosure's residual before the margin. The trim is a fixed point of the
sampled map to $10^{-14}$ (\path{scripts/trimOffset.m}).

Effect at 8~m/s on a straight road, default box, first scheme: the first
coefficient bound drops from 0.11--0.16 to 0.05--0.08 and the certified set
grows from 0.63~m, 0.054~rad to 0.99~m, 0.078~rad, where the box binds. With
the wide box the set reaches 1.1--1.5~m (Table~\ref{tab:eight}).

\subsection{Force-level input map (feedback linearization of the front tire)}
The virtual input becomes $w=[\Delta F_{yf};\Delta b]$, the deviation of the
front lateral force and of the braking ratio from the trim's; the steering
angle is the one whose front slip gives that force on the Fiala curve below
its peak, $\delta=\operatorname{atan2}(v_y+l_f r,v_x)-\alpha(F,b)$ with
$\alpha$ the closed-form inverse of $F=-C\tan\alpha\,(1-q+q^2/3)$,
$q=C|\tan\alpha|/(3\mu F_z\eta)$, $\eta=\sqrt{1-b^2}$. The controller is
$w=Ke$, still a static gain, plus one closed-form inversion per hold. The
Jacobians of the sampled map through the inversion are computed by the chain
rule (the inversion's derivatives by central differences). The input box is
0.4 of the front lateral capacity (about 3~kN) and 0.125 of braking ratio.

Alone (pointwise Jacobians) the map gives little (0.68~m against 0.63~m):
within a 50-ms hold the steering is held while the slip moves, so the force
is not held and the front tire still enters the Jacobians. Combined with the
secant enclosure and the self-consistent growth it gives the largest sets of
the study at the required decay: 1.73~m and 0.138~rad at 8~m/s straight,
1.81~m and 0.140~rad on the curve, and the box (2~m) at 15~m/s
(Table~\ref{tab:eight}, Table~\ref{tab:points}).

\subsection{Self-consistent growth instead of a final fixed-gain step}
Each round of the first scheme designed $(P,K)$ against the enclosure of the
previous set along the previous gain, so the pair was never certified by the
enclosure it was designed with, and the final fixed-gain step had to repair
that. The study replaces it: round $i$ encloses the ray averages over
$\gamma\,\Omega_i$ and over inputs within $\delta\,\bar u$ of $K_i e$ (a
random band), and solves the LMI with the new set inside $\gamma\,\Omega_i$
($S_{i+1}\preceq\gamma S_i$) and the new gain within the band on it
($|(K_{i+1}-K_i)_j e|\le\delta\bar u_j$ on $\Omega_{i+1}$, a linear row in
$(S,Y)$). Every round's pair is then certified by its own enclosure; the
round with the largest fill is kept. The iteration starts from the plain LMI
inside half the box, because a wide box otherwise gives a first enclosure
over the whole box and a trivial LMI. The parameters matter
(Table~\ref{tab:eight}): $\gamma=1.3$, $\delta=0.1$ is the best of the
settings tried for both maps ($\delta=0.05$ stalls the gain and the growth;
$\delta=0.25$ widens the enclosure). The production script uses
$\gamma=1.3$, $\delta=0.1$, ten rounds.

\subsection{Saturation hull (Hu--Lin)}
The controller $\operatorname{sat}(Ke)$ at the input box, with the convex
hull $\operatorname{co}\{(D_iK+(I-D_i)H)e\}$ over the four saturation
patterns and an auxiliary gain $H$ bounded on the set ($|Ke|$ allowed up to
four times the box). No gain: 0.84~m against 0.99~m (first scheme) and
1.00~m against 1.14~m (self-consistent, same $\gamma,\delta$). The four
patterns quadruple the vertex blocks, and the certified contraction drops
(3.30~s). The steering box was not the binding limit, as the lever study of
October 10 had already found.

\subsection{Poly-quadratic Lyapunov function}
$V=e'P(\theta)e$ with $P$ bilinear in the two normalized axle slips
$\theta$ (ray-averaged in secant mode, computable online in closed form),
common slack $G$ and a constant gain (the de Oliveira--Bernussou--Geromel
form), all pairs (current vertex, next vertex), and the Jacobian deviations
regressed on $\theta$ before the principal directions of the remainder. The
regression leaves more residual than the principal directions alone (0.0096
against 0.0035 with three remainder directions), so the sets are smaller:
0.51--0.60~m; with six remainder directions (1024 vertex blocks, 30 minutes
per synthesis) 0.90~m, still below the common quadratic's 0.99~m with the
same enclosure budget. The scheduling that would help (heading and speed
errors as well as the slips) multiplies the vertex pairs beyond what SeDuMi
handles here.

\subsection{Slower decay}
With the secant enclosure and the wide box, $T=20$~s gives 1.45~m (first
scheme 1.79~m) and $T=1000$~s 1.37~m, against 1.14~m at $T=4$~s with the
same $\gamma,\delta$ and 1.73~m with the force map at $T=4$~s. The decay
rate is not what limits the set; the growth of the enclosure with the set
is.

\subsection{Interval certificate (one $P$, $K$ over a speed range)}
One certificate for the trims at 8, 9.5 and 11.5~m/s (curvatures 0 and
0.005) is infeasible at the first round (the trim-to-trim variation puts the
first coefficient bound at 0.4), and one over 11.5--15~m/s returns only a
0.11-m set. The error dynamics change too much with the cruise speed for one
quadratic; the former linear study's interval CLF did not carry the tire
variation. A gain-scheduled $(P(v_0),K(v_0))$ would be the next step; it was
not built.

\subsection{Polytope against ellipsoid on the same vertex set}
\path{scripts/polytopeCeiling.m} takes the final enclosure and gain of a
secant certificate, drops the residual, and compares the largest-fill
ellipsoid of the LMI at fixed $K$ with the maximal $\lambda$-contractive
polytope inside the box and the input rows under the same $K$ and the same
vertex matrices (Blanchini's set recursion, $\lambda=\sqrt{\rho}$, until it
stops changing). With four principal directions (16 vertices) the recursion
converges in 8 rounds to a 264-facet polytope with extents
$[2~\text{m},0.2~\text{rad},0.50,0.3,0.15]$, the box in four of five
coordinates, while the ellipsoid gives $[0.88,0.070,0.46,0.3,0.15]$
(\path{polytope-ceiling-comp4.txt}). Neither is a certificate (the residual
is dropped, and the polytope extends beyond the set the enclosure was sampled
on), but on the same linear-parameter-varying vertex set the polyhedral class
is 2.3 times larger in lateral error and 2.9 times in heading, which is the
universality of polyhedral Lyapunov functions for such systems. Making it a
certificate needs the residual's cone rows (each facet's condition becomes
$h'M_vx+\varepsilon\|h\|\,\|[I;K]x\|\le\lambda$, convex but not linear) and a
vertex representation of the polytope at each round; that is the most
promising construction not implemented here. With six directions the
recursion's redundancy removal did not finish
(\path{polytope-ceiling-comp6-unfinished.txt}).

\subsection{Not tried, and why}
Sum-of-squares certificates (the atan, $1/v_x$ and $\sqrt{1-b^2}$ terms need
rational recasting and the RK4 map is of high degree); the Lur'e function with
sector conditions (its benefit, secants in place of tangents, is what the
ray-averaged enclosure obtains exactly on the sampled map); funnel libraries,
the rollout-defined set and the safe-stop set (constructions of the planner's
terminal test, not of the certificate, and the campaign below shows that the
certified set is no longer the planner's limitation); the translation-symmetry
slab (a terminal set about any parallel line, one certificate by symmetry;
its membership and invariance were derived in the brainstorm and it remains a
design option); Hamilton--Jacobi reachability of the five-dimensional error
space as a ceiling; interval arithmetic for a rigorous enclosure (H3 stays a
sampled hypothesis with a margin).

\section{The production certificate}
\label{sec:production}
The production synthesis (\path{scripts/synthesizeClfMatrices.m}) uses the
ray-averaged enclosure (8 nodes, quadrature residual carried) and the
self-consistent growth ($\gamma=1.3$, $\delta=0.1$, ten rounds, start inside
half the box; a round with no design is retried with half and a quarter of
the growth and then with a band two and four times as wide, and the entry
records the growth and band of the kept round) on the steering-level
controller $u=u^*+Ke$, with the certification box widened to
$[2~\text{m},0.2~\text{rad},1~\text{m/s},0.3~\text{m/s},0.2~\text{rad/s}]$
and the input box unchanged (0.075~rad, 0.125). The certified sets
(\path{synthesis.txt}; \path{config/clfMatrices.json}) are in
Table~\ref{tab:production}: 1.49 and 1.59~m of lateral error at 8~m/s
(straight, curve) with headings of 0.142 and 0.144~rad, and the 2-m box at
15~m/s with 0.115~rad. Against the certificate of \texttt{8faf395} the
lateral extent is 2.4 to 2.8 times larger at 8~m/s and 2 to 2.9 times at
15~m/s; against the former unproven linear certificate (1.9~m, 0.18~rad at
8~m/s) it is about 0.8 of the size at 8~m/s and larger at 15~m/s. The hold
factors are 1.005--1.025, the independent sampled check finds worst
contractions of 0.941--0.963 against the required 0.975, and the enclosure
samples (over the 1.3-times inflated set and the input band) reach front
slips of 0.061--0.080~rad against the former 0.027~rad. The 8-m/s curve
needed the band fallback: with the band at 0.1 its second round admits no
design at any growth; the round with the band doubled does, and the later
rounds return to the narrow band.

For the record, the force-level controller with the same enclosure and
growth (commit \texttt{e2046e0}, the first adoption) certified 2.00~m at
every operating point with headings of 0.167~rad (8~m/s) and 0.118~rad
(15~m/s), hold factors 1.004--1.012; its campaign is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:campaign} beside the steering-level one. It was not kept
because its steering command passes through the inverse of the tire model,
which the user did not want the controller's input to depend on.

\section{What is proven and what is assumed}
Unchanged in structure from \path{TERMINAL_CONTROLLER_CERTIFICATE_20261010.tex}:
the terminal controller is an explicit linear feedback, its contraction on
the certified set follows from the vertex LMIs by the mean-value identity,
and the hold factor from the partial-hold enclosures. The hypotheses are H1
(the declared RK4 sampled model with exact state), H2 (the target follows its
forecast) and H3, now: the ray-averaged Jacobians $\bar G(e,Ke)$ along the
controller over the certified set lie in the recorded zonotope. H3 is
sampled (1200 boundary and 600 interior samples of the 1.3-times inflated set
and of the input band, margin 1.25, the quadrature's gap carried as a
rank-one residual) and re-checked on fresh samples by a unit test. The input
band is part of the certificate: the gain the LMI returns lies within it by
a constraint, so the enclosure covers the controller actually used.
